## 1\. What a Toolbar Actually Is
A toolbar is a **grouped set of controls that act on a view or a selection** — buttons, toggles, menus, and separators lined up so related actions sit together and stay in reach. Think a text editor's formatting bar (bold, italic, lists, alignment), a table's action bar (filter, sort, export, delete-selected), or a canvas app's tool palette (select, pen, shape, zoom).

That sounds like "a row of buttons," and that's exactly the trap. The word hides a real distinction people collapse:

*   **Toolbar (WAI-ARIA** **`role="toolbar"`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*):** a container of related controls that is **one Tab stop**. You Tab _to_ the toolbar, then **arrow keys** move between the controls inside it. This is called _roving tabindex_ and it is the defining feature. It exists to keep a 12-icon formatting bar from costing keyboard users twelve Tab presses to get past.
*   **A plain row of buttons:** no shared semantics, no `role`, every button is its own Tab stop. Fine for two or three unrelated buttons (a "Cancel / Save" dialog footer is _not_ a toolbar). Wrong the moment you have a dense cluster of related actions.
*   **Menubar (****`role="menubar"`****):** looks like a toolbar but its children are `role="menuitem"` that open menus (the File / Edit / View bar of a desktop app). Different pattern, different keyboard contract.
*   **Navigation bar:** a row of _links_ that take you elsewhere. That's a `<nav>` full of `<a>`, not a toolbar of actions.

The single most common mistake: building a visually-correct toolbar and leaving every control in the tab order. It _looks_ done. It fails the pattern. **If it's a related cluster of actions on a view, it's a toolbar, and a toolbar is one tab stop with arrow-key navigation — anything less is just buttons wearing a toolbar's clothes.**
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The toolbar is where a view's _power_ lives. Every capability a user has over the current document, table, or canvas is expressed as a control in some bar. That gives it outsized weight:

*   **Efficiency is the whole point.** Toolbars exist to put frequent actions one click away instead of buried in menus. Get the grouping, ordering, and density wrong and you've built a worse menu with a bigger footprint.
*   **It's the densest accessibility surface you'll ship.** Ten to twenty controls in a strip, most of them icon-only, is the hardest keyboard-and-screen-reader problem in everyday UI. The roving-tabindex contract is non-negotiable, and it's the one senior interviewers probe because juniors always skip it.
*   **Icon-only means labels are load-bearing.** Toolbars lean on glyphs to stay compact. Every icon-only control needs a real accessible name or it's an unlabeled button to a screen reader — a wall of "button, button, button."
*   **It sets the "this app is serious" tone.** A formatting bar that reflows badly, hides actions with no overflow, or has no pressed state on the active tool feels amateur even when every action works. Perceived competence is mostly toolbar polish.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy of a Toolbar
Every well-built toolbar is composed of predictable parts:

*   **Container:** the bar itself. Carries `role="toolbar"`, an `aria-label`, an orientation, padding, and a background. This is the single Tab stop.
*   **Controls:** the interactive children — push buttons (actions), toggle buttons (`aria-pressed`), menu buttons (`aria-haspopup` / `aria-expanded`), and occasionally inputs (a zoom % field, a search box).
*   **Groups:** logical clusters of related controls (text-style group, alignment group, insert group). Often a `role="group"` with its own `aria-label`.
*   **Separators:** the thin dividers between groups. `role="separator"` with the correct `aria-orientation` — decorative to the eye, structural to the semantics.
*   **Segments:** a run of mutually-exclusive options (left/center/right align) built as a `radiogroup` of `role="radio"` controls, not a pile of independent toggles. See the Segmented Control sibling.
*   **Overflow trigger:** the "⋯ More" menu button that holds controls that don't fit. It's the pressure-release valve for responsive toolbars.
*   **Roving focus token:** exactly one control at any time has `tabindex="0"`; every other has `tabindex="-1"`. Invisible, but it's the skeleton.
*   **Tooltips:** the hover/focus labels for icon-only controls — reinforcement, never the _only_ accessible name.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes & Density
Toolbars live in chrome, so they run **denser** than primary content. Height is driven by the control size plus the bar's own padding. A typical scale:

| Density | Bar height | Control target | Icon | Gap | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact | 32px | 28px (44px hit area via padding) | 16px | 2px | Dense editors, data grids, pro tools |
| Default | 40px | 32px | 20px | 4px | Most app toolbars |
| Comfortable | 48px | 40px | 24px | 8px | Touch-first, mobile formatting bars |

Rules that matter:

*   **Visual size ≠ hit target.** A 28px-tall compact button still needs a **≥44px** effective tap target — expand it with padding or a pseudo-element, don't shrink the touchable area to match the icon.
*   **Group gaps > control gaps.** Controls within a group sit tight (2–4px); groups are separated by a larger gap or a separator. The spacing _is_ the grouping.
*   **Height comes from a token,** not a magic number, so compact/default/comfortable are one variable swap across every toolbar in the product.
*   **Don't mix densities in one bar.** A 40px bar with one 48px control reads as broken.
* * *

## 5\. States
A toolbar has states at **two levels**: the container and each control. Missing the container-level and toggle states is the tell of an amateur build.

**Container states:**
1. **Enabled (rest)** — the baseline bar.
2. **Focus-within** — a control inside holds focus. The bar itself has no focus ring, but the focused _control_ does (`:focus-visible`).
3. **Orientation** — horizontal (default) or vertical, reflected in `aria-orientation` because it changes which arrow keys navigate.
4. **Overflowing / collapsed** — some controls have moved into the overflow menu because the bar doesn't fit.
5. **Sticky / floating** — pinned to a scroll edge or following a text selection.
6. **Disabled (whole bar)** — rare, but a toolbar over an empty/loading view can be fully inert.

**Per-control states** (inherit everything from the Button doc, plus toolbar-specific ones):
1. **Default / hover / focus-visible / active** — same as any button.
2. **Pressed (toggle on)** — `aria-pressed="true"`. The active tool (Bold is on) must look _persistently_ engaged, not just hovered.
3. **Selected (radio segment)** — `aria-checked="true"` for one-of-many runs (alignment).
4. **Disabled** — the action isn't currently valid (no selection, so "Delete" is off). The nuance in the roving model — skip it or keep it focusable — is §10's crux.
5. **Roving-active vs roving-inactive** — exactly one control is the current arrow-key target (`tabindex="0"`); the rest are `-1`. Not visible, but it's a real state you manage.
6. **In-overflow** — the control has been moved into the "⋯" menu; it still must be reachable and operable.

The distinction juniors miss: **hover is transient, pressed is persistent.** A toggle button that only changes on hover and forgets it's "on" is the classic broken formatting bar.
* * *

## 6\. Types & Variants
Toolbars vary by **content**, **orientation**, and **behavior**.

By what they contain:
*   **Action toolbar:** push buttons that do something now (export, print, delete-selected). The table action bar.
*   **Formatting toolbar:** mostly toggle buttons and segments reflecting the state of a selection (bold on, left-aligned). The text-editor bar.
*   **Tool palette:** a single-select run — one active tool at a time (select / pen / eraser). Really a `radiogroup` styled as a bar.
*   **Mixed toolbar:** groups of each, separated by dividers — the common real-world case (a rich-text editor: undo/redo actions · bold/italic toggles · alignment segment · insert menu).

By orientation:
*   **Horizontal (default):** left-to-right run. `aria-orientation="horizontal"`; Left/Right arrows navigate.
*   **Vertical:** a stacked rail (design apps, IDE gutters). `aria-orientation="vertical"`; Up/Down arrows navigate.

By behavior:
*   **Static:** always in the same spot in the chrome.
*   **Sticky:** pinned to the top/bottom of a scroll container so it never scrolls away.
*   **Floating / contextual:** appears near a selection (the "bubble" formatting bar over highlighted text, the row-hover action bar in a table).
*   **Overflow / responsive:** collapses lower-priority controls into a "⋯" menu when space runs out (priority-plus pattern).
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a toolbar when the user has **several related actions on the same view or selection** and you want them persistent and one click away. Don't reach for one when:

*   You have **two or three unrelated buttons** — that's just a button row (a dialog's "Cancel / Save" footer is not a toolbar; don't bolt `role="toolbar"` onto it).
*   The actions are **navigation between destinations** — that's a `<nav>` of links, or tabs.
*   You have **many one-of-many choices that ARE the content** — a full segmented control or a select, not a toolbar of toggles.
*   The actions are **rare or destructive-heavy** — bury them in a menu; a toolbar is for frequency.

Placement heuristics:
*   **Top of the view for global actions** (document-level: save, share, export). Left-aligned, reading-order = priority order.
*   **Contextual bar near the selection** for selection-scoped actions (formatting the highlighted text). It appears on selection, dismisses on blur.
*   **Order by frequency, group by concept.** Most-used group first; within a group, most-used control first. Undo/redo live together; alignment lives together.
*   **Keep the primary/destructive actions apart** — "Delete all" should not sit one pixel from "Save."
* * *

## 8\. Toolbars Across Design Systems
Same component, different philosophies. Knowing these makes you fluent in any codebase.

*   **Material Design (Google):** the "top app bar" and contextual action bars. Elevation on scroll, overflow into a three-dot menu is a first-class pattern (`MenuItem` with `showAsAction`). Icon buttons with ripples; leading nav icon + trailing action cluster.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** the `UIToolbar` / macOS toolbar. Restrained, SF Symbols, heavy use of the **flexible spacer** to push groups apart. iOS 26 renders it in **Liquid Glass**. Toolbars are customizable by the user on macOS — a signature Apple trait.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** the `CommandBar` — the canonical overflow toolbar. Primary commands on the left, an automatic overflow "⋯" that _measures_ and moves commands as the window resizes. The gold standard for priority-plus.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** less a dedicated `Toolbar` and more `Space` + `Button.Group` + `Dropdown` composed in table headers and page headers. Dense, action-heavy, built for admin UIs.
*   **Radix UI (headless):** ships a real `Toolbar` primitive with roving focus, `ToolbarButton`, `ToolbarToggleGroup` (single/multiple), `ToolbarSeparator`, and `ToolbarLink` baked in. The 2024+ web reference for correct semantics.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** no prescribed toolbar; you compose one and are responsible for wiring the roving tabindex yourself (the part people skip). Usually wrapped in a `Toolbar` component to enforce it once.
*   **Bootstrap:** `.btn-toolbar` as a flex container holding `.btn-group`s. Pure layout — it gives you the visual grouping and `role="toolbar"`/`aria-label` in the docs, but roving tabindex is on you.
* * *

## 8b. Visual Styles (the same toolbar, eleven skins)
Design systems are _whose_ rules; visual styles are _which look_. The same semantic toolbar can wear any of these. Full guidance in the [Design Styles gallery](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551).

*   **Flat:** solid bar, crisp separators, no shadow. Reads instantly, scales fast.
*   **Material:** subtle elevation that lifts on scroll, ripple on each control, separators as thin dividers.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent bar with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over content. Common for floating formatting bars — watch contrast on the icons.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe material. The bar refracts what scrolls behind it with a specular rim and a sheen that shifts with motion. Floating toolbars are where this sings — a bubble bar over a document. SwiftUI emits it natively; the web approximates it.
*   **Neumorphism:** the bar and controls share one surface with dual light/dark shadows. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent use only, and pressed states get hard to read.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** the classic "real toolbar" — beveled buttons, inset groove separators, gradient bar. The Office-2003 lineage.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** hard offset shadow on the bar, thick borders between controls, zero radius, high-personality color.
*   **Claymorphism:** puffy rounded controls, soft inner light, friendly. Works for playful creative tools.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue bar on dark. Premium; honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-invisible bar, generous whitespace as the grouping, hairline separators, type does the work.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod controls on a deep Eminence/Night bar, confident radius, a warm glow on the active tool and the focus ring. The house default; the dark saturated bar makes a Liquid-Glass floating variant sing.

**Rule that never changes:** style is skin, behavior is skeleton. Roving tabindex, `role="toolbar"`, the arrow-key contract, `aria-pressed`, separators, and the ≥44px targets are identical across all eleven. Guard glass / liquid-glass / neumorphism for icon contrast; guard aurora / liquid-glass for `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 9\. The Code

### 9.1 HTML (the foundation)
The semantics first, before any framework abstracts them.

```plain
<!-- Horizontal formatting toolbar: ONE tab stop, arrow keys move within -->
<div role="toolbar" aria-label="Text formatting" aria-orientation="horizontal" class="toolbar">

  <!-- Group 1: history actions (push buttons) -->
  <div role="group" aria-label="History" class="toolbar__group">
    <button type="button" class="tb-btn" aria-label="Undo" tabindex="0">
      <svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><!-- undo icon --></svg>
    </button>
    <button type="button" class="tb-btn" aria-label="Redo" tabindex="-1">
      <svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><!-- redo icon --></svg>
    </button>
  </div>

  <div role="separator" aria-orientation="vertical" class="toolbar__sep"></div>

  <!-- Group 2: text style (TOGGLE buttons, independent on/off) -->
  <div role="group" aria-label="Text style" class="toolbar__group">
    <button type="button" class="tb-btn" aria-label="Bold" aria-pressed="false" tabindex="-1">
      <svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><!-- bold icon --></svg>
    </button>
    <button type="button" class="tb-btn" aria-label="Italic" aria-pressed="false" tabindex="-1">
      <svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><!-- italic icon --></svg>
    </button>
  </div>

  <div role="separator" aria-orientation="vertical" class="toolbar__sep"></div>

  <!-- Group 3: alignment (RADIO segment, one-of-many) -->
  <div role="radiogroup" aria-label="Alignment" class="toolbar__group">
    <button type="button" class="tb-btn" role="radio" aria-checked="true"  aria-label="Align left"   tabindex="-1"></button>
    <button type="button" class="tb-btn" role="radio" aria-checked="false" aria-label="Align center" tabindex="-1"></button>
    <button type="button" class="tb-btn" role="radio" aria-checked="false" aria-label="Align right"  tabindex="-1"></button>
  </div>

  <div role="separator" aria-orientation="vertical" class="toolbar__sep"></div>

  <!-- Overflow: a menu button holding what doesn't fit -->
  <button type="button" class="tb-btn" aria-label="More actions"
          aria-haspopup="menu" aria-expanded="false" tabindex="-1">⋯</button>
</div>
```

Key HTML rules:
*   **`role="toolbar"`** **+** **`aria-label`** **on the container.** The label is what a screen reader announces when focus enters ("Text formatting toolbar").
*   **Exactly one control has** **`tabindex="0"`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*;** every other is `tabindex="-1"`. That's the roving-tabindex resting state — the whole bar is one Tab stop.
*   **`aria-orientation`** must match reality, because it tells assistive tech (and your own JS) which arrows navigate.
*   **Icon-only buttons need** **`aria-label`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*;** the `<svg>` is `aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"` so it never becomes its own stop or duplicate label.
*   **Separators are** **`role="separator"`** with `aria-orientation` — in a horizontal bar a vertical divider separates horizontally-arranged items, so its own orientation is `vertical`.
*   **Toggle =** **`aria-pressed`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*; one-of-many =** **`role="radio"`** **+** **`aria-checked`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*.** Never fake a segment with three independent toggles.

The load-bearing detail: the `[aria-pressed="true"]` / `[aria-checked="true"]` styling. A toolbar whose active tool looks identical to its inactive tools is the single most common visual bug — the ARIA state and the visual state must move together.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (roving tabindex, the real component)
The longest block, because this is where the pattern actually lives. A reusable `Toolbar` that manages roving focus, orientation, and disabled-skip, plus toggle and radio children.

```typescript
import {
  createContext, useContext, useRef, useCallback,
  KeyboardEvent, ButtonHTMLAttributes, ReactNode,
} from "react";

type Orientation = "horizontal" | "vertical";

interface ToolbarCtx {
  orientation: Orientation;
  register: (el: HTMLButtonElement | null) => void;
  onKeyDown: (e: KeyboardEvent) => void;
}
const Ctx = createContext<ToolbarCtx | null>(null);

export function Toolbar({
  orientation = "horizontal",
  label,
  children,
}: {
  orientation?: Orientation;
  label: string;
  children: ReactNode;
}) {
  // Live list of focusable controls, in DOM order.
  const items = useRef<HTMLButtonElement[]>([]);
  const active = useRef(0);

  const register = useCallback((el: HTMLButtonElement | null) => {
    if (el && !items.current.includes(el)) items.current.push(el);
    // React calls this ref with the node on mount and null on unmount. Prune any
    // control that's been detached from the DOM, or the roving list leaks stale
    // nodes and the tab stop / arrow focus can land on a control that's gone.
    items.current = items.current.filter((n) => n.isConnected);
    // First control is the tab stop; the rest are removed from tab order.
    items.current.forEach((n, i) => (n.tabIndex = i === 0 ? 0 : -1));
  }, []);

  const move = useCallback((dir: 1 | -1) => {
    // Drop controls that have unmounted since the last render, then skip disabled.
    items.current = items.current.filter((n) => n.isConnected);
    const list = items.current.filter(
      (n) => !n.hasAttribute("disabled") && n.getAttribute("aria-disabled") !== "true",
    );
    if (!list.length) return;
    const curr = list.indexOf(items.current[active.current]);
    const next = (curr + dir + list.length) % list.length;   // wraps
    const el = list[next];
    items.current.forEach((n) => (n.tabIndex = n === el ? 0 : -1));
    active.current = items.current.indexOf(el);
    el.focus();   // move REAL focus, not just the visual
  }, []);

  const onKeyDown = useCallback(
    (e: KeyboardEvent) => {
      const nextKey = orientation === "horizontal" ? "ArrowRight" : "ArrowDown";
      const prevKey = orientation === "horizontal" ? "ArrowLeft" : "ArrowUp";
      if (e.key === nextKey) { e.preventDefault(); move(1); }
      else if (e.key === prevKey) { e.preventDefault(); move(-1); }
      else if (e.key === "Home") { e.preventDefault(); active.current = 0; move(0 as 1); }
      else if (e.key === "End") {
        e.preventDefault();
        active.current = items.current.length - 1; move(0 as 1);
      }
    },
    [orientation, move],
  );

  return (
    <Ctx.Provider value={{ orientation, register, onKeyDown }}>
      <div
        role="toolbar"
        aria-label={label}
        aria-orientation={orientation}
        onKeyDown={onKeyDown}
        className="toolbar"
      >
        {children}
      </div>
    </Ctx.Provider>
  );
}

interface TbBtnProps extends ButtonHTMLAttributes<HTMLButtonElement> {
  label: string;           // mandatory accessible name (icon-only)
  pressed?: boolean;       // toggle → aria-pressed
  icon: ReactNode;
}
export function ToolbarButton({ label, pressed, icon, ...rest }: TbBtnProps) {
  const ctx = useContext(Ctx)!;
  return (
    <button
      type="button"
      ref={ctx.register}
      aria-label={label}
      aria-pressed={pressed}
      className="tb-btn"
      {...rest}
    >
      <span aria-hidden="true">{icon}</span>
    </button>
  );
}
```

Usage:

```typescript
<Toolbar label="Text formatting" orientation="horizontal">
  <ToolbarButton label="Undo" icon={<UndoIcon />} onClick={undo} />
  <ToolbarButton label="Bold" icon={<BoldIcon />} pressed={isBold}
                 onClick={() => toggleBold()} />
  <ToolbarButton label="Delete" icon={<TrashIcon />} disabled={!hasSelection}
                 onClick={del} />
</Toolbar>
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:
*   **Roving tabindex is managed centrally,** not sprinkled on each button. The container owns the single-tab-stop invariant.
*   **Arrow direction follows** **`orientation`** — the same component is correct horizontal or vertical, and `aria-orientation` stays in sync with the keys.
*   **`move()`** **filters out disabled controls,** so arrows skip them (the WAI-ARIA default). Flip that filter to _keep_ disabled-but-focusable controls when discoverability matters — §10 covers the tradeoff.
*   **Home/End jump to ends** — part of the toolbar keyboard contract, not optional.
*   **`label`** **is a required prop,** so an icon-only button can't be created without an accessible name. TypeScript enforces the a11y rule.
*   **Real focus moves** (`el.focus()`), not just a highlight class — screen-reader and keyboard focus must actually travel.
*   **Unmounted controls are pruned** — the ref callback and `move()` drop any detached node, so a dynamic bar (overflow, adaptive) never leaves a stale control holding the tab stop or catching arrow focus.

### 9.4 Overflow (the priority-plus pattern)
The hard part of a real toolbar: when it doesn't fit, lower-priority controls collapse into a "⋯ More" menu, and those items **stay keyboard-reachable**. Measure, don't guess.

```typescript
import { useState, useRef, useLayoutEffect } from "react";

// Each action has a priority; higher stays visible longest.
type Action = { id: string; label: string; priority: number; run: () => void };

export function OverflowToolbar({ actions }: { actions: Action[] }) {
  const barRef = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
  const [visibleCount, setVisibleCount] = useState(actions.length);
  const sorted = [...actions].sort((a, b) => b.priority - a.priority);

  useLayoutEffect(() => {
    const bar = barRef.current;
    if (!bar) return;
    const ro = new ResizeObserver(() => {
      const avail = bar.clientWidth - 40;         // reserve room for the ⋯ button
      let used = 0, fit = 0;
      for (const child of Array.from(bar.querySelectorAll<HTMLElement>("[data-tb-item]"))) {
        used += child.offsetWidth + 4;
        if (used <= avail) fit++; else break;
      }
      setVisibleCount(fit);
    });
    ro.observe(bar);
    return () => ro.disconnect();
  }, [actions]);

  const shown = sorted.slice(0, visibleCount);
  const hidden = sorted.slice(visibleCount);

  return (
    <div role="toolbar" aria-label="Actions" ref={barRef} className="toolbar">
      {shown.map((a) => (
        <button key={a.id} data-tb-item type="button" className="tb-btn"
                aria-label={a.label} onClick={a.run} />
      ))}
      {hidden.length > 0 && (
        <OverflowMenu items={hidden} />   {/* aria-haspopup="menu" trigger + role=menu */}
      )}
    </div>
  );
}
```

Overflow rules people get wrong:
*   **The overflow trigger is a menu button** (`aria-haspopup="menu"`, `aria-expanded`), and its menu uses `role="menu"` / `role="menuitem"` — the items moved into it become menu items, not orphaned buttons. See the Dropdown Menu sibling.
*   **Hidden ≠ gone.** Every action that collapses into "⋯" must still be operable by keyboard and screen reader. Never `display:none` an action out of existence — move it.
*   **Priority, not source order,** decides who survives. The "Delete selected" you can't afford to hide gets the highest priority.
*   **Measure with** **`ResizeObserver`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*,** not window `resize` — the toolbar can shrink because a sidebar opened, not because the window changed.
*   **Reserve room for the trigger** in the math, or you get the flicker where adding the "⋯" button pushes one more item out, which removes the "⋯"…

### 9.5 Vanilla JavaScript (the roving core, no framework)
The pattern everything else abstracts. Worth knowing cold.

```javascript
function makeToolbar(el) {
  const horizontal = el.getAttribute("aria-orientation") !== "vertical";
  const nextKey = horizontal ? "ArrowRight" : "ArrowDown";
  const prevKey = horizontal ? "ArrowLeft" : "ArrowUp";
  const items = () =>
    [...el.querySelectorAll('[role="button"],button,[role="radio"]')]
      .filter((n) => !n.disabled && n.getAttribute("aria-disabled") !== "true");

  // Resting state: first control is the only tab stop.
  items().forEach((n, i) => (n.tabIndex = i === 0 ? 0 : -1));

  el.addEventListener("keydown", (e) => {
    const list = items();
    const curr = list.indexOf(document.activeElement);
    if (curr < 0) return;
    let next = curr;
    if (e.key === nextKey) next = (curr + 1) % list.length;
    else if (e.key === prevKey) next = (curr - 1 + list.length) % list.length;
    else if (e.key === "Home") next = 0;
    else if (e.key === "End") next = list.length - 1;
    else return;
    e.preventDefault();
    list[curr].tabIndex = -1;
    list[next].tabIndex = 0;
    list[next].focus();
  });
}
document.querySelectorAll('[role="toolbar"]').forEach(makeToolbar);
```

Note the guard: `preventDefault()` only on the keys the toolbar owns, so Tab still escapes the bar and typing still works in any embedded input.

### 9.11 Web Component (framework-agnostic)
A toolbar that works in any framework or none, encapsulating the roving logic in one custom element.

```typescript
class UiToolbar extends HTMLElement {
  connectedCallback() {
    this.setAttribute("role", "toolbar");
    if (!this.hasAttribute("aria-orientation"))
      this.setAttribute("aria-orientation", "horizontal");

  disconnectedCallback() { this.removeEventListener("keydown", this._onKey); }

          : e.key === "End" ? list.length - 1 : i;
    if (j === i) return;
    e.preventDefault();
    list[i].tabIndex = -1; list[j].tabIndex = 0; list[j].focus();
```

### 9.13 Testing the Toolbar
A toolbar is a _behavior contract_, so you test the contract: one tab stop, arrow navigation, correct ARIA, and no axe violations.

```typescript
import { render, screen } from "@testing-library/react";
import userEvent from "@testing-library/user-event";
import { axe } from "jest-axe";
import { describe, it, expect } from "vitest";
import { EditorToolbar } from "./EditorToolbar";

describe("Toolbar", () => {
  it("exposes a labelled toolbar role", () => {
    render(<EditorToolbar />);
    expect(screen.getByRole("toolbar", { name: /formatting/i })).toBeInTheDocument();
  });

  it("is a SINGLE tab stop", async () => {
    render(<><button>before</button><EditorToolbar /><button>after</button></>);
    await userEvent.tab();                       // before
    await userEvent.tab();                        // enters toolbar (one control)
    expect(screen.getByRole("button", { name: /bold/i })).toHaveFocus();
    await userEvent.tab();                        // should LEAVE the toolbar entirely
    expect(screen.getByRole("button", { name: /after/i })).toHaveFocus();
  });

  it("moves focus with arrow keys, not Tab", async () => {
    render(<EditorToolbar />);
    await userEvent.tab();
    expect(screen.getByRole("button", { name: /bold/i })).toHaveFocus();
    await userEvent.keyboard("{ArrowRight}");
    expect(screen.getByRole("button", { name: /italic/i })).toHaveFocus();
    await userEvent.keyboard("{Home}");
    expect(screen.getByRole("button", { name: /bold/i })).toHaveFocus();
  });

  it("announces toggle state via aria-pressed", async () => {
    render(<EditorToolbar />);
    const bold = screen.getByRole("button", { name: /bold/i, pressed: false });
    await userEvent.click(bold);
    expect(screen.getByRole("button", { name: /bold/i, pressed: true })).toBeInTheDocument();
  });

  it("has no axe violations", async () => {
    const { container } = render(<EditorToolbar />);
    expect(await axe(container)).toHaveNoViolations();
  });
});
```

What to actually assert (and what to skip):
*   **Do test:** the `toolbar` role is labelled, Tab enters once and Tab _leaves_ (the single-stop proof), arrows move within, Home/End jump, toggles flip `aria-pressed`, overflow items are reachable, axe is clean.
*   **Don't test:** which control happens to hold `tabindex="0"` at rest, exact class names, icon SVG paths. Implementation details that break tests without catching bugs.
*   **The one test juniors skip:** the "Tab _leaves_ the toolbar" assertion. That's the entire reason the pattern exists; if Tab walks through every control, the roving model is broken and this test is the only thing that catches it.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
This is the section that separates a toolbar from a row of buttons. Semantics and keyboard here are the product.

**Roles & structure:**
*   Container: `role="toolbar"` + a required `aria-label` (or `aria-labelledby`). Native `<div>` styled as a bar without the role is invisible-as-a-toolbar to assistive tech.
*   `aria-orientation="horizontal" | "vertical"` — must match the visual layout; it tells AT and your JS which arrows navigate.
*   Groups: `role="group"` + `aria-label` for logical clusters; `role="radiogroup"` for one-of-many segments.
*   Separators: `role="separator"` with the correct `aria-orientation`.

**Keyboard map (the contract):**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| `Tab` | Move into the toolbar (lands on the one control with `tabindex="0"`), and next `Tab` moves out — the toolbar is a single stop |
| `→` / `←` (horizontal) or `↓` / `↑` (vertical) | Move focus between controls; the moved-to control becomes the new tab stop |
| `Home` / `End` | Jump to first / last control |
| `Enter` / `Space` | Activate the focused control (toggle, run, open menu) |
| `Esc` | Close an open overflow/menu, returning focus to its trigger |

**Roving tabindex (the mechanism):** exactly one control is `tabindex="0"`; all others `tabindex="-1"`. Arrow keys move real focus and hand the `0` to the newly-focused control. This is what makes the toolbar one Tab stop. It is the single defining accessibility feature of the pattern — miss it and you don't have a toolbar, you have a slow row of buttons. Persist the last-focused index so that when the user Tabs back into the toolbar they re-enter where they left off, not at the first control every time.

**Disabled controls — the nuance:** WAI-ARIA lets you choose:
*   **Skip (****`disabled`****):** the default; arrows jump over disabled controls. Cleaner traversal, but the disabled action becomes _undiscoverable_ to a keyboard user (they never learn it exists).
*   **Keep focusable (****`aria-disabled="true"`****, not** **`disabled`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*):** the control stays in the roving rotation, is announced as "dimmed/unavailable," but doesn't activate. Preferred in dense toolbars where discoverability matters — the user learns the action is there and why it's off. You then block activation in JS.
*   Pick per toolbar. Rule of thumb: transient/obvious disables (Delete with no selection) can skip; persistent/important ones should stay focusable and explain themselves.

**Labels:** every icon-only control needs an accessible name (`aria-label` or visually-hidden text). Tooltips reinforce it visually but are **never the only** name — a tooltip that only appears on hover is invisible to keyboard-first screen-reader users. Names should read out of context ("Align left," not "Left").

**State to screen readers:** `aria-pressed` (toggle on/off), `aria-checked` (radio segment), `aria-expanded` + `aria-haspopup` (overflow/menu trigger), `aria-disabled` (unavailable-but-present).

**Contrast & targets:** icon/glyph vs. bar background ≥ 3:1 (non-text UI, WCAG 1.4.11); text ≥ 4.5:1. Every control keeps a ≥44px effective tap target even when the visual glyph is 16–20px. Never signal the active tool by color alone — the pressed background plus a border or fill change.

**Focus visible:** the bar has no ring, but the focused control must show a clear `:focus-visible` ring, and focus must be _visibly_ tracked as arrows move.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The toolbar looks solved but keeps evolving:

*   **Contextual "bubble" toolbars:** the formatting bar that materializes over a text selection (Medium, Notion, Google Docs). It follows the selection, dismisses on blur, and is the dominant modern formatting pattern — less chrome, action where the eye already is.
*   **AI-native controls:** a "✨ Ask AI / Improve / Summarize" control as a first-class toolbar item with a streaming/progress state replacing the old spinner. Toolbars are becoming the launchpad for agentic actions on a selection.
*   **Adaptive / usage-ranked toolbars:** the bar reorders or promotes controls based on what this user actually uses, pushing the rest to overflow. Powerful, but pin the layout enough that muscle memory survives.
*   **Liquid Glass floating bars:** on Apple platforms the contextual bar is now a refractive glass pill that reacts to the content scrolling beneath it (iOS 26). The web approximates with `backdrop-filter`.
*   **CSS Anchor Positioning + Popover API:** floating and overflow toolbars can now be positioned and toggled declaratively (`popover`, `anchor()`), cutting the JS that used to place a bubble bar.
*   **Container-query toolbars:** the bar decides its own density and overflow from its _own_ width via `container-type`, not the viewport — correct inside resizable panels and split views.
*   **Voice & command-palette parity:** the same actions exposed as toolbar controls _and_ as a ⌘K command palette, sharing one handler map.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers (the silent mistakes)
None of these throw an error. They just make the app feel slow, broken, or amateur — and they're all fixable in an afternoon.

*   **Every control left in the tab order.** The cardinal sin. A keyboard user hits Tab twelve times to get past your formatting bar. It works with a mouse, so it ships, and it fails every accessibility audit. Roving tabindex or it isn't a toolbar.
*   **Icon-only buttons with no accessible name.** A screen reader reads "button, button, button." The bar is unusable non-visually. Every glyph needs a real label.
*   **Active tool that doesn't look active.** No pressed state on the on-toggle means the user can't tell if Bold is engaged. The `aria-pressed` state and the visual must move together.
*   **No overflow — the toolbar just clips or wraps.** On a narrow view, controls vanish off the edge or the bar reflows into three ugly rows. Priority-plus overflow into "⋯" is the fix; wrapping is not.
*   **Overflow that hides actions from the keyboard.** Collapsing controls into a menu that isn't a real `role="menu"` (or worse, `display:none`) makes them unreachable. Hidden must still mean operable.
*   **Fake segments.** Three independent toggle buttons for left/center/right lets all three be "on" at once and confuses screen readers. One-of-many is a `radiogroup`.
*   **Tooltip as the only label.** Hover-only text is invisible to touch and keyboard-first users. Tooltip reinforces; `aria-label` names.
*   **Sub-44px touch targets crammed edge to edge.** A dense mobile formatting bar with 24px targets is a mis-tap generator. Keep the hit area even when the glyph shrinks.
*   **Density mismatch / no grouping.** A flat run of fifteen identical icons with no separators is a search task every time. Group by concept; let spacing and separators do the work.

**The through-line:** every one of these fails _quietly_. No crash, just a bar that a subset of users can't operate and everyone finds slightly harder than it should be. Audit the toolbar's keyboard path before you audit anything visual.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
The "senior move" versions of the patterns above.

**Sticky / floating with the Popover API + anchor positioning.** A contextual bar can be a top-layer `popover` anchored to the selection, no z-index war and no manual JS positioning:

```plain
<button popovertarget="fmt-bar" id="sel-anchor" hidden></button>
<div id="fmt-bar" popover role="toolbar" aria-label="Selection formatting"
     style="position-anchor: --sel; bottom: anchor(--sel top);">…</div>
```

Open it on `selectionchange`, and it lives in the top layer above everything, dismissing on outside interaction for free.

**Roving tabindex vs.** **`aria-activedescendant`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*.** Two valid ways to run the keyboard:
*   _Roving tabindex_ (this doc's default): real DOM focus moves; simplest, works with native buttons.
*   _`aria-activedescendant`_: DOM focus stays on the container, and `aria-activedescendant` points at the "virtually focused" child. Better when the toolbar also hosts a live text field, or for very large control counts where moving real focus is expensive. Trade-off: you render the focus ring yourself.

**Design tokens for density.** One tier drives the whole bar's compact/default/comfortable switch:

```css
:root {
  --tb-h: 32px; --tb-gap: 4px; --tb-icon: 20px; --tb-pad: 4px;
}
[data-density="compact"]     { --tb-h: 28px; --tb-gap: 2px; --tb-icon: 16px; }
[data-density="comfortable"] { --tb-h: 40px; --tb-gap: 8px; --tb-icon: 24px; }
.tb-btn { block-size: var(--tb-h); }
```

**TypeScript-enforced labels.** Make an icon-only toolbar button impossible to create without a name:

```typescript
type IconControl = {
  icon: React.ReactNode;
  "aria-label": string;   // MANDATORY — no label, no compile
  pressed?: boolean;
  onClick: () => void;
};
```

**Focus restoration.** When the overflow menu closes (Esc or select), return focus to the "⋯" trigger — never drop focus to `<body>`. When a floating bar dismisses, return focus to the editor/selection it acted on. Losing focus to the top of the page is the most common keyboard regression in toolbars.

**RTL & logical properties.** In right-to-left layouts the whole bar mirrors: use `padding-inline`, and in the keydown handler the _visual_ meaning of ArrowRight/ArrowLeft flips. Read direction from `dir` and swap `fwd`/`back` so "next" always means "next in reading order."

**Toolbar + Panel pattern.** Toolbar items that toggle a side panel (Inspector, Layers, Comments) wire the toggle to the region it controls: `aria-pressed` for the on/off state _plus_ `aria-controls` pointing at the panel's `id` and `aria-expanded` mirroring whether it's open.

```plain
<button aria-pressed="true" aria-controls="inspector-panel" aria-expanded="true">
  Inspector
</button>
<aside id="inspector-panel" role="region" aria-label="Inspector">
  <!-- panel content -->
</aside>
```

**Sticky toolbar with a scroll shadow.** A bar that sticks on scroll should signal "you've scrolled under me." Detect it without a scroll listener by watching a 1px sentinel above the bar with `IntersectionObserver`, and toggle a shadow class when it leaves the viewport:

```javascript
const toolbar = document.querySelector('.toolbar--sticky');
const observer = new IntersectionObserver(
  ([entry]) => toolbar.classList.toggle('is-scrolled', !entry.isIntersecting),
  { threshold: 1, rootMargin: '-1px 0px 0px 0px' }
);
observer.observe(document.querySelector('.toolbar-sentinel')); // invisible 1px element above toolbar
```

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A toolbar feels free. A dense one on every row of a table isn't.

*   **The icon-import trap.** The same mistake as buttons, multiplied by control count. Named imports (`import { Bold } from "lucide-react"`) tree-shake; a namespace import (`import * as Icons`) drags the whole library in for a bar of eight glyphs.
*   **Don't mount a full toolbar per table row.** A row-hover action bar rendered for 500 rows is 500 toolbars of listeners. Render _one_ shared bar and position it on the hovered row, or use event delegation on the table body.
*   **`ResizeObserver`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*, not** **`resize`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*, and debounce it.** Overflow measurement on every resize frame thrashes layout. Observe the bar, coalesce, and only recompute `visibleCount` when it actually crosses a boundary.
*   **Measure once, cache widths.** Re-measuring every control's `offsetWidth` on each observer tick forces reflow. Cache intrinsic widths and only re-measure when the control set changes.
*   **Compositor-only transitions.** Animate the floating bar's appearance with `transform`/`opacity`, never `top`/`width`, so it doesn't reflow the document behind it.
*   **`content-visibility`** **/** **`contain`** on an offscreen or collapsed toolbar lets the browser skip its layout work until it's shown.

## 15\. Security
A toolbar is a container of buttons, so its security surface is its actions' — inherit everything from the Button doc's server-side rules. The toolbar-specific reminders:

*   **The UI is not the boundary.** Hiding a "Delete selected" control (or pushing it into overflow, or disabling it) is UX, never protection. Every action endpoint re-checks auth, authorization, and validation independently. "The button was in the overflow menu they didn't open" must never mean "the action can't happen."
*   **Destructive actions in a dense bar get confirm + distance.** A "Delete all" one pixel from "Save" in a cramped toolbar is an incident waiting to happen. Separate it, and gate it behind explicit confirmation.
*   **Don't trust control-supplied context.** The selected-row IDs a bulk-action button carries are attacker-controllable. Authorize server-side that _this_ user may act on _those_ resources.
*   **Rate-limit bulk actions.** A "bulk export / bulk delete" toolbar button is a cost-and-abuse target. Rate-limit and audit-log it (who, what, when) server-side.
*   **CSRF on state-modifying actions.** A toolbar button is only the trigger; a "Publish," "Delete," or "Deploy" that mutates server state needs CSRF protection on the underlying API call, not just on the visible control.
*   **User-customizable toolbars = an allowlist.** If users can add/remove or reorder items through a preferences API, validate every item ID against a server-side allowlist. Never let a crafted payload inject an arbitrary action, and never render a user-supplied item label without escaping — a malicious label is an XSS vector.
*   **Clipboard operations need a secure context.** Cut/Copy/Paste controls call `navigator.clipboard`, which requires HTTPS and a user gesture. The button click satisfies the gesture, but handle permission denial gracefully with a fallback (e.g. `document.execCommand` or a manual copy prompt).
*   **Scope Undo/Redo history to the user.** When undo state is stored server-side for collaborative editing, scope it to the acting user's permissions — undo must never revert another user's authorized action.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a toolbar is "done":
- [ ] Container has `role="toolbar"` + `aria-label` and correct `aria-orientation`.
- [ ] **Roving tabindex:** exactly one control is `tabindex="0"`, the rest `-1`; the toolbar is a single Tab stop.
- [ ] Arrow keys move within (direction follows orientation); Home/End jump to ends; Tab _leaves_ the bar.
- [ ] Every icon-only control has a real accessible name; decorative SVGs are `aria-hidden`.
- [ ] Toggles use `aria-pressed`; one-of-many segments use `radiogroup` + `aria-checked`.
- [ ] Active/pressed controls look persistently engaged, not just hovered.
- [ ] Separators are `role="separator"` with correct orientation; groups are labelled.
- [ ] Overflow ("⋯") is a real menu button; collapsed items stay keyboard-operable.
- [ ] Disabled-control strategy chosen deliberately (skip vs. keep focusable) and consistent.
- [ ] Tooltips reinforce labels, never replace them.
- [ ] ≥44px targets; icon contrast ≥3:1; no color-only state.
- [ ] Focus returns to the trigger when an overflow/floating bar closes.
- [ ] `prefers-reduced-motion` honored for any sheen/slide/floating animation.
- [ ] Action endpoints re-check auth and validate regardless of toolbar state.

## 17\. Visual Styles
The same semantic toolbar across the eleven visual languages. Skin only — the roving tabindex, `role="toolbar"`, arrow contract, `aria-pressed`, separators, and ≥44px targets never change.

*   **Flat:** solid bar, hairline separators, no shadow, crisp radius. The safe default for dense/data work.
*   **Material:** low elevation that lifts on scroll, ripple on each control, active tool in the primary tint, thin divider separators.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent bar, `backdrop-filter: blur()`, faint border. Reserve for floating bars over rich content; verify icon contrast against whatever scrolls behind.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026):** refractive translucent pill with a specular rim and motion-reactive sheen; the floating selection bar is its natural home. Native on Apple (`.glassEffect()`), approximated on web. Guard for reduced-motion and contrast.
*   **Neumorphism:** bar and controls share one surface with soft dual shadows; pressed controls inset. Contrast-poor — accent tools only, and test the pressed state hard.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** beveled controls, inset-groove separators, gradient bar. The classic desktop-suite toolbar; heavy but tactile.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick borders between controls, hard offset shadow on the bar, zero radius, loud active color. Campaign / high-personality tools.
*   **Claymorphism:** puffy controls, soft inner light, generous radius. Friendly creative apps.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue bar on dark; premium hero tools. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-invisible bar, whitespace as grouping, hairline separators, type-led. Editorial and pro tools.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod controls on a deep Eminence/Night bar; the active tool carries a warm Goldenrod glow, and the `:focus-visible` ring is Goldenrod at 2px offset. Separators are a low-opacity Platinum. On the dark saturated bar, a Liquid-Glass floating variant reads beautifully — lead with it for contextual selection bars, keep Flat/Swiss for the dense editing chrome.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin, the behavior is the skeleton. A UJG-brand toolbar and a Neo-Brutalist one are byte-for-byte identical in semantics, keyboard behavior, focus order, and target size. Guard glass / liquid-glass / neumorphism for icon contrast; guard aurora / liquid-glass for reduced-motion.
* * *

**Bottom line:** a toolbar is not a row of buttons — it's a row of buttons plus one contract, and that contract is roving tabindex. Get the grouping and density right so the bar reads at a glance, wire the one-tab-stop-arrow-key-navigation so keyboard users aren't punished for your density, label every icon, give overflow a real menu, and make the active tool _look_ active. Nail those and you've built the densest accessible surface in the app — the thing that makes power users fast and makes the whole product feel like it was built by people who knew what they were doing.
