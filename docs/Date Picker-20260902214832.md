## 1\. What a Date Picker Actually Is
A date picker is a control that lets a user **enter a date (or range of dates) by picking from a calendar and/or typing it in**. That one sentence hides the single most important design decision in the whole component: _the calendar popup is an affordance, not the input._ The input is the source of truth; the calendar is one convenient way to fill it.

People conflate a pile of distinct things here, and the confusion causes real bugs:
*   **Date input vs. date picker.** `<input type="date">` is a _native date input_ — the browser supplies the calendar. A "date picker" usually means a _custom_ widget you built because the native one couldn't do what you needed (ranges, styling, non-Gregorian calendars).
*   **Date vs. datetime vs. time.** A birthday is a _date_ (no time, no timezone). A meeting start is a _datetime_ (has a timezone, and that changes everything — see §9 and §12). A daily alarm is a _time_. Do not reach for a datetime picker when the thing you're capturing is a plain calendar date; you'll invent a timezone bug that doesn't need to exist. Time-of-day lives in the sibling **Time Picker** doc.
*   **Single vs. range vs. multiple.** Single date (one day), range (a start and an end, inclusive — hotel check-in/out, report window), or multiple discrete days (a set). Each has a different keyboard and announcement model.
*   **Calendar vs. date field.** A "date field" is _just_ the text input with masking/parsing, no popup. A "calendar" is _just_ the grid. A full date picker is the two composed together, wired so that editing either updates the other.

The rule of thumb that saves you: **if you don't have a hard reason to build custom, use** **`<input type="date">`****\*\*\*\*.** It's free accessibility, free localization, free mobile pickers, and zero JS. You build a custom calendar when — and only when — you genuinely need range selection, blackout dates, a two-month view, non-Gregorian calendars, or brand-heavy styling the native control won't allow.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The date picker sits on the critical path of the highest-value flows in most products, and it's where users quietly fail.
*   **Conversion lives in the range.** Booking (flights, hotels, rentals), scheduling (appointments, demos), and checkout delivery windows all route through a date picker. A confusing calendar is abandonment measured in real revenue. The travel industry has A/B-tested date range pickers harder than almost any other component on earth.
*   **It's the #1 i18n trap.** `03/04/2026` is March 4th in the US and April 3rd almost everywhere else. A date picker that silently guesses wrong doesn't throw an error — it books the wrong day and generates a support ticket, a refund, or a missed flight. Locale-correct parsing and display is not polish; it's correctness.
*   **It's one of the hardest accessibility problems in the library.** A calendar is a two-dimensional grid with a full keyboard model, a live region for month changes, and per-cell labels. Most custom date pickers on the web are inaccessible. Getting it right is a genuine competence signal.
*   **Timezones make it a data-integrity issue.** The gap between "date-only" and "datetime" is where junior and senior engineers separate. Store a date-only value as a UTC timestamp and users near the international date line will see it shift by a day. That's not a UI bug; it's a data bug that outlives the session.

## 3\. Anatomy of a Date Picker
A full picker is more parts than people expect. Naming them makes the code and the a11y tractable.
*   **Trigger / text input:** the field the user sees at rest. Often _is_ the input (typeable) plus a calendar icon button that opens the popup. Sometimes an icon-only button next to a plain field.
*   **Popover / dropdown surface:** the floating container that holds the calendar. Positioned against the input, dismissible on Esc / outside-click. (See the sibling **Popover** doc — the same positioning and dismissal rules apply.)
*   **Header:** month + year label, plus previous/next month buttons. Often the month/year label is itself a button that opens a month or year quick-picker.
*   **Weekday row:** the Su Mo Tu We Th Fr Sa header. Localized, and its _start day_ changes by locale (Sunday in the US, Monday across most of Europe).
*   **The grid:** the calendar body. `role="grid"`, rows are weeks (`role="row"`), cells are days (`role="gridcell"`). This is the heart of the keyboard model.
*   **Day cell:** one date. Carries selected / today / disabled / outside-month / in-range / range-endpoint state, plus a full `aria-label` ("Tuesday, July 22, 2026").
*   **Footer (optional):** "Today" shortcut, Clear, Apply/Cancel (for ranges that only commit on Apply), preset ranges ("Last 7 days," "This month").
*   **Live region:** a visually-hidden `aria-live` node that announces the focused month/year as the user navigates, so non-sighted users know where they are.
*   **The value model:** the actual stored value — a date-only string (`2026-07-22`), a range (`{start, end}`), or a datetime with a timezone. This is the part the UI exists to produce.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes & Density
The _field_ follows your form's input scale; the _calendar surface_ has its own density decisions.

| Element | Compact | Default | Comfortable | Notes |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Field height | 32px | 40px | 48px | Match your text-input scale exactly |
| Day cell | 32×32px | 36×36px | 40×40px | Visual size; hit target must still reach 44px |
| Cell font | 12px | 14px | 15px | Tabular-nums so digits don't jitter |
| Header label | 13px | 15px | 16px | Month + year |
| Grid gap | 0 | 2px | 4px | 0 for a solid grid, gap for a "chips" look |
| Popover width | ~260px | ~300px | ~340px | Two-month range view ≈ doubles this |

Rules that matter:
*   **Day cells look small but must still tap at ≥44px.** A 36px visual cell needs padding or an expanded hit area, especially on mobile. Cramped calendars are a mis-tap machine.
*   **Use** **`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`** on the grid so the columns don't shift as digits change (1 vs. 11 vs. 28).
*   **Fix the grid to 6 rows (42 cells).** Months span 4–6 visual weeks; if you let the height change per month, the popover jumps every time the user clicks next/prev. Always render 6 weeks, padding with the trailing/leading days of adjacent months.
*   **Two-month view** for ranges roughly doubles the popover width — plan the positioning (and the mobile fallback to a single stacked month or a full-screen sheet).

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A date picker has _layers_ of state: the popup, the field, and — uniquely — per-cell state across a whole grid.

**Component-level (the popup/field):**
1. **Closed (rest)** — field shows the formatted value or a placeholder hint (`MM/DD/YYYY`).
2. **Open** — popover visible, focus moved into the grid, `aria-expanded="true"` on the trigger.
3. **Focus** — field or a day cell has the visible focus ring (`:focus-visible`).
4. **Filled** — a value is committed; field shows it in the locale format.
5. **Invalid** — typed text couldn't be parsed, or the date violates min/max/blackout. `aria-invalid="true"` + an error message wired via `aria-describedby`.
6. **Disabled / read-only** — the whole control is inert.
7. **Loading** — rare, but real when available dates come from the server (booking availability). Show a skeleton grid, don't let users pick into the unknown.

**Per-day-cell state (this is what makes it a** **_grid_****\*\*\*\*, not a list):**
1. **Default** — a selectable day in the current month.
2. **Today** — visually marked _and_ announced ("Today, ...") — never _only_ color.
3. **Selected** — the chosen day (single), or a range endpoint.
4. **In-range** — days between start and end (range mode), a tinted band.
5. **Range-preview (hover)** — after picking a start, hovering a candidate end previews the whole band before commit. The detail that makes a range picker feel expensive.
6. **Outside month** — leading/trailing days of adjacent months; muted, often still selectable (or hidden).
7. **Disabled / unavailable** — outside min/max, a blackout date, or a booked day. `aria-disabled` + a reason where possible.
8. **Focused** — the single cell that has DOM focus (roving tabindex — exactly one cell is focusable at a time).

The tell of an amateur build: color-only state. "Today" is a gold ring _and_ a label; "selected" is a fill _and_ `aria-selected`; "unavailable" is muted _and_ `aria-disabled`. Color alone fails colorblind users and WCAG 1.4.1.

## 6\. Types & Variants
*   **Single date.** One day. The default. Birthday, due date, publish date.
*   **Date range.** Start + end, inclusive. Two clicks (or two fields). Hover-preview between them. Hotel stays, report windows, campaign flights.
*   **Multiple dates.** A set of discrete days (not a contiguous span). Availability marking, multi-day event RSVPs.
*   **Date + time (datetime).** A calendar plus a time picker plus — critically — a timezone. The moment you add time, you inherit the whole timezone problem (§12).
*   **Month / quarter / year picker.** Coarser granularity: pick a whole month (billing period), quarter (finance), or year. Same grid pattern, bigger cells, no day layer.
*   **Inline (embedded) calendar.** No popup — the calendar lives directly in the page (a dashboard filter, a booking page). Same grid, no trigger/dismiss layer.
*   **Preset + custom.** A menu of quick ranges ("Today," "Last 7 days," "This month," "Last quarter") next to a custom two-month calendar. The analytics-dashboard standard (see Google Analytics, Stripe).
*   **Native field.** `<input type="date">` / `type="month">` / `type="week">` / `type="datetime-local">`. Zero JS, full a11y, limited styling and no range.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Reach for a date picker when the value is genuinely a **calendar date the user thinks of as a date**, and precision/validity matters.

**Prefer the native** **`<input type="date">`** **when:**
*   You need a single date, no range, no blackout logic beyond min/max.
*   Mobile matters — native pickers are excellent and familiar (the iOS wheel, the Android calendar).
*   You want free accessibility and localization and can live with limited styling.

**Build a custom calendar only when you truly need:**
*   Range selection with hover preview, or a two-month view.
*   Blackout / disabled-date logic more complex than a single min/max window.
*   Server-driven availability (booked days greyed out).
*   Non-Gregorian calendars, or brand styling the native control forbids.

**Don't use a calendar at all when:**
*   **The date is far in the past** (birthdate, historical date). Scrolling a calendar back 40 years is misery — use three selects or a masked text field (`MM / DD / YYYY`). A 1985 birthday should be _typed_, not clicked.
*   **The set of valid dates is tiny** (three delivery slots). That's a radio group or a select, not a calendar.
*   **Only a relative range matters** ("last 30 days"). Offer presets first; the calendar is the escape hatch.

Placement heuristics: the popover opens below the field by default, flips above when there's no room (see the **Popover** doc), and on mobile a bottom sheet or the native control beats a cramped floating calendar every time.

## 8\. Date Pickers Across Design Systems
Same component, very different philosophies — and very different quality of accessibility.
*   **Material Design (Google):** Two flavors — a _docked_ text-field-plus-dropdown-calendar for desktop and a full _modal_ calendar/input for mobile, with an explicit toggle between calendar and keyboard-entry modes. Range picker is a full-screen two-pane on mobile. Strong on the type-or-pick duality.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** `UIDatePicker` — the iconic wheel, plus a compact calendar popover on iOS 14+. macOS has the stepper-style field with a graphical calendar. Apple leans on _native_ controls hard; SwiftUI's `DatePicker` gives you all of it for one line.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** `DatePicker` and `Calendar` components, keyboard-first (Fluent's enterprise heritage), with strong support for `minDate`/`maxDate` and restricted dates. Compact, neutral, built for forms-heavy admin UIs.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** The most feature-dense — `DatePicker`, `RangePicker`, `MonthPicker`, `WeekPicker`, `QuarterPicker`, presets, and `disabledDate` callbacks. Built on `dayjs`. If you want to see every affordance a date picker _can_ have, read Ant's.
*   **Carbon (IBM):** Wraps `flatpickr`, exposes single/range/simple variants, opinionated about label + helper-text + error wiring.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** No prescribed picker — you compose one or pull in a headless library. Freedom plus the risk of shipping an inaccessible grid. Usually wrapped around `react-day-picker`.
*   **shadcn/Radix:** The 2024+ web standard — shadcn's `Calendar` _is_ `react-day-picker` styled with Tailwind, popped in a Radix `Popover`. Behavior/a11y from react-day-picker, looks fully yours. This is what most modern React apps ship.
*   **React Aria (Adobe):** The accessibility gold standard. `useDatePicker`/`useCalendar` + `@internationalized/date` give you a fully WAI-ARIA-correct, locale-aware, _calendar-system-aware_ (Gregorian, Buddhist, Hebrew, Islamic, Japanese) picker. If a11y and i18n are non-negotiable, start here.

## 9\. The Code
The biggest section. A native input, a headless-but-accessible custom calendar, the library options, parsing/formatting done right, the server contract, and every framework's take.

### 9.1 Native `input[type=date]` (start here, seriously)
The control most teams should ship. Zero JavaScript, full keyboard support, screen-reader labeled, a native mobile picker, and locale-aware _display_ — the browser renders the value in the user's locale format even though the _value_ is always ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`.

```plain
<label for="dob">Date of birth</label>
<input
  type="date"
  id="dob"
  name="dob"
  min="1900-01-01"
  max="2026-07-22"
  value="1990-05-15"
  required />

<!-- Range = two native inputs, wired so end can't precede start -->
<label for="from">From</label>
<input type="date" id="from" name="from" max="2026-12-31">
<label for="to">To</label>
<input type="date" id="to" name="to">

<!-- Related native types -->
<input type="month">          <!-- 2026-07 -->
<input type="week">           <!-- 2026-W30 -->
<input type="datetime-local"> <!-- 2026-07-22T14:30  (NO timezone — see §12) -->
```

The critical, non-obvious facts:
*   **The wire format is always** **`YYYY-MM-DD`** regardless of what the user sees. The browser shows `07/22/2026` to a US user and `22.07.2026` to a German user, but `input.value` is `"2026-07-22"` for both. This is the ISO 8601 date format, and it's exactly what you want to send to the server. You get locale display _for free_ and a stable machine value _for free_.
*   **`min`****/****`max`** disable out-of-range dates natively. Set both to bound the picker.
*   **The gaps** are why people go custom: no range selection, no blackout dates (only a single min/max window), very limited styling (you can restyle the field, barely touch the calendar), and no non-Gregorian calendars. `datetime-local` also has **no timezone** — it's a wall-clock value, and that's a footgun the moment you persist it.
*   **Validate on the server anyway.** `min`/`max` are a UX hint; a `curl` can post any string.

### 9.2 CSS (states done right for a custom grid)
The custom calendar's states, driven by tokens and data-attributes, never by color alone.

```css
.cal { width: 300px; padding: 12px; border-radius: 12px;
  background: var(--surface); color: var(--text);
  font: 14px/1 system-ui, sans-serif; }

.cal__grid { display: grid; grid-template-columns: repeat(7, 1fr); gap: 2px; }

.cal__cell {
  aspect-ratio: 1; min-block-size: 44px;      /* real tap target */
  display: grid; place-items: center;
  border: 1px solid transparent; border-radius: 8px;
  font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums;         /* digits don't jitter */
  cursor: pointer;
}
.cal__cell:hover { background: color-mix(in oklch, var(--accent) 15%, transparent); }

/* Keyboard focus only — the roving-tabindex cell */
.cal__cell:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--focus); outline-offset: 2px; }

/* State via data-attrs + a non-color cue, never color alone */
.cal__cell[data-today]    { font-weight: 700; box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 2px var(--accent); }
.cal__cell[aria-selected="true"] { background: var(--accent); color: var(--on-accent); }
.cal__cell[data-in-range] { background: color-mix(in oklch, var(--accent) 22%, transparent); border-radius: 0; }
.cal__cell[data-range-start] { border-start-start-radius: 8px; border-end-start-radius: 8px; }
.cal__cell[data-range-end]   { border-start-end-radius: 8px; border-end-end-radius: 8px; }
.cal__cell[data-outside]  { opacity: .4; }
.cal__cell[aria-disabled="true"] {
  opacity: .35; cursor: not-allowed;
  text-decoration: line-through;             /* not-color cue */
}

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { .cal * { transition: none; } }
```

Logical properties (`min-block-size`, `border-start-start-radius`) mean the range rounding and layout flip correctly in RTL without a second stylesheet.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (a headless, accessible calendar grid)
The heart of a custom build. This is a _from-scratch_ grid so you can see the whole keyboard and roving-tabindex model — in production you'd usually reach for react-day-picker or React Aria (§9.7), but you should understand what they're doing for you.

```typescript
import { useState, useRef, useCallback, KeyboardEvent } from "react";

type ISO = `${number}-${number}-${number}`;
const iso = (d: Date): ISO =>
  `${d.getFullYear()}-${String(d.getMonth() + 1).padStart(2, "0")}-${String(
    d.getDate()
  ).padStart(2, "0")}` as ISO;

// Month/year math WITHOUT JS date overflow. `new Date(2026, 4, 31)` + one month
// naively becomes July 1 (June has 30 days), silently skipping a month. Anchor on
// day 1, then clamp the day to the target month's length (May 31 -> Jun 30).
const shiftMonths = (from: Date, months: number): Date => {
  const y = from.getFullYear();
  const m = from.getMonth() + months;
  const lastDay = new Date(y, m + 1, 0).getDate(); // day 0 of next month = last day
  return new Date(y, m, Math.min(from.getDate(), lastDay));
};

interface CalendarProps {
  value?: ISO;
  onChange?: (v: ISO) => void;
  min?: ISO;
  max?: ISO;
  isDisabled?: (d: Date) => boolean;   // blackout dates
  weekStartsOn?: 0 | 1;                // 0 = Sunday (US), 1 = Monday (EU)
  locale?: string;                     // "en-US", "de-DE", ...
  today?: ISO;                         // injected so "today" is testable + timezone-correct
}

export function Calendar({
  value, onChange, min, max, isDisabled,
  weekStartsOn = 0, locale = "en-US",
  // Inject "today" as a date-only ISO string (in production, derive it once from the
  // user's zone, e.g. @internationalized/date's `today(getLocalTimeZone())`) rather
  // than calling `new Date()` per cell — deterministic, testable, timezone-explicit.
  today = "2026-07-22",
}: CalendarProps) {
  const selected = value ? new Date(value + "T00:00:00") : undefined;
  // The "focused" date drives roving tabindex; starts at selection or today.
  const [focus, setFocus] = useState<Date>(selected ?? new Date());
  const gridRef = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);

  const monthLabel = new Intl.DateTimeFormat(locale, {
    month: "long", year: "numeric",
  }).format(focus);

  // Build a fixed 6-row (42-cell) grid so the popover never resizes.
  const firstOfMonth = new Date(focus.getFullYear(), focus.getMonth(), 1);
  const startOffset = (firstOfMonth.getDay() - weekStartsOn + 7) % 7;
  const gridStart = new Date(firstOfMonth);
  gridStart.setDate(1 - startOffset);
  const days = Array.from({ length: 42 }, (_, i) => {
    const d = new Date(gridStart);
    d.setDate(gridStart.getDate() + i);
    return d;
  });

  const outOfBounds = (d: Date) =>
    (min && iso(d) < min) || (max && iso(d) > max) || (isDisabled?.(d) ?? false);

  const move = useCallback((next: Date) => {
    setFocus(next);
    // Move real DOM focus to the newly-focused cell after render.
    queueMicrotask(() => {
      gridRef.current
        ?.querySelector<HTMLButtonElement>(`[data-iso="${iso(next)}"]`)
        ?.focus();
    });
  }, []);

  function onKeyDown(e: KeyboardEvent) {
    let d = new Date(focus);
    switch (e.key) {
      case "ArrowLeft":  d.setDate(d.getDate() - 1); break;
      case "ArrowRight": d.setDate(d.getDate() + 1); break;
      case "ArrowUp":    d.setDate(d.getDate() - 7); break;
      case "ArrowDown":  d.setDate(d.getDate() + 7); break;
      case "Home":       d.setDate(d.getDate() - ((d.getDay() - weekStartsOn + 7) % 7)); break;      // week start
      case "End":        d.setDate(d.getDate() + (6 - ((d.getDay() - weekStartsOn + 7) % 7))); break; // week end
      // Overflow-safe: shiftMonths anchors on day 1 + clamps, so May 31 + PageDown = Jun 30, not Jul 1.
      case "PageUp":     d = shiftMonths(focus, e.shiftKey ? -12 : -1); break;
      case "PageDown":   d = shiftMonths(focus, e.shiftKey ? 12 : 1); break;
      case "Enter":
      case " ":
        if (!outOfBounds(focus)) onChange?.(iso(focus));
        e.preventDefault();
        return;
      default: return;
    }
    e.preventDefault();
    move(d);
  }

  const weekdays = Array.from({ length: 7 }, (_, i) =>
    new Intl.DateTimeFormat(locale, { weekday: "short" }).format(
      new Date(2024, 0, 7 + ((i + weekStartsOn) % 7))   // a known Sunday
    )
  );

  return (
    <div className="cal" role="group" aria-label={monthLabel}>
      <div className="cal__header">
        {/* Preserve the focused day-of-month across months (WAI-ARIA date grid), clamp-safe. */}
        <button aria-label="Previous month"
          onClick={() => move(shiftMonths(focus, -1))}>‹</button>
        {/* Live region + the grid's accessible name (referenced by aria-labelledby below). */}
        <span id="cal-label" aria-live="polite">{monthLabel}</span>
        <button aria-label="Next month"
          onClick={() => move(shiftMonths(focus, 1))}>›</button>
      </div>

      <div role="grid" aria-labelledby="cal-label" ref={gridRef} onKeyDown={onKeyDown}>
        <div role="row" className="cal__grid">
          {weekdays.map((w) => (
            <span role="columnheader" key={w} aria-label={w}>{w.slice(0, 2)}</span>
          ))}
        </div>
        {Array.from({ length: 6 }, (_, week) => (
          <div role="row" className="cal__grid" key={week}>
            {days.slice(week * 7, week * 7 + 7).map((d) => {
              const disabled = outOfBounds(d);
              const isSel = selected && iso(d) === iso(selected);
              const isFocus = iso(d) === iso(focus);
              const isToday = iso(d) === today;
              const outside = d.getMonth() !== focus.getMonth();
              // Full label + state words so AT hears "... today", "... selected",
              // "... unavailable" — never color/CSS alone (WCAG 1.4.1, §5/§10/§16).
              const base = new Intl.DateTimeFormat(locale, {
                weekday: "long", year: "numeric", month: "long", day: "numeric",
              }).format(d);
              const state = [
                isToday && "today",
                isSel && "selected",
                disabled && "unavailable",
              ].filter(Boolean).join(", ");
              return (
                <button
                  role="gridcell"
                  key={iso(d)}
                  data-iso={iso(d)}
                  type="button"
                  // Roving tabindex: exactly ONE cell is tabbable.
                  tabIndex={isFocus ? 0 : -1}
                  aria-selected={isSel || undefined}
                  aria-disabled={disabled || undefined}
                  data-today={isToday || undefined}
                  data-outside={outside || undefined}
                  aria-label={state ? `${base}, ${state}` : base}
                  // Move roving focus too, not just the value — keeps the focused
                  // cell in sync and advances the view when an outside-month day is clicked.
                  onClick={() => {
                    if (disabled) return;
                    move(d);
                    onChange?.(iso(d));
                  }}
                >
                  {d.getDate()}
                </button>
              );
            })}
          </div>
        ))}
      </div>
    </div>
  );
}
```

Why this is the senior version:
*   **Roving tabindex** — one `tabIndex={0}` cell, all others `-1`. The grid is a single Tab stop; arrows move _within_ it. Tabbing through 42 buttons is the #1 custom-calendar a11y failure.
*   **The full keyboard model** — arrows move a day, Home/End the week, PageUp/Down the month, Shift+PageUp/Down the year, Enter/Space select. This is the WAI-ARIA grid pattern, not something you get to invent.
*   **Overflow-safe month math** — both the header buttons and PageUp/PageDown route through `shiftMonths`, which anchors on day 1 and clamps to the target month's length. A naive `setMonth` on May 31 lands on July 1 (June has no 31st) and silently skips a month; clamping lands it on June 30 and preserves the focused day-of-month.
*   **Fixed 42-cell grid** — the popover never resizes between months.
*   **`Intl.DateTimeFormat`** **for every human string** — weekday headers, the month label, and the per-cell `aria-label` are all locale-derived. No hardcoded English month names. Each cell's label also carries its state ("... today", "... selected", "... unavailable"), so state is never conveyed by color alone.
*   **Injected** **`today`** — "today" is passed in as a date-only ISO string rather than read from `new Date()` per cell, so the grid is deterministic and testable and the `[data-today]` marking is actually driven.
*   **`aria-live`** **on the month label** — paging the month is announced, and that same node is the grid's accessible name via `aria-labelledby="cal-label"`.
*   **The value is an ISO date-only string** — no `Date` objects crossing the boundary, no timezone in the value.

### 9.4 The text input: parsing, masking & the locale trap
The calendar is optional; the _field_ is not. Power users type. And typing is where the #1 i18n bug lives.

```typescript
// NEVER parse a localized date with `new Date(str)`.
// `new Date("03/04/2026")` is March 4 in a US-locale runtime and can be
// Invalid Date or April 3 elsewhere. It guesses. It is a bug generator.

import { parse, isValid, format, type Locale } from "date-fns";
import { enUS, enGB, de, ja, fr } from "date-fns/locale";

// One source of truth per supported locale: the display/parse format AND the
// date-fns locale object (needed for month/weekday tokens in other formats).
const LOCALES: Record<string, { fmt: string; locale: Locale }> = {
  "en-US": { fmt: "MM/dd/yyyy", locale: enUS }, // 07/22/2026
  "en-GB": { fmt: "dd/MM/yyyy", locale: enGB }, // 22/07/2026
  "de-DE": { fmt: "dd.MM.yyyy", locale: de },   // 22.07.2026
  "ja-JP": { fmt: "yyyy/MM/dd", locale: ja },   // 2026/07/22
  "fr-FR": { fmt: "dd/MM/yyyy", locale: fr },   // 04/03/2026 = 4 March
};

// Reference date only supplies fields the format omits; a full date overrides all of it.
const REF = new Date(2026, 0, 1);

/** Parse typed text against the user's *known* locale format.
 *  If the locale is unsupported we do NOT silently fall back to ISO — that is
 *  exactly the MM/DD-vs-DD/MM guess §12 warns against. Refuse and warn instead. */
function parseDate(input: string, locale = "en-US"): Date | null {
  const cfg = LOCALES[locale];
  if (!cfg) {
    console.warn(`[DatePicker] Unsupported locale "${locale}" — refusing to guess date order. Add it to LOCALES.`);
    return null;
  }
  const d = parse(input.trim(), cfg.fmt, REF, { locale: cfg.locale });
  return isValid(d) ? d : null;
}

/** Display a stored ISO date in the user's locale (falls back to en-US display only). */
function displayDate(isoStr: string, locale = "en-US"): string {
  const cfg = LOCALES[locale] ?? LOCALES["en-US"];
  return format(new Date(isoStr + "T00:00:00"), cfg.fmt, { locale: cfg.locale });
}
```

Masking as the user types (`MM/DD/YYYY` with auto-inserted slashes) reduces ambiguity, but the mask _pattern itself must be localized_ — showing `MM/DD/YYYY` to a German user teaches them the wrong order. Show `TT.MM.JJJJ`. The single most valuable habit: **parse against a locale you decided on, never let** **`new Date()`** **guess.**

### 9.5 Date range picker (start/end + hover preview)
The range is where the money is and where the state gets interesting: a range is half-built between the two clicks, and the hover previews the band.

```typescript
type Range = { start?: ISO; end?: ISO };

function useDateRange() {
  const [range, setRange] = useState<Range>({});
  const [hover, setHover] = useState<ISO | undefined>();

  function pick(day: ISO) {
    setRange((r) => {
      // No start yet, or a complete range exists -> begin fresh.
      if (!r.start || (r.start && r.end)) return { start: day, end: undefined };
      // Second click: order the pair so end >= start.
      return day < r.start ? { start: day, end: r.start } : { start: r.start, end: day };
    });
  }

  // While choosing the end, preview the band start..hover.
  const previewEnd = range.start && !range.end ? hover : undefined;
  const inBand = (day: ISO) => {
    const lo = range.start;
    const hi = range.end ?? previewEnd;
    if (!lo || !hi) return false;
    const [a, b] = lo <= hi ? [lo, hi] : [hi, lo];
    return day >= a && day <= b;
  };

  return { range, pick, setHover, inBand };
}
```

Range-specific rules that separate good from janky:
*   **Order the endpoints on commit,** not on click — users click end-before-start constantly.
*   **Two-month view** so a Jul→Aug range is visible without paging. On mobile, stack or go full-screen.
*   **Announce the range to screen readers** — "Selected July 22 to July 29, 7 nights" in a live region, because the hover-preview visual is invisible to them.
*   **Presets first** ("Last 7 days," "This month") — most users want a named range, not to click twice.
*   **Min-nights / max-nights** constraints (hotels: 2-night minimum) disable invalid ends _relative to the chosen start_.

```typescript
// app/actions.ts
"use server";
import { z } from "zod";

// Accept ONLY YYYY-MM-DD, and only a REAL date. The shape regex alone would pass
// "0000-00-00", a 5-digit-year overflow attempt, or "2026-02-31"; strict-parse rejects them.
function isRealDate(s: string): boolean {
  const m = /^(\d{4})-(\d{2})-(\d{2})$/.exec(s);
  if (!m) return false;
  const [y, mo, da] = [Number(m[1]), Number(m[2]), Number(m[3])];
  if (y < 1 || mo < 1 || mo > 12 || da < 1 || da > 31) return false;
  const dt = new Date(Date.UTC(y, mo - 1, da));
  return dt.getUTCFullYear() === y && dt.getUTCMonth() === mo - 1 && dt.getUTCDate() === da;
}
const Booking = z.object({
  date: z.string().refine(isRealDate, "Expected a valid date-only value (YYYY-MM-DD)"),
});

export async function createBooking(formData: FormData) {
  const parsed = Booking.safeParse({ date: formData.get("date") });
  if (!parsed.success) throw new Error("Invalid date");
  // Store as a DATE column, not a timestamp — no timezone, no drift.
  await db.booking.create({ data: { date: parsed.data.date } });
}
```

### 9.8 The server contract & storage (get this wrong and data rots)
The endpoint the picker feeds. The rules here outlive every UI decision.

```typescript
import { z } from "zod";

// The shape regex is necessary but NOT sufficient: /^\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2}$/ happily
// accepts "0000-00-00" and "2026-02-31". Strict-parse: build the date and require
// it to round-trip to the same y-m-d, so impossible calendar dates are rejected.
function isRealDate(s: string): boolean {
  const m = /^(\d{4})-(\d{2})-(\d{2})$/.exec(s);
  if (!m) return false;
  const [y, mo, da] = [Number(m[1]), Number(m[2]), Number(m[3])];
  if (y < 1 || mo < 1 || mo > 12 || da < 1 || da > 31) return false;
  const dt = new Date(Date.UTC(y, mo - 1, da));               // explicit UTC, no local drift
  return dt.getUTCFullYear() === y && dt.getUTCMonth() === mo - 1 && dt.getUTCDate() === da;
}

const DateOnly = z.string().refine(isRealDate, "Expected a real calendar date (YYYY-MM-DD)");
const RangeBody = z.object({ start: DateOnly, end: DateOnly })
  .refine((r) => r.start <= r.end, "end must not precede start");

app.post("/api/reservations", requireAuth, async (req, res) => {
  const parsed = RangeBody.safeParse(req.body);
  if (!parsed.success) return res.status(422).json(parsed.error.issues);
  // Re-validate business rules server-side: availability, blackout, min-nights.
  if (await hasConflict(parsed.data)) return res.status(409).json({ error: "Dates unavailable" });
  const r = await db.reservation.create({ data: parsed.data });  // DATE columns
  res.status(201).json(r);
});
```

The storage rules, in order of how often they're violated:
*   **A calendar date is date-only — store it as a** **`DATE`****\*\*\*\*, not a** **`TIMESTAMP`****\*\*\*\*.** The instant you store `2026-07-22` as a timestamp, you've picked a time (midnight) and a timezone, and a user in another zone will read it back as the 21st or the 23rd. Postgres `DATE`, MySQL `DATE`, and an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` string are all timezone-free. Keep it that way.
*   **A** **_datetime_** **(has a time) should be stored as UTC** — an instant (`timestamptz` / an ISO string with `Z`) — and converted to the user's timezone only for display. Store the instant, plus the originating IANA timezone (`America/New_York`) if the _local_ wall-clock matters (recurring 9am meetings survive DST only if you keep the zone, not just the UTC instant).
*   **Never trust** **`min`****/****`max`****\*\*\*\*, blackout, or availability from the client.** Re-check every business rule server-side. The disabled cell is UX; the 409 is the boundary.
*   **Validate the format strictly** — a regex for `YYYY-MM-DD`, reject anything with a `T` or a `Z` on a date-only field.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
A calendar is a `grid`, and grids have a _specification_. This is the section most custom pickers fail.

**Semantics / roles:**
*   Calendar body: `role="grid"` with an accessible name (the month/year). Weeks are `role="row"`, weekday headers `role="columnheader"`, day cells `role="gridcell"`.
*   Selected day: `aria-selected="true"`. Range endpoints and in-range days: `aria-selected` on the endpoints; describe the span in the live region.
*   Unavailable days: `aria-disabled="true"` (kept in the grid, still focusable so users can hear _why_), not removed from the DOM.
*   The trigger: `aria-haspopup="dialog"` (or `grid`), `aria-expanded`, and `aria-controls` pointing at the popup.

**The keyboard model (the WAI-ARIA date-picker grid pattern) — memorize it:**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| Left / Right arrow | Previous / next day |
| Up / Down arrow | Same weekday, previous / next week |
| Home / End | First / last day of the current week |
| PageUp / PageDown | Previous / next month |
| Shift + PageUp / PageDown | Previous / next year |
| Enter / Space | Select the focused day |
| Esc | Close the popup, return focus to the trigger |

**Focus management:**
*   On open, move focus **into the grid**, onto the selected day — or today if nothing is selected. Never dump focus at the top of the popup.
*   **Roving tabindex:** exactly one cell has `tabIndex="0"`; the rest are `-1`. Arrows move focus and shift which cell is tabbable. The grid is one Tab stop.
*   On close (Esc or select), return focus to the trigger. Trap nothing you don't restore.

**Announcements:**
*   A visually-hidden `aria-live="polite"` region announces the month/year as the user pages, and — for ranges — the selected span and its length.
*   Every cell carries a full, unambiguous `aria-label`: **"Tuesday, July 22, 2026,"** not just "22." Include state where relevant ("... unavailable", "... today").

**Contrast & non-color cues:** state ≥ 3:1 for UI/graphics, text ≥ 4.5:1; "today," "selected," and "disabled" each need a shape/weight/line cue in addition to color (WCAG 1.4.1). **Target size:** day cells ≥ 44px hit area even when the visual is smaller.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
*   **The Temporal API.** JavaScript's long-awaited `Temporal` (Stage 3, shipping across engines through 2025–2026) finally gives a _date-only_ type — `Temporal.PlainDate` — with no time and no timezone, exactly the model a date picker needs. `Temporal.ZonedDateTime` handles datetimes with a real IANA zone. It ends two decades of `new Date()` guessing. Libraries are already exposing Temporal-native pickers; when it lands broadly, most of the parsing pain in §9.4 evaporates.
*   **Natural-language date entry.** "next friday," "in 3 weeks," "end of Q3" parsed inline (Chrono.js, Sunsama, Linear). The calendar becomes the confirmation, not the primary path.
*   **AI-assisted scheduling.** Availability-aware pickers that grey out conflicts pulled from a connected calendar, or suggest the best slot — the datetime picker as an agent surface.
*   **`@internationalized/date`** **\+ non-Gregorian by default.** React Aria's model makes Buddhist, Hebrew, Islamic, and Japanese calendars a locale switch, not a rewrite — increasingly table stakes for global products.
*   **CSS** **`anchor-positioning`** for the popover, retiring the JS positioning libraries (see the Popover doc).
*   **Container queries** so an embedded calendar switches between one-month and two-month layouts based on _its_ width, not the viewport.

## 12\. Conversion & UX Killers (the silent ones)
None of these throw. They just book the wrong day and generate refunds.
*   **The MM/DD vs DD/MM ambiguity, unhandled.** The single most expensive date-picker bug. `03/04` guessed wrong is a flight on the wrong day. Fix: show the format hint in the field (`MM/DD/YYYY` localized), parse against a _known_ locale, and echo the parsed date back in an unambiguous long form ("Wed, March 4, 2026") so the user can catch it.
*   **Storing a date-only value as a UTC timestamp.** The classic. A birthday saved as `2026-07-22T00:00:00Z` shows as July 21 to anyone west of UTC. Store `DATE`, not `TIMESTAMP`.
*   **`datetime-local`** **persisted without its timezone.** It's a wall-clock string with no zone; save it raw and "3pm" becomes ambiguous the moment it's read in another zone. Attach the IANA zone or convert to a UTC instant at capture.
*   **A calendar for a birthdate.** Making someone page back 40 years is a rage-quit. Type it.
*   **No loading/availability state on a server-driven calendar.** Users pick a day that turns out to be booked, hit submit, get a 409, and blame you. Grey out unavailable days _before_ they pick, with a skeleton while availability loads.
*   **The popover resizing between months.** A 4-week month then a 6-week month makes the calendar jump and the next-arrow move out from under the cursor. Fixed 6-row grid.
*   **Range with no hover preview.** Users can't tell what they're about to select. The preview band is the whole UX.
*   **Wrong first-day-of-week for the locale.** Showing a Sunday-start week to a Monday-start locale reads as broken to half your users. Derive it from the locale.
*   **Color-only "today" / "selected" / "disabled."** Fails colorblind and screen-reader users at once.

The through-line: date bugs fail _quietly and asymmetrically_ — they look fine to you (in your timezone, your locale) and break for the user on the other side of the world. Test in a non-US locale and a non-UTC timezone before you ship.
* * *

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
*   **Token-driven theming.** Primitive → semantic → component tokens (see the Button doc's §9.23). The calendar references `--cal-selected-bg`, `--cal-range-band`, `--cal-today-ring`, `--focus`; a rebrand swaps primitives, dark mode swaps semantics. Nothing hardcodes a hex.
*   **Timezone-explicit value objects.** Never pass a bare `Date` across a component boundary. Pass a `PlainDate` (date-only) or a `{ instant, zone }` pair (datetime). The type makes "does this have a timezone?" un-forgettable.
*   **Compile-time mode safety.** A discriminated union so a range picker _requires_ `onRangeChange` and a single picker _requires_ `onChange` — you can't wire the wrong handler.
*   **Server-authoritative availability.** The calendar renders disabled days from a server availability map, and the endpoint re-checks on submit (409 on conflict). The disabled cell and the 409 are defense in depth, exactly like the button's disabled-plus-idempotency pair.
*   **Preset + custom range architecture.** Presets emit the same `{start, end}` the calendar does, so the downstream contract is identical whether the user clicked "Last 7 days" or picked by hand.
*   **`inputmode="numeric"`** **\+ a localized mask** on the text field so mobile keyboards show digits and the slashes auto-insert in the locale's order.
* * *

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
*   **The date-library trap.** Moment.js is ~70KB gzipped and immutable-unfriendly; it's in maintenance mode — don't add it to new code. Prefer `date-fns` (tree-shakeable, import only the functions you use), `Luxon` (great timezone support, one object), `Day.js` (~2KB, Moment-compatible API), or native `Intl` + the coming `Temporal`. A namespace import of a date library is the calendar's version of the icon-import trap.
*   **`Intl.DateTimeFormat`** **is not free to construct.** Building a formatter per cell per render is measurable on a 42-cell grid. Construct the formatters once (memoize by locale) and reuse them.
*   **Render only the visible month.** Don't mount twelve months to make paging feel instant; mount one (or two for ranges) and swap. Virtualize only if you build an unusual infinite-scroll calendar.
*   **Memoize the grid.** The 42 cells shouldn't re-render when only the hovered day changes — drive hover state via a data-attribute/CSS where possible, or memoize cells by ISO + state.
*   **Lazy-load the popup.** The calendar and its date library don't need to be in the initial bundle — load them when the field is focused or the trigger is clicked. The native `<input type="date">` costs _zero_ bytes, which is the best performance story of all.
* * *

## 15\. Security
A date picker feels harmless, but its value is user input that reaches the server, and every rule you enforced in the UI is a suggestion until the endpoint enforces it too. Never trust the client-formatted string: parse the incoming value server-side against one explicit format (`YYYY-MM-DD`, rejecting a stray `T` or `Z`) and, beyond the shape, require a _real_ calendar date — construct it and confirm it round-trips to the same y/m/d, so a year of `0000`, a five-digit year meant to overflow a downstream cast, and an impossible day like `2026-02-31` are all rejected rather than silently coerced. The `min`/`max` bounds and the greyed-out blackout cells are UX only; a booking day you disabled in the calendar is still bookable with a raw `curl`, so re-enforce the allowed range, availability, and blackout rules on the server — the disabled cell and the 409 are defense in depth, not substitutes for each other. When the date drives a query — an availability lookup, a report window, an export — treat it as an untrusted query parameter: parameterize it (never string-concatenate a date into SQL) and, critically, **bound the span**, because an unbounded or absurdly wide range (`2020-01-01` to `9999-12-31`) is a cheap way to trigger an expensive full-table scan and take the endpoint down. Date ranges also enable enumeration — an attacker can probe which dates read back as “taken” to map out your booking calendar — so rate-limit availability endpoints and return uniform responses that leak nothing the UI wouldn’t already show. The through-line matches the rest of this doc: the picker is a convenience, the server is the boundary, and a date is data you validate, bound, and parameterize like any other input.

- [ ] First-day-of-week, weekday/month names, and format all locale-derived; RTL verified.
- [ ] Range: endpoints ordered on commit, hover-preview band, two-month view, min/max-nights.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same calendar, eleven skins)
Style is skin; the grid semantics, keyboard model, focus ring, roving tabindex, and 44px targets never change. Full guidance in the [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) doc.
*   **Flat:** solid selected fill, crisp cells, no shadow. Reads instantly, scales fast.
*   **Material:** the selected day is a filled circle with a ripple; today is an outlined circle; elevation on the popover surface.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent popover with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over a vivid page. Watch contrast on the day numbers — the #1 glass failure.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe material — the popover refracts the content behind it with a specular rim and a sheen that reacts to motion; the selected day is a floating glass pill. Guard for `prefers-reduced-motion` and contrast; SwiftUI emits it natively via `.glassEffect()`, the web is an honest approximation.
*   **Neumorphism:** the calendar surface and cells share one color, selection is an inset/extruded shadow. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent use only, never the primary booking flow.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** a "real" tear-off wall calendar — paper texture, page curl, bound top. High charm, heavy assets.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black grid lines, hard offset shadow on the popover, zero radius, a clashing selected color. Loud personality.
*   **Claymorphism:** puffy rounded cells, soft inner light, big radius. Friendly, consumer, kid/health apps.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue selected state and header on a dark surface. Premium — honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** type and grid do the work, hairline dividers, near-zero decoration, sharp corners. Excellent for dense/data calendars.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod selection on deep Eminence, the `today` ring in Goldenrod, in-range band a translucent Eminence, warm focus glow — the Afro-Futurist house default. Lean Liquid-Glass accents over the dark Eminence surface for the hero booking flow; keep Flat/Swiss for dense scheduling grids.

**Rule that never changes:** the skin is cosmetic. A Neo-Brutalist calendar and a Liquid-Glass calendar have the identical `role="grid"`, keyboard map, focus behavior, and tap targets. Guard glass/neumorphism for contrast, and aurora/liquid-glass for reduced motion.

**Bottom line:** a date picker is deceptively small and quietly one of the hardest components in the library. The default answer is `<input type="date">` — free accessibility, free localization, zero JS — and you should have to _justify_ going custom. When you do go custom, the whole difficulty compresses into three disciplines: the WAI-ARIA grid (real `role="grid"`, the full keyboard model, roving tabindex, per-cell labels, a live region), locale-correct parsing and display (never let `new Date()` guess MM/DD vs DD/MM), and the date-only-vs-datetime storage boundary (store `DATE` as `DATE`, store instants as UTC). Master those three and you've mastered the component that trips up almost everyone else. Cross-link the **Time Picker**, **Combo box**, **Popover**, and **Field** docs — a date picker is where all four meet.
