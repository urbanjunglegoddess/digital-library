## 1\. What an Image Actually Is
An image is an embedded, replaced element that renders external pixel or vector data into the document. That's the plain version. The word hides distinctions people constantly blur, and each one changes the correct markup:

*   **Content image (****`<img>`****):** part of the meaning of the page. A product photo, a chart, a diagram, a headshot. It belongs in the DOM as a real element with `alt` text because _if it vanished, information would be lost._
*   **Decorative image:** pure ornament — a background texture, a divider flourish, a hero gradient. It carries no information the surrounding text doesn't already give. It should either be a CSS `background-image` (no semantics) or an `<img>` with an explicitly empty `alt=""` so assistive tech skips it.
*   **Functional image:** an image that _is_ a control — a logo inside a link, an icon inside a button. Here the alt text describes the **destination or action**, not the picture. The alt on a linked logo is "Urban Jungle Goddess home," not "green leaf monogram."
*   **`<picture>`****\*\*\*\*:** not a different image — a _wrapper_ that lets the browser choose between sources (format or art-direction) and fall back to a single `<img>`. The `<img>` inside is still the real element; `<picture>` just gives the browser options.
*   **Background image (CSS):** a paint layer on a box, not a document node. No `alt`, not in the accessibility tree, not (by default) discovered by the preload scanner. Great for décor, wrong for content.

The single most common mistake: treating all five as "just put the picture on the page." A decorative texture shipped as a content `<img>` with a filename-as-alt spams every screen reader on the site. A logo-in-a-link with `alt="logo"` tells a blind user nothing about where the link goes. **The element and the** **`alt`** **are a semantic decision before they are a visual one.** Get that decision right and half the accessibility work is already done.

Rule of thumb: **if losing the image loses meaning, it's a content** **`<img>`** **with real alt; if it's pure decoration, it's** **`background-image`** **or** **`alt=""`****\*\*\*\*; if it's inside a link or button, the alt describes the action, never the picture.**

## 2\. Why It Matters
Images are simultaneously the most valuable and the most expensive thing on a page. That tension is the whole story.

*   **Weight lives here.** On the median web page, images are the single largest resource category — routinely 50%+ of total bytes. Your slow site is almost never your JavaScript first; it's a 3.2 MB hero JPEG someone dropped in at full camera resolution. **The biggest performance lever you have is usually an image, not a framework.**
*   **The LCP is almost always an image.** Largest Contentful Paint — Google's headline Core Web Vital — is, on most pages, the hero image. How fast that one image arrives _is_ your loading score, and loading score feeds search ranking and bounce rate. Get the LCP image wrong (lazy-load it, ship it as a 4000px original, hide it behind lazy JS) and you tank the metric that matters most.
*   **CLS is usually an image.** Cumulative Layout Shift — content jumping as things load — is dominated by images that arrive without reserved space, shoving text down as they pop in. An image with no `width`/`height` is _the_ number-one cause of layout shift on the web. This is a trust and usability killer: users tap the wrong button because the page jumped.
*   **Accessibility runs through** **`alt`****\*\*\*\*.** For blind and low-vision users, the `alt` attribute _is_ the image. A missing, wrong, or lazy `alt` ("image123.jpg") makes whole swaths of content invisible — and it's a legal exposure (ADA / WCAG / Section 508) that shows up in real lawsuits.
*   **It sets the perceived quality.** A stretched, blurry, wrongly-cropped, or slow-fading image makes a premium brand feel cheap instantly. For UJG's visual-first work, the image _is_ the pitch.

The image is where performance, SEO, accessibility, and brand all collide in one tag. That's why it deserves this much doc.

## 3\. Anatomy of an Image
Every production image is more than a `src`. The parts:

*   **The element** — `<img>` (content/functional) or `<picture>` wrapping an `<img>` (when you need format or art-direction choice), or a CSS `background-image` box (décor only).
*   **`src`** — the default/fallback source URL. Even with `srcset`, this is the guaranteed baseline the browser can always load.
*   **`alt`** — the text alternative. The spine of the whole element (see §10). Present on every `<img>`, always — empty (`alt=""`) for decorative, meaningful for content, action-describing for functional.
*   **`srcset`** **+** **`sizes`** — the responsive engine. `srcset` lists candidate files (by width or density); `sizes` tells the browser how wide the image will _render_ so it can pick before layout.
*   **`width`** **+** **`height`** **(or** **`aspect-ratio`****\*\*\*\*)** — the space reservation. Intrinsic dimensions let the browser compute the box before the bytes arrive, killing layout shift.
*   **`loading`** — `eager` (default) or `lazy` (defer until near viewport).
*   **`decoding`** — `sync`, `async`, or `auto`; hints whether to block rendering on image decode.
*   **`fetchpriority`** — `high` / `low` / `auto`; nudges the browser's fetch queue (raise the LCP image, lower the below-fold clutter).
*   **`object-fit`** **+** **`object-position`** — how the pixels fill a box whose aspect ratio doesn't match theirs (cover, contain, crop anchor).
*   **The placeholder** — what shows in the gap before load: reserved empty box, skeleton shimmer, dominant-color block, or a blur-up LQIP (low-quality image placeholder).
*   **`<figure>`** **+** **`<figcaption>`** — when the image needs a visible, associated caption and should be referenced as a unit ("see figure 3").

Miss any of the middle five and the image still _renders_ — it just renders slow, janky, or inaccessible. That's what makes images deceptive: the broken version looks fine on your fast laptop.

## 4\. Sizes, Density & the `sizes` Math
Two different "size" concepts collide in images, and confusing them is a rite of passage.

**Rendered size** = how big the image box is on screen (CSS pixels). **Intrinsic size** = how many actual pixels are in the file. **Device pixel ratio (DPR)** = how many physical pixels per CSS pixel (a Retina phone is 2x or 3x). A 400px-wide slot on a 3x phone wants a _1200px_ file to look crisp.

A practical responsive scale for a content image:

| Slot context | Rendered (CSS px) | Serve at 1x | Serve at 2x | Serve at 3x |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Thumbnail / avatar | 48–96px | 96px | 192px | 288px |
| Card / grid tile | 300–400px | 400px | 800px | 1200px |
| Article body (max) | 640–768px | 768px | 1536px | — |
| Full-bleed hero | 100vw | 1280px | 2560px | 3840px |

Rules that matter:

*   **Don't ship one giant file to everyone.** A 4000px original downscaled by the browser to a 400px slot wastes ~99% of its bytes and still decodes at full size (a memory cost, see §14).
*   **The** **`sizes`** **attribute is a** **_layout_** **promise, not a wish.** It tells the browser how wide the image renders _at each breakpoint_ so it can choose from `srcset` before CSS is applied. Get it wrong and the browser over- or under-fetches.
*   **`sizes`** **is width, not columns.** `sizes="(max-width: 600px) 100vw, 400px"` reads: below 600px viewport the image is full-width; otherwise it's 400px wide. Match it to your actual CSS.
*   **Cap the top end.** Beyond ~3x DPR the human eye stops noticing; there's no reason to serve a 5K file. Most systems cap candidate widths around 1920–2560 for heroes.
*   **Density vs width descriptors.** Use `x` descriptors (`img@2x.jpg 2x`) for a fixed-size image (logo, avatar). Use `w` descriptors (`img-800.jpg 800w`) plus `sizes` for anything that reflows across breakpoints. Mixing them is invalid.

## 5\. States (the part image-builders forget)
People think an image has one state: loaded. A production image has at least seven, and skipping the in-between ones is what makes a page feel janky.

1. **Reserved / empty (pre-load)** — space is already booked via `width`/`height` or `aspect-ratio`, but no pixels yet. Done right, this is invisible: nothing shifts when the image lands.
2. **Placeholder** — something occupies the reserved box: a skeleton shimmer, a dominant-color fill, or a blurred LQIP. Better than a blank hole; masks latency.
3. **Loading / decoding** — bytes arriving; the browser is decoding. With `decoding="async"` this doesn't block the main thread.
4. **Loaded (rest)** — the real image, sharp, at full quality. Ideally cross-faded in from the placeholder, not a hard pop.
5. **Error / broken** — the fetch failed or the file is corrupt. The default is the browser's ugly broken-image glyph _plus the alt text_. Production replaces it with an intentional fallback (a branded placeholder, an avatar's initials — see **Avatar**).
6. **Lazy / not-yet-triggered** — `loading="lazy"` and still far below the fold; the browser hasn't fetched it. Space is still reserved.
7. **Zoomed / lightbox (optional)** — an interactive full-resolution view triggered on click. Its own focus-management and keyboard story (Escape closes, focus trapped, restored on close).

Bonus states worth naming: **selected** (in a gallery/grid), **priority** (the LCP image, fetched eager + high), and **animated** (GIF/APNG/animated WebP/AVIF, which should respect `prefers-reduced-motion`).

The through-line: **reserve the space first, then manage the transition into it.** Amateur builds have exactly two states — nothing, then a sudden pop that shoves the page. That pop is the CLS problem in §12.

## 6\. Types & Formats
Two axes: the _element type_ (§1) and the _file format_. Format choice is a real engineering decision with byte-weight consequences.

**Raster formats (photographs, complex imagery):**
*   **AVIF** — the 2026 front-runner. Best compression (often 30–50% smaller than JPEG at equal quality), wide-gamut and HDR support, alpha, animation. Slightly slower to encode; decode is fine. Ship it _first_ with fallbacks.
*   **WebP** — the safe modern default. ~25–35% smaller than JPEG, near-universal support now, alpha + animation. The reliable middle rung.
*   **JPEG** — the universal fallback for photos. Everything decodes it. Lossy, no alpha.
*   **PNG** — lossless, alpha transparency. Right for crisp UI, screenshots, logos with transparency — _wrong_ for photos (huge files). WebP/AVIF lossless usually beats it.
*   **GIF** — obsolete for everything. For animation, an animated WebP or AVIF (or better, an actual `<video>` with `muted autoplay loop`) is dramatically smaller. Only reason to keep GIF is a legacy platform that demands it.

**Vector:**
*   **SVG** — resolution-independent, tiny for icons/logos/diagrams, styleable with CSS, animatable. Perfect for anything geometric. _Not_ for photos. Note the security caveat: SVG can carry script — sanitize any user-uploaded SVG (§9 security note).

**The delivery rule:** offer the newest format the browser accepts and fall back gracefully. `<picture>` with `<source type="image/avif">`, then `<source type="image/webp">`, then a JPEG `<img>` is the canonical pattern. A CDN/image service (Cloudinary, imgix, Next/Image, Cloudflare Images) does this content-negotiation automatically via the `Accept` header — which is why most teams stop hand-writing `<picture>` for format and reserve it for art-direction.

**Art-direction vs resolution-switching** — the distinction that decides whether you need `<picture>` at all:
*   **Resolution switching** = _same image_, different sizes. Handled by `srcset` + `sizes` on a plain `<img>`. Browser picks the best-fitting file.
*   **Art direction** = _different crop/composition_ per breakpoint (a wide cinematic hero on desktop, a tight vertical crop on mobile so the subject's face survives). This _requires_ `<picture>` with `media` conditions, because you're changing _what_ is shown, not just how big.

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use an `<img>` when the pixels carry meaning the surrounding markup doesn't. Reach for something else when:

*   **It's an icon** → use an inline `<svg>` (styleable, crisp, no extra request) or an icon font, not a raster `<img>`.
*   **It's pure decoration** → CSS `background-image`, or `<img alt="">`. Don't pollute the accessibility tree.
*   **It's text** → _never_ bake text into an image. It can't be selected, translated, resized, or read by screen readers, and it blurs on zoom. Use real text over an image via CSS.
*   **It's animation/video** → a silent looping clip should be `<video muted autoplay loop playsinline>`, not a GIF. Order-of-magnitude smaller.
*   **It's a chart/diagram that must stay legible and themeable** → inline SVG beats a flattened PNG.

Placement heuristics:
*   **The LCP image goes first and loads eager.** Hero images are the one place you _want_ the browser to fetch immediately — never lazy (§12).
*   **Below-fold images lazy-load.** Everything the user hasn't scrolled to yet defers, freeing bandwidth for what's visible.
*   **Reserve every image's box** regardless of position, so nothing shifts (§12).
*   **In a gallery or slider, this is a Carousel problem** — see the **Carousel** doc for the lazy-load-adjacent-slides and preload-neighbor patterns, which layer on top of everything here.

## 8\. Images Across Design Systems
Same element, different opinions about how much to wrap it.

*   **Material Design (Google):** treats images as content within cards and lists, with defined aspect ratios and a "loading" scrim. Emphasizes responsive sizing and the ripple/scrim on interactive imagery. Strong guidance on avoiding layout shift via fixed ratios.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** SF Symbols cover icons (vector, weight-matched to text); photographs get `AsyncImage` in SwiftUI with explicit placeholder/success/failure phases. Emphasis on `@2x`/`@3x` asset catalogs and Dynamic Type-aware layout.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** persona (avatar) and image components with built-in fallback/initials logic and shape tokens. Enterprise-grade fallback behavior baked in.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** `<Image>` with built-in preview (lightbox), lazy-load, placeholder, and fallback props. Data-dense admin defaults — small, controlled, fallback-first.
*   **Tailwind (utility-first):** no image component; you compose `object-cover`, `aspect-[16/9]`, `w-full` etc. yourself. Freedom plus the risk of forgetting the CLS guard.
*   **shadcn/Radix:** no dedicated image primitive (images aren't an interaction problem), but the **Avatar** primitive _is_ shadcn/Radix — `Avatar` / `AvatarImage` / `AvatarFallback` with automatic fallback on load error. That's the pattern to steal for any content image that needs a graceful failure state.
*   **Next.js / Nuxt / Astro:** framework-level `<Image>` components that automate format negotiation, `srcset` generation, lazy-loading, and dimension enforcement. The modern default for React/Vue/Astro apps (§9).

## 8b. Visual Styles (the same image, eleven aesthetics)
The image content is fixed; the _frame and treatment_ is the skin. See §17 for the full eleven. In short: the pixels don't change, but the border, radius, shadow, overlay, and hover treatment do — and none of them touch the alt text, the reserved dimensions, or the load behavior.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin. The `alt`, the reserved box (no CLS), the lazy/eager decision, and the ≥4.5:1 contrast of any text _over_ the image stay identical across all eleven skins. Full guidance in [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551).

### What's trending in 2026
*   **AVIF as default, AVIF-animation replacing GIF.** The format war is effectively over for new builds; AVIF-first with WebP/JPEG fallback is the house pattern.
*   **Framework** **`<Image>`** **everywhere.** Hand-written `<picture>` for _format_ is fading; teams let Next/Nuxt/Astro/CDN negotiate format and generate `srcset`, and reserve `<picture>` for genuine art-direction.
*   **LQIP everywhere, done cheaply.** Blur-up placeholders generated at build time (BlurHash, ThumbHash, or a base64 20px inline) so the perceived-load story is solid without a runtime cost.
*   **Priority Hints mainstream.** `fetchpriority="high"` on the LCP image and `="low"` on below-fold clutter is now standard tuning, not exotic.
*   **AI-generated + on-the-fly variants.** CDNs generating crops, focal points, and even format per request; content-aware cropping that keeps the subject centered across art-direction breakpoints.
*   **HDR and wide-gamut (P3) imagery** shipping to capable displays via AVIF, with SDR fallbacks.

**For UJG:** default every content image to AVIF→WebP→JPEG through the CDN, blur-up LQIP tinted toward Eminence so the placeholder feels branded, and reserve `<picture>` art-direction for hero crops where the subject would be lost on mobile. Text over imagery always gets a scrim to hold ≥4.5:1 contrast against the busy Afro-Futurist backdrops.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML (the foundation everyone should master first)

```plain
<!-- Content image: real alt, reserved dimensions, responsive sources -->
<img
  src="/photos/greenhouse-800.jpg"
  srcset="/photos/greenhouse-400.jpg 400w,
          /photos/greenhouse-800.jpg 800w,
          /photos/greenhouse-1600.jpg 1600w"
  sizes="(max-width: 600px) 100vw, 800px"
  width="800" height="600"
  alt="Rows of seedlings under grow lights in a vertical farm"
  loading="lazy" decoding="async">

<!-- Decorative image: empty alt so screen readers skip it -->
<img src="/decor/vine-divider.svg" alt="" width="1200" height="40">

<!-- Functional image: alt describes the ACTION, not the picture -->
<a href="/">
  <img src="/brand/ujg-monogram.svg" width="48" height="48"
       alt="Urban Jungle Goddess home">
</a>

<!-- The LCP hero: eager + high priority, NEVER lazy -->
<img
  src="/hero-1600.avif"
  srcset="/hero-800.avif 800w, /hero-1600.avif 1600w, /hero-2560.avif 2560w"
  sizes="100vw"
  width="1600" height="900"
  alt="Founder standing in a neon-lit indoor jungle"
  fetchpriority="high" decoding="async">
<!-- note: no loading="lazy" here. Lazy-loading the LCP is a self-inflicted wound. -->

<!-- picture: FORMAT switching with graceful fallback -->
<picture>
  <source type="image/avif" srcset="/product.avif">
  <source type="image/webp" srcset="/product.webp">
  <img src="/product.jpg" width="600" height="600"
       alt="Handwoven basket in goldenrod and deep purple" loading="lazy">
</picture>

<!-- picture: ART DIRECTION — different crop per breakpoint -->
<picture>
  <source media="(max-width: 600px)" srcset="/hero-portrait.avif" type="image/avif">
  <source media="(min-width: 601px)" srcset="/hero-wide.avif" type="image/avif">
  <img src="/hero-wide.jpg" width="1600" height="900" alt="Product hero" fetchpriority="high">
</picture>

<!-- figure + figcaption: image with an associated, visible caption -->
<figure>
  <img src="/chart-800.png" width="800" height="450"
       alt="Bar chart: monthly revenue climbing from March to July 2026">
  <figcaption>Monthly revenue, Q2 2026. Source: internal dashboard.</figcaption>
</figure>
```

Key HTML rules:
*   **`alt`** **is mandatory on every** **`<img>`** — present, not absent. `alt=""` (empty) is a _deliberate_ signal "this is decorative, skip it." A _missing_ `alt` makes screen readers fall back to reading the filename aloud ("greenhouse-800-final-v2-dot-jpg"). Empty and missing are not the same.
*   **Never write "image of…" or "picture of…"** in alt. The screen reader already announces "image"; you'd get "image, image of a basket." Describe the content directly: "Handwoven basket in goldenrod."
*   **Always set** **`width`** **and** **`height`** (or `aspect-ratio` in CSS). This is the single most important CLS fix — it lets the browser reserve the correct box before the bytes arrive.
*   **`loading="lazy"`** **for below-fold, never for the LCP/above-fold image.** Lazy-loading your hero delays the exact thing the LCP metric measures.
*   **`<picture>`** **is for choice** (format or art-direction). If you just need different _sizes_ of the _same_ image, that's `srcset`/`sizes` on a plain `<img>` — no `<picture>` needed.

### 9.2 CSS (states & fit done right)

```css
/* Reserve space intrinsically: modern browsers use width/height attrs to
   compute aspect-ratio automatically, but set it explicitly for fluid images. */
img {
  max-width: 100%;
  height: auto;            /* preserve ratio when width is constrained */
  display: block;          /* kill the inline-baseline gap under images */
}

/* Fixed box with a mismatched image: fit + focal point */
.thumb {
  aspect-ratio: 4 / 3;     /* reserves space even before the file loads */
  width: 100%;
  object-fit: cover;       /* fill the box, crop overflow */
  object-position: 50% 30%;/* bias the crop toward the top (faces survive) */
  background: #1c1420;     /* dominant-color placeholder shows through the gap */
}

.contain-fit {
  aspect-ratio: 1 / 1;
  object-fit: contain;     /* letterbox: show whole image, no crop (logos, art) */
}

/* Blur-up: the LQIP scales up blurred, real image fades in over it */
.blur-up {
  filter: blur(12px);
  transform: scale(1.05);  /* hide blurred edge bleed */
  transition: filter .4s ease, transform .4s ease;
}
.blur-up.is-loaded { filter: blur(0); transform: none; }

/* Skeleton shimmer placeholder while loading */
.skeleton {
  background: linear-gradient(100deg, #1c1420 30%, #2a1f34 50%, #1c1420 70%);
  background-size: 200% 100%;
  animation: shimmer 1.2s infinite linear;
}
@keyframes shimmer { to { background-position: -200% 0; } }

/* Honor reduced motion: no shimmer, no fade */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .skeleton { animation: none; }
  .blur-up { transition: none; }
}
```

The `object-fit`/`object-position` pair is the workhorse: `cover` fills and crops, `contain` letterboxes, and `object-position` decides _which_ part survives the crop. Setting `object-position` toward the subject (often `50% 25–35%` for people) is why professional galleries never behead their subjects on mobile.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (a proper reusable Image with placeholder, skeleton & error fallback)

```typescript
import { useState, ImgHTMLAttributes } from "react";

type Props = Omit<ImgHTMLAttributes<HTMLImageElement>, "onLoad" | "onError"> & {
  src: string;
  alt: string;                 // REQUIRED — no accidental missing alt
  width: number;               // REQUIRED — forces a CLS-safe reserved box
  height: number;
  blurDataURL?: string;        // tiny base64 LQIP for blur-up
  fallbackSrc?: string;        // branded placeholder on error
  priority?: boolean;          // LCP image: eager + high, never lazy
};

export function Image({
  src, alt, width, height,
  blurDataURL, fallbackSrc, priority = false,
  className = "", ...rest
}: Props) {
  const [status, setStatus] = useState<"loading" | "loaded" | "error">("loading");
  const showSrc = status === "error" && fallbackSrc ? fallbackSrc : src;

  return (
    <span
      className={`img-wrap ${className}`}
      style={{ aspectRatio: `${width} / ${height}`, display: "block", position: "relative" }}
    >
      {/* placeholder layer: blur-up LQIP or skeleton, hidden once loaded */}
      {status === "loading" && (
        blurDataURL
          ? <img src={blurDataURL} alt="" aria-hidden="true"
                 className="blur-up" style={coverFill} />
          : <span className="skeleton" aria-hidden="true" style={coverFill} />
      )}

      {status === "error" && !fallbackSrc && (
        // honest, accessible failure — not the browser's broken glyph
        <span role="img" aria-label={alt} className="img-error" style={coverFill}>
          Image unavailable
        </span>
      )}

      <img
        src={showSrc}
        alt={alt}
        width={width}
        height={height}
        // LCP image loads eager + high; everything else defers
        loading={priority ? "eager" : "lazy"}
        fetchPriority={priority ? "high" : "auto"}
        decoding="async"
        onLoad={() => setStatus("loaded")}
        onError={() => setStatus((s) => (s === "error" ? s : "error"))}
        style={{
          ...coverFill,
          objectFit: "cover",
          opacity: status === "loaded" ? 1 : 0,
          transition: "opacity .4s ease",
        }}
        {...rest}
      />
    </span>
  );
}

const coverFill = {
  position: "absolute" as const,
  inset: 0,
  width: "100%",
  height: "100%",
} as const;
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:
*   **`alt`****\*\*\*\*,** **`width`****\*\*\*\*,** **`height`** **are non-optional in the type.** The compiler _forces_ a reserved box and a text alternative — the two things juniors forget — before the code runs. A whole class of CLS and a11y bugs becomes a build error.
*   **Three real states** — loading (blur-up or skeleton), loaded (fade-in), error (branded fallback or an accessible "unavailable" that _keeps the alt_). No raw broken-image glyph.
*   **`priority`** **collapses the LCP decision to one prop:** `eager` + `fetchPriority="high"` and _no_ lazy. Pass it on your hero, leave it off everywhere else.
*   **`aspectRatio`** **on the wrapper reserves space even before the LQIP loads** — the box never resizes, so nothing on the page shifts.
*   **Extends native** **`ImgHTMLAttributes`** so `sizes`, `srcSet`, `crossOrigin`, `referrerPolicy`, `data-*` all pass through for free.

This is exactly the shape the **Avatar** component specializes: an Image with a circular clip, an initials fallback instead of "Image unavailable," and a fixed small size. Build the general Image first, then Avatar is a thin wrapper.

### 9.4 Next.js `<Image>` (what it automates for you)
The Next.js `<Image>` is the reference implementation of "do all of §9.1–9.3 for me." It's the single biggest reason hand-rolled `<picture>`/`srcset` is fading in React apps.

```typescript
import Image from "next/image";

// Above-the-fold hero: priority disables lazy AND sets fetchpriority=high
export function Hero() {
  return (
    <Image
      src="/hero.jpg"
      alt="Founder in a neon-lit indoor jungle"
      width={1600}
      height={900}
      priority                      // eager + high; use ONLY on the LCP image
      sizes="100vw"
      placeholder="blur"            // needs blurDataURL (auto for static imports)
      blurDataURL={heroBlur}
    />
  );
}

// Fluid, fill-a-container image (unknown intrinsic size)
export function CardImage() {
  return (
    <div style={{ position: "relative", aspectRatio: "4 / 3" }}>
      <Image
        src="/card.jpg"
        alt="Handwoven basket in goldenrod and deep purple"
        fill                        // absolutely fills the positioned parent
        sizes="(max-width: 600px) 100vw, 400px"
        style={{ objectFit: "cover" }}
      />
    </div>
  );
}
```

What Next.js `<Image>` does automatically, so you don't:
*   **Format negotiation** — serves AVIF/WebP with fallback based on the `Accept` header. No hand-written `<picture>`.
*   **`srcset`** **+** **`sizes`** **generation** — creates the responsive candidate set from your `sizes` and the configured device widths. You never list widths by hand.
*   **Lazy-loading by default** — everything is `loading="lazy"` _unless_ you pass `priority`. This is why forgetting `priority` on your hero is the classic Next.js LCP regression.
*   **Dimension enforcement** — you _must_ give `width`/`height` or `fill`, so the reserved box (CLS guard) is mandatory by API design.
*   **Blur-up placeholder** — `placeholder="blur"` (auto blurDataURL for static imports) with zero runtime cost.
*   **On-demand optimization + caching** — resizes and re-encodes at the edge and caches the result.

The two gotchas: (1) **`priority`** **is the LCP override — use it on exactly one image**, the hero; sprinkling it everywhere defeats prioritization. (2) **`fill`** **needs a positioned, dimensioned parent** or it collapses. Nuxt `<NuxtImg>`, Astro `<Image>`, and Gatsby's plugin all mirror this same feature set.

### 9.5 Vanilla JS (lazy-load fallback + load/error handling)
Native `loading="lazy"` covers ~all modern browsers, but you still hook load/error for the fade-in and fallback. `IntersectionObserver` is the manual version for custom triggers.

```javascript
// Fade-in on load, branded fallback on error — no framework
document.querySelectorAll("img[data-fade]").forEach((img) => {
  const done = () => img.classList.add("is-loaded");
  if (img.complete && img.naturalWidth) done();       // already cached
  img.addEventListener("load", done, { once: true });
  img.addEventListener("error", () => {
    img.src = "/fallback/placeholder.svg";             // intentional fallback
    img.classList.add("is-error");
  }, { once: true });
});

// Manual lazy-load for a custom margin (native handles the common case)
const io = new IntersectionObserver((entries, obs) => {
  for (const e of entries) {
    if (!e.isIntersecting) continue;
    const img = e.target;
    img.src = img.dataset.src;                 // swap placeholder -> real src
    if (img.dataset.srcset) img.srcset = img.dataset.srcset;
    obs.unobserve(img);
  }
}, { rootMargin: "200px" });                   // start loading 200px early
document.querySelectorAll("img[data-src]").forEach((img) => io.observe(img));
```

The `naturalWidth` check catches the trap where an image loads from cache _before_ your JS attaches the `load` listener — without it, cached images never fade in and sit invisible at `opacity: 0`.

### 9.8 Node.js (the endpoint behind an upload / on-the-fly resize)
When users _upload_ images, the server becomes responsible for validation, re-encoding, and generating the responsive set. `sharp` is the standard tool.

```typescript
import express from "express";
import multer from "multer";
import sharp from "sharp";
import { z } from "zod";

const upload = multer({ limits: { fileSize: 8 * 1024 * 1024 } }); // cap bytes
const router = express.Router();
const WIDTHS = [400, 800, 1600];

router.post("/api/images", requireAuth, upload.single("file"), async (req, res) => {
  if (!req.file) return res.status(400).json({ error: "No file" });

  // 1. Verify it's really an image by decoding the header, not trusting the name
  let meta;
  try { meta = await sharp(req.file.buffer).metadata(); }
  catch { return res.status(422).json({ error: "Not a valid image" }); }

  // 2. Reject absurd dimensions (decompression-bomb defense)
  if ((meta.width ?? 0) * (meta.height ?? 0) > 40_000_000) {
    return res.status(422).json({ error: "Image too large" });
  }

  // 3. Re-encode to strip EXIF (privacy: GPS!) and generate AVIF/WebP variants
  const base = crypto.randomUUID();
  await Promise.all(WIDTHS.flatMap((w) => [
    sharp(req.file.buffer).rotate().resize(w).avif({ quality: 55 })
      .toFile(`/cdn/${base}-${w}.avif`),
    sharp(req.file.buffer).rotate().resize(w).webp({ quality: 72 })
      .toFile(`/cdn/${base}-${w}.webp`),
  ]));

  res.status(201).json({ id: base, widths: WIDTHS });
});

export default router;
```

Server concerns every image upload silently depends on:
*   **Validate by decoding, not by extension or MIME.** A file named `cat.jpg` can be a script. `sharp().metadata()` throwing = not an image. This is the core defense.
*   **Strip EXIF on re-encode.** Camera photos carry GPS coordinates and device serials. Re-encoding removes them — a real privacy leak if you don't.
*   **Auto-rotate (****`.rotate()`****)** applies the EXIF orientation flag before stripping it, so portrait photos don't come out sideways.
*   **Cap dimensions and bytes** — a 50000×50000 PNG is a "decompression bomb" that can OOM your server on decode. Reject by pixel count.
*   **Generate the responsive set server-side** so the frontend `srcset` has real files to point at.
*   **Never serve user-uploaded SVG inline without sanitizing** — SVG can embed `<script>` (stored XSS). Sanitize (e.g. DOMPurify with an SVG profile) or serve with `Content-Disposition: attachment` / a restrictive CSP.

### 9.9 Python (backend re-encode with Pillow)
Same shape in a Python stack — validate, strip, re-encode, generate variants.

```python
from fastapi import FastAPI, UploadFile, HTTPException, Depends
from PIL import Image, ImageOps
import io, uuid

app = FastAPI()
Image.MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS = 40_000_000        # decompression-bomb guard
WIDTHS = [400, 800, 1600]

@app.post("/api/images", status_code=201)
async def upload_image(file: UploadFile, user=Depends(require_auth)):
    raw = await file.read()
    if len(raw) > 8 * 1024 * 1024:
        raise HTTPException(413, "Too large")
    try:
        img = Image.open(io.BytesIO(raw))
        img.verify()                        # validate structure...
        img = Image.open(io.BytesIO(raw))   # ...then reopen to actually use
    except Exception:
        raise HTTPException(422, "Not a valid image")

    img = ImageOps.exif_transpose(img)      # apply orientation, drop EXIF
    base = uuid.uuid4().hex
    for w in WIDTHS:
        h = round(img.height * (w / img.width))
        img.resize((w, h)).save(f"/cdn/{base}-{w}.webp", "WEBP", quality=72)
        img.resize((w, h)).save(f"/cdn/{base}-{w}.avif", "AVIF", quality=55)
    return {"id": base, "widths": WIDTHS}
```

**Rendering** an image server-side (Django/Jinja) is the same responsive `<img>` — the template's job is to emit correct `srcset`/`sizes`/`width`/`height`/`alt`; Python's job is to have generated the files and to defend the upload endpoint.

### 9.11 Web Component (framework-agnostic)
A reusable image-with-fallback that works anywhere:

```typescript
class SmartImg extends HTMLElement {
  static observedAttributes = ["src", "alt", "width", "height"];
  connectedCallback() {
    if (!this.shadowRoot) this.attachShadow({ mode: "open" });
    this.render();
  }
  attributeChangedCallback() { if (this.shadowRoot) this.render(); }
  render() {
    const src = this.getAttribute("src") ?? "";
    const alt = this.getAttribute("alt") ?? "";     // author must supply real alt
    const w = this.getAttribute("width") ?? "";
    const h = this.getAttribute("height") ?? "";
    const root = this.shadowRoot!;
    root.textContent = "";                            // clear the previous render

    // Static stylesheet only. width/height are numeric, so coerce them before
    // interpolating — never drop an author string straight into the sheet.
    const wNum = Number(w) || 0, hNum = Number(h) || 0;
    const style = document.createElement("style");
    style.textContent = `
      :host { display: block; }
      img { width: 100%; height: auto; aspect-ratio: ${wNum} / ${hNum};
            object-fit: cover; background: #1c1420; }
      img.err { object-fit: contain; padding: 12px; }`;

    // Build the <img> with the DOM API + setAttribute so src/alt/width/height are
    // treated as DATA, never parsed as HTML. Interpolating them into innerHTML is
    // an XSS hole (a crafted alt='"><script>...' breaks out) — see §15.
    const img = document.createElement("img");
    img.setAttribute("src", src);
    img.setAttribute("alt", alt);                     // real, author-supplied alt
    if (w) img.setAttribute("width", w);
    if (h) img.setAttribute("height", h);
    img.setAttribute("loading", "lazy");
    img.setAttribute("decoding", "async");
    // Fallback exactly once. { once: true } removes the listener after it fires,
    // so a failing /fallback.svg can't re-trigger it into an infinite error loop
    // (the bug an inline onerror="this.src=..." creates). Mirrors §9.5.
    img.addEventListener("error", () => {
      img.src = "/fallback.svg";
      img.classList.add("err");
    }, { once: true });

    root.append(style, img);
  }
}
customElements.define("smart-img", SmartImg);
// Usage: <smart-img src="/x.jpg" alt="Real description" width="800" height="600"></smart-img>
```

### 9.13 Testing the Image (unit + a11y)
An image's contract is: it renders, it has an honest text alternative, it reserves space, and it degrades on error. Test the contract, not the pixels.

```typescript
      .toBeInTheDocument();
  });

  it("has no automatically-detectable a11y violations (axe)", async () => {
    const { container } = render(
      <Image src="/x.jpg" alt="Seedlings under grow lights" width={800} height={600} />
    );
    expect(await axe(container)).toHaveNoViolations();   // catches missing alt, etc.
  });

  it("decorative images are hidden from the a11y tree", () => {

  });

```

## 10\. Accessibility (the alt attribute is the spine)
For an image, accessibility is 80% one attribute done thoughtfully. The rest is contrast and motion.

**The alt decision tree** — the core skill:
*   **Informative image** (photo, illustration that adds meaning): write alt that conveys the _same information_ the image gives a sighted user. "Rows of seedlings under grow lights in a vertical farm." Be specific, be concise, stop before you narrate every pixel.
*   **Decorative image** (ornament, texture, a photo purely for mood next to text that already says it all): `alt=""` — empty but present. This tells assistive tech to skip it entirely. A _missing_ alt is the bug; an _empty_ alt is a decision.
*   **Functional image** (image inside a link or button — logo, icon): alt describes the **destination or action**, not the appearance. Linked logo → "Urban Jungle Goddess home." Magnifying-glass icon button → "Search." Never describe the glyph.
*   **Image of text** (avoid entirely, but if unavoidable): alt must contain the _exact_ text in the image.
*   **Complex image** (chart, graph, infographic): short alt names it ("Bar chart of monthly revenue, Q2 2026"); the _detail_ goes in adjacent text, a `<figcaption>`, a `longdesc`\-style linked description, or a data table. The alt is the summary, not the whole dataset.

**Never do:**
*   **"image of…" / "picture of…" / "graphic of…"** — the screen reader already announces the role "image." You'd double it. Just describe the content.
*   **Filenames as alt** (`alt="IMG_4023.jpg"`) — worse than useless, it's noise. This is what a _missing_ alt produces by default in some readers.
*   **Keyword-stuffing alt for SEO** — search engines penalize it and screen-reader users suffer. Alt is for humans first.
*   **Redundant alt** — if the caption right beside the image already says "Founder in the greenhouse," don't repeat it in alt; use `alt=""` and let the caption do the work, or make them complementary.

**Beyond alt:**
*   **`<figure>`****/****`<figcaption>`** associates a visible caption with the image as a unit; the caption is available to everyone, the alt still describes the image itself (they can differ — caption gives context, alt gives content).
*   **Contrast of text over images** — any text laid over a photo must hold ≥4.5:1 (WCAG AA) against the _busiest_ part of the image behind it. Use a scrim/overlay; never trust a raw photo to be a safe text background.
*   **Animated images** (GIF/animated WebP/AVIF) must respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — offer a static frame, or don't autoplay. Flashing content must stay under 3 flashes/second (seizure safety).
*   **`role="img"`** **on non-\*\*\*\*****`<img>`** — if you build an "image" out of an SVG or a `<span>` of emoji, give it `role="img"` and an `aria-label` so it has a name and a role.
*   **Don't put critical info in image-only form** — if the only place a price or a status appears is inside an image, non-visual users lose it.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The humble `<img>` keeps gaining superpowers:
*   **AVIF-first pipelines + HDR/P3 wide-gamut** delivery to capable displays, SDR fallback for the rest — richer color without blowing up SDR users' bytes.
*   **ThumbHash / BlurHash LQIP** — a ~20–30 byte string that decodes to a blurred, color-accurate placeholder client-side. Better than a base64 thumbnail, near-free.
*   **Content-aware / focal-point cropping** at the CDN — the service detects the subject and keeps it centered across every art-direction crop automatically, so mobile never beheads the subject.
*   **Priority Hints + Speculation** — `fetchpriority` plus the browser's preload scanner and speculative fetching getting smarter about which image is the LCP.
*   **View Transitions API** — native morphing of an image from a grid thumbnail into a full-screen detail view (shared-element transitions) with no heavy JS. Pairs beautifully with a **Carousel**\-to-lightbox flow.
*   **`<img>`** **in a container query world** — art-direction driven by the _container's_ width, not the viewport, so a component looks right in any slot.
*   **AI generation + on-the-fly variants** — CDNs generating alt-text drafts (still needs human review), background removal, and per-request recomposition.
*   **`decoding`** **+** **`content-visibility: auto`** — letting the browser skip off-screen image work entirely until it's near the viewport.

## 12\. Conversion & UX Killers (the silent ones)
None of these throw an error. They just quietly bleed speed, trust, and money.

*   **No** **`width`****/****`height`** **→ layout shift (the #1 sin).** The image pops in, shoves the text down, the user taps the wrong thing. It's the single biggest CLS source on the web, it hurts your Core Web Vitals _and_ your search ranking, and the fix is two attributes. **Every image gets a reserved box. No exceptions.**
*   **Lazy-loading the LCP / hero.** `loading="lazy"` on the above-the-fold hero delays the exact pixels the LCP metric measures — a self-inflicted ranking and speed wound. Above-fold = eager (+`fetchpriority="high"`). Below-fold = lazy. Getting these two backwards is the most common image-perf bug in the wild.
*   **Shipping the original.** A 4000×3000, 3 MB camera JPEG dropped into a 400px card. It downloads 20× the bytes it needs, decodes at full size (a memory cost on cheap phones), and it's invisible to you on fast wifi. Resize _and_ re-encode; never trust the source file.
*   **Old formats.** Serving JPEG/PNG when AVIF/WebP would be 30–50% smaller is free money left on the table. Turn on a CDN or framework `<Image>` and it's automatic.
*   **Missing or lazy alt.** Empty accessibility, legal exposure, and (with filename fallback) audible garbage for screen-reader users. Costs nothing to fix, costs real money to ignore.
*   **Wrong** **`object-fit`****\*\*\*\*.** `object-fit: fill` (or no fit on a mismatched box) stretches faces and products into funhouse distortion. Use `cover` + a focal `object-position`, or `contain` for things that must not crop.
*   **No error fallback.** The browser's broken-image glyph on a product page reads as "this store is broken." A branded placeholder or an Avatar-style initials fallback keeps trust.
*   **No placeholder on slow loads.** A blank white hole for two seconds on 3G feels broken. A skeleton, dominant-color block, or blur-up LQIP masks the latency and reads as "loading," not "broken."
*   **Text baked into images.** Un-selectable, un-translatable, invisible to search and screen readers, blurry on zoom, and it re-downloads on every copy change. Real text over an image, always.

**The through-line:** every one of these looks fine on the developer's fast laptop with a warm cache. They only hurt real users on real networks — which is exactly why they survive to production. Audit images before you blame the market.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
Senior moves that lean on the platform and the type system.

**Force the CLS guard at compile time.** Make `width`/`height` (or an explicit `decorative`/`fill` escape hatch) _required_ in the type so an image without reserved space can't compile — same trick as the Button's mandatory-`aria-label` union:

```typescript
type Dimensioned = { width: number; height: number; fill?: false };
type Filled = { fill: true; width?: never; height?: never }; // must have a sized parent
type ImgBase = { src: string; alt: string };                 // alt always required
type SafeImageProps = ImgBase & (Dimensioned | Filled);
// <SafeImage src alt /> won't compile without width+height OR fill. CLS guard enforced.
```

**Alt as a discriminated union** — make decorative _explicit_ so nobody forgets alt:

```typescript
type Informative = { decorative?: false; alt: string };
type Decorative  = { decorative: true;  alt?: never };  // forces alt="" internally
type ImgAlt = Informative | Decorative;
```

**Preload the LCP image** in the document head so the browser fetches it before it even parses the `<img>` — shaves real milliseconds off LCP:

```plain
<link rel="preload" as="image" href="/hero-1600.avif"
      imagesrcset="/hero-800.avif 800w, /hero-1600.avif 1600w" imagesizes="100vw"
      fetchpriority="high">
```

**Design tokens for image treatment** — radius, ratio, and scrim as tokens so the whole system reskins at once:

```css
:root {
  --img-radius: 12px;
```

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
Images _are_ the performance conversation on most sites. The levers, in order of impact:

*   **Serve the right** **_size_****\*\*\*\*.** Match served pixels to displayed pixels × DPR (§4). This is usually the single biggest win — bigger than any format change. A correctly-sized image beats a badly-sized AVIF.
*   **Serve the right** **_format_****\*\*\*\*.** AVIF → WebP → JPEG fallback. 30–50% fewer bytes for the same quality, automatic through a CDN or framework `<Image>`.
*   **Prioritize correctly.** Exactly one `fetchpriority="high"` (the LCP image), everything below the fold `loading="lazy"` (and optionally `fetchpriority="low"`). Preload the LCP image in `<head>`.
*   **Decode off the main thread.** `decoding="async"` so a big image doesn't block rendering while it decompresses.
*   **Mind the** **_decoded_** **memory, not just the download.** A 4000×3000 image is ~48 MB _decoded in RAM_ regardless of how small the compressed file is. On a cheap Android with many images, that's how you crash the tab. Serving the right dimensions fixes the download _and_ the memory.
*   **Don't thrash layout.** Fade images in with `opacity` (compositor-only), never by animating `width`/`height`. The reserved box means the fade never reflows.
*   **Cache aggressively.** Images are immutable once published — fingerprint the filename (`hero.a1b2c3.avif`) and serve with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`.
*   **CDN + edge resize.** Let the edge generate and cache per-size, per-format variants on demand rather than pre-building or (worse) shipping originals.
*   **Cap the candidate set.** Beyond ~2560px for heroes and ~3x DPR there's no visible gain — every extra candidate width is more storage and cache churn for nothing.

For **Carousel** specifically, all of this compounds: a 10-slide carousel that eager-loads and full-sizes every slide ships 10× the waste. Lazy-load non-visible slides, preload only the immediate neighbors, and size each slide to its actual rendered width. See the **Carousel** doc.

## 15\. Security (uploads & remote images)
Most images are static and low-risk. The moment users _upload_ or you _proxy remote_ images, it's an attack surface (details in §9.8):

*   **Validate by decoding, not by extension/MIME** — a `.jpg` can be anything. If the decoder throws, reject it.
*   **Strip EXIF on re-encode** — camera GPS + device serials are a real privacy leak.
*   **Guard against decompression bombs** — cap pixel count and bytes before decode (`Image.MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS`, `sharp` metadata check).
*   **Sanitize or never inline user SVG** — SVG can carry `<script>` = stored XSS. Sanitize with an SVG-aware sanitizer or serve as an attachment with a strict CSP.
*   **Don't blindly proxy remote image URLs** — an attacker-supplied `src` your server fetches is an SSRF vector (internal metadata endpoints, port scans). Allowlist hosts.
*   **Set** **`referrerPolicy`** **/** **`crossorigin`** deliberately for third-party images so you're not leaking referrers or breaking CORS-tainted canvas reads.
*   **CSP** **`img-src`** — constrain where images may load from so an injected `<img src=evil>` can't beacon out.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before an image is "done":
- [ ] Correct element: content `<img alt="…">`, decorative `alt=""`/`background-image`, functional alt describes the _action_, `<picture>` only for format/art-direction choice.
- [ ] `alt` is present on every `<img>` — meaningful for content, empty for decorative, never "image of…", never a filename.
- [ ] `width`/`height` (or `aspect-ratio`/`fill`) set on every image — reserved box, zero layout shift.
- [ ] Responsive: `srcset` + `sizes` (or a framework `<Image>`), sized to real rendered width × DPR — not the original.
- [ ] Modern format offered: AVIF/WebP with a JPEG/PNG fallback.
- [ ] LCP/hero image is `eager` + `fetchpriority="high"` (+ `<link rel=preload>`); **never** `loading="lazy"`.
- [ ] Below-fold images are `loading="lazy"` + `decoding="async"`.
- [ ] `object-fit`/`object-position` chosen so nothing stretches and no subject is cropped out.
- [ ] Placeholder for slow loads (skeleton / dominant color / blur-up LQIP) and an intentional error fallback — no raw broken glyph.
- [ ] Text over images has a scrim holding ≥4.5:1 contrast; animated images respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- [ ] Uploads validated by decode, EXIF stripped, dimensions/bytes capped, user SVG sanitized.
- [ ] axe/Lighthouse in CI fails the build on missing alt or an un-dimensioned/regressed LCP image.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same image, eleven skins)
Style is skin; the alt, the reserved box, the lazy/eager decision, and text-contrast-over-image never change. The frame does.

*   **Flat:** no border, no shadow, maybe a small radius. The image speaks; the container disappears. Fastest, most neutral.
*   **Material:** image sits in a card with elevation shadow, a defined aspect ratio, and a subtle scrim for any overlaid text; ripple only if it's interactive.
*   **Glassmorphism:** image behind or within a translucent `backdrop-filter: blur()` panel; watch text contrast over the blur, and never blur the _content_ image into illegibility.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026):** the image framed by a refractive translucent bezel with a specular rim and a sheen that shifts with motion/content — glassmorphism grown up. Web is an honest approximation (`backdrop-filter` + layered highlights); SwiftUI emits native `.glassEffect()`. Guard contrast and `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Neumorphism:** image inset into a same-color surface with dual light/dark shadows, like a pressed-in photo. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent use only.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** faux photo-frame — beveled mat, inner shadow, drop shadow, the "print on a desk" look. Tactile and literal.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** hard offset shadow, thick black border, zero radius, raw. The image is presented like a pasted photocopy — high personality.
*   **Claymorphism:** big radius, soft inner top-light + bottom-shadow, puffy pastel frame. Friendly, approachable galleries.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** image over or bordered by an animated multi-hue gradient glow on dark; premium and expressive. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (kill the animation).
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** generous whitespace, a strict grid, hairline or no border, precise aspect ratios. Typography and alignment do the work.
*   **UJG Brand:** Afro-Futurist house style — confident radius, a warm Goldenrod hairline or glow on deep Eminence/Night surfaces, a scrim tinted toward Eminence so overlaid Platinum text always clears 4.5:1, blur-up LQIP tinted to the brand so even the placeholder feels like UJG.

**Rule that never changes:** the eleven skins reskin the _frame and treatment_. The image's alt text, its reserved dimensions, its load priority, and the contrast of any text over it stay identical across all of them.
* * *

**Bottom line:** an image is a two-attribute component with an outsized blast radius — `alt` decides whether the content exists for everyone, and `width`/`height` decide whether the page holds still. Master those two, then layer on responsive sizing, modern formats, correct load priority, honest placeholders and fallbacks, and upload defense, and you've mastered the heaviest, most-shipped, most-often-botched element on the web. Build the general Image well and **Avatar** and every image inside a **Carousel** become thin specializations of it.
