## 1\. What a Carousel Actually Is
A carousel is a **space-constrained container that shows one slice of a horizontal (occasionally vertical) sequence at a time, with controls to move through the rest.** That's the honest definition. It trades _visibility_ for _space_: you hide N-1 items to save room, and hope the user does the work to see them. Most don't.

The word gets slapped on three genuinely different things people lump together:
**Carousel / Slider (hero rotator):** a single large stage that rotates through full-bleed slides — the marketing homepage classic. Usually auto-advancing. This is the abused one.
**Content slider / Multi-item carousel:** shows several items at once (product cards, testimonials, logos) and scrolls a row of them. Rarely auto-advances. This is the _useful_ one.
**Gallery / Lightbox:** a set of images you page through deliberately, often full-screen, user-driven. Different intent — the user _came to look_.

Two more distinctions seniors keep straight:
**Carousel vs. Tabs:** tabs show _labeled, unordered_ panels the user picks between; a carousel shows an _ordered sequence_ the user pages through. If your slides have meaningful names ("Overview," "Pricing," "Reviews"), you want Tabs ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216711](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216711)), not a carousel.
**Carousel vs. a plain scroll region:** a `overflow-x: auto` row _is_ a carousel in the honest sense — CSS scroll-snap turns any overflow row into a carousel with zero JS. The fancy library is often solving a problem the browser already solved.

The single most common mistake: **building a hero rotator because the stakeholder asked for one, when the real need was "we have five messages and can't decide which is #1."** A carousel is not a decision-making tool. It's a way to _avoid_ deciding — and the data says users pay for that indecision.

## 2\. Why It Matters — and Why You Probably Shouldn't Build One
Lead with the uncomfortable truth, because this is the component where "should we?" matters more than "how?"

**Users rarely see slide 2.** The canonical data is old but has never been contradicted:
Notre Dame's **Erik Runyon** instrumented the university homepage carousel: the **first slide got ~84% of all clicks. Slides 2–5 combined got the remaining ~16%**, with slide 5 down around 1%. Whatever you put past slide 1 is nearly invisible.
Nielsen Norman Group's usability work found users routinely **banner-blind auto-rotating carousels entirely** — motion in the hero region reads as an ad and the eye skips it. NN/g's summary is blunt: "Auto-forwarding carousels annoy users and reduce visibility."
The community site [**shouldiuseacarousel.com**](http://shouldiuseacarousel.com) exists precisely because the answer is usually _no_.

So the stakes cut two ways:
**Conversion:** if 84% of engagement lands on slide 1, a five-slide hero is a one-slide hero wearing a costume — and the rotation actively _steals_ clicks from slide 1 by moving the CTA out from under the user's cursor mid-reach. That's measurable lost revenue.
**Accessibility & trust:** an auto-advancing carousel is one of the most common **WCAG failures** shipped to production (moving content with no pause control fails SC 2.2.2). It's also a motion-sickness and cognitive-load hazard. A carousel that yanks the viewport or steals focus makes an app feel broken and hostile.
**Performance:** carousels ship heavy — a JS library, every slide's images (often _all_ eager-loaded), and layout thrash on every transition. The hero carousel is a top offender in Largest Contentful Paint regressions.

**The senior move:** before building, ask "would a single strong hero + a clear content hierarchy below it beat this?" Usually yes. If you must build one, build the _multi-item, user-driven, non-auto-advancing_ kind, and treat autoplay as a liability to be justified, not a default. **For UJG:** on the marketing site, prefer one confident Goldenrod-on-Eminence hero statement over a rotator. Reserve carousels for genuinely equivalent sets — a testimonial row, a portfolio strip — where sequence doesn't imply priority.

## 3\. Anatomy of a Carousel
Every well-built carousel is composed of predictable parts. The parts you _skip_ are usually the accessibility ones.
**Viewport (the window):** the clipping container with `overflow: hidden` (or `overflow-x: auto` for scroll-snap). Defines how much you see.
**Track (the rail):** the wide flex/grid row that holds all slides and slides/scrolls behind the viewport.
**Slides (the items):** each panel. Semantically a `group` labeled "X of N." Usually a Cards ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200471](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200471)) or an image.
**Previous / Next controls:** real `<button>`s. Must be reachable, labeled ("Previous slide"/"Next slide"), and disabled or wrapped at the ends (non-loop).
**Pagination / dots:** the position indicator. Done right, this is a **tablist** (each dot a `tab` controlling a slide `tabpanel`) or a native `scroll-marker-group`. Done wrong, it's a row of unlabeled `<div>`s.
**Pause / Play control:** mandatory the instant anything auto-advances. This is the WCAG 2.2.2 hinge.
**Live region:** an off-screen `aria-live` announcer that tells screen-reader users "Slide 3 of 6" when the slide changes from a control (never on autoplay).
**Peek / gutter:** the sliver of the next slide left visible at the edge — the single best affordance that "there's more."
**Progress indicator (optional):** a bar or counter ("3 / 6") — more honest than dots for large counts.

## 4\. Sizing, Scale & Density
Carousel sizing is really about **how many items are visible and how much of the next one peeks**. Real starting values:

| Pattern | Slides in view | Peek | Gap | Snap | Typical use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Hero / single | 1 | 0 | 0 | `center`/`start` | Full-bleed marketing (reconsider) |
| Hero w/ peek | 1 + edges | 8–16% | 16px | `center` | Mobile "swipe for more" hint |
| Product row | 2–4 | 10–20% | 16–24px | `start` | E-commerce, portfolio |
| Logo strip | 4–8 | — | 32px | none/`start` | Social proof, marquee |
| Thumbnail nav | 5–10 | — | 8px | `start` | Gallery sub-nav |

Rules that matter:
**Always leave a peek** on touch. A slide that fills the viewport edge-to-edge gives zero signal there's a slide 2 — the #1 reason multi-slide content dies at slide 1. `scroll-padding-inline` + a partial-width slide does it.
**Drive item width off the viewport**, not a fixed px, so the same carousel shows 1.2 slides on mobile and 4.2 on desktop: `flex: 0 0 clamp(...)` or `width: calc(100% / var(--per-view) - gap)`.
**Controls sit outside the swipe path** on touch (dragging is the primary input); on desktop, prev/next overlay the edges but keep ≥44px targets and don't cover slide content.
**Aspect ratio locks the viewport height** (`aspect-ratio: 16/9`) so slides of different content heights don't cause layout shift — a CLS killer otherwise.

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A production carousel juggles far more state than a button. Missing states are the tell of a demo build that never met real content.
**Default / at-rest** — showing the current slide(s), controls in their resting look.
**At-start** — first slide active: Previous control disabled (non-loop) or hidden.
**At-end** — last slide active: Next control disabled (non-loop).
**Scrolling / in-transition** — mid-animation. Block re-triggers that would fight the current tween; snap will settle.
**Dragging / grabbing** — pointer is down and dragging the track; cursor `grabbing`, no snap until release.
**Autoplaying** — advancing on a timer. _Must_ expose a Pause control and _must_ pause on hover, on focus-within, and on `prefers-reduced-motion`.
**Paused** — autoplay stopped (by user, hover, focus, or tab hidden). Play control shows.
**Focus-within** — a control or slide has keyboard focus. Autoplay pauses; focused slide must be the visible one.
**Slide focused / active** — the current slide is the interactive one; off-screen slides are `inert`.
**Loading / lazy** — off-screen slide media not yet fetched; show a skeleton/placeholder, decode on approach.
**Empty / single-item** — 0 or 1 slides: render _no_ controls, _no_ dots, _no_ autoplay. A one-slide carousel is just an image — don't announce it as a carousel.
**Error** — a slide's media failed; show a fallback, don't leave a blank stage that breaks the count.

The four juniors miss every time: **at-start/at-end** (controls that do nothing at the ends), **paused-on-focus**, **inert off-screen slides**, and **single-item collapse**.

## 6\. Types & Variants
**Single-slide hero (rotator):** one stage, full-bleed. Auto-advance optional (and usually a mistake). The abused archetype.
**Multi-item / content slider:** several items visible, scrolls a row. The genuinely useful pattern.
**Peek carousel:** single or multi, with the next item's edge visible to signal "more." Best mobile affordance.
**Infinite / looping:** wraps end-to-start seamlessly (usually by cloning slides). Great feel, real a11y cost — see §12.
**Center-mode:** the active slide is centered and enlarged, neighbors shrink/dim. Editorial, testimonial galleries.
**Fade carousel:** slides cross-fade in place instead of sliding. No horizontal motion — gentler, but can't peek.
**Thumbnail-synced (gallery):** a main stage + a thumbnail strip that drives it; the PDP (product-detail-page) pattern. Two carousels, one shared index.
**Marquee / ticker:** continuous logo/announcement scroll. Technically a carousel; almost always should honor reduced-motion by stopping.
**Vertical carousel:** scrolls up/down (story reels, some feeds). Same rules, `scroll-snap-type: y`.
**Full-page / scroll-snap sections:** each "slide" is a viewport-height section — a carousel at page scale.

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a carousel when items are **genuinely equivalent, sequence doesn't imply priority, and space is the real constraint** — a testimonial set, a product image gallery, a related-products row, a logo wall. In all of those the user either _came to browse_ or the content is truly peer-level.

Don't use one when:
**You have one important message and four less-important ones.** That's a hierarchy problem. Show the one; put the rest below it. A carousel hides your best content behind a click 84% of users won't make.
**The slides are labeled, distinct sections.** Use Tabs ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216711](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216711)) or an accordion — users can jump straight to the one they want instead of paging.
**The content is critical to conversion.** Never bury a primary CTA on slide 3. If it matters, it's not in a carousel.
**It's your homepage hero, "to keep everyone happy."** The carousel-as-political-compromise is the single most common reason bad ones ship. Name it: a carousel doesn't resolve the disagreement, it hides it from users while satisfying no one.

Placement heuristics:
**Peek the next slide** so the affordance is visible without motion.
**Controls in thumb reach** on mobile; **don't** rely on tiny dots as the only control.
**Never auto-advance** where a CTA lives — the moving target steals the click.
If you must auto-advance, **≥5 seconds per slide minimum** (users need time to read), pause on any interaction, and provide a visible pause button.

## 8\. Carousels Across Design Systems
Knowing each system's stance makes you fluent in any codebase — and reveals that the _good_ systems are quietly hostile to the hero rotator.
**Material Design (Google):** Ships a real "Carousel" (hero, multi-browse, uncontained, full-screen layouts) built on _item resizing_ — the leading item is largest and items shrink toward the edge. Motion-forward, but Material's own guidance steers you toward multi-item browsing, not auto-rotating heroes.
**Apple HIG (iOS):** Calls it a **page control** (the row of dots) over a paged scroll view. Auto-advance is discouraged; the platform default is user-driven paging with a clear page indicator. `UIPageControl` + `UICollectionView` compositional layout.
**Fluent (Microsoft):** Has a Carousel in the web components / older frameworks, but enterprise Fluent leans toward decks and lists. Conservative motion.
**Ant Design / enterprise:** `<Carousel>` with `autoplay`, `dotPosition`, `fade`, `effect`. Built for dashboards and marketing pages; dense and configurable. Ant's is one of the more complete OOTB APIs.
**Bootstrap:** The ubiquitous `.carousel` with `data-bs-ride`, `.carousel-item`, indicators and controls baked in. Accessible-ish out of the box (it wires `aria-label`s and a live region) but auto-rides by default — _turn that off_.
**Tailwind / utility-first:** No carousel primitive — you compose scroll-snap utilities (`snap-x snap-mandatory`, `snap-start`) or reach for a headless lib. This is increasingly the _right_ answer: `snap-x` + `overflow-x-auto` is a carousel.
**shadcn/Radix:** shadcn's `Carousel` wraps **Embla** — headless behavior + your Tailwind skin, accessibility wired (`role="region"`, `aria-roledescription="carousel"`, slide groups, keyboard). The 2024+ web default when you need JS.
**Headless JS libraries:** **Embla** (tiny, ~4–6KB, precise, framework-agnostic, great a11y) and **Swiper** (huge feature set — virtual slides, coverflow, thumbs — but heavy, ~40KB+). Splide sits between. Choose Embla unless you need Swiper's exotic modes.

## 8b. Visual Styles (the same carousel, eleven skins)
Design systems are _whose_ rules; visual styles are _which look_. The same semantic carousel wears any skin — full guidance in 🎨 Design Styles (visual languages) ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551)).
**Flat:** solid slide backgrounds, crisp dots, no shadow. Fast, legible.
**Material:** elevated slides, ripple on control press, item-resize motion, filled arrow buttons.
**Glassmorphism:** translucent controls/dots floating over imagery with `backdrop-filter: blur()`. Watch control contrast against unpredictable slide content.
**Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language — pill-shaped controls of refractive translucent material with a specular rim and a sheen that reacts as slides move behind them. Glassmorphism grown up. SwiftUI emits native `.glassEffect()`; web is an honest approximation.
**Neumorphism:** soft dual-shadow arrows and dots on a same-color surface. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent only, never the sole control.
**Skeuomorphism:** beveled arrows, a "filmstrip" track texture, inset viewport. The literal physical-carousel look.
**Neo-Brutalism:** hard offset-shadow arrows, thick borders, zero-radius slides, oversized dots. High personality, great for editorial.
**Claymorphism:** puffy rounded controls, soft inner-light, big-radius slides. Friendly, playful galleries.
**Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue backdrop behind the stage. Premium — _must_ honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
**Minimal / Swiss:** no arrows at all, a thin progress bar and a "3 / 6" counter, type and grid do the work.
**UJG Brand:** Goldenrod arrows and active dot on deep Eminence/Night slides, confident radius, a warm glow on the active indicator. Peek the next slide's edge in Platinum. The house default.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin. The carousel's semantics, keyboard map, focus management, pause control, and 44px targets stay identical across all eleven. Guard glass/liquid-glass/neu control contrast against arbitrary slide imagery; guard aurora/liquid-glass and _all autoplay_ for `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 9\. The Code
The biggest section. The through-line: **the browser now ships most of a carousel natively.** Reach for JS only when you need drag physics, virtualization, or loop.

### 9.1 HTML (semantics first)
Structure the accessibility in _before_ any styling. This is the skeleton every other target dresses.

```plain
<section class="carousel" aria-roledescription="carousel" aria-label="Featured work">
  <!-- Live region: announces slide changes to screen readers -->
```

```plain
<div class="carousel__viewport">
    <ul class="carousel__track">
      <li class="carousel__slide" role="group"
          aria-roledescription="slide" aria-label="1 of 3">
        <img src="a.webp" alt="Command Core dashboard" />
      </li>
      <li class="carousel__slide" role="group"
          aria-roledescription="slide" aria-label="2 of 3" inert>
        <img src="b.webp" alt="Data Alchemy report" />
      </li>
      <li class="carousel__slide" role="group"
          aria-roledescription="slide" aria-label="3 of 3" inert>
        <img src="c.webp" alt="Sensory Studio poster" />
      </li>
    </ul>
```

```plain
<!-- Controls are REAL buttons, labeled -->
  <button type="button" class="carousel__prev" aria-label="Previous slide">‹</button>
```

›

```plain
<!-- Pause is mandatory the moment anything auto-advances -->
  <button type="button" class="carousel__toggle" aria-label="Pause carousel"
      aria-pressed="false">⏸</button>
```

```plain
<!-- Pagination as a tablist controlling slides -->
  <div class="carousel__dots" role="tablist" aria-label="Choose slide">
    <button role="tab" aria-selected="true"  aria-label="Slide 1" aria-controls="s1"></button>
    <button role="tab" aria-selected="false" aria-label="Slide 2" aria-controls="s2" tabindex="-1"></button>
    <button role="tab" aria-selected="false" aria-label="Slide 3" aria-controls="s3" tabindex="-1"></button>
  </div>
</section>
```

Key HTML rules:
`aria-roledescription="carousel"` on the region and `aria-roledescription="slide"` on each slide is the APG-blessed labeling — screen readers announce "carousel" / "slide," not "region" / "group."
Each slide is a `role="group"` labeled **"N of M"** so the total is spoken.
Off-screen slides get **`inert`** — removed from tab order _and_ the accessibility tree, so Tab can't land on a hidden slide's link.
Controls are `<button>`, never `<div onclick>`. The dots are a **tablist** (roving tabindex: one `tabindex="0"`, rest `-1`).

### 9.2 CSS Scroll-Snap Carousel (the zero-JS foundation)
This is the honest baseline: **a carousel with no JavaScript at all.** Overflow + snap does the paging; the browser handles momentum, drag, and keyboard scroll for free.

```css
.carousel__viewport {
  overflow-x: auto;
  scroll-snap-type: x mandatory;      /* snap to each slide */
  scroll-behavior: smooth;
  scroll-padding-inline: 16px;        /* where snapped slides rest */
  -webkit-overflow-scrolling: touch;
  scrollbar-width: none;              /* hide the bar; keep keyboard scroll */
}
.carousel__track {
  display: flex;
  gap: 16px;
  margin: 0; padding: 0; list-style: none;
}
.carousel__slide {
  scroll-snap-align: start;           /* snap edge */
  scroll-snap-stop: always;           /* don't fly past slides on a fast flick */
  /* 1.1 slides on mobile → peek; ~3.2 on desktop */
  flex: 0 0 clamp(78%, 70vw, calc(100% / 3 - 16px));
```

}

```css
/* Kill autoplay motion for users who ask */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .carousel__viewport { scroll-behavior: auto; }
}
```

`scroll-snap-stop: always` is the senior touch — without it a hard swipe skips five slides; with it every flick advances exactly one. `scroll-padding-inline` is what leaves the peek gutter. This alone is a production carousel for anything that doesn't need loop or autoplay.

### 9.3 The Native CSS Carousel (the 2026 path — `::scroll-button()` + `::scroll-marker`)
New in Chromium 135+ (2025) and the CSS Overflow 5 spec: the browser can now generate **prev/next buttons and a marker dot group** from a scroll container — a _fully functional, accessible_ carousel with **zero JavaScript**. This is the direction the platform is going.

```css
.carousel {
  overflow-x: auto;
  scroll-snap-type: x mandatory;
  /* Register the marker group this scroller feeds */
  scroll-marker-group: after;         /* dots rendered after the content */
}
```

.carousel > .slide { scroll-snap-align: center; }

```css
/* Prev / Next buttons the BROWSER generates and wires up */
.carousel::scroll-button(left)  { content: "‹" / "Previous slide"; }
.carousel::scroll-button(right) { content: "›" / "Next slide"; }
```

.carousel::scroll-button(_):disabled { opacity: .4; } /_ auto-disabled at ends \*/

```css
/* The marker (dot) group + each marker */
.carousel::scroll-marker-group {
  display: flex; gap: 8px; justify-content: center;
}
.carousel > .slide::scroll-marker {
  content: ""; width: 10px; height: 10px; border-radius: 50%;
  background: var(--platinum); border: 1px solid var(--eminence);
}
.carousel > .slide::scroll-marker:target-current {   /* the active dot */
  background: var(--goldenrod);
}
```

What you get for free, no script:
`::scroll-button(left/right)` render real buttons that scroll the container and **auto-disable at the ends** (`:disabled`). The `content: "‹" / "Previous slide"` syntax supplies both glyph _and_ accessible name.
`::scroll-marker` on each item builds a **tablist of dots** the browser keeps in sync; `:target-current` styles the active one; clicking a marker scrolls to that slide and **arrow keys move between markers** — all native.
Focus order and the roving tabindex are handled by the UA.

The catch in 2026: **support is still landing** (Chromium shipped it, Safari/Firefox trailing). Treat it as **progressive enhancement** — the scroll-snap carousel (§9.2) is the baseline; these pseudo-elements _upgrade_ it where supported. Feature-detect with `@supports (scroll-marker-group: after)`. This is the single most important "what's new" in carousels this decade: the component is being absorbed into CSS.

### 9.4 Vanilla JavaScript (when you need control, no library)
For loop/autoplay/announcements over the scroll-snap base. Note it drives the _native scroller_ — it doesn't reinvent movement.

```javascript
const root = document.querySelector(".carousel");
const viewport = root.querySelector(".carousel__viewport");
const slides = [...root.querySelectorAll(".carousel__slide")];
const status = root.querySelector("#carousel-status");
const toggle = root.querySelector(".carousel__toggle");
```

let current = 0, timer = null, playing = false;

```javascript
function go(i, announce = true) {
  current = (i + slides.length) % slides.length;
  slides[current].scrollIntoView({ behavior: "smooth", inline: "start", block: "nearest" });
  slides.forEach((s, n) => {
    n === current ? s.removeAttribute("inert") : s.setAttribute("inert", "");
  });
  // Announce ONLY on user/explicit change — never on each autoplay tick
  if (announce) status.textContent = `Slide ${current + 1} of ${slides.length}`;
```

}

```javascript
root.querySelector(".carousel__next").addEventListener("click", () => go(current + 1));
```

root.querySelector(".carousel\_\_prev").addEventListener("click", () => go(current - 1));

```javascript
// Autoplay — pausable, and self-suspending on hover / focus / hidden tab
function play()  { if (playing) return; playing = true; toggle.setAttribute("aria-pressed","false"); timer = setInterval(() => go(current + 1, false), 6000); }
function pause() { playing = false; toggle.setAttribute("aria-pressed","true"); clearInterval(timer); }
toggle.addEventListener("click", () => (playing ? pause() : play()));
root.addEventListener("mouseenter", pause);
root.addEventListener("focusin",  pause);   // keyboard users never fight the timer
```

document.addEventListener("visibilitychange", () => document.hidden && pause());

```javascript
// Reduced motion: don't autoplay at all
const rm = matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)");
if (!rm.matches) play();      // else leave it paused; user can press play
```

The non-negotiables encoded here: autoplay pauses on `focusin` (WCAG), on hover, and on tab-hide; `prefers-reduced-motion` suppresses it entirely; the live region announces on control-driven changes but **not** on every timer tick (that would spam the screen reader).

function usePrefersReducedMotion() {
  const [reduced, setReduced] = React.useState(false);
  React.useEffect(() => {
    const mq = matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)");
    const on = () => setReduced(mq.matches);

### 9.9 Lazy-Loading & Data (the slide-media contract)
A carousel's real cost is _images_, and the classic mistake is eager-loading all of them. Cross-reference the **Image** doc; the carousel-specific rules:

```plain
<!-- Slide 1 (LCP candidate): eager + high priority. Slides 2+: lazy. -->
<img src="hero.webp" alt="…" fetchpriority="high" decoding="async" />
<img src="s2.webp"   alt="…" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<img src="s3.webp"   alt="…" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
```

```javascript
// Decode the NEXT slide's image just before it enters view, so the swipe is instant.
const io = new IntersectionObserver((entries) => {
  for (const e of entries) {
    if (e.isIntersecting) {
      const img = e.target.querySelector("img[data-src]");
      if (img) { img.src = img.dataset.src; img.removeAttribute("data-src"); }
    }
  }
}, { root: viewport, rootMargin: "0px 200% 0px 0px" });  // preload 2 viewports ahead
slides.forEach((s) => io.observe(s));
```

Rules: **slide 1 eager (it's your LCP), the rest lazy**; preload the _next_ slide ~1–2 viewports ahead so paging feels instant; for a large or infinite set use **virtualization** (Swiper's `virtual` or windowing) so you never mount 200 slides. Never `loading="lazy"` the first slide — you'd delay LCP and tank the score.

## 10\. Accessibility (the carousel is a minefield — non-negotiable)
Carousels fail accessibility audits more than almost any component. Here's the full map. This follows the W3C ARIA Authoring Practices "Carousel" pattern.

**Autoplay is the #1 failure — WCAG 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide, Level A).** Any content that _auto-updates, moves, or scrolls, starts automatically, and lasts more than 5 seconds_ must have a mechanism to **pause, stop, or hide it**. An auto-rotating carousel with no pause button is a _hard WCAG failure_. Requirements:
A **visible, persistent Pause/Play button** with an accessible name (`aria-label="Pause carousel"`, `aria-pressed`).
Autoplay **pauses on hover and on keyboard focus anywhere inside** (WCAG requires it not to interfere; a rotating CTA under a user's cursor is hostile).
**≥5 seconds per slide** minimum — reading + comprehension time.
**Kill autoplay entirely under** **`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`**\*\*\*\***.** Don't autostart; leave it paused with the Play control available.

**Never auto-advance content that also moves focus.** If a slide change moves focus (e.g. auto-focusing the new slide's field), an autoplaying carousel would yank focus every few seconds — catastrophic. Rule: **autoplay changes the** **_visible_** **slide only; it never moves focus and never fires the live-region announcement** (announcing every tick spams the AT). Focus moves and announcements happen _only_ on explicit user navigation.

**Roles & labeling (ARIA APG):**
Container: `aria-roledescription="carousel"` + an `aria-label`/`aria-labelledby`. The role stays `region` (or `group`) — you _rename_ it with roledescription, not replace it.
Each slide: `role="group"` + `aria-roledescription="slide"` + `aria-label="N of M"`.
The rotation area often gets `aria-atomic="false"` and the live region is _separate and polite_.

**The tablist-of-dots pattern.** When pagination dots also _label_ slides, model them as a **tablist**: container `role="tablist"`, each dot `role="tab"` with `aria-selected` and `aria-controls` pointing at its slide `tabpanel`. Roving tabindex: the selected tab is `tabindex="0"`, the rest `-1`; Left/Right arrows move between tabs. (If dots are _only_ position indicators and not the primary control, simpler `<button aria-label="Go to slide N">` is acceptable — but don't leave them as unlabeled `<div>`s.)

**Keyboard map:**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| `Tab` | Moves into the carousel (controls), then into the visible slide's content — never a hidden slide's |
| `Shift+Tab` | Reverse |
| `←` / `→` | Previous / Next slide (when the carousel or its tablist has focus) |
| `Enter` / `Space` | Activate the focused control (prev/next/pause/dot) |
| `Home` / `End` | First / last slide (in the tablist pattern) |

**Focus management — the trap everyone ships:** off-screen slides frequently contain links and buttons that are _visually hidden but still tabbable_. A keyboard user Tabs "into nothing," focus scrolls the viewport unexpectedly, and they're lost. **Fix: mark every non-visible slide** **`inert`** (removes it from tab order _and_ the a11y tree). `display:none`/`visibility:hidden` also remove tabbability but destroy scroll-snap animation; `inert` is the modern, animation-safe answer. With a looping carousel, base `inert` on which slides are actually in view, not on index.

**Live region:** an `aria-live="polite"` off-screen element updated with "Slide N of M" (or the slide's title) **on user navigation only**. Polite, never assertive; never on autoplay ticks.

**Contrast & targets:** control buttons and dots ≥ **44×44px** hit area (dots can _look_ tiny with a padded target). Controls overlaid on imagery need a scrim or shadow to hold **≥3:1** (non-text) / **4.5:1** (any label) against _unpredictable_ slide backgrounds — the hardest contrast problem in the component, because you don't control what's behind the arrow. Never signal position with color alone.

**Touch:** swipe must have a keyboard/button equivalent (it always will if controls exist). Don't hijack vertical scroll — a horizontal carousel that eats vertical page scroll traps mobile users.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas (2026-aware)
**Native CSS carousel** (`::scroll-button()`, `::scroll-marker`, `scroll-marker-group`, `:target-current`): the headline shift — accessible prev/next + dot navigation with **zero JS**, shipping in Chromium and spec'd in CSS Overflow 5. Over the next two years this eats most carousel libraries. Progressive-enhance today.
**Scroll-driven animations** (`animation-timeline: view()` / `scroll()`): tie a slide's opacity/scale/parallax to its scroll position natively — center-mode and coverflow effects with no JS timer, GPU-composited, reduced-motion-aware by construction.
**View Transitions API for slide changes:** morph between slides (shared-element transitions) natively instead of hand-tweened transforms.
**`content-visibility: auto`** **per slide:** the browser skips rendering off-screen slides' subtrees entirely — free virtualization for long carousels.
**AI-curated ordering:** the "slides" reorder per user (personalized hero) — which makes the "84% see slide 1" data a _feature_ if slide 1 is chosen for _this_ user, not a fixed marketing compromise.
**Liquid Glass controls:** refractive pill arrows/dots that react to the imagery sliding behind them (Apple platforms native; web approximation).

## 12\. Conversion & UX Killers (mistakes that bleed money silently)
None throw an error. They just quietly drop your numbers.
**The hero rotator as a decision-avoidance tool.** The root sin. Five slides because five stakeholders each wanted one. Users see slide 1 (~84%) and leave. _Pick one message._ If you can't, that's a strategy problem a carousel won't solve — it'll just hide it.
**Auto-advance stealing the click.** The CTA moves out from under the cursor mid-reach; the user clicks the wrong slide or gives up. Auto-rotation actively _lowers_ slide-1 conversion. If you autoplay, ≥5s and pause on hover.
**No peek, no affordance.** A slide filling the viewport edge-to-edge gives zero signal there's more. Users never swipe. Always leave the next slide's edge showing.
**Banner blindness.** Motion in the hero reads as an ad; eyes skip the whole region (NN/g). Your most expensive real estate, ignored.
**Dots as the only control.** 8px unlabeled dots are un-tappable and invisible to screen readers. Provide real prev/next and label the dots.
**All images eager-loaded.** Ten hero images fetched on load tank LCP and burn mobile data. Slide 1 eager, rest lazy.
**Content shift on slide change.** Slides of different heights make the page jump. Lock the viewport aspect ratio.
**Infinite loop with broken a11y.** Cloned slides confuse screen readers ("Slide 1 of 6" announced twice), duplicate focus targets, and break the count. The loop _feels_ nice and _costs_ real accessibility — only loop if you fix the clone semantics (mark clones `aria-hidden`, base counts on real slides).
**Trapping vertical scroll.** A horizontal carousel that swallows the page's vertical scroll on mobile strands the user.

**The through-line:** every one fails _quietly_ — no crash, just a lower number that's easy to blame on "the market." And the biggest one is upstream of code: _building the carousel at all_ when a single strong hero would convert better. Audit the _decision_ before you audit the implementation.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
**Thumbnail-synced galleries (the PDP pattern):** a main stage + a thumbnail strip sharing one index — two carousels, one source of truth. Embla's `useEmblaCarousel` twice with a `select` handler syncing both; thumbnails are a tablist controlling the main `tabpanel`.
**Center-mode with scroll-driven scale:** `scroll-snap-align: center` + `animation-timeline: view()` scaling the centered slide up — no JS, GPU-composited, honors reduced-motion.
**Virtualized / infinite feeds:** render a window of slides (Swiper `virtual`, or your own windowing) so a 500-item carousel mounts ~5 nodes. Pair with `content-visibility: auto`.
**Free-scroll vs. snap modes:** a toggle between mandatory snap (paged) and `proximity` snap (free-drag that only snaps when close) for logo walls and long rows.
**Design tokens for the carousel:** `--carousel-peek`, `--carousel-gap`, `--carousel-per-view`, `--carousel-snap`, `--carousel-control-size` — retheme every carousel by swapping the block; drive `per-view` responsively so one component covers 1.2-up mobile to 4-up desktop.
**TS-enforced a11y:** a discriminated union that _requires_ `autoplayMs` to be paired with a rendered pause control at the type level, so you can't ship autoplay without the WCAG control:

```typescript
type CarouselBase = { label: string; children: React.ReactNode };
type Static   = CarouselBase & { autoplayMs?: null };
type Autoplay = CarouselBase & { autoplayMs: number; pauseLabel: string }; // pauseLabel REQUIRED
type CarouselProps = Static | Autoplay;  // autoplay can't compile without a pause label
```

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A carousel _feels_ free and is one of the heaviest things on a marketing page.
**Library weight:** Swiper is ~40KB+ gzipped with its features; **Embla is ~5KB**; **scroll-snap CSS is 0KB**; the **native CSS carousel is 0KB**. Default to the lightest tier that meets the need — most "carousels" need no JS at all.
**Image weight dominates.** The carousel's real cost is media, not code. Slide 1 eager (LCP), rest lazy, preload next slide, serve `srcset`/AVIF/WebP (see the Image doc). One un-lazy hero set can add megabytes.
**Animate only** **`transform`****/****`opacity`**\*\*\*\***.** Sliding a track by animating `left`/`margin` reflows every frame; `translateX` is compositor-only. Native scroll-snap is already GPU-composited.
**`content-visibility: auto`** **per slide** lets the browser skip off-screen slide rendering — free virtualization for long carousels.
**Don't mount 200 slides.** Virtualize past ~20–30. Every mounted slide is DOM, listeners, and images the browser pays for whether seen or not.
**Event economy:** one delegated listener / one IntersectionObserver on the viewport beats per-slide listeners.
**Autoplay timers cost battery.** A `setInterval` running while the tab is hidden wastes cycles — suspend on `visibilitychange` (also the a11y-correct behavior).

## 15\. Security
A carousel's security surface is small but real — it renders external media and often user-submitted content.

**Slide content is an XSS vector.** If slide markup, titles, or descriptions come from a CMS, user input, or a third-party feed, they must be sanitized before rendering. Never use `innerHTML` or `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` with untrusted slide data. Framework interpolation (JSX children, Vue `{{ }}`, Svelte `{expr}`) escapes by default — use it.

**Validate and sanitize media URLs.** Slides load images, videos, and occasionally iframes. A malicious `src` can point at a phishing page, a tracking pixel, or a `javascript:` URI. Allow-list protocols (`https:` only), validate URL structure server-side, and set `Content-Security-Policy` `img-src` / `media-src` directives that restrict origins.

**Sandbox embedded slides.** If any slide contains an `<iframe>` (a video embed, a third-party widget), apply `sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin"` at minimum, never allow `allow-top-navigation` — a malicious embed could redirect the whole page. Prefer `<video>` over iframes for media.

**Lazy-loaded media and SSRF.** If a server-side process fetches slide media (thumbnail generation, resizing), validate the URL isn't a private/internal address (`127.0.0.1`, `10.x`, `169.254.x`) — the classic SSRF pattern.

**Clickjacking on overlay controls.** Carousel prev/next buttons overlaid on full-bleed slides can be targeted by a transparent iframe layered on top of your page. Set `X-Frame-Options: DENY` / `frame-ancestors 'none'` on pages with interactive carousels.

**Autoplay +** **`prefers-reduced-motion`** **isn't just a11y — it's trust.** An auto-advancing carousel that moves content without user consent can be weaponized in dark-pattern audits and regulatory complaints (GDPR consent banners that auto-scroll past the opt-out). The pause control isn't just WCAG — it's a liability shield.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a carousel is "done":
**First, justify it exists** — would one strong hero + clear hierarchy convert better? If yes, don't build it.
Real `<button>` controls, labeled ("Previous slide" / "Next slide"), ≥44px targets.
`aria-roledescription="carousel"` on the region with an accessible name; each slide `role="group"` + `aria-roledescription="slide"` + "N of M".
Off-screen slides are `inert` — Tab can't reach a hidden slide's links.
Autoplay (if any) has a **visible pause control** (WCAG 2.2.2), pauses on hover _and_ focus, ≥5s/slide, and is **fully disabled under** **`prefers-reduced-motion`**.
Autoplay moves the visible slide only — never moves focus, never fires the live-region announcement.
`aria-live="polite"` region announces "Slide N of M" on **user** navigation only.
Dots are a tablist (roving tabindex) or labeled buttons — never unlabeled `<div>`s.
Keyboard: arrows navigate, controls activate with Enter/Space, focus never lands off-screen.
End controls disabled/hidden at start/end (non-loop); loop clones marked `aria-hidden` and don't break the count.
Peek the next slide; lock viewport aspect ratio (no CLS).
Slide 1 eager (LCP), rest lazy; preload the next slide; virtualize long sets.
Single-item collapses to a plain image — no controls, no dots, no autoplay.
Every slide is in the server-rendered HTML (SEO + no-JS).

**Bottom line:** the carousel is the rare component where the senior move is often _not building it._ The engineering is real — scroll-snap, the new native CSS carousel, `inert` focus management, a pausable autoplay that satisfies WCAG 2.2.2 — but it sits downstream of a harder question the conversion data already answered: _users barely see slide 2, so is hiding your content behind a swipe ever the right call?_ Master the accessibility minefield (pausable autoplay, inert off-screen slides, the carousel/slide roles, the tablist of dots), reach for the zero-JS scroll-snap and native-CSS paths before a library, and treat every autoplay as a liability you have to earn. Do that and you'll ship the ~15% of carousels that deserve to exist — and talk the other 85% back into a single, confident hero.
