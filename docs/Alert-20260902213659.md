## 1\. What an Alert Actually Is
An **inline alert** is a persistent, contextual status message rendered _in the page flow_ that communicates the outcome or condition of something — success, information, warning, error/danger. It sits where the content is, it stays until it's no longer true (or the user dismisses it), and it does **not** block the rest of the UI.

That one sentence rules out four things people constantly lump together with "alert." Getting these distinctions right is the whole game, because each one carries a _different accessibility contract_:

*   **Alert (inline banner):** persistent, in-flow, non-blocking, non-modal. "Your payment failed — update your card." Lives on the page until resolved. This doc.
*   **Toast / Snackbar:** _transient_, floats over the UI (usually a corner), **auto-dismisses** after a few seconds. "Saved ✓." Because it disappears, it's for low-stakes, non-essential confirmation — never for anything the user must act on, because they'll miss it. A toast is an alert that gave up its permanence for unobtrusiveness.
*   **Message bar / Global banner:** a full-bleed strip pinned to the top of the viewport or a region ("Scheduled maintenance Sunday 2am"). Same semantics as an inline alert, different _placement scope_ — it's page/app-global rather than tied to one section of content.
*   **Alertdialog:** this is a **dialog**, not an alert. `role="alertdialog"` is a modal that traps focus, blocks the page, and demands a decision ("Delete this account? Cannot be undone."). If it stops the world and needs a button press to continue, it's an alertdialog and belongs in the Dialog reference — _not here._
*   **Inline field error:** the little red text under a specific form input ("Email is required"). Scoped to one control, wired via `aria-describedby`, usually **not** `role="alert"`. A form-level alert summarizing "3 fields need attention" _is_ an alert; the per-field message is a different pattern (see §12).

The single most common mistake: **treating "alert" and "****`role=alert`****" as the same thing.** They are not. "Alert" is a UI pattern (a status banner). `role="alert"` is an ARIA role that means "interrupt the screen reader _right now_ and read this." A calm, static "Welcome back" banner that renders on page load is an alert-the-pattern but must **never** be `role="alert"` — nobody's screen reader should be hijacked by a greeting. The role is earned by _urgency + dynamism_, not by the component's name. Section 10 is the spine of this whole doc for exactly that reason.

Rule of thumb: **if it blocks, it's a dialog; if it vanishes on a timer, it's a toast; if it stays in the flow and reports a condition, it's an alert.**

## 2\. Why It Matters
The alert is where your interface tells the truth about what just happened. That gives it outsized weight across three axes:

*   **Trust lives here.** An error that's swallowed silently — the form clears, nothing explains why — is the fastest way to make an app feel broken and dishonest. Conversely, a clear, specific, recoverable error message ("Card declined — try another card") keeps users in the flow instead of bouncing to support. Perceived reliability is mostly _error handling made visible_.
*   **It's a raw accessibility frontier.** Sighted users see a red box appear. Screen-reader users get _nothing_ unless you wired a live region correctly. The alert is one of the few components where a purely visual implementation locks out an entire class of users at 100% — they never learn the payment failed. Get the live-region timing right and you close that gap; get it wrong and it fails silently in exactly the moments that matter most.
*   **It governs interruption budget.** Every `role="alert"` is an interruption — it barges past whatever the screen reader was saying. Spend that budget on genuinely urgent, actionable messages and users trust your alerts. Spend it on "cookie preferences saved" and you've trained them (and their AT) to tune you out — the boy-who-cried-wolf failure mode that makes the _next_, real alert invisible.
*   **Conversion and completion.** Checkout, signup, and settings flows die at the error message. A vague "Something went wrong" with no recovery path is an abandoned cart. The alert is the difference between a recoverable stumble and a lost customer.

## 3\. Anatomy of an Alert
Every well-built alert is composed of predictable parts:

*   **Container / region:** the box. Has padding, a border (or left accent bar), a tinted background, and a role. The container is what must exist in the DOM _before_ text is injected (see §10).
*   **Severity icon:** the leading glyph that reinforces meaning — check for success, i for info, triangle for warning, octagon/x for error. Decorative-but-meaningful: it needs a text equivalent or an `aria-hidden` + a visible text label, because icon-alone fails colorblind and screen-reader users.
*   **Title (optional):** a short bold summary line ("Payment failed"). Not every alert needs one; a single sentence often reads better without.
*   **Message body:** the actual content. One or two sentences. Names _what happened_ and, ideally, _what to do next_.
*   **Action(s) (optional):** inline buttons or links — "Retry," "Update card," "Learn more." Turns a dead-end notice into a recovery path.
*   **Dismiss control (optional):** the × button. Only present if the alert is _dismissible_ (see §5). Needs an `aria-label="Dismiss"`, a ≥44px hit target, and it must not be the only way to get rid of a still-true condition.
*   **Semantic color:** the tint (green/blue/amber/red) that signals severity — always paired with an icon and text, never carrying meaning alone (§10).

## 4\. Sizes, Density & Scale
Alerts don't have a "size" the way buttons do, but they scale by **density** and **prominence**. Real values worth standardizing:

| Token | Padding | Icon | Title | Body | Radius | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact | 8px 12px | 16px | — | 13px | 6px | Inline field-group notices, dense dashboards |
| Default | 12px 16px | 20px | 14px | 14px | 8px | The workhorse inline alert |
| Prominent | 16px 20px | 24px | 16px | 15px | 10px | Section-level errors, empty-state warnings |
| Global bar | 10px 24px | 20px | — | 14px | 0 | Full-bleed top-of-page message strip |

Rules that matter:
*   **The accent is a token, not a magic number.** A 4px left border, an icon color, and a background tint should all derive from one severity token (`--alert-danger`), so re-theming touches one line.
*   **Global bars go full-bleed, radius 0**, so they read as "system-level," distinct from a rounded in-content alert.
*   **Any dismiss/action control keeps a ≥44px tap target** even when the alert is compact — expand the hit area with padding, don't shrink the button.
*   **Max line length still applies.** An alert stretched to 1400px wide is unreadable; cap the text measure at ~70ch even inside a full-width bar.

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
An alert has fewer _interaction_ states than a button, but more _lifecycle_ states — and the lifecycle is where the accessibility bugs hide.

1. **Default (rest):** the baseline rendered alert — icon, message, correct severity styling.
2. **Injected / appearing:** the moment it enters the DOM dynamically. This is the critical one: for `role="alert"`/`role="status"` to announce, the region must already exist and the _text_ must be what changes. Appear-animate with `opacity`/`transform` only.
3. **Persistent:** it stays because the condition is still true. Reloading the page and it's still there means it's persistent (a validation summary, an outage banner).
4. **Dismissible → dismissed:** the user closed it via the × or an action. It should not reappear for the same instance. Dismissal state often needs to persist (localStorage / server) so a "we've updated our terms" bar doesn't nag on every navigation.
5. **Auto-resolving:** the condition clears itself (network reconnects) and the alert removes itself — announce the _resolution_ too if it matters ("Back online").
6. **With-actions / pending:** an action button inside is mid-request (Retry spinning). The alert now owns a mini async state (see Button's loading state).
7. **Stacked / collapsed:** multiple alerts in one region. Do you show all, cap at N with a "+3 more," or collapse to a summary? (See §12 — stacking is a top killer.)
8. **Focus (when interactive):** if the alert contains a link/button/dismiss, those get visible `:focus-visible` rings. The alert _container_ itself is generally not focusable unless you're moving focus to it deliberately (rare; usually the live region is enough).

The state juniors ship without: **injected.** They render the alert conditionally in a component that mounts _with_ the text already inside, the live region and its content appear in the same paint, and some screen readers never announce it because there was no "change" to a pre-existing region. State 2 is 90% of alert a11y bugs.

## 6\. Types & Variants (severity)
Alerts vary along one primary axis — **severity** — plus a couple of functional variants. Severity maps to semantic color, an icon, and a role decision.

*   **Success:** green. Confirms an action completed. "Profile updated." Usually `role="status"` (polite) or a toast; rarely urgent.
*   **Info / neutral:** blue (or brand). Contextual, non-urgent. "Your trial ends in 5 days." `role="status"` if injected, or no role at all if static.
*   **Warning:** amber/orange. Something needs attention but isn't broken yet. "Your storage is 90% full." Polite-to-assertive depending on immediacy.
*   **Error / Danger:** red. Something failed or is invalid. "Payment declined." This is the one that most often earns `role="alert"` (assertive) — _when dynamically injected in response to a user action._
*   **Critical / System:** a heavier error, often a global bar. "Service degraded — some features unavailable." Assertive, and frequently persistent + non-dismissible until resolved.

Functional variants that cross-cut severity:
*   **Static vs. dynamic:** rendered on page load vs. injected in response to an event. This decides the role more than severity does (§10).
*   **Dismissible vs. persistent:** has an × or not.
*   **With-actions vs. notice-only:** contains recovery buttons/links or is purely informational.
*   **Inline vs. global bar:** scoped to a content section vs. pinned full-bleed to the page/app.

**Never use color as the only difference between these.** Success-green and error-red are indistinguishable to red-green colorblind users (~8% of men). The icon + the word ("Error," "Success") + the shape carry the meaning; color reinforces it. This is the non-negotiable rule of the whole component (§10).

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use an inline alert when the user needs to know the **state or outcome of a thing tied to the content they're looking at**, and that knowledge should persist until it's resolved or acknowledged.

Reach for something else when:
*   **The message must block a decision → alertdialog.** "Delete permanently?" needs focus trapped and a choice made. An inline alert can be ignored; a confirm can't.
*   **It's a fleeting, low-stakes confirmation → toast.** "Copied to clipboard" doesn't deserve a permanent box. If missing it costs the user nothing, let it auto-dismiss.
*   **It's scoped to one form field → inline field error.** "Password too short" belongs under the field via `aria-describedby`, not in a page-level banner (though a _summary_ alert at the top is a great addition, see §12).
*   **It's a long-lived promotional message → that's not an alert, it's a banner/promo.** Don't borrow alert severity colors to sell things; you'll dilute the signal.
*   **It fires constantly / on every keystroke → debounce or suppress.** An alert that reappears on every render trains users to ignore alerts.

Placement heuristics:
*   **Put the alert where the cause is.** A form error goes at the top of _that form_ (and/or next to the field), not in a global toast the user has to hunt for.
*   **Global/system messages go full-bleed at the top** of the viewport or app shell, above the content.
*   **Move focus to a form-error summary** after a failed submit so keyboard users land on the explanation (and can click through to the offending field). This is the one time you deliberately manage focus for an alert.
*   **Don't stack more than a few.** Three alerts is a smell; collapse or summarize.

## 8\. Alerts Across Design Systems
Same component, different names and philosophies. Fluency here means you can read any codebase.

*   **Material Design (Google):** Calls the inline version a **Banner** (persistent, in-flow, with up to two actions) and the transient corner version a **Snackbar**. Material 3 leans on tonal surface colors keyed to severity. Banners are deliberately rare in Material — reserved for important, medium-emphasis messages.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** Reserves the word **Alert** for the _modal_ (what the web calls an alertdialog) — a centered blocking box with 1–2 buttons. For inline/non-blocking status, HIG points you at labels, notification banners (transient, top-of-screen), and in-content text. Worth knowing because "Alert" means the opposite thing in Apple-land.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** **MessageBar** — a horizontal strip with an intent (`info`, `success`, `warning`, `error`), an icon, optional actions, and a dismiss. This is the cleanest mental model for the inline alert; Fluent's `MessageBarType` maps 1:1 to severity.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** `<Alert>` with `type` (`success | info | warning | error`), `banner` mode for full-width, `showIcon`, `closable`, and an optional `description` for a two-line title+body. The most feature-complete inline alert API in the ecosystem.
*   **Bootstrap:** `.alert` + `.alert-{success,info,warning,danger}` contextual classes, `.alert-dismissible` with a close button, `role="alert"` baked into the markup they hand you (which is _wrong_ for static alerts — see §9.12).
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** no prescribed component; you compose the tint, border, icon, and role yourself — which means the role decision is on _you_, and it's the thing most Tailwind alert snippets get wrong.
*   **shadcn/Radix:** shadcn ships an `<Alert>` (styled, `role="alert"` by default in older versions — audit it) and Radix provides the primitives for toasts. The behavior/role is yours to set correctly.

## 8b. Visual Styles (the same alert, eleven aesthetics)
Design systems are _whose_ rules; visual styles are _which look_. The same semantic alert — same role, same icon, same dismiss behavior — can wear any of these skins. See §17 for the full skin-by-skin breakdown. The rule that never changes: **the style is skin; the role, the live-region timing, the icon-plus-text severity, the keyboard behavior, and the 44px dismiss target stay identical across all eleven.**

### What's trending in 2026
The dominant force this year is **Liquid Glass** (Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language, glassmorphism's successor): a translucent material that _refracts_ what's behind it with a specular rim and a moving sheen. For alerts this is tempting and dangerous — a refractive, semi-transparent error banner over busy content can drop below the 4.5:1 contrast floor exactly when legibility matters most. Around it, the 2026 palette:
*   **Liquid Glass** system banners on Apple platforms; a performance-aware "frosted-lite" on the web — always with a solid contrast fallback for text.
*   **AI-native surfaces:** streaming/agentic status messages ("Analyzing… 3 of 12 files") replacing the static spinner, and alerts that summarize what an agent just did.
*   **Motion-forward but restrained:** alerts slide/fade in with `prefers-reduced-motion` honored by default — no bouncing error banners.
*   **Expressive Material 3:** springier banner entrances, bolder tonal severity surfaces.

**For UJG:** keep alerts on the Flat/Minimal end for legibility; reserve Liquid Glass for _informational_ system bars over the deep Eminence backdrop where the refraction sings, never for a critical error whose text must survive any background. Contrast and reduced-motion are non-negotiable no matter how on-trend the skin.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML (the foundation everyone should master first)
The role you choose is the whole ballgame. Read the comments — they're the point.

```plain
<!-- STATIC alert, present on page load: NO role="alert". -->
<!-- A live region that already contains text on load will not (reliably) announce. -->
<div class="alert alert--info">
  <svg class="alert__icon" aria-hidden="true"><!-- info glyph --></svg>
  <div class="alert__body">
    <strong>Heads up:</strong> your trial ends in 5 days.
  </div>
</div>

<!-- DYNAMIC error, injected after a user action: role="alert" (assertive, interrupts). -->
<!-- The container should exist first; the TEXT is what gets injected (see §10). -->
<div class="alert alert--danger" role="alert">
  <svg class="alert__icon" aria-hidden="true"><!-- error octagon --></svg>
  <div class="alert__body">
    <strong>Payment failed.</strong> Your card was declined — try another card.
  </div>
  <button type="button" class="alert__action">Update card</button>
</div>

<!-- DYNAMIC but non-urgent success: role="status" (polite, waits its turn). -->
<div class="alert alert--success" role="status">
  <svg class="alert__icon" aria-hidden="true"><!-- check --></svg>
  <div class="alert__body">Profile updated.</div>
</div>

<!-- Dismissible: the × needs a real label and a real target. -->
<div class="alert alert--warning" role="status">
  <svg class="alert__icon" aria-hidden="true"></svg>
  <div class="alert__body">Storage is 90% full.</div>
  <button type="button" class="alert__dismiss" aria-label="Dismiss">&times;</button>
</div>
```

Key HTML rules:
*   **Never hardcode** **`role="alert"`** **on a static, page-load alert.** It either does nothing useful or hijacks the screen reader on every load. Static contextual alerts get _no_ live role.
*   **`role="alert"`** **is implicitly** **`aria-live="assertive"`** **+** **`aria-atomic="true"`****\*\*\*\*.** `role="status"` is implicitly `aria-live="polite"`. Don't double them up.
*   **The icon is** **`aria-hidden="true"`** and the severity is carried by _visible text_ ("Payment failed") — never by the icon or color alone.
*   **The dismiss** **`×`** **glyph is not a label.** Give it `aria-label="Dismiss"`; the `×` character is invisible to most screen readers or reads as "times."

### 9.2 CSS (severity done right, color never alone)

```css
.alert {
  display: flex;
  gap: 12px;
  align-items: flex-start;
  padding: 12px 16px;

  border-radius: 8px;

}

.alert__dismiss {
  flex: none;
  min-width: 44px; min-height: 44px;       /* target size even if the glyph is small */

}
.alert__dismiss:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid var(--alert-ring, #DCA424);
  outline-offset: 2px;
}

/* Severity: color + border + icon tint — but the ICON and TEXT carry meaning, not the hue */
.alert--success { background: #ecfdf3; border-color: #067647; color: #054f31; }
.alert--info    { background: #eff6ff; border-color: #1849a9; color: #0b3a82; }
.alert--warning { background: #fffaeb; border-color: #b54708; color: #7a2e0e; }
.alert--danger  { background: #fef3f2; border-color: #b42318; color: #7a271a; }

/* Appear animation — compositor-only, and OFF for reduced motion */
@keyframes alert-in { from { opacity: 0; transform: translateY(-4px); } to { opacity: 1; transform: none; } }
.alert { animation: alert-in .18s ease-out; }
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { .alert { animation: none; } }

/* Forced-colors: don't let the tint vanish */

  .alert { border: 1px solid CanvasText; forced-color-adjust: none; }
}
```

The senior detail: severity is expressed in **three redundant channels** — background tint, border/icon color, _and_ the icon shape + word in the body. Kill any one channel and the meaning still survives. That's what "never color alone" actually looks like in CSS.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (a proper reusable component)
The API's job is to make the _role decision_ hard to get wrong. Note how `live` is derived from severity + whether it's dynamic, not left to the caller's memory.

```typescript
import { forwardRef, HTMLAttributes, ReactNode } from "react";

type Severity = "success" | "info" | "warning" | "danger";

interface AlertProps extends HTMLAttributes<HTMLDivElement> {
  severity?: Severity;
  title?: ReactNode;
  /** true = injected in response to an event → gets a live role.
   *  false/undefined = static on load → NO live role. */
  dynamic?: boolean;
  /** force politeness; defaults are derived from severity when dynamic */
  live?: "assertive" | "polite" | "off";
  onDismiss?: () => void;
  icon?: ReactNode;
  actions?: ReactNode;
}

const ICON_LABEL: Record<Severity, string> = {
  success: "Success", info: "Information", warning: "Warning", danger: "Error",
};

export const Alert = forwardRef<HTMLDivElement, AlertProps>(function Alert(
  { severity = "info", title, dynamic = false, live, onDismiss,
    icon, actions, children, className = "", ...rest },
  ref
) {
  // The role decision, centralized so callers can't botch it:
  // - static (dynamic=false) → no role, no aria-live
  // - dynamic danger/warning → assertive (role="alert")
  // - dynamic success/info   → polite   (role="status")
  const politeness =
    live ?? (dynamic ? (severity === "danger" || severity === "warning" ? "assertive" : "polite") : undefined);
  const role = politeness === "assertive" ? "alert" : politeness === "polite" ? "status" : undefined;

  return (
    <div
      ref={ref}
      role={role}
      aria-live={politeness && politeness !== "off" ? politeness : undefined}
      className={`alert alert--${severity} ${className}`}
      {...rest}
    >
      <span className="alert__icon" aria-hidden="true">{icon}</span>
      <div className="alert__body">
        {/* visible severity word so meaning never rides on color alone */}
        <span className="sr-only">{ICON_LABEL[severity]}: </span>
        {title && <strong className="alert__title">{title}</strong>}
        <div>{children}</div>
        {actions && <div className="alert__actions">{actions}</div>}
      </div>
      {onDismiss && (
        <button type="button" className="alert__dismiss"
                aria-label="Dismiss" onClick={onDismiss}>&times;</button>
      )}
    </div>
  );
});
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:
*   **`dynamic`** **drives the role, not the caller.** The #1 real-world alert bug is a static banner hardcoded to `role="alert"`. Here you _can't_ get a live role unless you say the alert is injected.
*   **Severity picks politeness sanely by default** (danger interrupts, success waits) but `live` is an escape hatch when you know better.
*   **A visually-hidden severity word** (`sr-only`) means the meaning survives even for someone who can't see the color _or_ the icon.
*   **Extends native** **`HTMLAttributes`** so `id`, `data-*`, `onClick` pass through — you can wire dismissal-persistence or analytics without new props.

The real-world dynamic pattern — the live region exists first, the text arrives after:

```typescript
function CheckoutErrors() {
  const [error, setError] = useState<string | null>(null);

  async function pay() {
    setError(null);
    try {
      await api.pay();
    } catch (e) {
      setError("Your card was declined — try another card.");
    }
  }

  return (
    <>
      {/* Region is always rendered; only its TEXT changes → reliable announcement */}
      <div aria-live="assertive" role="alert">
        {error && <Alert severity="danger" dynamic>{error}</Alert>}
      </div>
      <button onClick={pay}>Pay $40</button>
    </>
  );
}
```

That outer always-present live region is the trick most tutorials skip. If you mount the `<Alert>` _and_ its `role="alert"` in the same render as the text, VoiceOver and some versions of NVDA may not fire, because there was no change to an _existing_ region — the region and its content appeared together (§10).

### 9.4 Does the alert reach the server?
Mostly no — an alert is a display component. But the _content_ of an error alert almost always comes from a server response, and that's where two rules apply:

**1\. Never leak raw errors into the alert.** A 500 stack trace, a SQL error, or an internal exception message rendered into a user-facing alert is an information-disclosure vulnerability _and_ useless to the user. Map server errors to safe, human messages client-side.

```typescript
// Frontend: translate machine errors into human, safe alert text
function toAlertMessage(err: ApiError): string {
  switch (err.code) {
    case "card_declined":   return "Your card was declined — try another card.";
    case "rate_limited":    return "Too many attempts. Wait a minute and try again.";
    case "validation":      return "Some fields need attention — see below.";
    default:                return "Something went wrong on our end. Please try again."; // never err.raw
  }
}
```

**2\. The server decides** **_what's true_****\*\*\*\*; the alert only reports it.** A "success" alert must reflect a confirmed server result, not an optimistic guess that might roll back. If you show "Saved ✓" before the request resolves and it then fails, you've lied in the most trust-sensitive component you have. Show a pending state, then resolve to success/error on the real response.

```plain
// Node/Express: shape errors so the client can map them to safe alert copy
app.post("/api/pay", requireAuth, async (req, res) => {
  const parsed = PaySchema.safeParse(req.body);
  if (!parsed.success) return res.status(422).json({ code: "validation", fields: parsed.error.flatten() });
  try {
    const receipt = await charge(parsed.data);
    return res.status(201).json({ ok: true, receipt });
  } catch (e) {
    // Log the real error server-side; send only a stable code to the client.
    logger.error(e);
    return res.status(402).json({ code: "card_declined" });   // no raw message leaks
  }
});
```

### 9.5 Vanilla JavaScript (inject text into an existing region)
The building block every framework abstracts. The pattern that makes announcements reliable: **create the region up front, change only the text.**

```javascript
// One persistent live region in the DOM, created at load, kept empty:
//   <div id="alerts" aria-live="polite" aria-atomic="true"></div>
const region = document.getElementById("alerts");

function showAlert(message, severity = "info", { assertive = false } = {}) {
  // Switch politeness by swapping the whole region's live setting BEFORE writing text
  region.setAttribute("aria-live", assertive ? "assertive" : "polite");

  const el = document.createElement("div");
  el.className = `alert alert--${severity}`;
  // Build only the static structure with innerHTML; NEVER interpolate the message (or any
  // server/user value) into an HTML string — that's the §16 XSS vector. Inject text nodes.
  el.innerHTML = `
    <svg class="alert__icon" aria-hidden="true"></svg>
    <div class="alert__body"><span class="sr-only"></span><span class="alert__message"></span></div>
    <button type="button" class="alert__dismiss" aria-label="Dismiss">&times;</button>`;
  el.querySelector(".sr-only").textContent = `${severity}: `;
  el.querySelector(".alert__message").textContent = message;   // escaped — no HTML injection

  el.querySelector(".alert__dismiss").addEventListener("click", () => el.remove());
  region.appendChild(el);   // text change inside an existing region → announced
  return () => el.remove();  // caller can dismiss programmatically when the condition clears
}

// Usage
showAlert("Your card was declined — try another card.", "danger", { assertive: true });
```

Why the region pre-exists: if you create the `aria-live` node _and_ its text in one shot, assistive tech has no "before" to diff against and may stay silent. A stable, empty region that you append into is the durable fix.

### 9.7 shadcn/ui (the 2024+ standard)
Copy it into your repo and _own_ it — which means owning the role. shadcn's default `<Alert>` has historically hardcoded `role="alert"`; the senior move is to make the role a prop so static alerts don't hijack the screen reader.

```typescript
import * as React from "react";
import { cva, type VariantProps } from "class-variance-authority";
import { cn } from "@/lib/utils";

const alertVariants = cva(
  "relative flex gap-3 w-full items-start rounded-lg border border-l-4 px-4 py-3 text-sm [&>svg]:size-5 [&>svg]:shrink-0",
  {
    variants: {
      severity: {
        success: "border-emerald-700 bg-emerald-50 text-emerald-900",
        info:    "border-blue-700 bg-blue-50 text-blue-900",
        warning: "border-amber-700 bg-amber-50 text-amber-900",
        danger:  "border-red-700 bg-red-50 text-red-900",
      },
    },
    defaultVariants: { severity: "info" },
  }
);

export interface AlertProps
  extends React.HTMLAttributes<HTMLDivElement>,
    VariantProps<typeof alertVariants> {
  /** true when injected in response to an event; false/omitted for static alerts */
  dynamic?: boolean;
}

export const Alert = React.forwardRef<HTMLDivElement, AlertProps>(
  ({ className, severity, dynamic, ...props }, ref) => {
    const role = dynamic ? (severity === "danger" || severity === "warning" ? "alert" : "status") : undefined;
    return (
      <div
        ref={ref}
        role={role}
        aria-live={dynamic ? (role === "alert" ? "assertive" : "polite") : undefined}
        className={cn(alertVariants({ severity }), className)}
        {...props}
      />
    );
  }
);
Alert.displayName = "Alert";

export const AlertTitle = ({ className, ...p }: React.HTMLAttributes<HTMLHeadingElement>) => (
  <h5 className={cn("mb-1 font-semibold leading-none", className)} {...p} />
);
export const AlertDescription = ({ className, ...p }: React.HTMLAttributes<HTMLParagraphElement>) => (
  <div className={cn("text-sm [&_p]:leading-relaxed", className)} {...p} />
);
```

Usage: `<Alert severity="danger" dynamic><AlertTitle>Payment failed</AlertTitle><AlertDescription>Try another card.</AlertDescription></Alert>`. The audit-worthy line is `role = dynamic ? … : undefined` — that single ternary is the difference between a correct alert and one that either stays mute or barges in on every page load.

### 9.8 Next.js (App Router: server-rendered vs. client-injected)
Next sharpens the static/dynamic split that decides the role. A validation summary rendered from a Server Component after a failed Server Action is _server-rendered on the response_ — the user navigated/submitted, the new page paints with the alert already in it. A client-side fetch error is _injected into an existing region_. They want different treatment.

```typescript
// app/checkout/page.tsx — Server Component

// region) is the right tool: move focus to the summary so keyboard users land on it.
export default function Checkout({ searchParams }: { searchParams: { error?: string } }) {
  return (
    <>
      {searchParams.error && (
        <div role="alert" tabIndex={-1} id="form-error" className="alert alert--danger">
          {mapError(searchParams.error)}
        </div>
      )}
      <PaymentForm />
    </>
  );
}
```

```typescript
// app/checkout/PaymentForm.tsx — Client Component
"use client";
import { useActionState } from "react";
import { pay } from "@/app/actions";

export function PaymentForm() {
  const [state, action] = useActionState(pay, { error: null });
  return (
    <form action={action}>
      {/* Region always present; useActionState swaps only the text → announced */}
      <div aria-live="assertive" role="alert">

```

## 10\. Accessibility (the spine of this component)
This is not a section you skim. For an alert, accessibility _is_ the component — a beautiful red box that never announces is a 100% failure for screen-reader users.

### The role decision, definitively

| Situation | Role | Politeness | Why |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Dynamically injected, urgent/error | `role="alert"` | assertive (interrupts) | The user must know now — payment failed, session expiring |
| Dynamically injected, non-urgent | `role="status"` | polite (waits its turn) | "Saved," "3 results" — worth announcing, not worth interrupting |
| Static, present on page load | no role | — | It's just content; a live role here either does nothing or hijacks every load |
| Blocking, needs a decision | `role="alertdialog"` | (modal) | Not this component — that's a Dialog. Traps focus, blocks the page |

`role="alert"` **is** `aria-live="assertive"` + `aria-atomic="true"` implicitly. `role="status"` **is** `aria-live="polite"`. Don't stack both.

### The timing rule (the bug behind 90% of "my alert doesn't announce")
A live region announces when its **text content changes while the region already exists in the accessibility tree.** If you mount the region _and_ its text in the same paint, there's no change to detect and several screen readers stay silent.

**The fix:** render the empty live region up front (on page load), then inject/replace only the _text_ when the event fires. In React that's the always-present wrapper `<div aria-live="assertive" role="alert">{error && …}</div>` from §9.3. In vanilla JS it's the pre-created `#alerts` node from §9.5. Never create the region and its message together for a dynamic alert.

Corollary: for a **server-rendered** alert (present in the initial HTML — a validation summary after a form POST), the live region won't fire because it was there on load. That case wants **focus management** instead: give the summary `tabindex="-1"` and `.focus()` it after the submit so keyboard and SR users land directly on the explanation (§9.8).

### Never color alone
Severity must survive three deletions: no color, no icon, no title. The redundant channels:
*   **A word:** "Error," "Warning," "Success" — visible or `sr-only`.
*   **An icon with a text equivalent:** the icon is `aria-hidden`, but its meaning is duplicated in the body text.
*   **Shape/position** as reinforcement, never as sole signal.

WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) is a hard failure if red-vs-green is the only difference between a success and an error alert.

### Contrast & target size
*   Body text vs. its tinted background ≥ **4.5:1** (WCAG AA); large text ≥ 3:1. The tinted-background alerts are the sneakiest contrast failures — a pale amber body on a pale amber fill often fails. Test every severity.
*   The dismiss/action controls keep a ≥ **44px** (WCAG 2.5.5 / Apple) hit target even when the × looks tiny.
*   Non-text severity indicators (the border, the icon) vs. adjacent colors ≥ 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).

### Keyboard & focus
*   The alert container itself is generally **not** focusable and **not** in the tab order — that's correct; a live region does its job without focus.
*   Any interactive children (action buttons, links, the dismiss ×) are normally tabbable and get visible `:focus-visible` rings.
*   **Exception:** the server-rendered form-error summary you deliberately `.focus()` after a failed submit — that one gets `tabindex="-1"` so it's programmatically focusable but not a tab stop.
*   Dismissing an alert must not strand focus. If focus was on the × you just removed, move it somewhere sensible (the element after, or the region).

### The one-line summary
**Earn** **`role="alert"`** **with urgency + dynamism; render the region before the text; carry severity in text, not color; keep the dismiss target ≥44px and 4.5:1 contrast.** Get those four right and the rest is styling.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The alert looks solved but keeps evolving:
*   **AI/agentic status alerts:** streaming progress ("Analyzing 3 of 12 files…") replacing the binary spinner, and post-hoc alerts that summarize what an agent did and offer an undo.
*   **Priority queues for announcements:** libraries that _serialize_ competing live-region messages so an assertive error doesn't get shredded by a polite one firing in the same tick — solving the "two alerts, one got dropped" bug.
*   **`prefers-reduced-motion`****\*\*\*\*-aware entrances** as a default, not an afterthought: alerts that slide only when motion is allowed.
*   **Contextual inline recovery:** the alert embeds the fix (a mini-form to re-enter a card) rather than sending the user elsewhere.
*   **Native popover/****`alert`****\-adjacent HTML:** the platform is growing more declarative surfaces; watch the space, but the inline status alert stays a live-region job for now.
*   **Auto-expiring** **_visual_** **but persistent** **_semantic_****\*\*\*\*:** the box fades from view after a while yet stays reachable in a "notifications" region, splitting visual transience from record-keeping.

## 12\. Conversion & UX Killers (mistakes that bleed trust silently)
None of these throw an error. They just quietly erode trust and completion.
*   **Static alert hardcoded to** **`role="alert"`****\*\*\*\*.** Every page load hijacks the screen reader with a greeting or a stale notice. Trains AT users to dread your app. The #1 alert a11y bug.
*   **The region and text mount together, so nothing announces.** The visual box appears; screen-reader users get silence at the exact moment (payment failed) it matters most. Pre-render the region.
*   **Color-only severity.** Success and error look identical to colorblind users. Add the word and the icon.
*   **Vague error copy.** "Something went wrong" with no cause and no next step is an abandoned checkout. Name what happened and what to do: "Card declined — try another card."
*   **Leaking raw server errors** into the alert — ugly, useless, and an info-disclosure risk. Map to safe human copy.
*   **Alert overload / stacking.** Five alerts stacked at the top means users read none. Collapse to a summary, cap the count, or fix the thing generating five alerts.
*   **Crying wolf with assertive.** Making every alert assertive so it "gets noticed" means real emergencies don't. Reserve assertive for genuine interruptions.
*   **Toast for something important.** Auto-dismissing the one message the user needed to act on. If missing it costs them, it's an alert, not a toast.
*   **Per-field errors with no summary.** On a long form, ten inline errors and no top-of-form alert leaves keyboard/SR users hunting. Pair field errors with a focused summary alert that links to each field.
*   **Dismiss that doesn't stick.** The "we updated our terms" bar reappearing on every navigation. Persist dismissal.

**The through-line:** every one fails _quietly_ — no crash, just eroded trust and a lower completion number that's easy to blame on "the funnel." Audit your alerts before you audit your copywriter.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
### Semantic severity tokens (theme + dark mode in one place)
Three token tiers so a re-theme touches one line, and dark mode is a token swap, not new alert CSS:

  --alert-danger-bg: var(--red-50);
  --alert-danger-accent: var(--red-700);

.alert--danger {
  background: var(--alert-danger-bg);
  border-color: var(--alert-danger-accent);
  color: var(--alert-danger-fg);

  :root {
    --alert-danger-bg: color-mix(in oklch, var(--red-700) 22%, #000);

### TypeScript: make the role impossible to get wrong
A discriminated union can _force_ the caller to declare whether the alert is dynamic, so a live role can never attach to a static banner by accident:

```typescript
type StaticAlert  = { dynamic?: false;  severity: Severity };            // never gets a live role
type DynamicAlert = { dynamic: true;    severity: Severity; live?: "assertive" | "polite" };
type AlertConfig  = StaticAlert | DynamicAlert;
// A static alert simply has no `live` field to set — the type removes the footgun.
```

### A single global announce() utility
Centralize the live region so the whole app announces through one honest, pre-existing node — no scattered `role="alert"` divs mounting with their text:

```typescript
// mount once at app root: <div id="sr-assertive" aria-live="assertive" aria-atomic="true" class="sr-only" />
//                         <div id="sr-polite"    aria-live="polite"    aria-atomic="true" class="sr-only" />
export function announce(msg: string, assertive = false) {
  const region = document.getElementById(assertive ? "sr-assertive" : "sr-polite")!;
  region.textContent = "";                     // clear so identical repeat messages re-announce
  requestAnimationFrame(() => { region.textContent = msg; });   // change into an existing region
}
```

The `textContent = ""` then re-set on the next frame is the trick that makes _repeated identical_ messages ("Still offline") announce again instead of being deduped into silence.

### Form-error summary that links to fields
The accessible pattern for a failed multi-field submit: one focused summary alert, each item an in-page link to the offending control.

```plain
<div role="alert" tabindex="-1" id="err-summary" class="alert alert--danger">
  <strong>There are 2 problems:</strong>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="#email">Enter a valid email</a></li>
    <li><a href="#pw">Password must be at least 8 characters</a></li>
  </ul>
</div>
<!-- After a failed submit: document.getElementById('err-summary').focus() -->
```

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
An alert feels free — and mostly is — but a few traps:
*   **The icon-import trap** (identical to Button's): `import { AlertTriangle } from "lucide-react"`, never `import * as Icons`. A namespace import can drag hundreds of KB in for one triangle.
*   **Don't animate layout on entrance.** Animate `opacity`/`transform` only; animating `height`/`margin` to "slide open" forces reflow every frame. If you must expand height, prefer a `grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr` transition or accept the cost knowingly.
*   **Don't thrash the live region.** Re-injecting the same alert on every render (a `useEffect` with a bad dep array) can spam assertive announcements and lock a screen reader into a stutter. Gate announcements on _actual_ message change.
*   **CSS containment** on a stack of alerts isolates their reflow: `.alert { contain: content; }`.
*   **Delegate the dismiss handler** for long alert lists — one listener on the container reading `data-*`, not one per ×.

## 15\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before an alert is "done":
- [ ] Right pattern chosen: inline alert (not a toast, message bar, or alertdialog).
- [ ] Static alerts have **no** live role; only dynamically-injected ones get `role="alert"`/`status`.
- [ ] Urgency mapped correctly: danger/critical → `alert` (assertive); success/info → `status` (polite).
- [ ] The live region exists in the DOM **before** the text is injected.
- [ ] Server-rendered alerts use focus management (`tabindex=-1` + `.focus()`), not a live role.
- [ ] Severity conveyed by icon + word + color — **never color alone** (WCAG 1.4.1).
- [ ] Body-vs-background contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for every severity; icon/border ≥ 3:1.
- [ ] Icon is `aria-hidden`; its meaning is duplicated in text.
- [ ] Dismiss has `aria-label`, a ≥44px target, sticky dismissal where needed, and doesn't strand focus.
- [ ] Error copy is specific and recoverable; raw server errors never leaked.
- [ ] Assertive reserved for genuine interruptions (no crying wolf); alerts don't stack unbounded.
- [ ] Entrance animation is `transform`/`opacity` only and off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- [ ] Manually verified announcement in at least one real screen reader (VoiceOver/NVDA) — `getByRole` alone doesn't prove it voices.

## 16\. Security (the error alert is an information-disclosure surface)
Lighter than Button's, but real:
*   **Never render raw server errors.** Stack traces, SQL errors, internal IDs, or exception messages in a user-facing alert leak your internals to anyone (including attackers). Map server responses to stable codes → safe human copy client-side (§9.4). Log the real error server-side only.
*   **Don't reflect unsanitized user/server input into alert HTML.** `el.innerHTML = serverMessage` is an XSS vector if the message contains attacker-controlled content. Use `textContent`, or sanitize, or render through your framework's escaped interpolation.
*   **Don't confirm things the server hasn't.** A "success" alert must reflect a confirmed result, not an optimistic guess — otherwise you've told the user (and possibly logged) that something happened when it didn't.
*   **Watch enumeration in auth alerts.** "No account with that email" vs. "Wrong password" tells an attacker which emails are registered. Use a single neutral message ("Email or password is incorrect") for login/reset alerts.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same alert, eleven skins)
Style is skin; the role, live-region timing, icon-plus-text severity, keyboard behavior, and 44px dismiss target are the skeleton and never change. Each skin below keeps all four severities distinguishable by more than hue.

*   **Flat:** solid tint fill, 4px accent border, crisp edges, no shadow. Reads instantly — the safest default for alerts because legibility is maximal.
*   **Material:** tonal severity surface (M3 color roles), a subtle elevation shadow, a filled leading icon. Banner sits flush; snackbar variant floats.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent tint + `backdrop-filter: blur()`. **Guard hard for contrast** — an error message over a busy background can drop below 4.5:1. Provide a solid fallback layer behind the text.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026):** refractive translucent material with a specular rim; gorgeous for _informational_ system bars over the deep Eminence backdrop, risky for critical errors. Always back the text with a solid contrast layer; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for the sheen. Web = approximation; SwiftUI emits native `.glassEffect()`.
*   **Neumorphism:** dual light/dark shadows on a same-tone surface. Contrast-poor by nature — acceptable only for _info_ severity, never for error, and never as the sole severity signal.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** beveled edge, inner highlight, gradient tint — the "physical notice card" look. The icon becomes a glossy badge.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black border, hard offset shadow, zero radius, unapologetic severity color. High personality; keep the body text on a solid fill for contrast.
*   **Claymorphism:** big radius, soft inner top-light + bottom-shadow, puffy friendly tint. Good for success/info; reads too gentle for critical errors.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue gradient border or fill on a dark surface. Premium; **honor** **`prefers-reduced-motion`** and keep the text on a solid patch.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-zero decoration — a thin accent rule, the icon, and typographic hierarchy do all the work. Excellent for dense/data UIs.
*   **UJG Brand:** Afro-Futurist house style — Goldenrod accent and icon on a deep Eminence/Night tinted surface, warm glow, confident radius. Severity tints shift toward the brand's warm-to-cool range (Dark Green success, Eminence info, Goldenrod warning, Spanish Orange/Sienna danger) while keeping every one ≥4.5:1 on the dark field. The house default for in-product alerts.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin. Across all eleven, `role="alert"` still means assertive, the region still pre-exists its text, severity still rides on icon-plus-word, and the dismiss target stays ≥44px. Full guidance on the [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551).
* * *

**Bottom line:** an alert is a small box with a heavy contract. The visual part — a tinted panel and an icon — is the easy 20%. The 80% that separates a hobby build from production software is the accessibility spine: earning `role="alert"` only through urgency and dynamism, rendering the live region before the text so it actually announces, carrying severity in words and icons instead of color, and never leaking raw errors or crying wolf. Master that, and you've mastered the component that decides whether your app tells the truth about what just happened.
