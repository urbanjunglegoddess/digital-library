# Tag/Chip

# The Tag/Chip: A Senior Engineer's Complete Breakdown
The small, dismissible surface that labels, filters, and selects. Here's everything from first principles to production code.

**Try it live:** the interactive playground (built in a later pass) will let you toggle size, variant, removability, and selection state, then output code for every target.

**Audit a chip:** the companion audit tool checks contrast, target size, dismiss accessibility, and keyboard navigation, then exports a client-ready report.

This doc follows the Private ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200531](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200531)) and its visual styles are drawn from the Private ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-87871/838qa-200551](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-87871/838qa-200551)).

* * *
## 1\. What a Tag/Chip Actually Is
A **chip** (also called tag, pill, token, or badge-label) is a compact, interactive element that represents an attribute, filter, selection, or action. It's the UI equivalent of a Post-it note stuck to something: small, contextual, often removable.

The term confusion:

**Chip/Tag (this doc):** a compact interactive surface with a label (and optionally an icon, avatar, or remove button). Can be selected, can be dismissed, can trigger a filter.
**Badge:** purely decorative/informational count or status indicator. NOT interactive, NOT removable. A badge sits ON something (an avatar, a nav item); a chip IS something.
**Label (HTML):** a form element. Not this.
**Pill:** a visual shape (fully-rounded chip). A chip can be pill-shaped, but "pill" isn't a separate component.

Rule of thumb: **if it can be selected, filtered by, or dismissed, it's a chip. If it just displays a count, it's a badge.**

* * *
## 2\. Why It Matters
**Filter UX lives here.** E-commerce filtering, tag-based search, multi-select dropdowns all surface choices as chips. A bad chip (too small to tap, no keyboard removal, no clear "selected" state) breaks the entire filter experience.

**Information density.** Chips pack meaning into tiny space. A user's selected interests, an email's labels, a task's tags, a product's attributes. Without chips you'd need full-width list items for each.

**Accessibility is non-obvious.** A removable chip is two interactive elements (the chip + the dismiss) in a tiny footprint. Getting the keyboard model right (arrow between chips, Delete/Backspace to remove, clear focus management when one is removed) is the senior-level challenge.

* * *
## 3\. Anatomy
**Container:** the rounded-rect or pill surface with a tinted background or border.
**Leading element (optional):** an icon, avatar, or color dot for visual context.
**Label:** the text content. Short, typically one or two words.
**Remove/Dismiss button (optional):** the × that deletes/deselects the chip. Needs its own accessible name.
**Selection indicator (optional):** a checkmark icon or filled background that shows selected state.

* * *
## 4\. Sizes / Scale

| Token | Height | Padding | Font | Icon | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| XS | 20px | 4px 8px | 11px | 12px | Dense dashboards, metadata |
| S | 24px | 4px 10px | 12px | 14px | Compact filter bars |
| M | 28px | 6px 12px | 13px | 16px | Default (start here) |
| L | 32px | 8px 14px | 14px | 18px | Touch-friendly, mobile |
| XL | 36px | 8px 16px | 15px | 20px | Hero filter sections |

The dismiss × must maintain a ≥44px tap target regardless of chip size. Expand the hit area with padding, don't shrink the button.

* * *
## 5\. States
**Default:** the resting chip, unselected.
**Hover:** subtle background shift or elevation.
**Focus:** visible focus-visible ring (the whole chip or the dismiss specifically, depending on which element has focus).
**Active/Pressed:** depressed on click/tap.
**Selected:** visually distinct (filled background, checkmark, or both). For filter chips.
**Disabled:** muted, non-interactive, cursor-not-allowed.
**Removable (dismiss hover):** the × highlights independently.
**Removing (animating out):** the chip shrinks/fades as it's dismissed.

Keyboard states: chips in a group use roving tabindex. Arrow keys move between chips, Delete/Backspace removes the focused chip, Enter/Space selects.

* * *
## 6\. Types / Variants
**Input chip:** represents user-generated content in a field (email recipients, tags in a tag input). Always removable.
**Filter chip:** represents an active filter. Selectable + removable. Often in a horizontal scroll bar.
**Choice chip:** single-select from a set (like radio buttons but chip-shaped). Only one active at a time.
**Action chip:** triggers an action ("Add to cart", "Share"). Less common, feels like a mini button.
**Assist chip:** suggests a next step (Google Assistant style). Ephemeral.

Visual variants: Outlined (border only), Filled (solid tint), Elevated (shadow), Pill (full radius).

* * *
## 7\. When to Use (and When Not To)
**Use chips when:**
*   Displaying selected filters that can be individually removed
*   Showing tags/categories on content
*   Multi-select results in a compact space
*   Email/user recipients in a compose field

**Use something else when:**
*   It's a count or status indicator → Badge
*   It's a full clickable action → Button
*   It's a single on/off → Toggle
*   It's navigation → Link or Tab
*   The content is too long for a chip (3+ words) → reconsider the taxonomy

* * *
## 8\. Across Design Systems
**Material Design:** "Chip" is the canonical name. Four types: Assist, Filter, Input, Suggestion. M3 adds tonal and elevated variants.
**Apple HIG:** No explicit chip component. Uses rounded-rect tokens in context (Tags in Finder, recipients in Mail). Build custom.
**Fluent (Microsoft):** "Tag" in older versions, evolving. Persona chips in Outlook for recipients.
**Ant Design:** `<Tag>` with closable, color, and checkable props.
**Chakra/Radix:** `<Tag>` with size, variant (subtle/solid/outline), and close button.
**Bootstrap:** `.badge` handles both badges and chip-like elements (confusingly). No dismissible chip out of the box.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript

```typescript
import { forwardRef, HTMLAttributes, ReactNode } from "react";

type ChipVariant = "filled" | "outlined" | "elevated";
type ChipSize = "xs" | "sm" | "md" | "lg";

interface ChipProps extends HTMLAttributes<HTMLSpanElement> {
  variant?: ChipVariant;
  size?: ChipSize;
  selected?: boolean;
  disabled?: boolean;
  onRemove?: () => void;
  leadingIcon?: ReactNode;
}

export const Chip = forwardRef<HTMLSpanElement, ChipProps>(function Chip(
  { variant = "filled", size = "md", selected, disabled, onRemove, leadingIcon, children, className = "", ...rest },
  ref
) {
  return (
    <span
      ref={ref}
      role={onRemove ? "option" : undefined}
      aria-selected={selected}
      aria-disabled={disabled || undefined}
      className={`chip chip--${variant} chip--${size} ${selected ? "chip--selected" : ""} ${className}`}
      {...rest}
    >
      {leadingIcon && <span className="chip__icon" aria-hidden="true">{leadingIcon}</span>}
      <span className="chip__label">{children}</span>
      {onRemove && (
        <button type="button" className="chip__remove" aria-label={`Remove ${children}`}
                onClick={(e) => { e.stopPropagation(); onRemove(); }} disabled={disabled}>
          &times;
        </button>
      )}
    </span>
  );
});
```

### 9.6 Next.js (App Router, Server + Client)

```typescript
// app/components/chip.tsx
"use client";

import { forwardRef, HTMLAttributes, ReactNode, useTransition } from "react";
import { removeFilter } from "@/app/actions";

type ChipVariant = "filled" | "outlined" | "elevated";
type ChipSize = "xs" | "sm" | "md" | "lg";

interface ChipProps extends HTMLAttributes<HTMLSpanElement> {
  variant?: ChipVariant;
  size?: ChipSize;
  selected?: boolean;
  disabled?: boolean;
  filterId?: string;
  onRemove?: () => void;
  leadingIcon?: ReactNode;
}

export const Chip = forwardRef<HTMLSpanElement, ChipProps>(function Chip(
  { variant = "filled", size = "md", selected, disabled, filterId, onRemove, leadingIcon, children, className = "", ...rest },
  ref
) {
  const [isPending, startTransition] = useTransition();

  const handleRemove = () => {
    if (filterId) {
      // Server Action for filter removal (updates URL/searchParams)
      startTransition(() => removeFilter(filterId));
    }
    onRemove?.();
  };

  return (
    <span ref={ref} role="option" aria-selected={selected}
          aria-disabled={disabled || isPending || undefined}
          className={`chip chip--${variant} chip--${size} ${selected ? "chip--selected" : ""} ${isPending ? "chip--pending" : ""} ${className}`}
          {...rest}>
      {leadingIcon && <span className="chip__icon" aria-hidden="true">{leadingIcon}</span>}
      <span className="chip__label">{children}</span>
      {(onRemove || filterId) && (
        <button type="button" className="chip__remove" aria-label={`Remove ${children}`}
                onClick={(e) => { e.stopPropagation(); handleRemove(); }}
                disabled={disabled || isPending}>
          {isPending ? <Spinner size="xs" /> : <>&times;</>}
        </button>
      )}
    </span>
  );
});

// app/actions.ts
"use server";
import { redirect } from "next/navigation";

export async function removeFilter(filterId: string) {
  // Remove filter from URL searchParams and redirect
  // In practice: parse current searchParams, remove the filter, redirect
  const url = new URL(/* current path */);
  url.searchParams.delete(filterId);
  redirect(url.pathname + url.search);
}
```

* * *
## 10\. Accessibility
**Keyboard model for chip groups:** roving tabindex. One chip in the group is tab-focusable (tabindex 0), the rest are -1. Arrow keys move focus between chips. Delete or Backspace removes the focused chip. Enter/Space selects (for filter chips).

**When a chip is removed:** move focus to the next chip, or the previous if it was the last. Never strand focus on a removed element.

**The dismiss button** needs `aria-label="Remove [chip label]"`. The × character alone reads as "times" to screen readers.

**Contrast:** chip text vs. background ≥ 4.5:1. Selected state must also pass. The dismiss target ≥ 44px.

**Color alone:** never distinguish "selected" from "unselected" by color alone. Add a checkmark icon, a border change, or an `aria-selected` announcement.

* * *
## 11\. Innovative / Emerging Ideas
*   **AI-generated filter chips:** auto-suggested filters based on search context
*   **Animated removal:** chips shrink and siblings reflow smoothly (layout animations)
*   **Drag-to-reorder:** chips in a tag input that can be rearranged
*   **Chip input with autocomplete:** typing creates chips from a suggestion list

* * *
## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers
*   **No clear "selected" state:** users can't tell what's active
*   **Dismiss too small:** mobile users can't hit the ×
*   **No keyboard removal:** keyboard users are trapped with chips they can't delete
*   **Focus lost on removal:** the page focus jumps to top after dismissing
*   **Too many chips:** 20+ chips with no collapse/overflow is visual noise
*   **Chip text too long:** wrapping inside a chip breaks the pattern; truncate or rethink

* * *
## 13\. Advanced Patterns
**Chip input (token field):** a text input that converts typed/selected values into chips. The input and chips live in the same visual container. Keyboard: left arrow from input start focuses last chip; Delete removes it.

**Overflow handling:** when chips exceed container width, options: horizontal scroll, wrap to next line, collapse to "+N more" with a popover.

**Animated layout:** use `layout` animations (Framer Motion) or `View Transitions` so removing a chip doesn't cause a jarring reflow.

* * *
## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
*   Chips are lightweight. The cost is in _quantity_: 500 filter chips rendered at once (tag clouds) can be expensive. Virtualize if the list exceeds ~100.
*   Avoid re-rendering the entire chip group when one chip changes. Key each chip by a stable ID.
*   The dismiss animation should use `transform`/`opacity` only (compositor thread).

* * *
## 15\. Security
*   **XSS in chip labels:** if chip content comes from user input (tags, email addresses), sanitize. Never `innerHTML` user-provided text into a chip.
*   **Enumeration via autocomplete:** if the chip input has a suggestion list backed by a database, rate-limit the autocomplete endpoint.

* * *
## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist
*   Chip group uses roving tabindex for keyboard navigation
*   Delete/Backspace removes focused chip and moves focus correctly
*   Dismiss button has `aria-label="Remove [label]"`
*   Selected state is conveyed by more than color (icon + `aria-selected`)
*   Chip text vs. background ≥ 4.5:1 in all states
*   Dismiss target ≥ 44px even on small chips
*   Overflow handled (wrap, scroll, or collapse)
*   Removal animation is compositor-only and respects `prefers-reduced-motion`
*   Labels sanitized against XSS when user-generated

* * *
## 17\. Visual Styles
The same chip rendered across eleven aesthetics. The style is skin; keyboard behavior, focus ring, target size, and ARIA never change.

**Flat:** solid tint fill, crisp pill shape, no shadow. The default.
**Material:** tonal surface (M3 color roles), subtle elevation on hover, ripple on press.
**Glassmorphism:** translucent chip over a blurred background. Guard contrast hard.
**Liquid Glass:** refractive material with specular rim. Beautiful for filter bars over dark UJG backgrounds; ensure text contrast fallback.
**Neumorphism:** soft extruded pill, dual shadows. Decorative only; selected state needs an extra indicator since depth alone is ambiguous.
**Skeuomorphism:** beveled edge, inner highlight, slight gradient. The "physical label" look.
**Neo-Brutalism:** thick black border, hard offset shadow, bold fill. High personality.
**Claymorphism:** puffy, big radius, soft inner glow. Friendly/playful.
**Aurora/Gradient:** animated gradient border or fill on dark. Premium feel.
**Minimal/Swiss:** thin border, no fill, sharp type. Maximum information density.
**UJG Brand:** Goldenrod text/border on Eminence-tinted surface, warm glow on hover. The house default.
