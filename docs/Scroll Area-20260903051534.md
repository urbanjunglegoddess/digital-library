# Scroll Area

# Scroll Area

A styled, custom-scrollbar container that keeps native scrolling intact. The whole job of this component is to make a scrollbar look like it belongs to your brand without breaking the thing a scrollbar is for: scrolling. Get greedy with the styling and you break keyboard access, wheel momentum, and touch — which is exactly the failure mode this page exists to prevent.

**Try it live:** every variant, orientation, and overflow state in this doc is wired up in `playground/scroll-area.html`. Flip the controls (vertical / horizontal / both, auto-hide vs always-on, scroll shadows on/off, gutter stable/auto) and the panel outputs live code across all targets with one-click copy.

**Audit a scroll area:** paste your container markup into `scroll-area-a11y-audit.html` to run live checks — is the container focusable, does it have an accessible name, does keyboard scrolling still work, is `overscroll-behavior` set, does it survive `prefers-reduced-motion` — then export a client-ready report.
* * *

## 1\. What It Actually Is

A Scroll Area is a bounded container whose content overflows, and whose scrollbar has been restyled (thinner, themed, auto-hiding) or replaced with a fully custom overlay — while the underlying element keeps its native scroll behavior: wheel, trackpad momentum, touch drag, keyboard, and the browser's own scroll-into-view.

The word "scroll area" hides three genuinely different implementations people lump together, and picking the wrong one is where accessibility dies:

*   **Native-styled scroll (recommended default):** a real overflow container (`overflow: auto`) with `scrollbar-width` / `scrollbar-color` (and legacy `::-webkit-scrollbar`) applied. The browser still owns scrolling. Keyboard, wheel, and touch are free and correct.
*   **Custom overlay scrollbar (Radix ScrollArea, OverlayScrollbars):** the native scrollbar is visually hidden and a `div`\-based thumb/track is drawn on top, synced to `scrollTop`/`scrollLeft`. The _element_ still scrolls natively; only the _visual_ bar is fake. Done right, keyboard and wheel are untouched. Done wrong, it hijacks the wheel and traps focus.
*   **Fake JS scroll (the trap):** content is translated with `transform: translateY()` on wheel events and native overflow is turned off entirely. This looks the same for two seconds and then breaks keyboard scrolling, Find-in-Page, screen-reader virtual cursor, anchor navigation, and touch momentum. **Do not build this.** If you catch yourself listening to `wheel` and moving content manually, stop.

The single most common mistake: reaching for a custom scrollbar when the native one, lightly styled, would have been better _and_ accessible. The second most common: building the overlay variant and forgetting the container needs `tabindex="0"` and an accessible name so keyboard-only users can actually reach and drive it.

**Rule of thumb:** if the element has real overflow and the browser gives it a scrollbar, it is a scroll area — style that scrollbar, don't replace the scrolling. Only reach for a custom overlay when a design system genuinely requires a bar the platform can't produce (e.g. a floating 6px thumb that never reserves layout width), and even then keep native overflow underneath.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters

*   **Accessibility (the big one):** a scrollable region that keyboard users cannot reach is a WCAG 2.1.1 (Keyboard) failure, and content clipped with no way to scroll to it is a 1.4.10 (Reflow) / 2.4.3 (Focus Order) problem. If a `div` scrolls but isn't focusable and has no accessible name, keyboard and screen-reader users are locked out of whatever overflows. `tabindex="0"` + `role="region"` + `aria-label` is the fix, and it costs nothing.
*   **Trust & platform feel:** native scrollbars differ across OS/browser (Windows' thick opaque bar reserves ~17px and shifts your layout; macOS overlay bars auto-hide and reserve nothing). A tastefully styled scroll area makes the product feel consistent and intentional — but a _badly_ faked one feels broken the instant someone hits PageDown and nothing happens. Broken scrolling is one of the fastest ways to make a slick UI feel cheap.
*   **Layout stability (CLS):** when a scrollbar appears on hover or on content load, it can steal width from the content and reflow everything — a Cumulative Layout Shift and a visible jump. `scrollbar-gutter: stable` reserves the space up front so nothing shifts. On dashboards and reading views this is the difference between calm and jittery.
*   **Performance & correctness of chaining:** without `overscroll-behavior: contain`, scrolling to the bottom of a modal's inner list keeps going and scrolls the page behind it (scroll chaining), or triggers pull-to-refresh on mobile. That's not cosmetic — it dumps users out of the context they were in. The scroll area is where you stop the chain.
*   **Discoverability & conversion:** in dropdowns, chat threads, and long forms, users must know there's more below the fold. Auto-hiding scrollbars that hide _too_ well cause missed content and abandoned flows. Scroll shadows / fade indicators quietly say "keep going," which directly affects whether people find the submit button or the last message.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy

An interactive scroll area has more moving parts than it looks — this is why the "just style the bar" instinct undersells it.

*   **Viewport (the scroll container):** the clipped, overflowing element itself. Owns `overflow`, `scrollbar-gutter`, `overscroll-behavior`, `scroll-behavior`, `tabindex="0"`, `role="region"`, and the accessible name. This is the element that actually scrolls.
*   **Content wrapper:** the inner element holding the real content. In overlay implementations this is what's measured (`scrollHeight` vs `clientHeight`) to compute thumb size and whether a bar is even needed.
*   **Scrollbar track:** the rail the thumb travels in. Native: styled via `::-webkit-scrollbar-track` / the second color of `scrollbar-color`. Overlay: a positioned `div`, often transparent until hover.
*   **Scrollbar thumb:** the draggable indicator. Its length is proportional to `clientHeight / scrollHeight`; its position maps `scrollTop / (scrollHeight - clientHeight)`. Native: `::-webkit-scrollbar-thumb`. Overlay: a `div` you translate and, on pointerdown, drag.
*   **Corner (both-axis only):** the small square where vertical and horizontal bars meet (`::-webkit-scrollbar-corner`). Easy to forget; looks broken when it's a default gray box in a dark theme.
*   **Scroll shadows / fade indicators:** gradient overlays at the overflow edges that appear only when there's more content in that direction. Purely a discoverability affordance — decorative, `aria-hidden`, and pointer-events-none so they never eat clicks or wheel events.
*   **Gutter:** the reserved lane for the scrollbar (`scrollbar-gutter: stable [both-edges]`). Not a DOM node — a layout reservation that stops content from reflowing when the bar appears.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes / Scale / Density

Scrollbar width is the primary size axis, and it's a real accessibility tradeoff: thin bars look elegant but shrink the drag target. The 44px minimum applies to the _interactive_ dimension of a draggable thumb where the thumb is the primary control — in practice most scroll areas are driven by wheel/keyboard/touch, so a thin bar is acceptable _provided_ those paths work. If the thumb is the only way to scroll, it must be a comfortable target.

| Tier | Bar width | Thumb min-length | Token | Use case |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Thin / overlay | 6px | 32px | `--sa-bar-w: 0.375rem` | Chat threads, side menus, dense dashboards where the bar is secondary to wheel/touch |
| Default | 8–10px | 40px | `--sa-bar-w: 0.5rem` | Dropdown panels, popovers, card bodies — the house default |
| Comfortable / touch-adjacent | 12–14px | 48px | `--sa-bar-w: 0.875rem` | Kiosks, TV/remote UIs, anywhere the thumb is a primary drag target |

Rules that matter:

*   **Thumb minimum length:** never let the thumb collapse below ~32px even with enormous content, or it becomes an un-grabbable sliver. WebKit does this for you; overlay implementations must clamp `Math.max(minThumb, ratio * trackLength)`.
*   **Gutter reservation:** reserve `scrollbar-gutter: stable` at the _default_ width so switching between overflow/no-overflow doesn't shift layout. `both-edges` keeps content optically centered.
*   **Responsive behavior:** on coarse pointers (touch), collapse to native overlay scrolling — `@media (pointer: coarse) { .scroll-area { scrollbar-width: auto; } }` and skip the custom thumb entirely. Fingers don't drag 6px rails; they flick.
*   **Spacing tokens:** pad the content wrapper, not the viewport, so the scrollbar sits flush to the edge and doesn't float in the middle of your padding.
* * *

## 5\. States

Every state described — no "same as X."

*   **Idle (not overflowing):** content fits. No scrollbar, no shadows, no gutter jump if `stable` is set. The container is still focusable but scrolling is a no-op; screen readers may or may not announce it as scrollable depending on the engine.
*   **Idle (overflowing, auto-hide):** content overflows but the user isn't scrolling. In auto-hide mode the thumb is faded (opacity 0 or ~0.2). Risk: too invisible to discover — this is where scroll shadows earn their keep.
*   **Hover (container):** in auto-hide mode, hovering the viewport reveals the thumb at partial or full opacity. Hover cannot be the _only_ reveal — keyboard and touch users never hover.
*   **Scrolling (active):** thumb at full opacity, tracking `scrollTop`. Shadows update at both edges. If `scroll-behavior: smooth` is set, programmatic jumps animate — unless reduced-motion is on.
*   **Thumb hover / active (drag):** the thumb itself is hovered or being dragged (`:active` / pointer captured). Usually darkens or thickens slightly for feedback. During drag, native selection must be suppressed and pointer capture used so the drag survives leaving the track.
*   **Focus (keyboard):** the container has a visible focus ring (`:focus-visible`). This is mandatory for the overlay variant — it's the signal that Arrow/PageDown will scroll _this_ region. Never remove the ring without replacing it.
*   **At-start edge:** scrolled to top (or left). The leading shadow/fade is hidden; trailing shadow shows. `scrollTop === 0`.
*   **At-end edge:** scrolled to bottom (or right). Trailing shadow hidden; leading shadow shows. `scrollTop + clientHeight >= scrollHeight - 1` (the `-1` absorbs sub-pixel rounding on zoom/HiDPI).
*   **Both-edge (mid-scroll):** both shadows visible — content overflows in both directions.
*   **Overscroll / chain-blocked:** user scrolls past the end. With `overscroll-behavior: contain`, the gesture stops here and does not scroll the parent; with `none` it also disables the bounce/pull-to-refresh. Momentum decays inside the container.
*   **Disabled scroll (locked):** occasionally you freeze scrolling (e.g. during an inline load) with `overflow: hidden` plus preserved `scrollbar-gutter` so the layout doesn't jump. Announce the reason; don't just silently trap the user.
* * *

## 6\. Types / Variants

*   **Vertical scroll:** the default. `overflow-y: auto; overflow-x: hidden`. Bounded height, variable content — dropdowns, chat, card bodies.
*   **Horizontal scroll:** `overflow-x: auto; overflow-y: hidden`. Filter chips, image carousels, table columns. Horizontal is _less discoverable_ — always pair with edge fades or visible affordances, and remember Shift+Wheel / trackpad-horizontal must work.
*   **Bidirectional (both):** `overflow: auto`. Data grids, canvases, code blocks with long lines. Needs corner styling and both-axis shadows.
*   **Auto-hide scrollbar:** bar appears on hover/scroll, hides at idle (macOS-style). Elegant but the least discoverable — mandatory scroll shadows.
*   **Always-visible scrollbar:** bar is always present (Windows-style, or accessibility preference). The safest for discoverability; reserve the gutter so it never causes shift.
*   **With scroll shadows:** inset gradient at overflow edges that fades content into the boundary. The CSS-only `background-attachment: local` technique (Lea Verou) needs zero JS.
*   **With fade / mask indicators:** uses `mask-image` linear gradients at the edges to literally fade content out, updated by edge state. Cleaner than overlay shadows on busy backgrounds.
*   **Custom overlay (Radix-style):** native bar hidden, `div` thumb drawn on top with full drag support. Use only when the design demands a bar the platform can't render — and keep native overflow underneath.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use (and When Not To)

**Use a (lightly styled) scroll area when:**

*   A container has a **bounded height** and **variable content** — dropdown/listbox panels, comboboxes, chat threads, side navigation with many items.
*   You need **visual consistency** of the bar across Windows/macOS/Linux for a branded product surface.
*   Content **overflows in a component**, not the whole page — code blocks, card bodies, table wrappers, popovers.
*   You need to **stop scroll chaining** — inner list in a modal, sticky sidebar next to a scrolling body.

**Don't use a custom scroll area / use something else when:**

*   **It's page-level scrolling** — let the document scroll natively. Custom bars on the whole page fight the browser's scroll restoration, anchor jumps, and Find-in-Page. Use the native document scroll.
*   **On touch/mobile primarily** — native overlay scrolling is faster, momentum is free, and finger drag doesn't want a 6px rail. Collapse to native (`pointer: coarse`).
*   **The content should always be fully visible** — don't hide content behind a scroll to save space; use auto-height, or a **Disclosure/Accordion** to fold it intentionally, or **Pagination** to chunk it.
*   **You're tempted to fake scroll with** **`transform`** — that's not a scroll area, it's a bug. If you need windowed rendering for huge lists, use a **virtualization** library (react-window, TanStack Virtual) that keeps native overflow and only recycles rows.
*   **Users need to compare two long lists** — consider a split view with two independent scroll areas, not one giant scroll.

**Placement heuristics:** put the overflow boundary on the _smallest_ element that needs to clip, pad the inner wrapper (not the viewport), and never nest a custom scroll area inside another scrolling axis of the same direction — nested same-axis scroll is a usability trap (users can't tell which one they're driving).
* * *

## 8\. Across Design Systems

*   **Material (MDC / M3):** Material historically leans on native scrolling and thin overlay bars; its lists and menus scroll natively, and M3 guidance treats scrollbars as platform chrome rather than a styled component. Elevation and edge scrims signal overflow more than a designed bar.
*   **Apple HIG:** overlay scrollbars that appear on scroll and fade at rest are the platform default (`NSScroller`/`UIScrollView`). HIG explicitly discourages permanently-visible custom bars on macOS and wants momentum, rubber-banding, and the system setting ("Show scroll bars: automatically / always") respected.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** Windows historically ships thick, always-visible bars; Fluent's web components style them thin and themed but keep them discoverable, and WinUI's `ScrollViewer` exposes `ScrollBarVisibility` (Auto/Visible/Hidden) as a first-class property.
*   **Ant Design:** doesn't ship a heavy custom scrollbar for generic containers, but components like `Table` and `Select` set `scroll`/`listHeight` to bound the region and rely on native overflow with light styling. Virtualization is offered for large tables rather than fancier bars.
*   **Tailwind CSS:** ships utilities for the standard properties — `scrollbar-gutter`, `overscroll-*`, `scroll-smooth`, `snap-*` — and the community `tailwind-scrollbar` plugin adds `scrollbar-thin` / `scrollbar-thumb-*` mapping to `scrollbar-width`/`scrollbar-color` and the WebKit pseudo-elements.
*   **shadcn/ui + Radix:** `ScrollArea` is the reference custom-overlay implementation. Radix hides the native bar, renders `Viewport` + `Scrollbar` + `Thumb` primitives, syncs to native scroll position, and — critically — keeps the native scroll working underneath so keyboard/wheel/touch are preserved. This is the "do it right" template for the overlay variant.
*   **Bootstrap 5:** no dedicated scrollbar component; provides scrollable utilities (`.overflow-auto`, scrollable modal `.modal-dialog-scrollable`, scrollspy) and leaves the bar to the platform, styled with plain CSS if needed.

### 8b. What's Trending in 2026

*   **Standardized** **`scrollbar-width`** **/** **`scrollbar-color`** are now baseline across Chromium, Firefox, and Safari — the community is moving _off_ `::-webkit-scrollbar` hacks toward the standard properties, keeping the fully-custom overlay only for design systems that truly need it.
*   **CSS Scroll-Driven Animations** (`animation-timeline: scroll()` / `view()`) let scroll shadows, progress rails, and reveal effects run on the compositor with zero scroll listeners — the biggest shift in how scroll UI is built.
*   **`scrollbar-gutter: stable`** adoption is now the default hygiene move to kill layout shift, and design systems bake it into their reset.
*   **Overlay-first, native-underneath** is the settled consensus: even branded bars (Radix/OverlayScrollbars) keep the real overflow element doing the scrolling — the fake-transform era is over.

### 9.1 HTML (semantic foundation with ARIA)

```html
<!--
  A scrollable region MUST be reachable and driveable by keyboard.
  tabindex="0"  -> keyboard users can focus it and use arrows/PageUp/Down/Home/End/Space
  role="region" -> announced as a landmark-ish region (use when the content is meaningful)
  aria-label    -> gives that region an accessible name
-->
<div
  class="scroll-area"
  tabindex="0"
  role="region"
  aria-label="Chat messages"
>
  <div class="scroll-area__content">
    <!-- real, native, findable content — NOT transformed by JS -->
    <p>Message one…</p>
    <p>Message two…</p>
    <!-- …many more… -->
  </div>
</div>
```

If the region is not independently meaningful (e.g. a generic card body), drop `role="region"` but keep `tabindex="0"` and a label so it's still keyboard-scrollable without adding landmark noise.

### 9.2 CSS (native-styled, states, gutter, chaining, reduced-motion)

```css
.scroll-area {
  /* the element that actually scrolls */
  overflow: auto;
  max-height: 20rem;

  /* reserve the bar's lane so appearing/disappearing content never shifts layout */
  scrollbar-gutter: stable both-edges;

  /* stop the scroll from chaining to the page / triggering pull-to-refresh */
  overscroll-behavior: contain;

  /* standard, cross-browser scrollbar styling (Chrome 121+, Firefox, Safari 18.2+) */
  scrollbar-width: thin;                 /* auto | thin | none */
  scrollbar-color: var(--sa-thumb, #5f2c82) var(--sa-track, transparent);

  /* smooth programmatic scrolling — gated on motion preference below */
  scroll-behavior: smooth;

  border-radius: 0.5rem;
}

/* visible focus ring so keyboard users know THIS region takes arrow keys */
.scroll-area:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid var(--sa-focus, #dca424);
  outline-offset: 2px;
}

/* legacy / fine-grained WebKit control (progressive enhancement, not required) */
.scroll-area::-webkit-scrollbar { width: 8px; height: 8px; }
.scroll-area::-webkit-scrollbar-track { background: transparent; }
.scroll-area::-webkit-scrollbar-thumb {
  background: var(--sa-thumb, #5f2c82);
  border-radius: 999px;
  min-height: 40px;                      /* never let the thumb collapse */
}
.scroll-area::-webkit-scrollbar-thumb:hover { background: var(--sa-thumb-hover, #7a3ca6); }
.scroll-area::-webkit-scrollbar-corner { background: transparent; }

/* touch: don't fight native momentum with a tiny rail */
@media (pointer: coarse) {
  .scroll-area { scrollbar-width: auto; }
  .scroll-area::-webkit-scrollbar { display: none; }
}

/* respect users who don't want motion */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .scroll-area { scroll-behavior: auto; }
}

/* CSS-only scroll shadows (Lea Verou technique): fixed edge gradients + local-attachment
   masking gradients that hide when you're at an edge. No JS, no scroll listeners. */
.scroll-area--shadows {
  background:
    linear-gradient(var(--sa-bg, #0a0a0a) 30%, rgba(10,10,10,0)) center top,
    linear-gradient(rgba(10,10,10,0), var(--sa-bg, #0a0a0a) 70%) center bottom,
    radial-gradient(farthest-side at 50% 0, rgba(0,0,0,.4), transparent) center top,
    radial-gradient(farthest-side at 50% 100%, rgba(0,0,0,.4), transparent) center bottom;
  background-repeat: no-repeat;
  background-size: 100% 40px, 100% 40px, 100% 14px, 100% 14px;
  background-attachment: local, local, scroll, scroll;
}
```

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (native-styled, accessible, forwardRef)

```tsx
import { forwardRef, HTMLAttributes } from "react";

type Orientation = "vertical" | "horizontal" | "both";

interface ScrollAreaProps extends HTMLAttributes<HTMLDivElement> {
  /** Accessible name — required when the region is meaningful. */
  label: string;
  orientation?: Orientation;
  /** Render as an ARIA region landmark. Omit for generic containers. */
  asRegion?: boolean;
  shadows?: boolean;
}

const overflowFor: Record<Orientation, React.CSSProperties> = {
  vertical:   { overflowY: "auto", overflowX: "hidden" },
  horizontal: { overflowX: "auto", overflowY: "hidden" },
  both:       { overflow: "auto" },
};

export const ScrollArea = forwardRef<HTMLDivElement, ScrollAreaProps>(
  ({ label, orientation = "vertical", asRegion = true, shadows = false,
     className = "", children, style, ...rest }, ref) => (
    <div
      ref={ref}
      tabIndex={0}
      role={asRegion ? "region" : undefined}
      aria-label={label}
      className={`scroll-area${shadows ? " scroll-area--shadows" : ""} ${className}`}
      style={{
        ...overflowFor[orientation],
        scrollbarGutter: "stable both-edges",
        overscrollBehavior: "contain",
        ...style,
      }}
      {...rest}
    >
      <div className="scroll-area__content">{children}</div>
    </div>
  )
);
ScrollArea.displayName = "ScrollArea";

// Usage:
// <ScrollArea label="Chat messages" style={{ maxHeight: "20rem" }} shadows>
//   {messages.map((m) => <Message key={m.id} {...m} />)}
// </ScrollArea>
```

### 9.4 Vanilla JavaScript (custom overlay thumb, native scroll preserved)

```js
// Draws a custom thumb ON TOP of a natively-scrolling element.
// The element still scrolls with wheel/keyboard/touch — we only sync a visual bar.
export function enhanceScrollArea(viewport) {
  const thumb = document.createElement("div");
  thumb.className = "sa-thumb";
  thumb.setAttribute("aria-hidden", "true"); // decorative; native scroll is the real control
  viewport.after(thumb);

  const update = () => {
    const { scrollTop, scrollHeight, clientHeight } = viewport;
    const overflowing = scrollHeight > clientHeight + 1;
    thumb.style.display = overflowing ? "block" : "none";
    if (!overflowing) return;
    const ratio = clientHeight / scrollHeight;
    const thumbH = Math.max(40, clientHeight * ratio);          // clamp min length
    const maxTop = clientHeight - thumbH;
    const top = (scrollTop / (scrollHeight - clientHeight)) * maxTop;
    thumb.style.height = `${thumbH}px`;
    thumb.style.transform = `translateY(${top}px)`;
    viewport.dataset.atStart = String(scrollTop <= 0);
    viewport.dataset.atEnd = String(scrollTop + clientHeight >= scrollHeight - 1);
  };

  // drag the thumb -> drive NATIVE scrollTop (never move content manually)
  let dragging = false, startY = 0, startScroll = 0;
  thumb.addEventListener("pointerdown", (e) => {
    dragging = true; startY = e.clientY; startScroll = viewport.scrollTop;
    thumb.setPointerCapture(e.pointerId);
    e.preventDefault();
  });
  thumb.addEventListener("pointermove", (e) => {
    if (!dragging) return;
    const { scrollHeight, clientHeight } = viewport;
    const trackTravel = clientHeight - Math.max(40, clientHeight * (clientHeight / scrollHeight));
    const scrollable = scrollHeight - clientHeight;
    viewport.scrollTop = startScroll + ((e.clientY - startY) / trackTravel) * scrollable;
  });
  thumb.addEventListener("pointerup", (e) => {
    dragging = false; thumb.releasePointerCapture(e.pointerId);
  });

  viewport.addEventListener("scroll", update, { passive: true });
  const ro = new ResizeObserver(update); ro.observe(viewport);
  const mo = new MutationObserver(update);
  mo.observe(viewport, { childList: true, subtree: true, characterData: true });
  update();

  return () => { ro.disconnect(); mo.disconnect(); viewport.removeEventListener("scroll", update); thumb.remove(); };
}

document.querySelectorAll("[data-scroll-area]").forEach(enhanceScrollArea);
```

// app/(dash)/messages/page.tsx  — a Server Component. Native scroll needs no JS at all.
import { ScrollArea } from "@/components/scroll-area"; // the RSC-safe version below

async function getMessages() {
  const res = await fetch("https://api.internal/messages", { next: { revalidate: 30 } });
  if (!res.ok) throw new Error(`Failed to load messages: ${res.status}`);
  return (await res.json()) as { id: string; body: string }[];

export default async function MessagesPage() {

          <li key={m.id} className="border-b border-white/10 p-3">{m.body}</li>
        ))}
      </ul>
    </ScrollArea>
  );
}

## 10\. Accessibility

Standalone and non-negotiable. A custom scrollbar that breaks any of the below is not shippable.

**Roles & ARIA**

*   A scrollable region that holds meaningful content should be a focusable `role="region"` with `aria-label` (or `aria-labelledby`). Omit `role="region"` for generic/decorative containers to avoid landmark spam, but **keep** `tabindex="0"` + a label so it's still keyboard-scrollable.
*   The custom thumb/track are decorative — mark them `aria-hidden="true"`. They are not the accessible control; the native overflow is.
*   Never put `role="scrollbar"` on a fake div unless you fully implement the ARIA scrollbar pattern (`aria-controls`, `aria-valuenow/min/max`, `aria-orientation`, and arrow-key handling). Almost nobody needs this — the native scroll is already accessible.
*   Do not set `aria-hidden` or `inert` on the viewport or you'll hide the content from assistive tech.

**Keyboard map (container focused)**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| Tab / Shift+Tab | Move focus into / out of the region (container is `tabindex="0"`) |
| Arrow Up / Down | Scroll vertically by a line |
| Arrow Left / Right | Scroll horizontally by a line (when horizontal overflow exists) |
| PageUp / PageDown | Scroll by roughly one viewport height |
| Space / Shift+Space | Scroll down / up by a page (when no focusable control consumes it) |
| Home / End | Jump to top / bottom (vertical) |
| — | All of the above are native when `tabindex="0"` is set. Your job is to not break them — don't `preventDefault` wheel/keydown |

**Focus management**

*   Focus is never trapped inside the scroll area. Tab must be able to leave. If focus can enter but not leave, that's a keyboard trap (WCAG 2.1.2) — a hard failure.
*   When content scrolls a focused child out of view, the browser auto-scrolls it back on focus — don't fight `scroll-into-view` with your own listeners.
*   The container's `:focus-visible` ring is mandatory for the overlay variant; it's the only signal that arrow keys drive _this_ region.

**Contrast & target size**

*   The thumb must meet **3:1** non-text contrast (WCAG 1.4.11) against its track/background so it's perceivable — a pale-gray-on-white thumb fails.
*   Where the thumb is the _primary_ way to scroll, honor the **44×44px** target (or at least a generous hit area via transparent padding). Where wheel/keyboard/touch are available, a thin bar is acceptable because it isn't the only path.

**Reduced motion**

*   Gate `scroll-behavior: smooth` behind `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` (or reset to `auto` under `reduce`). Smooth programmatic scroll can trigger vestibular discomfort.
*   Any scroll-driven animation (progress rails, reveals) must have a reduced-motion fallback.

**Common failures specific to this component**

*   `div` scrolls but has no `tabindex` → keyboard users can't reach or scroll it (2.1.1).
*   Focusable region with no accessible name → screen reader announces "region" with no idea what it contains.
*   `outline: none` on the viewport with no replacement → keyboard users can't tell it's focused.
*   Listening to `wheel`/`keydown` and calling `preventDefault` to "improve" scrolling → destroys native keyboard/wheel behavior.
*   Auto-hide bar with no scroll shadows → sighted users never learn there's more content (1.4.10 spirit).
*   Missing `overscroll-behavior: contain` in modals → scroll chains to the page and yanks the user out of context.

## 11\. Innovative / Emerging Ideas

*   **Scroll-driven animations (2026-era, no JS):** `animation-timeline: scroll(self block)` drives scroll shadows, edge fades, and a top progress rail entirely on the compositor — no `scroll` listeners, no jank. `view()` timelines can fade content in as it enters the viewport.
*   **`scrollbar-gutter: stable both-edges`** **as default hygiene:** ship it in your CSS reset so _every_ bounded container reserves its lane and never causes CLS. It's cheap and eliminates a whole class of layout jump.
*   **CSS** **`mask-image`** **edge fades over overlay shadows:** using `mask-image: linear-gradient(...)` at the edges literally fades content into the boundary, which reads cleaner on textured/photographic backgrounds than a stacked gradient shadow.
*   **Container-query-aware density:** with `@container`, the same scroll area can switch from thin overlay bars in a narrow sidebar to a comfortable always-visible bar when placed in a wide panel — the component adapts to its slot, not the viewport.
*   **`scroll-marker`****/****`::scroll-button`** **(CSS carousel primitives):** the emerging CSS Overflow scroll-marker and scroll-button pseudo-elements let horizontal scrollers grow native prev/next affordances and position dots without JS — promising for the horizontal variant's discoverability problem.
* * *

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers

*   **Auto-hide bar that's invisible until you already know to scroll:** users don't discover the overflow, miss content and CTAs below the fold, and bounce. _Fix:_ add scroll shadows/fades and reveal the bar on focus, not just hover.
*   **Layout shift when the bar appears:** content loads, a scrollbar pops in, everything jumps left, and the user mis-clicks. _Fix:_ `scrollbar-gutter: stable` (or `both-edges`) reserves the lane up front.
*   **Scroll chaining out of a modal:** user scrolls a modal's inner list to the bottom, keeps going, and the page behind scrolls or pull-to-refresh fires — they lose their place entirely. _Fix:_ `overscroll-behavior: contain` on the inner scroll area.
*   **Horizontal scroller with no affordance:** a row of chips or cards overflows sideways with no shadow, no arrows, no partial-peek of the next item — most users never realize there's more. _Fix:_ leave the next item half-visible, add edge fades, and support Shift+Wheel.
*   **Nested same-axis scroll:** a vertical scroll area inside another vertical scroll area — users can't tell which one their wheel is driving, and the inner one "eats" the gesture unexpectedly. _Fix:_ don't nest same-axis; if unavoidable, use clear visual boundaries and `overscroll-behavior`.
*   **Thin bar as the only scroll path on desktop with no wheel/keyboard support:** a 4px un-grabbable rail and nothing else works. _Fix:_ never remove native overflow; the bar is a secondary affordance, wheel/keyboard/touch are primary.
*   **Removed focus ring on the region:** keyboard users can't tell the scroll area is focused, so they don't know arrows will scroll it. _Fix:_ keep a visible `:focus-visible` outline.
* * *

## 13\. Advanced Patterns

*   **TypeScript-enforced accessible naming (discriminated union):** make it impossible to render a nameless region. A region _must_ have a label; a decorative container must _not_ claim the role.

```ts
type ScrollAreaProps =
  | { asRegion: true; label: string }              // region REQUIRES a name
  | { asRegion?: false; label?: string };          // decorative: name optional
// Consumers can't compile <ScrollArea asRegion /> without `label`.
```

*   **Design-token tiers for the scrollbar:** expose the bar as CSS custom properties layered from primitive → semantic → component, so theming (UJG Goldenrod-on-Eminence, or a client's palette) is a token swap, not a rewrite.

```css
:root { --color-brand-600: #5f2c82; --color-accent-500: #dca424; } /* primitive */
.scroll-area {
  --sa-thumb: var(--color-brand-600);   /* semantic */
  --sa-focus: var(--color-accent-500);
  --sa-bar-w: 0.5rem;                    /* component */
  scrollbar-color: var(--sa-thumb) transparent;
}
```

*   **Sync-on-scroll without listeners (scroll-driven timeline):** drive a progress indicator or shadow opacity from `animation-timeline: scroll(self)` so the "how far am I" state is compositor-owned and never triggers a layout/paint on the main thread.
*   **Windowing under native overflow:** for 10k-row lists, keep `overflow: auto` and native scrolling but virtualize the _rows_ (TanStack Virtual / react-window). The scrollbar reflects the _full_ `scrollHeight` via a spacer, so the native bar stays honest while only visible rows render.
*   **Programmatic scroll with focus safety:** `element.scrollTo({ top, behavior })` respecting reduced-motion, plus moving focus to the newly-revealed target so keyboard users land where the scroll took them (e.g. "jump to latest message").
* * *

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost

*   **Prefer CSS-only over JS overlay:** `scrollbar-width`/`scrollbar-color` and CSS scroll shadows cost zero JS and zero scroll listeners. A JS overlay adds a `scroll` handler, `ResizeObserver`, `MutationObserver`, and reflow reads (`scrollHeight`) — only pay that when the design truly needs a custom bar.
*   **Composite-only animation:** animate the thumb and shadows with `transform` and `opacity` only — never `top`/`height`/`width`, which trigger layout on every scroll frame. `will-change: transform` on the thumb during drag keeps it on the compositor.
*   **Passive scroll listeners + batched reads:** if you must listen, use `{ passive: true }` and read layout metrics once per frame (rAF-throttle), never per event, to avoid layout thrash and scroll-blocking jank.
*   **Virtualize large content:** don't render 10,000 DOM nodes inside a scroll area — window the rows. Fewer nodes means cheaper scroll, cheaper `MutationObserver`, and less memory. Lazy-load images inside the area with `loading="lazy"`.
*   **Tree-shaking / import discipline:** import only the primitives you use (`import { Root, Viewport } from "@radix-ui/react-scroll-area"`), and skip the JS library entirely for the native-styled variant. The lightest scroll area ships _no_ component JS at all.
* * *

## 15\. Security

The scroll area is a layout container with a genuinely tiny attack surface. It renders whatever children it's given — any XSS risk belongs to that **content**, not to the container, so escape/sanitize user-generated content where it's produced (see the Jinja2 `|e` and React's default escaping above), not here. The one container-specific caveat: if a scroll area is used as an **overlay** on top of other UI, apply the same clickjacking hygiene as any overlay (don't let a transparent scroll layer sit over actionable controls it can intercept), and if it fetches paginated content from an endpoint, that endpoint does its own authz and input validation — the scroll area just displays the result.
* * *

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist

Ship-ready gate — every item non-negotiable:

*   **Semantics:** the scrolling element is a real overflow container; native overflow is never replaced by transform hacks.
*   **Keyboard:** container is `tabindex="0"`; Arrow/PageUp/PageDown/Home/End/Space all scroll it, and nothing `preventDefault`s wheel/keydown.
*   **Accessible name:** meaningful regions have `role="region"` + `aria-label`/`aria-labelledby`; decorative ones drop the role but keep tabindex + a label.
*   **Focus:** visible `:focus-visible` ring on the viewport; focus is never trapped (Tab can leave).
*   **Contrast:** thumb meets 3:1 non-text contrast against its track/background.
*   **Target size:** where the thumb is the primary scroll control, it honors ~44px (or a padded hit area); thin bars only where wheel/keyboard/touch exist.
*   **Layout stability:** `scrollbar-gutter: stable` set so appearing/disappearing bars don't cause CLS.
*   **Chaining:** `overscroll-behavior: contain` (or `none`) on modal/nested scroll areas.
*   **States:** at-start/at-end edges, overflow vs no-overflow, and idle-vs-scrolling all handled; shadows/fades reflect real edge state.
*   **Reduced motion:** `scroll-behavior: smooth` and any scroll-driven animation gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Touch:** collapses to native overlay scrolling on `pointer: coarse`; momentum preserved.
*   **Performance:** no per-event layout reads; thumb/shadows animate on `transform`/`opacity`; large content virtualized.

## 17\. Visual Styles

The same accessible scroll area, eleven skins. In every one the viewport is `tabindex="0"` with a name, native scroll is intact, and the focus ring survives — only the paint changes.

1. **Flat:** a 8px solid thumb (single brand color, no shadow) on a transparent track, fully rounded ends; track only appears on hover. Clean, no ornament.
2. **Material:** thin overlay thumb that fades in on scroll and out at rest, riding on the container's elevation; edge scrims (not a heavy bar) signal overflow, matching M3's platform-chrome stance.
3. **Glassmorphism:** a frosted, semi-transparent thumb with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over the content, a hairline light border, and soft edge fades — the bar looks like etched glass sliding over the panel.
4. **Liquid Glass (2026 — Apple iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe):** the thumb refracts and specular-highlights the content beneath it, thickening slightly on grab and settling with a fluid ease; the track is nearly invisible until touched, echoing the auto-hiding system bar.
5. **Neumorphism:** the track is an inset groove pressed into a same-color surface (dual inner shadows), and the thumb a soft extruded pill of the same hue — tactile, but watch the 3:1 contrast or it disappears.
6. **Skeuomorphism:** a beveled, gradient-filled thumb with a subtle grip texture in the middle and a recessed track with an inner drop-shadow — a literal nod to a physical slider rail.
7. **Neo-Brutalism:** a chunky 12px thumb with a hard 2px black border and a solid offset drop-shadow, square-ish corners, high-contrast Spanish-Orange fill on a stark track — the bar is loud on purpose.
8. **Claymorphism:** a puffy, rounded thumb with soft dual shadows (inner light, outer soft) in a candy brand tone, floating slightly proud of an equally soft track — squishy and friendly.
9. **Aurora / Gradient:** the thumb carries a Goldenrod→Eminence gradient that shifts subtly as it travels; edge fades bleed the same gradient into the boundaries for a glowing overflow cue.
10. **Minimal / Swiss:** a 4–6px hairline thumb in a single neutral, no track, generous gutter, precise edge fades — the bar recedes almost entirely and lets the content grid speak.
11. **UJG Brand (house default):** Goldenrod (`#DCA424`) thumb on a near-transparent Eminence (`#5F2C82`) track over Night (`#0A0A0A`), fully rounded, auto-hide with mandatory scroll shadows, and a Goldenrod `:focus-visible` ring — warm, discoverable, on-brand.

**The rule that never changes:** style is skin, behavior is the skeleton. Across all eleven, the viewport is focusable and named, native wheel/keyboard/touch scrolling is untouched, the focus ring is visible, `overscroll-behavior` contains the chain, and reduced-motion is respected. Change the paint all you want — never the scrolling.
