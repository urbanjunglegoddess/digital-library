# Pricing Table

# Pricing Table

A tiered plan-comparison component: two or more priced offers shown side by side, each with its own feature list and call-to-action, usually topped by a monthly/annual billing toggle and one visually promoted "most popular" tier. It is the single highest-intent surface on most SaaS and service sites — the place where interest converts to revenue — so its semantics, honesty, and accessibility carry real money and real legal weight.
* * *

## 1\. What a Pricing Table Actually Is

A pricing table is a **decision instrument disguised as a comparison chart**. It presents 2–5 purchasable tiers so a visitor can (a) understand what each costs, (b) see how the tiers differ feature-by-feature, and (c) act — start a trial, buy, or contact sales — without leaving the page. Under the visuals it is either a set of semantic **cards** (one `<article>` or list item per tier) or a real **`<table>`** with plans as columns and features as rows. The right markup depends on whether the primary job is "pick a plan" (cards) or "compare features across plans in detail" (table). Most modern pricing pages use cards for the headline tiers and, below or inside them, a full feature-comparison that genuinely benefits from `<table>` semantics.

**The things people confuse it with, named and distinguished:**

*   **A data table.** A data table is read, sorted, and scanned for facts; a pricing table persuades and is acted on. Data tables rarely have a per-row CTA or a "recommended" row shouting for attention. If nobody buys anything, it's a data table.
*   **A feature-comparison matrix.** The matrix (features × products) is one _part_ of a rich pricing table, or a standalone below it — no prices, no CTAs. It answers "what's the difference," not "what should I pay." A pricing table wraps the matrix in price + action.
*   **A plan selector / radio group.** In checkout/upgrade the tiers become a single-select `role="radiogroup"` that feeds an order — a _form control_ with a submit. A marketing pricing table is navigation-and-persuasion (links to signup), not a bound field. Same visuals, different roles and keyboard model.
*   **A checkout / order summary.** The summary shows the _one_ plan already chosen plus tax and total; the pricing table shows _all_ options before choice. Confuse them and your table behaves like the sale is already made.

**Rule of thumb:** If the surface shows **multiple prices the user is choosing between**, and each option has **its own action**, it's a pricing table. If it shows **one price the user has already committed to**, it's a summary. If it shows differences but **no price and no action**, it's a comparison matrix. If it's **a bound single-select that submits**, it's a plan selector (radiogroup) — build it as a form, not as marketing cards.
* * *

## 2\. Why It's So Important

*   **Conversion — this is the money page.** The highest-intent traffic lands here. Tier ordering, the visual weight of the promoted plan, anchor pricing, the default billing period, and CTA copy each move purchase rate measurably. Three tiers with one confident recommendation and action-verb CTAs ("Start free trial") beat five equally-weighted tiers with "Learn more." Every point of friction is amplified because the user arrived _ready to spend_.
*   **Trust — pricing is where credibility is won or lost.** Buyers arrive primed to suspect a trick. Hidden fees at checkout, a toggle silently defaulting to the pricier option, "was $99, now $49" anchors that were never real, decoy tiers rigged to flatter one option — all detectable, and once detected they poison the relationship. Honest, legible pricing is a competitive advantage precisely because so many tables cheat.
*   **Accessibility — a long feature grid is a screen-reader minefield.** Sighted users scan by row/column position in seconds. A screen-reader user gets a linear stream of cells; without `<caption>`, `<th scope>`, and header association, "checkmark, checkmark, dash, $49" is meaningless. Icon-only "included/not included" cells with no text alternative are invisible to AT. Get header association wrong and the comparison is unusable for a whole class of buyers.
*   **Legal / regulatory — price display is regulated.** Depending on jurisdiction you may be _required_ to show tax-inclusive prices (EU/UK consumer law), disclose currency, state billing frequency, and avoid deceptive "reference price" claims (FTC, EU UCPD, ASA). Drip pricing and false urgency are increasingly enforced. The pricing table is the artifact regulators screenshot.
*   **Correctness / financial integrity.** The number shown must match the number charged. A price from stale cache, wrong currency, or a client-tampered value the server then trusts isn't just a UX bug — it's a chargeback, a support ticket, potentially fraud. Prices are data with money attached.

## 3\. Anatomy of a Pricing Table

Every named part, and what it does. A full pricing table is a small system, not one element.

*   **Container / region.** The wrapping `<section aria-labelledby>` landmark — lets AT find and name the whole thing.
*   **Billing-period toggle (segmented control).** The monthly/annual switch above the tiers: a two-option `role="radiogroup"` or `<button role="switch">` that updates every visible price and _announces the change_. Usually carries the "save 20%" incentive.
*   **Tier card / column.** One per plan — a semantic `<article>`/`<li>` in card layout, or a `<th scope="col">` column plus its data cells in table layout.
*   **Tier header.** Plan name (Starter / Pro / Enterprise) as the tier's accessible name, plus an optional positioning tagline.
*   **Price display.** Currency symbol/code + amount + period suffix ("/user/mo"), sometimes a struck anchor and the discounted number. The single most-scrutinized element on the page.
*   **Billing-frequency / fine print.** "billed annually," "per seat," "+ tax," "USD" — the qualifiers that keep the headline number honest, and the exact spot dark patterns hide.
*   **"Most popular" / recommended badge.** The visual flag (badge, border, elevation, scale) on the promoted tier — must be announced to AT, not conveyed by color/position alone.
*   **Feature list / comparison rows.** The differentiators: a card's included/excluded list, or a matrix's rows with a value cell per plan.
*   **Feature value cell.** A feature × plan intersection — check, dash/X, number ("10 seats"), or word ("Unlimited"). Icon-only cells MUST have a text alternative.
*   **Feature-group header.** Section rows chunking a long grid ("Collaboration," "Security," "Support") so 40 features don't read as one wall.
*   **Row header (feature name).** The `<th scope="row">` — the anchor that lets AT say "SSO: Pro — included."
*   **Column header (plan name).** The `<th scope="col">` — repeated header association for every value cell.
*   **Caption.** The `<caption>` ("Feature comparison across Starter, Pro, and Enterprise plans") — the first thing AT announces, orienting the user before cells stream by.
*   **Per-tier CTA.** Each plan's action link/button. Primary emphasis on the recommended tier; each label stands alone ("Start Pro trial," not "Choose").
*   **Savings / annual callout.** The "Save $120/yr" / "2 months free" incentive tied to the annual toggle.
*   **Footnote / disclaimer.** Tax, currency, seat minimums, fair-use, cancellation — legally load-bearing, so real text, not an image.
*   **Sticky header (long tables).** On tall matrices, the plan-name row pinned to the top so the user never loses which column they're reading.

## 4\. Sizes / Scale / Density

Pricing tables scale along two axes: **card width** (how many tiers fit side by side before wrapping) and **row density** (how tight the comparison grid is). Below, real tokens and pixel values.

| Density | Card min-width | Card gap | Row height (matrix) | Feature font | Price font | Use case |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact | `--pt-card-w-sm` = 240px | `--pt-gap-sm` = 12px | 40px | 13px / `--fs-xs` | 28px / `--fs-2xl` | 4–5 tiers, dense enterprise matrix, dashboards |
| Default | `--pt-card-w-md` = 288px | `--pt-gap-md` = 20px | 48px | 15px / `--fs-sm` | 40px / `--fs-4xl` | 3-tier SaaS marketing page (the norm) |
| Comfortable | `--pt-card-w-lg` = 340px | `--pt-gap-lg` = 32px | 56px | 16px / `--fs-base` | 52px / `--fs-6xl` | 2–3 premium tiers, big hero price, lots of whitespace |

**Rules that matter:**

*   **Target size:** every CTA and the toggle segments must be ≥ 44×44px, regardless of density. Compact density shrinks _type and rows_, never touch targets.
*   **Card width band:** keep cards in the 240–340px band. Below ~240px, prices and CTAs crowd; above ~360px, three tiers overflow common laptop widths and force horizontal scroll.
*   **Responsive collapse:** the 3-column grid holds until roughly 720px, drops to 2-up around 480–720px, and stacks to a single column below ~480px. The recommended tier should reorder to _first_ when stacked (`order: -1`) so the promoted option leads on mobile.
*   **Matrix → cards collapse:** a wide comparison `<table>` cannot stay a table on a 375px phone. Either allow horizontal scroll with a **pinned first column** (feature names) and `scope`\-associated headers preserved, or transform to stacked per-plan cards where each value re-states its feature name (so the row/column association isn't lost visually). Never let cells float free of their feature label.
*   **Spacing tokens:** internal card padding scales `--pt-pad` 16→24→32px across the tiers; the price-to-CTA vertical rhythm uses one shared `--pt-stack` token so every card's CTA sits at the same baseline.

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)

Every state, described — not just named.

*   **Default.** Resting tier: neutral border, base surface, price for the selected billing period.
*   **Hover (tier).** Slight elevation (`translateY(-4px)` + shadow) signalling attention. Purely decorative — never the only way to reveal a price or CTA.
*   **Focus-visible.** A clear `:focus-visible` ring on _interactive_ children (toggle segments, CTAs, tooltips). The card itself isn't focusable unless the whole card is a link.
*   **Highlighted / Recommended.** The promoted tier: brand border, elevation, scale (`1.04`), badge — and carried in the accessible name (heading includes "Most popular") so it isn't purely visual.
*   **Active / pressed (CTA).** Momentary depression on the tier's button — standard button active state.
*   **Toggle: monthly / annual selected.** The segmented control's two states; active segment `aria-checked="true"`. Switching updates all prices and fires a polite live-region announcement.
*   **Price loading.** During fetch (currency/geo, live plans) each price cell shows a skeleton and the region is `aria-busy="true"` so AT doesn't read half-loaded numbers.
*   **Price error.** On fetch failure, a real `role="alert"` fallback ("Pricing temporarily unavailable — contact sales"), never a silent blank or `$NaN`.
*   **Disabled / unavailable tier.** Sold-out, legacy, or region-blocked: dimmed, CTA `aria-disabled` with a reason ("Not available in your region"), never a dead grey button.
*   **Current-plan (in-app upgrade).** For a logged-in user their existing plan is marked "Your plan," CTA relabeled "Current plan" — announced, not just greyed.
*   **Expanded feature group.** If groups collapse, the group header is a `<button aria-expanded>` toggling its rows.
*   **Sticky-header engaged.** On scroll past a long matrix's header, the pinned plan-name row takes an elevated/compact treatment so it reads as an overlay, not a duplicate.

## 6\. Types & Variants

*   **Card-per-tier (the default).** 2–5 independent cards side by side, each self-contained with its own feature list and CTA. Best for marketing pages and ≤ ~8 differentiating features per tier. Semantic list of `<article>`s.
*   **Comparison matrix (features × plans).** A real `<table>`: rows are features, columns are plans, cells are values. Best when tiers differ across many features (15+) and buyers genuinely cross-reference. Needs caption, `scope`, and often a sticky header.
*   **Hybrid (cards + full matrix below).** The most common serious pattern: headline cards with the top 5–6 features for fast scanning, then an expandable full `<table>` for the detail-oriented buyer. Best of both; more to build.
*   **Toggle-driven (monthly/annual, or currency/region).** Any of the above with a segmented control that recomputes all prices client-side (or refetches). The toggle _is_ the interactive spine.
*   **Single-card + slider / quantity.** One card whose price scales with a usage slider or seat-count stepper ("$8 × 12 seats = $96/mo"). Best for pure usage-based or seat-based pricing where discrete tiers don't fit.
*   **Tooltip-augmented.** Feature names carry info affordances (`<button>` with popover) explaining jargon ("SSO," "SOC 2"). Best for technical products; the tooltip must be keyboard-reachable and dismissible.
*   **Collapsible feature groups.** Long matrices chunked into expandable sections (Collaboration / Security / Support). Best for enterprise tables with 40+ features.
*   **Enterprise "Contact Sales" hybrid.** Concrete prices for self-serve tiers plus a final "Custom / Let's talk" tier with no number and a contact CTA. Best when the top tier is genuinely quote-based — don't fake a number.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)

**Use a pricing table when:**

*   You have **2–5 distinct, comparable tiers** a buyer chooses between (SaaS plans, service packages, memberships, event ticket levels).
*   The differences between tiers are **material and legible** — features, limits, or support levels a buyer can weigh.
*   The page's job is **conversion**: the user is here to decide and act.

**Don't use one — use something else — when:**

*   **There's one price.** A single product at one price needs a **price + buy button**, not a table. A table implies a choice that doesn't exist.
*   **Pricing is fully custom/quote-based.** If everything is "it depends," use a **lead-capture form / "Contact Sales" CTA**, not a fake tier. Don't invent decoy numbers.
*   **There are more than ~5 tiers.** Too many options cause choice paralysis. Consolidate to 3–4, or use a **usage slider / plan configurator** instead of a wall of columns.
*   **The user has already chosen.** In checkout, show an **order summary**, not the full comparison again.
*   **It's a pure feature spec with no purchase.** That's a **comparison matrix / spec table** — a data table, governed by data-table rules, no CTAs.
*   **The choice is a bound form field** (upgrade flow). Use a **radiogroup plan selector** that submits, not marketing cards.

**Placement heuristics:** the recommended tier goes in the **visual center** of a 3-card row (center-stage bias) or clearly promoted in a matrix. Order tiers **low-to-high** left-to-right (or high-to-low if anchoring high intentionally — and honestly). Put the billing toggle **above and centered**, immediately visible before any price is read. Keep the primary CTA **above the fold** for the headline tiers.

## 8\. Pricing Tables Across Design Systems

Pricing tables are rarely a single primitive; every system composes them from Card, Button, Table, and a Segmented Control / Toggle. How each treats the pieces:

*   **Material (MDC / Material 3).** Composes from `Card` (elevated/filled/outlined) + `Button` + `SegmentedButton` for the billing toggle. Material 3's segmented button is the canonical monthly/annual control. Elevation tokens carry the "recommended" emphasis; the promoted card steps up an elevation level and uses the primary container color. No dedicated "pricing table" component — you assemble it.
*   **Apple HIG.** No pricing-table component; Apple ships pricing via `Product`/StoreKit and, on the web, plain semantic layout. HIG guidance is about _clarity and honesty_: show the real recurring price and period prominently, disclose terms, and use a segmented control (`UISegmentedControl` / SwiftUI `Picker(.segmented)`) for billing period. Liquid Glass (iOS 26) now skins the promoted tier's badge and CTA.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft).** Composed from `Card`, `Button`, and `SegmentedControl`/`TabList`. Fluent's data-grid handles the matrix variant with real column/row semantics. Emphasis via `appearance="filled-brand"` on the recommended CTA; neutral layers elsewhere. Strong tokens for currency/number formatting via `Intl` integration in their samples.
*   **Ant Design.** No first-class pricing table, but `Card`, `Segmented` (their exact monthly/annual control), `Table`, and `Statistic` (for the big price number) combine into one. `Segmented` is notable — it's a purpose-built segmented control with proper keyboard support out of the box. Ant's `Table` gives you `fixed` columns for the sticky first column on the matrix variant.
*   **Tailwind CSS (+ Tailwind UI / Catalyst).** Tailwind UI ships several _marketing/pricing_ section blocks — 3-tier cards with a ring-2 highlighted tier, a headless toggle, and `sr-only` labels baked in. Utility-first: the recommended ring, scale, and badge are all utility classes; the toggle is usually a Headless UI `RadioGroup` or `Switch`. The reference for "modern SaaS pricing look."
*   **shadcn/ui + Radix.** No packaged pricing table, but the ingredients are exact: `Card`, `Button`, `Badge`, and Radix `ToggleGroup` (`type="single"`) or `Tabs` for the billing toggle — Radix gives you the roving-tabindex, `aria-checked`, arrow-key segmented control for free. The community "pricing" blocks compose these. This is the most accessible off-the-shelf route on the web.
*   **Bootstrap 5.** Ships an actual **Pricing example** in its docs: `card-deck`/grid of `.card`s, a `.btn-toggle` or `.btn-group` for billing, and `.border-primary`/`.shadow` on the featured card. The classic, framework-light approach; the matrix variant uses a plain `.table` with `<th scope>`.

### 8b. What's Trending in 2026

*   **Interactive, calculator-first pricing.** Static tables are giving way to usage sliders and seat steppers that recompute a live estimate — "drag to your team size." The table becomes a configurator.
*   **AI-assisted "which plan fits me."** An inline agentic affordance ("Answer 3 questions, we'll recommend a tier") that highlights the matching column — persuasion moving from static badge to personalized recommendation.
*   **Liquid Glass promoted tiers.** On Apple platforms and web approximations, the "most popular" card and its badge get the refractive Liquid Glass treatment (iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe) — a translucent, light-bending highlight instead of a flat brand border.
*   **Transparency-forward design (anti-dark-pattern).** In response to EU/UK/FTC enforcement, "honest pricing" is itself a trend: tax-inclusive toggles, real (not fake) anchors, all-in totals, and prominent "no hidden fees" affordances as a differentiator.
*   **Localized, tax-aware price rendering by default.** Geo-detected currency and VAT-inclusive display computed server-side, with a visible currency switcher — expected, not optional, for global SaaS.
*   **Motion-restrained highlighting.** The recommended tier's emphasis animates in subtly (spring scale, sheen) but respects `prefers-reduced-motion` — no more aggressively pulsing "BEST DEAL" badges.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript — a reusable, typed pricing table

```tsx
import { useId, useState, useCallback } from "react";

type Billing = "monthly" | "annual";

interface Feature {
  label: string;
  included: boolean | string; // true, false, or a value like "10 GB"
}

interface Tier {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  priceMonthly: number | null; // null => "Custom"
  priceAnnual: number | null;
  currency: string;
  features: Feature[];
  cta: { label: string; href: string };
  featured?: boolean;
}

interface PricingTableProps {
  tiers: Tier[];
  defaultBilling?: Billing;
  locale?: string;
}

function formatPrice(value: number | null, currency: string, locale: string) {
  if (value === null) return "Custom";
  return new Intl.NumberFormat(locale, {
    style: "currency", currency, maximumFractionDigits: 0,
  }).format(value);
}

export function PricingTable({ tiers, defaultBilling = "monthly", locale = "en-US" }: PricingTableProps) {
  const [billing, setBilling] = useState<Billing>(defaultBilling);
  const titleId = useId();
  const statusId = useId();
  const [status, setStatus] = useState("");

  const onBillingChange = useCallback((next: Billing) => {
    setBilling(next);
    setStatus(next === "annual" ? "Prices now shown billed annually." : "Prices now shown billed monthly.");
  }, []);

  return (
    <section aria-labelledby={titleId} className="pricing">
      <h2 id={titleId}>Plans & pricing</h2>

      <fieldset className="billing-toggle">
        <legend className="sr-only">Choose billing period</legend>
        {(["monthly", "annual"] as const).map((period) => (
          <label key={period}>
            <input
              type="radio" name="billing" value={period}
              checked={billing === period}
              onChange={() => onBillingChange(period)}
            />
```

### 9.4 The backend side — a price/plans endpoint (Node/Express + Stripe)

The client must never decide the price. It requests plans for a currency; the server is the source of truth and validates any checkout against the same catalog.

```ts
import express, { Request, Response } from "express";
import Stripe from "stripe";

const stripe = new Stripe(process.env.STRIPE_SECRET_KEY!, { apiVersion: "2024-06-20" });
const app = express();
app.use(express.json());

const ALLOWED_CURRENCIES = new Set(["usd", "eur", "gbp"]);

// GET plans with server-authoritative prices for a currency
app.get("/api/plans", async (req: Request, res: Response) => {
  const currency = String(req.query.currency ?? "usd").toLowerCase();
  if (!ALLOWED_CURRENCIES.has(currency)) {
    return res.status(400).json({ error: "Unsupported currency" });
  }
  try {
    const prices = await stripe.prices.list({ active: true, currency, expand: ["data.product"] });
    const plans = prices.data.map((p) => ({
      id: (p.product as Stripe.Product).id,
      name: (p.product as Stripe.Product).name,
      amount: p.unit_amount, // in minor units — format on client with Intl
      currency: p.currency,
      interval: p.recurring?.interval ?? null,
    }));
    res.json({ plans });
  } catch (err) {
    console.error("plans fetch failed", err);
    res.status(502).json({ error: "Pricing temporarily unavailable" });
  }
});

// Checkout ALWAYS re-derives the price from the trusted price id — never from the body amount
app.post("/api/checkout", async (req: Request, res: Response) => {
  const { priceId } = req.body as { priceId?: string };
  if (!priceId || typeof priceId !== "string") {
    return res.status(400).json({ error: "Missing priceId" });
  }
  try {
    const session = await stripe.checkout.sessions.create({
      mode: "subscription",
      line_items: [{ price: priceId, quantity: 1 }], // server trusts the id, not a client amount
      success_url: `${process.env.APP_URL}/welcome`,
      cancel_url: `${process.env.APP_URL}/pricing`,
    });
    res.json({ url: session.url });
  } catch (err) {
    console.error("checkout failed", err);
    res.status(502).json({ error: "Could not start checkout" });
  }
});

app.listen(3000);
```

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)

STANDALONE. A pricing table combines a segmented control, a set of cards or a data table, and per-tier actions — each with its own accessibility contract.

**Roles & ARIA:**

*   Wrap the whole thing in `<section aria-labelledby>` so AT users can find and name it.
*   **Billing toggle:** use native radios in a `<fieldset>`/`<legend>`, or `role="radiogroup"` with `role="radio"` + `aria-checked`. If it's a true on/off, `<button role="switch" aria-checked>` is acceptable. Whatever you choose, **arrow keys move between the two options** and the group has an accessible label ("Billing period").
*   **Price change announcement:** an `aria-live="polite"` `role="status"` region says "Prices now shown billed annually" when the toggle flips — otherwise a screen-reader user hears nothing change.
*   **"Most popular" must reach AT:** put it in the accessible name (heading `sr-only` suffix "— most popular plan") and/or `aria-describedby` on the CTA. Never signal it with border/color alone.
*   **Feature cells:** icon-only "included/excluded" needs a text alternative — `sr-only` "Included"/"Not included" with `aria-hidden` on the glyph. A bare ✔ is silence to a screen reader.
*   **Comparison matrix:** real `<table>` with `<caption>`, `<th scope="col">` for plans, `<th scope="row">` for features. This is what lets AT announce "SSO, Pro: included."
*   **Per-tier CTA:** each link/button label must stand alone ("Start Pro trial," not "Choose" ×3 identical links).

**Keyboard map (interactive parts):**

| Key | Context | Action |
| ---| ---| --- |
| Tab | Anywhere | Move to next interactive element (toggle group, then each CTA, then tooltips) |
| Shift + Tab | Anywhere | Move to previous interactive element |
| Arrow Left / Right | Billing toggle (radiogroup) | Move selection between Monthly / Annual (and update prices) |
| Space | Billing toggle (`role="switch"`) | Toggle billing period |
| Enter / Space | CTA button/link | Activate the tier's action |
| Enter / Space | Feature-group header (`aria-expanded`) | Expand / collapse the feature group |
| Enter / Space / Esc | Feature tooltip trigger | Open / dismiss the info popover |

**Focus management:** the toggle group is a single tab stop (roving tabindex — only the checked radio is tabbable; arrows move within). Focus never gets trapped. If switching billing period re-renders prices, focus stays on the toggle control — never yank it to the top. Tooltips return focus to their trigger on close.

**Contrast:** price text and feature text ≥ 4.5:1 against the card surface; the "most popular" badge text ≥ 4.5:1 against the badge fill; the featured-tier border and any non-text state indicator ≥ 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11 non-text contrast). Struck-through anchor prices must still meet contrast — don't render them in illegible grey.

**Target size:** toggle segments and every CTA ≥ 44×44px. In compact density, shrink type and row height, never the hit targets.

**Reduced motion:** under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, drop the featured-tier scale/hover lift, the badge sheen, and any price count-up animation. The information is identical; only the motion goes.

**Common failures specific to pricing tables:**

*   Comparison cells with a checkmark and no text — invisible to AT.
*   Toggle that changes prices with no live-region announcement — silent to AT.
*   "Most popular" conveyed only by a colored border — not perceivable non-visually.
*   Three identical "Get started" links — a screen-reader link list becomes useless.
*   A matrix built from `<div>`s with visual columns but no `scope`/`<th>` — no header association.
*   Prices as images (for "design") — unreadable, un-zoomable, un-translatable.
*   On mobile, only the visible tier reachable in a non-swipeable carousel.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas

*   **Server-driven, geo-aware price rendering with a visible currency switcher.** Detect region, render VAT-inclusive prices server-side, and expose an explicit currency/region control so the user can correct a wrong guess — all prices authoritative from the catalog, never client-computed.
*   **Agentic "recommend my plan."** An inline assistant that asks 2–3 questions (team size, needed features) and highlights the matching column with an announced "Recommended for you" that replaces the generic "most popular" — personalization done honestly and accessibly.
*   **Live usage estimator merged into the table.** A seat/usage slider that recomputes each tier's effective price in real time and flags the break-even point where the next tier becomes cheaper — the table becomes a calculator.
*   **Liquid Glass promoted tier (2026-era platform APIs).** On iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe, render the featured card and its badge with the system Liquid Glass material (`.glassEffect()` in SwiftUI) for a refractive highlight; on the web, approximate with layered `backdrop-filter` and the emerging CSS refraction proposals — motion gated by `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **View Transitions for the monthly/annual swap.** Use the CSS/JS View Transitions API to cross-fade or slide the price digits when the toggle flips, giving continuity without re-layout jank — again, disabled under reduced-motion.
*   **`<fieldset>`****\-free segmented control via the Popover + Anchor Positioning APIs** for tooltips that explain feature jargon, positioned with CSS anchor positioning instead of JS, keyboard-and-AT-correct by construction.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers

Silent mistakes that cost money or trust.

*   **Hidden fees / drip pricing.** Headline "$29," but setup fees, seat minimums, overage, or tax only surface at checkout. Users feel baited; surviving conversions become chargebacks. **Fix:** show the all-in reality in the table — "+ tax," minimums, mandatory add-ons — not buried.
*   **Fake anchoring / phantom discounts.** "$99 ~~$199~~" where $199 was never charged is a deceptive reference-price claim (FTC / EU UCPD / UK ASA) and savvy buyers spot it. **Fix:** only strike a price the plan actually charged; if it's a launch promo, say so and date it.
*   **Decoy pricing dark patterns.** Crippling the middle tier so the top looks "obviously worth it," or a junk tier no one should pick to nudge the target — manipulation, trust-eroding, increasingly regulated. **Fix:** make every tier rational for _some_ buyer; guide with an honest "most popular," not a rigged set.
*   **Too many tiers / choice paralysis.** Six near-identical columns freeze the buyer. **Fix:** 3–4 tiers max; for more variation use a configurator or slider, not more columns.
*   **Toggle silently defaulting to the pricier period.** Loading on "Annual" without making the period obvious, so users think they're buying monthly. **Fix:** default to the expected period (usually monthly), make the active period unmistakable, announce switches.
*   **Vague, identical CTAs.** Three "Learn more" buttons give no reason to act and no distinction. **Fix:** tier-specific action verbs ("Start free trial," "Talk to sales") that also help screen-reader users.
*   **Feature jargon with no explanation.** "SSO," "SOC 2," "SLA" with no tooltip leave non-expert buyers unable to judge value. **Fix:** keyboard-reachable tooltips or plain-language labels.
*   **Currency/tax ambiguity.** A bare "$29" with no currency code or tax note confuses (and in the EU/UK may be non-compliant for) international buyers. **Fix:** show currency and tax treatment explicitly.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns

*   **TypeScript-enforced tier correctness (discriminated union).** Make illegal tiers unrepresentable: a self-serve tier _must_ have a numeric price; a contact-sales tier _must not_. The compiler enforces that CTAs and prices line up.

```ts
type SelfServeTier = {
  kind: "self-serve";
  id: string; name: string;
  priceMonthly: number; priceAnnual: number; currency: string;
  cta: { label: string; href: string };
  featured?: boolean;
};
type ContactTier = {
  kind: "contact";
  id: string; name: string;
  cta: { label: string; href: string }; // no price fields allowed
};
type Tier = SelfServeTier | ContactTier;

function renderPrice(t: Tier, billing: "monthly" | "annual", locale: string): string {
  switch (t.kind) {
    case "self-serve":
      return new Intl.NumberFormat(locale, { style: "currency", currency: t.currency, maximumFractionDigits: 0 })
        .format(billing === "monthly" ? t.priceMonthly : t.priceAnnual);
    case "contact":
      return "Custom"; // exhaustive — TS errors if a new kind is added and unhandled
  }
}
```

*   **Design-token tiers for skinnable pricing.** Drive every visual decision through CSS custom properties so one table reskins per brand/client without touching markup — and so the featured emphasis is a token flip, not a code change.

Keep the real `<caption>`/`scope` semantics in the DOM and only _visually_ restack with CSS grid — don't rebuild it as `<div>`s.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost

*   **Animate compositor-only properties.** The featured-tier hover lift and the price-swap transition should animate `transform` and `opacity`, never `width`/`height`/`top` (which trigger layout). A pricing page that jank-scrolls because badges animate `box-shadow`/`margin` reads as cheap on the exact page where trust matters most.
*   **Tree-shake the money formatter and icons.** Use the built-in `Intl.NumberFormat` instead of shipping a currency library; import only the icons you use (`import { Check } from "lucide-react"`), never the whole set. A pricing table needs ~2 icons — don't pull 300kB for a checkmark.
*   **Virtualize / lazy-load only the deep matrix.** The headline cards are tiny and must render instantly (LCP element is often the price). The full 40-row comparison table below the fold can be lazy-mounted on scroll or behind a "Compare all features" expander so it doesn't block first paint.
*   **Server-render prices; hydrate the toggle only.** Prices come from the server as static HTML (fast LCP, SEO-indexable, correct on no-JS). Only the billing toggle needs client JS — ship an island, not a fully client-rendered pricing page.
*   **Avoid layout thrash on toggle.** Pre-measure or reserve space for both monthly and annual price strings so switching doesn't reflow the whole card grid (a "$1,299" is wider than "$9"). Use `min-width`/tabular-nums so digits don't shift.
*   **`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`** on prices keeps columns aligned and prevents width jitter when digits change — a cheap perf-and-polish win.

## 15\. Security

STANDALONE. A pricing table looks like static marketing, but it sits directly on top of money — its security surface is real.

*   **Attack surface — the price is the target.** The core rule: **the client must never be the source of truth for what something costs.** Any amount rendered in the table is display-only. If a checkout request includes a price/amount from the client, an attacker can change "$29" to "$0.01" in DevTools. The server must re-derive the price from a trusted catalog / price ID and ignore any client-supplied amount (see §9.4 — checkout takes a `priceId`, not a dollar figure).
*   **Server-side validation for anything that reaches an endpoint.** Validate the requested plan/price ID against an allow-list of active plans, the requested currency against a supported set, and quantities/seats against min/max bounds. Reject unknown IDs rather than silently defaulting. Coupons and promo codes are validated and applied server-side only — never trust a client-computed discounted total.
*   **XSS if plan data comes from a CMS or user/partner input.** Plan names, feature labels, and taglines are often editor- or API-supplied. Render them as text through an auto-escaping template (React/Vue escape by default; Jinja2 with `autoescape=True` as in §9.14) — never `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`/`v-html`/`|safe` on plan content. A feature label of `<img onerror=...>` must render as inert text.
*   **Authorization for gated/contextual pricing.** If the table shows account-specific pricing (negotiated enterprise rates, region-locked plans, "your plan" markers), that data must be fetched under the authenticated user's session and authorized server-side. Don't ship every customer's custom price to the client and hide the wrong ones with CSS — that leaks pricing to anyone who opens DevTools.
*   **Integrity & anti-tamper.** Treat the price catalog like financial data: cache with sane TTLs, log price-fetch failures, and fail _closed_ (show "contact sales" / "pricing unavailable") rather than rendering a wrong or zero price. Webhooks that confirm payment (Stripe) must verify signatures before trusting the event.

Bottom line: the visible table is low-risk on its own, but it's the front door to a transaction. The security lives in _never trusting the client's number_ and _validating every plan/currency/coupon on the server_.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)

*   **Semantics:** cards are a real list of `<article>`s (or the matrix is a real `<table>` with `<caption>`, `<th scope="col">`, `<th scope="row">`) — not a grid of `<div>`s.
*   **Keyboard:** billing toggle is arrow-navigable, single tab stop (roving tabindex); every CTA and tooltip is reachable and operable by keyboard; no traps.
*   **Focus:** switching billing period keeps focus on the toggle; tooltips return focus to trigger; visible `:focus-visible` ring on all interactive children.
*   **"Most popular" is announced,** not color/border-only — it lives in an accessible name or `aria-describedby`.
*   **Toggle announces price changes** via `role="status"`/`aria-live="polite"`.
*   **Feature cells have text alternatives** — no icon-only included/excluded; matrix cells associate to both headers.
*   **Contrast:** text ≥ 4.5:1, featured border and non-text indicators ≥ 3:1, struck-through anchors still legible.
*   **Target size:** toggle segments and CTAs ≥ 44×44px at every density.
*   **Reduced motion:** featured scale, hover lift, sheen, and price count-up all disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Prices are server-authoritative;** checkout re-derives amount from a trusted price ID; currency, coupon, and plan validated server-side.
*   **Honesty:** currency + tax treatment shown; no fake anchors; no decoy tier; hidden fees disclosed up front.
*   **Responsive:** matrix collapses to stacked cards (or scrolls with a pinned first column) without losing header association; featured tier leads when stacked.
*   **Performance:** prices SSR'd, toggle hydrated as an island, deep matrix lazy/virtualized, animations compositor-only, tabular-nums on prices.

## 17\. Visual Styles

The same semantic pricing table — cards or matrix, a billing toggle, an announced featured tier, per-tier CTAs — wearing eleven skins. Each describes how _this_ component looks, not generic style notes.

*   **Flat.** Solid-fill cards, 1px borders, no shadow; the featured tier marked by a solid brand top-bar and flat badge, not elevation. Toggle is two solid pills. Reads instantly.
*   **Material.** Cards as elevation surfaces; the featured tier steps up an elevation level in the primary-container color; billing toggle is a Material 3 segmented button; CTAs are filled with ripple. Emphasis via shadow depth.
*   **Glassmorphism.** Translucent frosted cards over a vivid gradient with `backdrop-filter: blur()`; the featured card slightly more opaque to signal priority. Watch price/feature contrast — glass over a busy background can drop below 4.5:1.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026 — iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe).** Featured tier and "Most popular" badge are refractive glass bending the backdrop with a specular rim and moving sheen; the pill toggle reacts to motion. Non-featured tiers stay quiet matte so the promoted one pops.
*   **Neumorphism.** Cards as soft same-color extrusions with dual light/dark shadows; the featured tier pressed _outward_ more, CTAs as raised keys. Contrast-poor — accent only, keep prices in high-contrast text.
*   **Skeuomorphism.** Cards as physical price placards — beveled edges, paper/metal texture, an embossed "Most popular" ribbon on the featured corner; CTAs glossy gradient. The tactile "real object" pricing sheet.
*   **Neo-Brutalism.** Thick black borders, hard offset shadows, zero radius, clashing saturation; the featured tier is the loudest block with a sticker-style "POPULAR" tag. Prices in heavy monospace. Max personality.
*   **Claymorphism.** Big-radius puffy cards, inner top-light + bottom-shadow, soft glow; the featured card a larger friendlier clay blob, badge a rounded 3D pill. Approachable, consumer-app pricing.
*   **Aurora / Gradient.** Dark surface with animated multi-hue washes; the featured tier's border and CTA a glowing gradient stroke, prices in bright type. Premium — honor `prefers-reduced-motion` on the gradient.
*   **Minimal / Swiss.** Type and grid do the work: hairline dividers, whitespace, no badge beyond a bold "Recommended" label; featured tier distinguished only by weight and a thin rule. Prices are the biggest thing on the page.
*   **UJG Brand (house default).** Goldenrod accents on deep Eminence/Night cards; the featured "Pro" tier gets a 2px Goldenrod border, warm glow, and Eminence-on-Goldenrod badge; CTAs Goldenrod primaries with a Goldenrod focus ring; prices in Methanerse, features in Urbanist. Warm, confident, unmistakably UJG.

**The rule that never changes:** the style is skin. The pricing table's semantics (list of articles or a real table with caption + scoped headers), the billing toggle's keyboard model, the announced "most popular," the price-change live region, the focus ring, and the 44px targets stay identical across all eleven skins. Skin changes the paint; the skeleton — and the honesty — never move.
