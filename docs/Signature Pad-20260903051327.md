# Signature Pad

# Signature Pad

A bounded canvas where a person draws their signature with finger, stylus, or mouse, and the app captures that mark as an image, a vector, or raw stroke data for a document, consent record, or approval gate. The most physically expressive input on the web, and the one most tangled up in law.
* * *

## 1\. What It Actually Is

A signature pad is a **freehand drawing surface, scoped to one job: capturing a signature mark and the intent behind it.** Under the hood it is almost always an HTML `<canvas>` wired to pointer events, translating a sequence of `(x, y, pressure, time)` samples into rendered strokes and into an exportable artifact (PNG raster, SVG path, or JSON stroke log). The mark is only half of it — the other half is the _record_ that a specific person, at a specific time, deliberately signed a specific thing.

The three things people lump together and shouldn't:

*   **Signature pad (drawn mark):** freehand capture within a bounded box. Output is a picture or vector of a hand-drawn glyph. This page.
*   **Typed-signature field:** a text input where the user types their name, often rendered in a script font. It is a _text control_, not a canvas — fully keyboard-operable, trivially accessible, and in most jurisdictions just as legally valid as a drawn mark. It is the accessible fallback a real signature pad must offer, not a lesser cousin.
*   **General drawing/sketch canvas:** the same pointer-to-canvas machinery, but with no legal payload, no consent semantics, and usually with color/brush/layer tooling. A whiteboard or annotation tool is a drawing canvas, not a signature pad.

Also confused with it: a **checkbox "I agree" gate** (an assent control, not a signature — though a signature pad often sits behind one) and a **file upload of a photographed signature** (an image input, not live capture).

**Rule of thumb:** if the interaction produces a _hand-drawn mark tied to a signing event and an audit trail_, it's a signature pad. If it produces text, it's a typed-signature field. If it produces art or annotation with no legal weight, it's a drawing canvas. When in doubt, ask "does this need an audit trail?" — if yes, you're building a signature pad and the audit trail is not optional.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters

The signature pad carries weight far out of proportion to its size on screen, across four axes:

*   **Legal enforceability.** This is the whole point. Under the U.S. **ESIGN Act (2000)** and **UETA** (adopted in 49 states), and analogous regimes like the EU's **eIDAS**, an electronic signature is legally valid _when it captures intent to sign and is logically associated with the record_. The drawn squiggle alone is weak evidence; the squiggle **plus** a timestamp, an identity signal, an IP/device fingerprint, and a tamper-evident hash of the signed document is what holds up when a contract is disputed. A signature pad that captures the mark but throws away the metadata has built a decoration, not a signature. (This is general information, not legal advice — jurisdiction and use-case matter, and high-stakes contracts warrant an attorney.)
*   **Accessibility and equal access.** Drawing is an inherently visual, fine-motor task. A signature pad that is _only_ a canvas locks out keyboard users, screen-reader users, people with motor impairments, and anyone on a device without a good pointer. If the drawn pad is the only way to sign a lease or consent to care, that's not just bad UX — it can be a discrimination and compliance problem. The typed-name alternative is a legal and ethical requirement, not a nicety.
*   **Trust and fidelity.** A signature that renders jagged, laggy, or that silently clips when the box resizes makes the whole product feel broken at the exact moment the user is committing to something. Pressure-sensitive, high-DPI-crisp strokes with a responsive clear/undo signal "this is a serious tool that captured what I actually drew." Perceived integrity of the capture _is_ perceived integrity of the agreement.
*   **Data & storage cost.** The choice between storing a raster PNG (fixed, large, un-reflowable), an SVG/vector (scalable, small, replayable), or the raw stroke JSON (replayable, analyzable for biometric/forensic use, largest metadata surface) has downstream cost, privacy, and legal-evidence consequences. Getting it wrong means either bloated storage or unrecoverable, unverifiable signatures.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy

Every well-built signature pad is composed of these parts:

*   **Container / frame:** the bounded region that says "sign inside here." Dashed border when empty (an affordance to draw), solid when filled. Establishes the signing area's aspect ratio (commonly ~3:1, width-dominant, mimicking a signature line).
*   **Canvas element:** the actual `<canvas>` drawing surface. Sized in CSS pixels but backed by a bitmap scaled by `devicePixelRatio` so strokes are crisp on Retina/high-DPI screens. Carries `touch-action: none` so a finger drags ink instead of scrolling the page.
*   **Baseline / signature line:** an optional printed rule (a horizontal line, sometimes with an "✗" mark at the left) telling the user where to sign. Purely visual guidance; it is _not_ part of the captured stroke data.
*   **Placeholder / prompt:** centered "Sign here" text shown when the canvas is empty, cleared on first stroke. Distinct from the label.
*   **Stroke data model:** the in-memory array of strokes, each stroke an array of points `{x, y, pressure, t}`. The single source of truth. Rendering is derived from it, which is what makes undo, responsive resize, and vector export possible.
*   **Clear button:** resets the canvas and empties the stroke model. Must be a real, keyboard-operable `<button>`.
*   **Undo control:** removes the last stroke (pops one entry from the stroke model and re-renders). Optional but expected in serious pads.
*   **Typed-name fallback (tab or toggle):** a text input path — "Type your name" — for users who can't or won't draw. Often a segmented Draw / Type control. This is anatomy, not an add-on.
*   **Consent affordance:** the "I agree / I intend to sign this document" checkbox or statement that converts a drawn mark into a legally meaningful _signing event_. Frequently gates submission.
*   **Helper / instruction text:** below the pad — "Draw your signature above, then submit" — plus error text ("Please provide a signature before continuing").
*   **Hidden output field(s):** the `<input type="hidden">` (or form value) carrying the serialized signature (data URL / SVG / JSON) plus capture metadata to the server.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes / Scale / Density

Signature pads scale by context — an inline delivery-confirmation strip is not a full-page contract signing surface. The canvas' _CSS_ size drives layout; the _bitmap_ size is always CSS-size × `devicePixelRatio`.

| Tier | CSS height | CSS width | Aspect | Backing bitmap @2x DPR | Use case |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact | 96px | 240px | 2.5:1 | 480×192 | Inline delivery/receipt confirmation, mobile drawers |
| Default | 160px | 480px | 3:1 | 960×320 | Standard contract/consent form field |
| Large | 220px | 640px | ~2.9:1 | 1280×440 | Full-page e-sign ceremony, kiosk/tablet signing |
| Kiosk / full-bleed | 320px+ | 100vw − padding | fluid | width·DPR × 320·DPR | Dedicated tablet signing station, POS |

Rules that matter:

*   **Minimum drawable height ~96px, minimum width ~240px.** Below that, a finger-drawn signature is unrecognizable and legally weak. Never shrink the pad to "fit" — reflow the layout instead.
*   **The clear/undo buttons obey the 44×44px touch-target minimum** regardless of how small the pad is. The pad shrinks; the controls don't.
*   **Height comes from a spacing/size token, not a magic number** (`--sig-h-default: 160px`), so the whole system scales and re-themes.
*   **On mobile, go full-width.** A wide, short pad matches how people sign on a line and reduces the "cramped" feeling that produces bad signatures.
*   **Bitmap must track DPR** **_and_** **any CSS resize.** A pad that's 480 CSS-px wide on a 3× phone needs a 1440px-wide backing store, or the ink looks soft.
* * *

## 5\. States

A production signature pad has far more than "empty" and "full." Each state below is distinct and must be _described_, styled, and announced where relevant.

*   **Empty (rest):** dashed border, centered "Sign here" placeholder, baseline visible, clear/undo disabled (nothing to clear). This is the invitation-to-draw state.
*   **Drawing (active capture):** pointer is down and moving; ink is being laid in real time. Border goes solid, placeholder is gone, `touch-action: none` is actively suppressing scroll. Undo/clear become enabled.
*   **Filled / completed:** at least one stroke exists, pointer released. Solid border, ink visible, clear/undo enabled, submit gate satisfied (if the only requirement is "a mark exists").
*   **Hover (pointer devices):** the container invites interaction — cursor becomes `crosshair`, border may brighten. Non-existent on touch, so never the _only_ affordance.
*   **Focus:** the pad (or its interactive wrapper) has received keyboard focus. Because a bare canvas is a poor focus target, focus usually lands on the typed-name input or a "Start signing" affordance, with a visible `:focus-visible` ring. The clear/undo buttons have their own focus states.
*   **Disabled:** grayed out, no pointer capture, controls inert, `aria-disabled="true"`. Used when a prerequisite (e.g., "read the document," "check I agree") isn't met — but see §12 on _why_ dead-disabling with no explanation costs you.
*   **Invalid / error:** submission attempted with no signature (or below a minimum-stroke threshold). Red border, error text wired via `aria-describedby`, and — critically — the error is _announced_, not just colored.
*   **Loading / submitting:** signature serialized and in flight to the server. Pad locks (no further edits that could desync from what's being saved), a spinner or "Saving signature…" shows, re-submit is blocked to prevent duplicate signing events.
*   **Locked / signed (immutable):** after a successful signing event, the pad becomes read-only evidence — the mark is shown but can no longer be edited, matching the legal reality that a completed signature is a fixed record.
*   **Typed-mode active:** the fallback path is selected; the canvas is hidden/disabled and the text input is the live control. A distinct state because validation, output format, and focus all change.
* * *

## 6\. Types / Variants

*   **Freehand-only:** the pure drawn canvas. Simplest; also the least accessible on its own — acceptable only when a typed alternative lives elsewhere in the flow.
*   **Freehand + typed fallback (segmented Draw / Type):** the production default. A segmented control switches between drawing and typing your name (rendered in a signature font). This is the accessible, dual-path pattern nearly every real e-sign product ships.
*   **Freehand + typed + upload:** adds a third tab to upload a photographed/scanned signature image. Common in enterprise e-sign; raises its own file-validation and XSS-via-SVG concerns (see §15).
*   **Consent-gated pad:** the canvas is locked until an "I have read and agree" checkbox is checked, binding the drawn mark to explicit intent. The strongest simple pattern for legal defensibility.
*   **Pressure/stylus-optimized pad:** uses `PointerEvent.pressure` and tilt to vary stroke width, producing a natural pen feel on Apple Pencil / S-Pen / Wacom. Degrades gracefully to constant width on pressure-less pointers.
*   **Initials pad:** a compact square variant capturing initials on each page of a multi-page contract, sharing one stroke model per signer.
*   **Guided / dated pad:** pairs the canvas with an auto-stamped date and printed name field, assembling a complete "signed, printed, dated" block.
*   **Biometric-capture pad:** retains full raw stroke timing/pressure/velocity data (not just the final image) for forensic signature verification. Highest data-sensitivity variant — the raw timing _is_ biometric data in some jurisdictions.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use (and When Not To)

**Use a signature pad when:**

*   You need a **hand-drawn mark plus intent** for a contract, lease, waiver, or consent/authorization form.
*   The flow is **touch-first** (tablet at point of care, phone delivery confirmation, in-person kiosk) where drawing is natural and expected.
*   The signing carries **legal or compliance weight** and you'll capture the surrounding audit trail (timestamp, identity, hash).
*   A **visible, personal mark** materially improves trust or ceremony (closing documents, medical consent).

**Don't use one — reach for the alternative — when:**

*   **A typed name + "I agree" checkbox is legally sufficient.** For most clickwrap terms-of-service and low-stakes assent, a **checkbox + typed name** is faster, fully accessible, and just as enforceable under ESIGN/UETA. Don't demand a drawn squiggle to accept a newsletter.
*   **The flow is desktop-mouse-heavy.** Mouse-drawn signatures look like a toddler's crayon work and frustrate users. Default to the **typed-signature field**, offer draw as secondary.
*   **You need identity assurance, not just a mark.** A drawn signature proves little about _who_ drew it. For high-assurance signing, use a **qualified/digital-certificate e-signature** (PKI, eIDAS QES) — that's cryptographic identity, a different component entirely.
*   **You only need assent, not a signature.** A **checkbox** ("I accept the terms") is the right control for agreement without a signing ceremony.
*   **You're capturing a sketch, drawing, or annotation.** Use a general **drawing canvas** — no consent/audit machinery needed.

**Placement heuristics:** put the pad at the _end_ of the document flow, after the terms are visible, so intent is genuine. Keep the primary "Sign & submit" button near the pad and in thumb reach on mobile. Never place the pad above the content it commits the user to.

## 8\. Across Design Systems

Signature capture is rarely a _core_ primitive in the big systems (it's domain-specific), so teams compose it from each system's canvas/surface, form, and button primitives. How each ecosystem frames it:

*   **Material Design (Google):** no first-class "signature" component; built as a Material `Card`/surface wrapping a canvas, with a filled/outlined text field for the typed fallback and Material buttons for clear/submit. Ripple and elevation apply to the _controls_, never the canvas. Material's strong form-error and helper-text conventions carry the validation.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** the platform ships **PencilKit** (`PKCanvasView`) as the native drawing surface, purpose-built for Apple Pencil with pressure, tilt, and low-latency ink. HIG guidance leans on the system ink, generous margins, and the OS-level `.signature` semantics; the "signature" affordance in Apple Mail/Preview markup is the reference behavior.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** composed from Fluent surfaces + `InkCanvas` (the UWP/WinUI inking control) which natively handles stylus, pressure, and ink serialization to ISF. Compact, neutral chrome; primary accent reserved for the confirm action.
*   **Ant Design (enterprise):** no signature primitive, but the ecosystem pairs Ant's `Form`/`Upload`/`Button` with community canvas libs. Ant's dense, validation-heavy forms make it a common host for consent-gated signing in admin/back-office e-sign flows.
*   **Tailwind (utility-first):** no component — you style a bare `<canvas>` and the surrounding frame with utilities (`touch-none`, `border-dashed`, `rounded-lg`) and bring your own capture logic. Maximum freedom, zero built-in behavior.
*   **shadcn/Radix:** no signature component in the registry; the idiomatic build wraps a canvas in a shadcn `Card`, uses `Tabs` for Draw/Type, `Button` for clear/submit, and Radix `Label`/form primitives for the accessible name field. Behavior is yours; a11y scaffolding comes from Radix.
*   **Bootstrap:** no native component; the classic pattern is a bordered `.card` or `.form-control`\-framed canvas plus `.btn` controls, often paired with the widely-used **Signature Pad JS** library (szimek/signature\_pad) for the capture engine.

### § 8b. What's trending in 2026

*   **Native ink everywhere.** Web is closing the gap with `PointerEvent.getCoalescedEvents()` (batched high-frequency samples for smooth curves) and `getPredictedEvents()` (predicted points to hide input latency) — the same low-latency ink Apple Pencil users expect, now approachable in-browser.
*   **Liquid Glass framing.** Apple's 2026 language shows up as a translucent, refractive _frame_ around the signing surface (the canvas itself stays opaque and high-contrast for legibility) — the ceremony gets the glass, the ink stays readable.
*   **Verifiable-credential signing.** Movement toward binding the drawn mark to a W3C Verifiable Credential / decentralized identity, so the audit trail carries cryptographic identity, not just an IP address.
*   **On-device biometric-behavioral verification.** Stroke velocity/pressure signatures analyzed on-device (privacy-preserving) to flag forgeries, no raw biometrics leaving the device.
*   **AI-assisted fraud checks and, conversely, AI-forgery awareness** pushing products toward richer audit trails (device attestation, liveness) rather than trusting the mark alone.
* * *

## 9\. The Code

The signature pad's engine is the same everywhere: capture pointer samples into a stroke model, render the model to a DPR-scaled canvas, serialize the model on submit. Below, that engine in production shape across every major target. The shared model is `Stroke = { points: {x,y,p,t}[] }` and the shared exports are PNG (raster), SVG (vector), and JSON (replayable stroke log).

```html
    <input type="text" id="sig-typed" name="signature_typed" autocomplete="name"
           class="sigpad__typed" aria-describedby="sig-help">
  </div>

  <!-- Consent binds the mark to intent -->
  <label class="sigpad__consent">
    <input type="checkbox" id="sig-consent" name="consent" required>
    I intend this to be my electronic signature on this document.
  </label>

  <p id="sig-help" class="sigpad__hint">Draw your signature, or switch to “Type name.” Then confirm.</p>
  <p id="sig-error" class="sigpad__error" role="alert" hidden>Please provide a signature before continuing.</p>

  <!-- Serialized outputs sent to the server -->
  <input type="hidden" name="signature_image"  id="sig-out-png">
  <input type="hidden" name="signature_vector" id="sig-out-svg">
  <input type="hidden" name="signature_strokes" id="sig-out-json">
  <input type="hidden" name="signed_at"          id="sig-out-time">
</fieldset>
```

Key HTML rules: the canvas gets `role="img"` with a live-updated `aria-label` (empty → completed), never a bare unlabeled canvas. The **Type** tab is a real, focusable equivalent — the canvas being un-keyboardable is exactly why it can't stand alone. Consent is a `required` checkbox so the browser blocks submit without intent. All serialized formats ride in hidden inputs so the server gets image, vector, _and_ replayable strokes plus a timestamp.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (full reusable component)

```tsx
import {
  forwardRef, useCallback, useEffect, useImperativeHandle, useRef, useState,
} from "react";

export interface Point { x: number; y: number; p: number; t: number }
export type Stroke = Point[];
export interface SignatureValue {
  png: string;            // data URL
  svg: string;            // vector markup
  strokes: Stroke[];      // replayable log
  signedAt: string;       // ISO timestamp
  isEmpty: boolean;
}
export interface SignaturePadHandle {
  clear: () => void;
  undo: () => void;
  serialize: () => SignatureValue;
}
interface Props {
  penColor?: string;
  minWidth?: number;
  maxWidth?: number;
  onChange?: (isEmpty: boolean) => void;
  ariaLabel?: string;
}

export const SignaturePad = forwardRef<SignaturePadHandle, Props>(function SignaturePad(
  { penColor = "#0A0A0A", minWidth = 1.2, maxWidth = 3.2, onChange, ariaLabel = "Signature" },
  ref,
) {
  const canvasRef = useRef<HTMLCanvasElement>(null);
  const strokes = useRef<Stroke[]>([]);
  const current = useRef<Stroke | null>(null);
  const [isEmpty, setEmpty] = useState(true);

  // Fit the backing bitmap to CSS size × devicePixelRatio, then replay strokes.
  const resize = useCallback(() => {
    const canvas = canvasRef.current;
    if (!canvas) return;
    const dpr = window.devicePixelRatio || 1;
    const rect = canvas.getBoundingClientRect();
    canvas.width = Math.round(rect.width * dpr);
    canvas.height = Math.round(rect.height * dpr);
    const ctx = canvas.getContext("2d");
    if (!ctx) return;
    ctx.setTransform(dpr, 0, 0, dpr, 0, 0);   // draw in CSS-pixel coordinates
    render();
  }, []);

  const render = useCallback(() => {
    const canvas = canvasRef.current;
    const ctx = canvas?.getContext("2d");
    if (!canvas || !ctx) return;
    const dpr = window.devicePixelRatio || 1;
    ctx.clearRect(0, 0, canvas.width / dpr, canvas.height / dpr);
    ctx.lineCap = "round";
    ctx.lineJoin = "round";
    ctx.strokeStyle = penColor;
    for (const stroke of strokes.current) {
      for (let i = 1; i < stroke.length; i++) {
        const a = stroke[i - 1], b = stroke[i];
        ctx.beginPath();
        ctx.lineWidth = minWidth + (maxWidth - minWidth) * b.p;
        ctx.moveTo(a.x, a.y);
        ctx.lineTo(b.x, b.y);
        ctx.stroke();
      }
    }
  }, [penColor, minWidth, maxWidth]);

  useEffect(() => {
    resize();
    const ro = new ResizeObserver(resize);
    if (canvasRef.current) ro.observe(canvasRef.current);
    return () => ro.disconnect();
  }, [resize]);

  const pointFrom = (e: React.PointerEvent): Point => {
    const rect = canvasRef.current!.getBoundingClientRect();
    return {
      x: e.clientX - rect.left,
      y: e.clientY - rect.top,
      p: e.pressure > 0 ? e.pressure : 0.5,   // mice report 0 pressure
      t: performance.now(),
    };
  };

  const onPointerDown = (e: React.PointerEvent) => {
    e.preventDefault();
    canvasRef.current!.setPointerCapture(e.pointerId);
    current.current = [pointFrom(e)];
    strokes.current.push(current.current);
    if (isEmpty) { setEmpty(false); onChange?.(false); }
  };

  const onPointerMove = (e: React.PointerEvent) => {
    if (!current.current) return;
    // Coalesced events recover the high-frequency samples the browser batched.
    const events = (e.nativeEvent as PointerEvent).getCoalescedEvents?.() ?? [e.nativeEvent];
    for (const ev of events) {
      const rect = canvasRef.current!.getBoundingClientRect();
      current.current.push({
        x: ev.clientX - rect.left, y: ev.clientY - rect.top,
        p: (ev as PointerEvent).pressure > 0 ? (ev as PointerEvent).pressure : 0.5,
        t: performance.now(),
      });
    }
    render();
  };

  const onPointerUp = () => { current.current = null; };

  const clear = useCallback(() => {
    strokes.current = []; current.current = null;
    setEmpty(true); onChange?.(true); render();
  }, [onChange, render]);

  const undo = useCallback(() => {
    strokes.current.pop();
    const empty = strokes.current.length === 0;
    setEmpty(empty); onChange?.(empty); render();
  }, [onChange, render]);

  const serialize = useCallback((): SignatureValue => {
    const canvas = canvasRef.current!;
    return {
      png: canvas.toDataURL("image/png"),
      svg: strokesToSvg(strokes.current, canvas.clientWidth, canvas.clientHeight, penColor),
      strokes: strokes.current,
      signedAt: new Date().toISOString(),
      isEmpty: strokes.current.length === 0,
    };
  }, [penColor]);

  useImperativeHandle(ref, () => ({ clear, undo, serialize }), [clear, undo, serialize]);

  return (
    <div>
      <div className="sigpad__frame" data-empty={isEmpty}>
        <canvas
          ref={canvasRef}
          className="sigpad__canvas"
          role="img"
          aria-label={`${ariaLabel}: ${isEmpty ? "empty" : "completed"}`}
          onPointerDown={onPointerDown}
          onPointerMove={onPointerMove}
          onPointerUp={onPointerUp}
          onPointerCancel={onPointerUp}
          style={{ width: "100%", height: 160, touchAction: "none" }}
        />
        {isEmpty && <span className="sigpad__placeholder" aria-hidden>Sign here</span>}
      </div>
      <div className="sigpad__controls">
        <button type="button" onClick={undo} disabled={isEmpty}>Undo</button>
        <button type="button" onClick={clear} disabled={isEmpty}>Clear</button>
      </div>
    </div>
  );
});

export function strokesToSvg(strokes: Stroke[], w: number, h: number, color: string): string {
  const paths = strokes
    .filter((s) => s.length > 1)
    .map((s) => {
      const d = s.map((pt, i) => `${i ? "L" : "M"}${pt.x.toFixed(2)} ${pt.y.toFixed(2)}`).join(" ");
      return `<path d="${d}" fill="none" stroke="${color}" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"/>`;
```

```js
function createSignaturePad(canvas, { penColor = "#0A0A0A", min = 1.2, max = 3.2 } = {}) {
  const ctx = canvas.getContext("2d");
  let strokes = [], current = null;

  function resize() {
    const dpr = window.devicePixelRatio || 1;
    const rect = canvas.getBoundingClientRect();
    canvas.width = Math.round(rect.width * dpr);
    canvas.height = Math.round(rect.height * dpr);
    ctx.setTransform(dpr, 0, 0, dpr, 0, 0);
    render();
  }
  function render() {
    const dpr = window.devicePixelRatio || 1;
    ctx.clearRect(0, 0, canvas.width / dpr, canvas.height / dpr);
    ctx.lineCap = ctx.lineJoin = "round";
    ctx.strokeStyle = penColor;
    for (const s of strokes)
      for (let i = 1; i < s.length; i++) {
        ctx.beginPath();
        ctx.lineWidth = min + (max - min) * s[i].p;
        ctx.moveTo(s[i - 1].x, s[i - 1].y);
        ctx.lineTo(s[i].x, s[i].y);
        ctx.stroke();
      }
  }
  const pt = (e) => {
    const r = canvas.getBoundingClientRect();
    return { x: e.clientX - r.left, y: e.clientY - r.top, p: e.pressure > 0 ? e.pressure : 0.5, t: performance.now() };
  };

  canvas.addEventListener("pointerdown", (e) => {
    e.preventDefault();
    canvas.setPointerCapture(e.pointerId);
    current = [pt(e)]; strokes.push(current);
    canvas.setAttribute("aria-label", "Signature: completed");
  });
  canvas.addEventListener("pointermove", (e) => {
    if (!current) return;
    const evs = e.getCoalescedEvents ? e.getCoalescedEvents() : [e];
    for (const ev of evs) current.push(pt(ev));
    render();
  });
  const end = () => { current = null; };
  canvas.addEventListener("pointerup", end);
  canvas.addEventListener("pointercancel", end);

  window.addEventListener("resize", resize);
  resize();

  return {
    clear() { strokes = []; current = null; canvas.setAttribute("aria-label", "Signature: empty"); render(); },
    undo() { strokes.pop(); render(); },
    isEmpty: () => strokes.length === 0,
    toPNG: () => canvas.toDataURL("image/png"),
    toJSON: () => JSON.stringify(strokes),
  };
}
```

```ts
// app/sign/actions.ts
"use server";
import { createHash } from "node:crypto";
import { headers } from "next/headers";
import { auth } from "@/lib/auth";
import { z } from "zod";
import { db } from "@/lib/db";

const Payload = z.object({
  documentId: z.string().uuid(),
  png: z.string().startsWith("data:image/png;base64,"),
  svg: z.string().max(200_000),
  strokes: z.array(z.array(z.object({
    x: z.number(), y: z.number(), p: z.number().min(0).max(1), t: z.number(),
  }))).min(1),
  signedAt: z.string().datetime(),
});

export async function saveSignature(input: unknown) {
  const parsed = Payload.safeParse(input);
  if (!parsed.success) return { ok: false as const, message: "Invalid signature payload." };

  const session = await auth();
  if (!session?.user) return { ok: false as const, message: "You must be signed in to sign." };

  const doc = await db.document.findUnique({ where: { id: parsed.data.documentId } });
  if (!doc || doc.signerId !== session.user.id)
    return { ok: false as const, message: "Not authorized to sign this document." };

  // Tamper-evident: hash the document contents + the captured mark together.
  const h = await headers();
  const hash = createHash("sha256")
    .update(doc.contentHash + parsed.data.png + parsed.data.signedAt)
    .digest("hex");

  await db.signature.create({
    data: {
      documentId: doc.id,
      signerId: session.user.id,
      imagePng: parsed.data.png,
      vectorSvg: parsed.data.svg,
      strokeLog: parsed.data.strokes,
      signedAt: new Date(parsed.data.signedAt),
      ip: h.get("x-forwarded-for") ?? "unknown",
      userAgent: h.get("user-agent") ?? "unknown",
      integrityHash: hash,      // the audit trail that gives the mark legal weight
    },
  });
  return { ok: true as const };
}
```

```python
# app.py — FastAPI endpoint the signature POSTs to
import hashlib, json
from datetime import datetime, timezone
from fastapi import FastAPI, Depends, HTTPException, Request, Form
from pydantic import BaseModel, field_validator

app = FastAPI()

class SignatureIn(BaseModel):
    signature_image: str
    strokes_json: str
    consent: bool

    @field_validator("signature_image")
    @classmethod
    def must_be_png_data_url(cls, v: str) -> str:
        if not v.startswith("data:image/png;base64,"):
            raise ValueError("signature must be a PNG data URL")
        if len(v) > 2_000_000:
            raise ValueError("signature image too large")
        return v

    @field_validator("strokes_json")
    @classmethod
    def must_parse(cls, v: str) -> str:
        data = json.loads(v)               # rejects malformed JSON
        if not isinstance(data, list) or not data:
            raise ValueError("stroke log must be a non-empty array")
        return v

@app.post("/documents/{doc_id}/sign", status_code=201)
def sign(doc_id: str, request: Request, payload: SignatureIn = Depends(),
         user=Depends(require_auth), _=Depends(verify_csrf)):
    if not payload.consent:
        raise HTTPException(422, "Consent to sign is required")
    doc = db.get_document(doc_id)
    if doc is None or doc.signer_id != user.id:
        raise HTTPException(403, "Not authorized to sign this document")
    signed_at = datetime.now(timezone.utc).isoformat()
    # Tamper-evident audit trail: hash document + mark + when + who.
    integrity = hashlib.sha256(
        (doc.content_hash + payload.signature_image + signed_at + user.id).encode()
    ).hexdigest()
    db.save_signature(
        document_id=doc.id, signer_id=user.id,
        image=payload.signature_image, strokes=payload.strokes_json,
```

## 10\. Accessibility

Drawing is inherently visual and fine-motor. A signature pad can **never be fully keyboard-operable**, so accessibility here is not "make the canvas accessible" — it's "guarantee an equivalent, non-drawing path to the same legal outcome." That equivalent path is the load-bearing requirement, not an extra.

**Roles & ARIA:**

*   The `<canvas>` gets `role="img"` with an `aria-label` that updates with state: `"Signature: empty"` → `"Signature: completed"`. A bare canvas exposes nothing to assistive tech.
*   The whole control is grouped in a `<fieldset>` with a `<legend>` (or `role="group"` + `aria-labelledby`) so the signature field is announced as one labeled unit.
*   The Draw/Type switch uses real `role="tablist"`/`role="tab"`/`role="tabpanel"` with `aria-selected`, or a native segmented `<button>` group — never unlabeled `<div>`s.
*   Errors are wired via `aria-describedby` to a `role="alert"` region so "signature required" is _announced_, not just reddened.
*   The consent checkbox is a native `<input type="checkbox" required>` — fully accessible by construction.

**Keyboard map (the interactive controls; the canvas itself is pointer-only by nature):**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| Tab | Move between Draw/Type tabs, the canvas wrapper, Undo, Clear, consent, submit |
| Left / Right Arrow | Move between Draw and Type tabs (roving tabindex within the tablist) |
| Enter / Space (on tab) | Activate the focused tab, revealing its panel |
| Enter / Space (on Undo/Clear) | Undo last stroke / clear the pad |
| Space (on consent) | Toggle the "I intend to sign" checkbox |
| Enter (in typed field) | Move to submit (does not draw) |

**Focus management:** on switching to Type mode, move focus to the text input. On Clear, return focus to the canvas wrapper (or the Type tab) and announce "Signature cleared" via a live region. After a successful signing, move focus to the confirmation. Never trap focus on the canvas — a keyboard user must be able to Tab straight past it to the Type path.

**Contrast:** the ink vs. canvas background must meet **≥ 3:1 for the non-text graphic** (WCAG 1.4.11), and the surrounding label/helper/error text meets **≥ 4.5:1** (1.4.3). Dark ink (`#0A0A0A`) on the platinum canvas (`#E8E6E1`) clears this comfortably; a light-gray pen on white does not.

**Target size:** Undo, Clear, tabs, and submit are all **≥ 44×44px** (WCAG 2.5.8 / Apple) even when the pad is compact. The pad shrinks; the controls never drop below the target.

**Reduced motion:** under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, drop control transitions and any "ink flourish"/success animation; show the completed state statically. Never animate the ink appearing for the user who opted out.

**Common failures specific to this component:**

*   Shipping the canvas as the _only_ way to sign — locking out every keyboard/screen-reader/motor-impaired user. The typed alternative is mandatory.
*   A bare `<canvas>` with no role/label — invisible to assistive tech.
*   Placeholder "Sign here" implemented as canvas-drawn text (unreadable to AT) instead of a real DOM element.
*   Error state signaled by red border only, never announced.
*   Removing the focus ring from the clear/tab controls "because it looks cleaner."
*   Relying on hover to reveal the clear button — invisible on touch, unreachable by keyboard.

## 11\. Innovative / Emerging Ideas

*   **Predicted-ink low latency:** pair `getCoalescedEvents()` (recover batched samples) with `getPredictedEvents()` (draw a few predicted points ahead, discard on the next real frame) to make web ink feel as immediate as native Apple Pencil — the 2026 state of the art for in-browser drawing latency.
*   **Verifiable-credential-bound signatures:** bind the drawn mark to a W3C Verifiable Credential / decentralized identifier at signing time, so the audit trail carries cryptographic identity instead of a guessable IP address — moving drawn signatures toward the assurance level of qualified e-signatures.
*   **On-device behavioral biometrics:** analyze stroke velocity, acceleration, and pressure rhythm locally to score authenticity and flag likely forgeries, with the raw biometric never leaving the device (privacy-preserving liveness for signing).
*   **Content-hash anchoring:** at sign time, hash the exact document bytes + the mark + timestamp and optionally anchor that hash to a tamper-evident log (transparency log / notarization service), so any later edit to the document is provably detectable.
*   **OffscreenCanvas + Worker rendering:** move stroke rendering to a Web Worker via `OffscreenCanvas`, keeping the main thread free so long signing sessions on low-end tablets stay at 60fps.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers

*   **No mobile scroll suppression (****`touch-action`** **missing).** The single most common bug: a finger on the pad scrolls the page instead of drawing, so the signature is a useless smear or nothing at all. The user rage-quits the contract. Fix: `touch-action: none` on the canvas and `e.preventDefault()` on pointerdown.
*   **Resize wipes the signature.** The pad re-inits its bitmap on rotate/resize/keyboard-open and blanks the canvas after the user carefully signed — so they have to redo it, and some abandon. Fix: keep the stroke _model_ as the source of truth and _replay_ it after every resize; never treat the pixels as canonical.
*   **Blurry, low-DPI ink.** Skipping `devicePixelRatio` scaling makes the signature look soft and cheap on every modern phone, undermining trust at the commitment moment. Fix: back the canvas bitmap with CSS-size × DPR.
*   **No typed fallback / desktop mouse-only.** Forcing a mouse-drawn squiggle produces embarrassing scrawls, and forcing _only_ drawing locks out anyone who can't. Both cause drop-off and complaints. Fix: always offer Draw / Type.
*   **Premature disabling with no reason.** Graying out "Sign & submit" until some invisible condition is met, with no hint why, strands the user. Fix: let them try and surface the specific blocker ("check the consent box"), or clearly show what's missing.
*   **No clear/undo, or a tiny clear button.** A single bad stroke with no recovery forces a full restart; a sub-44px clear target on mobile causes mis-taps. Fix: real Undo + Clear at proper target size.
*   **Silent oversized payloads.** Serializing a huge PNG on every keystroke or shipping a multi-MB data URL stalls submit on slow connections with no feedback, reading as "broken." Fix: serialize once on submit, prefer vector/JSON, and show a saving state.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns

**1\. TypeScript-enforced accessible output (discriminated union).** Make it _impossible_ to persist a signature without either strokes or a typed name — the type system forces the accessible path to exist:

```ts
type DrawnSignature = {
  kind: "drawn";
  png: string;
  svg: string;
  strokes: { x: number; y: number; p: number; t: number }[][];  // required, non-empty
  signedAt: string;
};
type TypedSignature = {
  kind: "typed";
  typedName: string;   // required when kind==="typed"
  font: string;
  signedAt: string;
};
export type CapturedSignature = DrawnSignature | TypedSignature;

// The consumer must handle BOTH — the typed (accessible) branch can't be forgotten.
function persist(sig: CapturedSignature) {
  switch (sig.kind) {
    case "drawn": if (!sig.strokes.length) throw new Error("empty drawn signature"); break;
    case "typed": if (!sig.typedName.trim()) throw new Error("empty typed signature"); break;
  }
}
```

**2\. Design-token pipeline for the pad.** Drive every visual of the pad from tiered tokens so it re-skins per client without touching capture logic:

```css
:root {

  --night: #0A0A0A; --eminence: #5F2C82; --gold: #DCA424; --platinum: #E8E6E1;
```

**3\. Stroke-model-as-source-of-truth (resize-safe replay).** The senior move that fixes the "resize wipes it" bug class: never treat canvas pixels as canonical. Keep `Stroke[]` in memory (and in the hidden JSON field), and re-render from it on every DPR change, orientation change, and container resize (via `ResizeObserver`). This same model powers undo (pop a stroke), vector export (map points to SVG paths), and forensic replay (animate points by their `t` timestamps) — one data structure, four features.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost

*   **Render only the new segment on pointermove, not the whole model.** Re-clearing and re-stroking every prior point on every move is O(n²) over a long signature and drops frames on cheap tablets. Draw just the latest segment during capture; do the full replay only on resize/undo. (The reference components above keep this cheap by drawing point-to-point.)
*   **Coalesced events are a perf** **_feature_****, not just quality.** `getCoalescedEvents()` lets you process one batched `pointermove` per frame instead of firing a handler per hardware sample (which can be 120–240Hz). Fewer handler invocations, smoother line, less main-thread work.
*   **Animate only compositor-friendly properties.** Any success/flourish animation on the frame must use `transform`/`opacity`, never `width`/`height`/`border-width`, which force reflow every frame while the user is mid-signature.
*   **Serialize lazily and prefer vector/JSON.** `toDataURL("image/png")` is synchronous and can jank on a large high-DPI canvas — call it once on submit, not on every stroke. SVG/JSON output is far smaller than a raster PNG for the same mark, cutting payload and storage.
*   **Tree-shake the capture engine; don't ship a whole ink library for one pad.** Import the specific helper (`import { strokesToSvg } from "./SignaturePad"`), not `import * as sig`. If using `signature_pad`, import the single class, not a barrel. For heavy pressure/tilt math, lazy-load it only on the signing route.
*   **`OffscreenCanvas`****/Worker for kiosk sessions.** On dedicated signing stations doing hundreds of signatures a day, offload rendering to a worker so the UI thread never blocks.

## 15\. Security

The signature pad has a **real and multi-layered attack surface**, because it produces a legal artifact _and_ usually reaches a server. It cannot be treated as a low-surface control.

*   **Attack surface.** Everything the pad sends — the image data URL, the SVG, the stroke JSON, the timestamp, the "signer" identity — is fully client-controllable and forgeable. A signature payload can be fabricated with `curl`; the drawn mark proves nothing on its own. The server must treat the whole payload as untrusted input.
*   **XSS via SVG / uploaded signatures.** If you accept a _vector_ signature or an _uploaded_ signature image, an SVG can carry `<script>`, `onload`, and `foreignObject` payloads that execute when rendered inline. Never inject signature SVG with `innerHTML`/`dangerouslySetInnerHTML`. Sanitize server-side (strip scripts/event handlers, e.g. DOMPurify with SVG profile), or store and serve the signature as a rasterized PNG, or serve it from a sandboxed origin with a restrictive `Content-Security-Policy`. Data-URL PNGs must be validated to actually be PNG (magic bytes), not a disguised HTML/SVG blob.
*   **Server-side validation.** Re-validate on the endpoint (see §9.6 / §9.13): the PNG data URL prefix and size cap, the stroke JSON shape and non-emptiness, the consent flag present and true, the document id well-formed. Never persist a raw client string unchecked.
*   **Authorization.** Signing gates a document, so the server must verify _this authenticated user is the assigned signer for this specific document_ before recording the signature — the pad being visible is UX, not authorization. Bind the signature row to the session user id, not to a client-supplied signer field.
*   **Integrity / tamper-evidence.** Compute a server-side hash over `documentContent + mark + signedAt + signerId` and store it; this is what makes later tampering (swapping the doc, back-dating, substituting the mark) detectable and gives the electronic signature its evidentiary weight under ESIGN/UETA. Capture and store the audit trail (IP, user agent, timestamp, consent record) alongside it. Protect the signing endpoint with a **CSRF token** and idempotency so a double-submit can't create two signing events, and rate-limit to resist automated forgery attempts.
*   **Privacy of biometric stroke data.** Raw stroke timing/pressure/velocity can constitute _biometric_ data under laws like Illinois BIPA and GDPR. If you retain it for forensic verification, disclose it, get consent, minimize retention, and secure it accordingly — don't hoard it by default.

This is general security and legal information, not legal advice; a Georgia business attorney should review high-stakes signing flows before they carry real contracts.

## 17\. Visual Styles

The same signing skeleton — DPR-scaled canvas, stroke model, clear/undo, typed fallback — wears any of these skins. Each described specifically for _this_ component:

1. **Flat:** a crisp bordered rectangle, dashed when empty and solid when filled, flat platinum surface, dark ink, no shadow. Reads instantly as "sign in this box," scales fast, and keeps the ink maximally legible.
2. **Material:** the pad sits on an elevated `Card` with a resting shadow; the baseline mimics a Material text-field underline that thickens and turns to the accent color the moment the first stroke lands; Clear/Submit are Material buttons with ripple.
3. **Glassmorphism:** a translucent, `backdrop-filter: blur()` frame over a vivid backdrop — but the _canvas interior stays opaque and high-contrast_ so the signature never fights the blur. Glass is for the frame, never the ink.
4. **Liquid Glass (2026):** the frame is Apple's refractive material with a specular rim and a sheen that shifts as the tablet moves, giving the signing "ceremony" real depth; the canvas itself remains a flat, opaque, high-legibility signing surface beneath the glass chrome.
5. **Neumorphism:** the pad is an _inset_ soft-extruded well — dual light/dark inner shadows making the signing area look pressed into a same-color surface, like signing on embossed paper. Accent-only: the low contrast means the ink color must still clear 3:1.
6. **Skeuomorphism:** the literal look — a cream paper texture, a printed signature line with a small "✗" at the left, a faint drop shadow, ink that renders like a fountain pen with pressure-varied width. The "sign on the dotted line" metaphor made explicit.
7. **Neo-Brutalism:** a thick solid black border, zero radius, a hard offset drop shadow, and a raw "SIGN." label in a heavy grotesk; Clear is a loud clashing block. High personality for campaign or creative-agency signing flows.
8. **Claymorphism:** a big-radius, puffy, soft-glowing pad with an inner top-light and bottom-shadow, ink rendered slightly rounded and friendly. Approachable — good for consumer waivers where a formal contract vibe would intimidate.

**The rule that never changes:** style is skin, behavior is the skeleton. Across all eleven skins the semantics (`role="img"` + labeled group), the keyboard-operable typed equivalent, the focus ring, the 44px controls, `touch-action: none`, DPR crispness, and the resize-safe stroke model are identical. The pad can look like pressed clay or refractive glass; it must always capture a legible mark, offer a non-drawing path, and produce a tamper-evident record.
