## 1\. What a Dialog Actually Is
A dialog is a **container that appears on top of the page to hold a focused sub-task or message** — a conversation the app starts with the user. That's the definition. The confusion starts immediately, because four words get thrown around interchangeably and they are not the same thing:

*   **Dialog** — the _element/role_. A window layered over the page that groups content for a discrete task or message. It can be **modal** or **non-modal**. This is the noun.
*   **Modal** — an _adjective describing a mode_, not a separate component. A **modal dialog** blocks interaction with everything behind it: focus is trapped inside, the background is inert, and you must deal with the dialog before returning to the page. "A modal" in casual speech = "a modal dialog." Modality is the behavior; dialog is the thing that has it.
*   **Popover** — a **non-modal**, lightweight overlay anchored to a trigger (a menu, a date picker, a tooltip-with-actions). It does _not_ trap focus, does _not_ inert the page, and light-dismisses when you click outside. A popover is not a dialog; it's a transient surface. The native `popover` attribute and `<dialog>` are different primitives for different jobs.
*   **Alertdialog** — a dialog with `role="alertdialog"`: a _modal_ dialog that interrupts to communicate an urgent message requiring a response, usually a **destructive confirmation** ("Delete this account?"). It's a stricter dialog, not a fourth component.

The single most common architecture mistake: reaching for a modal dialog when a popover, an inline expand, or a full page would serve better — or worse, building a `<div>` overlay by hand that traps nothing, restores no focus, and is invisible to screen readers. **Rule of thumb: if it interrupts and demands resolution, it's a modal dialog; if it's a quick anchored choice you can click away from, it's a popover; if it's a full workflow, it's a page.**

The spine of everything below is the **modal ↔ non-modal split**. Get that right and the rest is detail.

*   **Modal dialog** — opened with `dialog.showModal()`. Renders in the browser's **top layer**, above everything, with a `::backdrop`. Focus is **trapped** inside; the rest of the document is **inert** (unfocusable, unclickable, hidden from assistive tech). The user _must_ act on it. Escape closes it for free.
*   **Non-modal dialog** — opened with `dialog.show()`. Appears on top but the page stays **fully live** underneath. Focus is **not** trapped; the user can click back to the page, keep scrolling, keep working. No backdrop, no inerting. Escape does _not_ close it automatically. Think: a non-blocking "changes saved" panel, a floating find-in-page bar, a persistent settings tray.

Those two modes have _different focus rules, different dismissal rules, and different use cases_. Treating a non-modal dialog like a modal (or vice-versa) is the #1 dialog bug after "no label."

## 2\. Why It Matters
A dialog is the most **disruptive** thing you can do to a user short of navigating them away. That disruption is exactly why it carries outsized weight:

*   **It's a conversion checkpoint.** Checkout confirmations, plan upgrades, "are you sure you want to cancel your subscription" — the highest-stakes yes/no moments in a product happen in dialogs. A confusing or janky dialog at the moment of purchase or cancellation is money leaving the building. And an over-used dialog (the newsletter popup that fires two seconds after landing) is the fastest way to make someone bounce.
*   **It's an accessibility cliff.** A hand-rolled modal is where accessibility goes to die: focus escapes to the page behind it, screen-reader users have no idea a dialog opened, Escape does nothing, focus lands nowhere on close. A _correct_ dialog — real semantics, trapped focus, restored focus, inert background — is the single biggest accessibility lift-or-fail in most apps. Modality is invisible to a screen reader unless you engineer it.
*   **It sets the trust tone.** A dialog that can't be dismissed, that loses your scroll position, that reopens the page at the top, or that traps you with no visible close, reads as _broken and hostile_. A dialog that opens with focus on the right control, closes on Escape, and drops you back exactly where you were, feels like the app respects your time.

The dialog is where "it works" and "it's usable" diverge most sharply.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy of a Dialog
Every well-built dialog is composed of predictable parts:

*   **Backdrop / scrim** — the dimmed layer behind a _modal_ dialog (native: `::backdrop`). Signals "the page is paused." Non-modal dialogs have none.
*   **Container / surface** — the panel itself: elevation, radius, padding, max-width, max-height. Lives in the **top layer** for native `<dialog>`.
*   **Header** — a title (the accessible name via `aria-labelledby`) and usually a close button (✕).
*   **Title** — the element the dialog is _labelled by_. Non-negotiable for screen readers.
*   **Body / content** — the message, form, or sub-task. Scrolls independently when tall.
*   **Footer / action bar** — the buttons. Confirm/cancel, save/discard. Primary action is the affirmative, styled per §7.
*   **Close affordance** — the ✕ button and/or a labelled "Cancel." Every dismissible dialog needs a visible one; Escape and backdrop-click are _additions_, not replacements.
*   **Focus sentinel / trap boundary** — for non-native implementations, the guard elements that keep Tab from escaping (native `showModal()` gives this for free; see the Modal doc's focus-trap engine).
*   **Initial focus target** — the element focused on open (the first input, or the least-destructive button — never the "Delete" button).
*   **Return focus target** — the element focus goes _back_ to on close (usually the trigger).

## 4\. Sizes & Scale
Dialogs are sized by _content weight_ and viewport, not by a fixed grid. A workable scale:

| Size | Max width | Padding | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| XS | 320px | 16px | Confirmation / alertdialog, one line + two buttons |
| SM | 420px | 20px | Simple message, single field |
| MD | 560px | 24px | Default form dialog (login, edit item) |
| LG | 720px | 32px | Multi-field forms, side-by-side layout |
| XL | 900px+ | 32px | Rich content, media, wizards |
| Full | 100dvw × 100dvh | 16px | Mobile sheets, immersive flows |

Rules that matter:

*   **Cap the height and scroll the body, not the page.** `max-height: min(90dvh, 720px)` with `overflow: auto` on the body keeps the header and footer pinned while long content scrolls. A dialog taller than the viewport with a footer you can't reach is a dead end.
*   **Use** **`dvh`****\*\*\*\*, not** **`vh`****\*\*\*\*,** so mobile browser chrome (the disappearing address bar) doesn't clip the footer.
*   **Go full-screen on small viewports.** Below ~600px, a centered card is worse than a sheet that uses the whole screen. Switch to `100dvw/100dvh` and slide from the bottom.
*   **Never let width exceed** **`min(92vw, maxWidth)`** or the dialog kisses the screen edges on tablets.
*   **The backdrop is not part of the size** — it always covers the full viewport regardless of the surface size.

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A production dialog has far more states than "open" and "closed." Missing states are the tell of an amateur build.

1. **Closed** — not in the DOM's top layer; trigger is focusable; page is fully live.
2. **Opening** — the enter transition (fade + scale/slide). Backdrop fades in. Focus is _moving_ to the initial target.
3. **Open (modal)** — top layer, backdrop present, focus trapped, background inert, Escape armed, scroll locked.
4. **Open (non-modal)** — on top, no backdrop, focus _not_ trapped, page live, Escape _not_ auto-wired.
5. **Focus-within** — an inner control is focused; the trap keeps Tab cycling inside (modal) or lets it flow to the page (non-modal).
6. **Submitting / busy** — an async action is in flight. Disable the confirm button, show a spinner, set `aria-busy`, and **block Escape/backdrop dismissal** so the user can't close mid-write.
7. **Error** — the action failed or validation failed. Surface the message _inside_ the dialog, keep it open, move focus to the error or the first invalid field.
8. **Success / done** — transient confirmation, then auto-close (or a "Done" state before closing).
9. **Closing** — exit transition running. Native `<dialog>` needs the close deferred until the animation ends (see §9.2, `allow-discrete`).
10. **Dismiss-blocked** — a "you have unsaved changes" guard: Escape/backdrop attempts trigger a confirm instead of closing (the `cancel` event, preventable).

Two dialog-specific state truths:

*   **A busy dialog must resist dismissal.** If the user hits Escape while a payment is processing, closing the dialog while the request is in flight is how you get charged-but-no-receipt. Guard it.
*   **An** **`alertdialog`** **has no "casual dismiss" state.** Backdrop-click and (often) Escape should _not_ silently confirm or cancel a destructive choice — force an explicit button.

## 6\. Types & Variants
Dialogs come in three functional families and two role variants. Know both axes.

**By purpose:**

*   **Message dialog** — informs, one action to acknowledge ("Your session expired. OK."). Lowest stakes. Often could be a toast instead (see §7).
*   **Confirmation dialog** — asks a yes/no before an action, especially a destructive or irreversible one ("Delete 42 files?"). This is where `role="alertdialog"` belongs.
*   **Form dialog** — holds an input sub-task without leaving the page (create item, edit profile, invite teammate). The most common and most complex; it reaches the server and needs validation, busy, and error states.

**By mode (the spine):**

*   **Modal** — blocks the page. `showModal()`. Default for confirmations, forms, and anything requiring resolution.
*   **Non-modal** — coexists with the page. `show()`. For find-in-page, floating tool panels, non-blocking notices you want to persist.

**By role:**

*   **`role="dialog"`** — the default for `<dialog>`. A dialog the user can generally dismiss and return from. Forms, editors, messages.
*   **`role="alertdialog"`** — a dialog that interrupts with an urgent message _requiring a response_. **Required** when the dialog is a destructive confirmation or an error that blocks progress. Two concrete behavioral consequences: (1) it's labelled by its title _and described_ by its message via `aria-describedby`, so the screen reader announces the whole warning on open; (2) initial focus goes to the **least destructive** action (usually "Cancel"), never the "Delete." Use it for "Discard unsaved changes?", "Delete account?", "Your file failed to upload." Do _not_ use it for a routine form dialog — over-using `alertdialog` is as wrong as never using it.

**A note on the native** **`<dialog>`** **and roles:** a native `<dialog>` has an implicit `role="dialog"`. To make it an alert dialog, set `role="alertdialog"` explicitly. Everything else (top layer, backdrop, focus trap from `showModal()`) still applies.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a dialog when the user must **focus on a discrete sub-task or answer a question** without losing the context of the page behind them. Reach for the _right mode and role_:

*   **Destructive/irreversible confirm →** modal `alertdialog`, focus on Cancel.
*   **Create/edit sub-task →** modal `dialog` (form), focus on first field.
*   **Non-blocking notice you want to persist →** non-modal `dialog`.
*   **Quick anchored choice →** _not a dialog._ Use a popover/menu.

Don't use a dialog when:

*   **A toast/inline message would do.** "Saved successfully" does not deserve a modal that the user must dismiss. Interrupting to say "it worked" trains people to reflexively close your dialogs — including the important ones.
*   **The task is big.** A multi-step wizard, a full editor, a settings surface with 20 fields — that's a **page or a route**, not a dialog. Dialogs are for _bounded_ tasks. If it needs its own URL, deep-link, or back-button behavior, it's a page.
*   **You're stacking dialogs.** A dialog opening another dialog is a smell. Redesign the flow; nested modals are a focus-management and cognitive nightmare.
*   **It's a menu, tooltip, or date picker.** Those are popovers. They shouldn't trap focus or inert the page.
*   **It fires unprompted to interrupt.** The entry-popup newsletter modal is the most-hated pattern on the web for a reason. If the user didn't ask, think hard.

Placement & behavior heuristics:

*   **Primary action bottom-right** (Western reading), destructive action separated from it — never adjacent, to prevent misclick.
*   **Affirmative button matches the verb:** "Delete" in a delete dialog, not "OK." The label names the outcome.
*   **Always provide a visible close/cancel.** Escape and backdrop are extras, not the only exit.
*   **Return focus to the trigger** so a keyboard user isn't dumped at the top of the page.

## 8\. Dialogs Across Design Systems
Same component, different philosophies. Fluency here means you can drop into any codebase.

*   **Material Design (Google):** Distinguishes **basic dialogs**, **full-screen dialogs** (mobile, for larger tasks), and **alert dialogs**. Scrim-backed, elevated surface, ripple on buttons. Material 3 leans into rounded corners and larger touch targets. Clear guidance to prefer full-screen dialogs on mobile over cramped cards.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** Splits **alerts** (small, title + message + up to a few buttons, for critical info) from **sheets** (slide-up modal for self-contained tasks) and **popovers** (non-modal, anchored, iPad/Mac). Strong stance: use alerts sparingly, and put destructive actions in red with cancel prominent. iOS 26 renders these in Liquid Glass.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** **Dialog** for modal decisions and **Panel/Flyout** for side-sliding non-modal surfaces. Subtle depth, acrylic/mica materials, blocking vs non-blocking dialog variants are explicit.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** `Modal` component with a rich API (`confirm()`, `info()`, `warning()`, `error()` static methods that spawn a dialog imperatively), plus `Drawer` for side panels. Built for data-dense admin flows; `Modal.confirm` is the workhorse destructive-confirm.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** No prescribed dialog; you compose it, usually over **Headless UI**'s `Dialog` (which handles focus trap, inert, Escape, and portal) and skin it with utilities.
*   **shadcn/Radix (modern web standard):** Ships **`Dialog`** _and_ a separate **`AlertDialog`** — the split is a first-class API decision, not a prop. Radix primitives handle focus trap, `aria-*`, Escape, scroll-lock, and portal; you own the styling. The AlertDialog forces an explicit action/cancel and won't light-dismiss. This distinction (Dialog dismissible, AlertDialog requires a choice) is the cleanest expression of the role split in any library.
*   **Bootstrap:** `.modal` component with data-attribute wiring (`data-bs-toggle="modal"`), backdrop and focus handling via its JS bundle. Ubiquitous, opinionated look, `static` backdrop option to prevent outside-click dismiss.

The through-line: **every serious system separates "dialog you can dismiss" from "alert you must answer," and "modal that blocks" from "non-modal that coexists."** Those two axes are universal.

## 8b. Visual Styles
The dialog's _skin_ can be any of the eleven visual languages; its behavior never changes. See them live in the [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) gallery.

*   **Flat:** solid surface, crisp edge, subtle scrim. Reads instantly.
*   **Material:** elevated surface with layered shadow, scrim at ~32% black, ripple buttons.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent surface + `backdrop-filter: blur()`. Watch text contrast against whatever shows through.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language. The dialog surface _refracts_ the dimmed page behind it with a specular rim and a soft moving sheen — glassmorphism grown up. Sings over a deep, saturated backdrop. SwiftUI emits it natively; the web is an honest approximation.
*   **Neumorphism:** same-color surface with dual light/dark shadow. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent only, never the whole dialog.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** beveled surface, inner highlight, "real panel" tactility.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** hard offset shadow, thick border, zero radius, loud. High personality; keep the scrim so it still reads as layered.
*   **Claymorphism:** big radius, soft inner light + shadow, puffy and friendly.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue backdrop or surface glow. Premium — honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** typography and generous whitespace, near-zero decoration, sharp corners.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod title and primary action over a deep Eminence/Night surface, confident radius, warm glow, a Night scrim at ~60%. The house default.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin. The dialog's modality, focus trap, Escape/close behavior, label wiring, and 44px targets are identical across all eleven. Guard glass / liquid-glass / neumorphism for contrast; guard aurora / liquid-glass for `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML — the native `<dialog>` (both modes)
The modern platform gives you a real dialog element. Learn it before any library.

```plain
<!-- The trigger -->
<button type="button" id="openBtn">Edit profile</button>

<!-- A MODAL form dialog, labelled by its title -->
<dialog id="editDialog" aria-labelledby="editTitle">
  <form method="dialog">
    <h2 id="editTitle">Edit profile</h2>
    <p>
      <label>Name <input name="name" autofocus></label>
    </p>
    <menu>
      <!-- method="dialog" + value: closes and sets returnValue -->
      <button value="cancel">Cancel</button>
      <button value="save" class="primary">Save</button>
    </menu>
  </form>
</dialog>
```

```plain
<!-- A destructive confirmation: role=alertdialog, described by the message -->
<dialog id="deleteDialog" role="alertdialog"
        aria-labelledby="delTitle" aria-describedby="delDesc">
  <h2 id="delTitle">Delete account?</h2>
  <p id="delDesc">This permanently removes your account and all data. This can't be undone.</p>
  <menu>
    <button id="cancelDelete" autofocus>Cancel</button> <!-- focus the SAFE action -->
    <button id="confirmDelete" class="danger">Delete account</button>
  </menu>
</dialog>
```

Key HTML rules:

*   **`showModal()`** **vs** **`show()`** **is the whole game.** `showModal()` puts the dialog in the **top layer**, renders `::backdrop`, **traps focus**, **inerts the rest of the page**, and wires **Escape to close**. `show()` opens a **non-modal** dialog — on top, but the page stays live, no backdrop, no trap, Escape does nothing. You pick the mode in JS, not markup.
*   **`aria-labelledby`** **is mandatory.** Point it at the title's `id`. Without an accessible name, the dialog announces as an unnamed "dialog" and screen-reader users are lost. For `alertdialog`, also wire `aria-describedby` to the message.
*   **`<form method="dialog">`** **is the magic close.** A submit button inside it closes the dialog _without_ navigating and sets `dialog.returnValue` to the button's `value`. No JS needed to close. (See §9.4 for the returnValue/close pattern.)
*   **`autofocus`** sets the initial focus target. On a form dialog, the first field; on an alertdialog, the **safe** button.
*   Don't hand-roll `role="dialog"` on a `<div>` if you can use `<dialog>` — you'd be re-implementing the top layer, focus trap, and inert for free behavior the browser already ships.

### 9.2 CSS (states done right, both modes)

```css
dialog {
  max-width: min(92vw, 560px);
  max-height: min(90dvh, 720px);
  padding: 24px;
  border: none;
  border-radius: 16px;
  background: #12101a;            /* Eminence-tinted surface */
  color: #E8E6E1;
  overflow: auto;                  /* body scrolls, header/footer can pin with sticky */
}

/* The backdrop only exists for showModal() (modal mode) */
dialog::backdrop {
  background: rgba(10, 10, 10, 0.6);   /* Night scrim */
}

/* Keyboard focus ring on every control inside */
dialog :focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid #DCA424;      /* Goldenrod */
  outline-offset: 2px;
}

/* --- Enter/exit transitions with the top layer --- */
dialog {
  opacity: 0;
  transform: translateY(8px) scale(0.98);
  transition: opacity .2s, transform .2s, overlay .2s allow-discrete, display .2s allow-discrete;
}
dialog[open] {
  opacity: 1;
  transform: none;
}
/* Starting style so it animates IN, not just out */
@starting-style {
  dialog[open] { opacity: 0; transform: translateY(8px) scale(0.98); }
}
dialog::backdrop { background: rgba(10,10,10,0); transition: background .2s allow-discrete; }
dialog[open]::backdrop { background: rgba(10,10,10,0.6); }

/* Lock the page scroll behind a modal (belt-and-suspenders with JS) */
:root:has(dialog[open]) { overflow: hidden; }

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  dialog, dialog::backdrop { transition: none; }
}
```

The subtle part: a native `<dialog>` toggles `display`, and elements in the **top layer** need `transition-behavior: allow-discrete` (plus `@starting-style`) to animate both in _and_ out — otherwise the exit animation is skipped because the element is removed from the top layer instantly. This is the single most-Googled native-dialog CSS problem.

### 9.3 Vanilla JS — driving both modes, and Escape/backdrop
The building block every framework abstracts.

```javascript
const dialog = document.querySelector("#editDialog");
const openBtn = document.querySelector("#openBtn");
let lastFocused = null;

// MODAL: traps focus, inerts page, wires Escape — all for free
openBtn.addEventListener("click", () => {
  lastFocused = document.activeElement;   // remember trigger for focus restore
  dialog.showModal();
});

// NON-MODAL alternative: dialog.show();  (page stays live, no trap, Escape NOT wired)

// Native Escape fires a 'cancel' event on a MODAL dialog. Intercept to guard unsaved work:
dialog.addEventListener("cancel", (e) => {
  if (hasUnsavedChanges()) {
    e.preventDefault();                   // block the close
    confirmDiscard();                     // ask first
  }
});

// Backdrop click = click that lands on the <dialog> element itself (not its children)
dialog.addEventListener("click", (e) => {
  if (e.target === dialog) dialog.close("dismiss");
});

// Restore focus to the trigger whenever it closes (any path)
dialog.addEventListener("close", () => {
  lastFocused?.focus();
});
```

Notes that separate senior from junior:

*   **`showModal()`** **gives you the focus trap and inert page for free.** You do _not_ hand-roll a Tab-cycling keydown handler for a native modal dialog. (You _do_ for a `<div>`\-based one — that's the focus-trap engine in the Modal doc.)
*   **Escape →** **`cancel`** **event →** **`close`** **event.** The `cancel` event is _preventable_; use it for "unsaved changes" guards. The `close` event fires on every close and is where you restore focus.
*   **Backdrop dismiss isn't automatic** — you detect a click whose `target` is the dialog element itself. For an `alertdialog`, _don't_ wire this: force an explicit button.
*   **Non-modal (****`show()`****) wires none of this** — no trap, no Escape, no backdrop. If you want Escape on a non-modal dialog you add it yourself, and you decide whether it should even close.

### 9.4 The returnValue / close-event pattern
Native dialogs have a built-in "what did the user choose?" channel most people never use. It's elegant and JS-light.

```javascript
const dialog = document.querySelector("#deleteDialog");

// Open and await the user's decision, no callbacks
function confirmDelete() {
  dialog.showModal();
  dialog.addEventListener("close", () => {
    // returnValue is set by the submit button's `value` (form method="dialog")
    // or by the argument passed to dialog.close(...)
    if (dialog.returnValue === "confirm") {
      performDelete();
    }
    // "cancel" / "dismiss" / "" → do nothing
  }, { once: true });
}
```

```plain
<dialog id="deleteDialog" role="alertdialog" aria-labelledby="t" aria-describedby="d">
  <h2 id="t">Delete this file?</h2>
  <p id="d">This can't be undone.</p>
  <form method="dialog">
    <button value="cancel" autofocus>Cancel</button>
    <button value="confirm" class="danger">Delete</button>
  </form>
</dialog>
```

How it works: `<form method="dialog">` closes the dialog on submit _and_ sets `dialog.returnValue` to the clicked button's `value`. Or call `dialog.close("confirm")` imperatively — the argument becomes `returnValue`. The `close` event then reads it. **One state channel, no boolean flags, works for keyboard and mouse identically.** This is the native answer to "how do I know if they clicked confirm or cancel" — no library needed.

### 9.5 React + TypeScript (a proper reusable component)
A dialog that wraps native `<dialog>`, supports both modes, restores focus, and guards dismissal. This is usually the longest block for a reason.

```typescript
import {
  forwardRef, useEffect, useImperativeHandle, useRef, useCallback,
  type ReactNode,
} from "react";

type DialogRole = "dialog" | "alertdialog";

interface DialogProps {
  open: boolean;
  onClose: (returnValue: string) => void;
  titleId: string;                 // must match the heading's id
  describedById?: string;          // required for alertdialog
  role?: DialogRole;
  /** modal blocks the page; non-modal coexists with it */
  modal?: boolean;
  /** block Escape / backdrop while an async action runs */
  dismissible?: boolean;
  children: ReactNode;
}

export const Dialog = forwardRef<HTMLDialogElement, DialogProps>(function Dialog(
  { open, onClose, titleId, describedById, role = "dialog",
    modal = true, dismissible = true, children },
  externalRef
) {
  const ref = useRef<HTMLDialogElement>(null);
  useImperativeHandle(externalRef, () => ref.current!, []);
  const triggerRef = useRef<HTMLElement | null>(null);

  // Open/close the native element and pick the MODE
  useEffect(() => {
    const el = ref.current;
    if (!el) return;
    if (open && !el.open) {
      triggerRef.current = document.activeElement as HTMLElement;
      modal ? el.showModal() : el.show();     // <-- the modal/non-modal switch
    } else if (!open && el.open) {
      el.close();
    }
  }, [open, modal]);

  // Escape (cancel) + close wiring
  useEffect(() => {
    const el = ref.current;
    if (!el) return;
    const onCancel = (e: Event) => {
      if (!dismissible || role === "alertdialog") { e.preventDefault(); }
    };
    const onNativeClose = () => {
      triggerRef.current?.focus();             // restore focus to trigger
      onClose(el.returnValue);
    };
    el.addEventListener("cancel", onCancel);
    el.addEventListener("close", onNativeClose);
    return () => {
      el.removeEventListener("cancel", onCancel);
      el.removeEventListener("close", onNativeClose);
    };
  }, [dismissible, role, onClose]);

  // alertdialog REQUIRES a description (§10) — enforce it in dev
  useEffect(() => {
    if (process.env.NODE_ENV !== "production" && role === "alertdialog" && !describedById) {
      console.warn('Dialog: role="alertdialog" needs describedById for an accessible description.');
    }
  }, [role, describedById]);

  // Backdrop click (modal only, and only when dismissible)
  const onClick = useCallback((e: React.MouseEvent<HTMLDialogElement>) => {
    if (!modal || !dismissible || role === "alertdialog") return;
    if (e.target === ref.current) ref.current?.close("dismiss");
  }, [modal, dismissible, role]);

  return (
    <dialog
      ref={ref}
      role={role === "alertdialog" ? "alertdialog" : undefined}
      aria-labelledby={titleId}
      aria-describedby={describedById}
      onClick={onClick}
      className="ujg-dialog"
    >
      {children}
    </dialog>
  );
});
```

Usage — a destructive confirm that awaits a real answer:

```typescript
function DeleteButton({ id }: { id: string }) {
  const ref = useRef<HTMLDialogElement>(null);   // Dialog forwards its <dialog> here
  const [open, setOpen] = useState(false);
  const [busy, setBusy] = useState(false);

  async function handleClose(value: string) {
    if (value !== "confirm") return setOpen(false);
    setBusy(true);
    try { await api.delete(id); toast.success("Deleted"); }
    catch { toast.error("Couldn't delete"); }
    finally { setBusy(false); setOpen(false); }
  }

  return (
    <>
      <button onClick={() => setOpen(true)}>Delete</button>
      {open && (
        <Dialog
          ref={ref}
          open={open}
          role="alertdialog"
          modal
          dismissible={!busy}          // can't Escape mid-delete
          titleId="del-title"
          describedById="del-desc"
          onClose={handleClose}
        >
          <h2 id="del-title">Delete this item?</h2>
          <p id="del-desc">This permanently removes it. This can't be undone.</p>
          <menu>
            <form method="dialog">
              <button value="cancel" autoFocus>Cancel</button>
            </form>
            <button className="danger" disabled={busy}
                    onClick={() => ref.current?.close("confirm")}>
              {busy ? "Deleting…" : "Delete"}
            </button>
          </menu>
        </Dialog>
      )}
    </>
  );
}
```

Why this is the senior version:

*   **The** **`modal`** **prop maps straight to** **`showModal()`** **vs** **`show()`** — the mode isn't an afterthought, it's the core switch.
*   **Focus restore is built in** (`triggerRef`), so a keyboard user always lands back on the trigger.
*   **`dismissible={!busy}`** blocks Escape/backdrop during async work — no closing mid-write.
*   **`alertdialog`** **requires** **`describedby`** **(dev-warns if missing) and disables casual dismissal** — the role changes behavior, not just the announcement.
*   **`forwardRef`** **+** **`useImperativeHandle`** so callers can `close("confirm")` imperatively for the affirmative path while the form's `method="dialog"` handles cancel.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
This is where dialogs live or die. The ARIA Authoring Practices dialog pattern, distilled:

*   **Use the native** **`<dialog>`** **+** **`showModal()`** when you can — it gives focus trap, inert background, top layer, and Escape for free. A `<div role="dialog">` means _you_ own all of that (see the Modal doc's focus-trap engine).
*   **Role:** `role="dialog"` (default) or **`role="alertdialog"`** for urgent messages requiring a response (destructive confirms, blocking errors). Native `<dialog>` is `dialog` implicitly; set `alertdialog` explicitly when needed.
*   **Accessible name is mandatory:** `aria-labelledby` pointing to the visible title (or `aria-label` if there's no visible title). An unnamed dialog is announced as just "dialog."
*   **Description for alertdialog:** `aria-describedby` pointing at the message, so the screen reader announces the full warning on open.
*   **Focus on open:** move focus _into_ the dialog — the first field for a form, the **least-destructive** button for an alertdialog. Never leave focus on the trigger behind an inert background.
*   **Focus trapped (modal):** Tab and Shift+Tab cycle _within_ the dialog; focus can't reach the inert page. `showModal()` does this natively.
*   **Focus restored on close:** return focus to the element that opened it (usually the trigger). A user dumped at the top of the page is a broken experience.
*   **Escape closes a modal dialog.** Native gives this free; for an `alertdialog` guarding a destructive choice, you may require an explicit button instead — but then the visible Cancel _must_ be obvious.
*   **Background inert:** the rest of the page is unfocusable, unclickable, and hidden from AT. `showModal()` inerts natively; hand-rolled dialogs use the `inert` attribute or `aria-hidden` on siblings.
*   **Non-modal caveat:** a non-modal dialog does _not_ trap focus and does _not_ inert the page — that's correct and intended. It still needs a name and a reachable close.
*   **Contrast & target size:** text ≥ 4.5:1, every control ≥ 44px, visible `:focus-visible` ring on all of them.
*   **Keyboard map:**

| Key | Modal dialog | Non-modal dialog |
| ---| ---| --- |
| `Tab` / `Shift+Tab` | cycles within (trapped) | flows to page (not trapped) |
| `Escape` | closes (fires `cancel`) | no default; wire deliberately |
| `Enter` | activates focused / default button | same |
| `Space` | activates focused button | same |

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas (2026-aware)
The dialog looks solved but the platform is moving fast:

*   **The** **`command`** **/** **`commandfor`** **attributes** now open and close dialogs **declaratively, zero JS**: `<button command="show-modal" commandfor="dlg">` opens dialog `#dlg` as modal; `command="close"` closes it. This is the successor to hand-wired open handlers and is shipping across browsers in 2025–2026.
*   **`::backdrop`** **+** **`@starting-style`** **+** **`allow-discrete`** finally make native dialogs animate in _and_ out cleanly without JS timers — the long-standing pain point is solved in modern browsers.
*   **The** **`closedby`** **attribute** (`<dialog closedby="any | closerequest | none">`) declaratively controls light-dismiss: `any` = click-outside closes, `none` = only explicit close, `closerequest` = Escape only. It formalizes the alertdialog "don't light-dismiss" rule in markup.
*   **Invoker + popover convergence:** the `popover` attribute (`popover="auto"` / `"manual"`) covers the _non-modal, light-dismiss_ end of the spectrum natively, letting `<dialog>` focus on the modal end. Knowing which primitive to reach for is the 2026 skill.
*   **View Transitions API** morphs a trigger into its dialog (card → expanded dialog) natively, replacing heavy layout-animation libraries.
*   **Liquid Glass dialogs** on Apple platforms: the sheet surface refracts the dimmed page with a specular rim — the web approximates with layered `backdrop-filter`.
*   **AI-native confirmation dialogs:** "the agent is about to do X — approve?" is becoming a first-class UI, and it's an `alertdialog` by nature: an urgent, blocking, requires-a-response interruption.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers (mistakes that bleed money silently)
None of these throw an error. They just quietly cost you.

*   **The uninvited modal.** A newsletter/discount modal that fires seconds after landing, before the user has seen anything. The most-hated pattern on the web; it spikes bounce and trains people to reflexively close your dialogs — including the ones that matter.
*   **No focus management.** Opening a dialog and leaving focus on the page behind it means keyboard users don't know it opened, and the first Tab lands _behind_ the modal. Instant accessibility failure, invisible in a mouse-only demo.
*   **No visible close.** Escape-only or backdrop-only dismissal with no ✕ or Cancel traps users who don't know the shortcut. Every dismissible dialog needs a _visible_ exit.
*   **Destructive action pre-focused or adjacent to Cancel.** Focus on "Delete," or "Delete" one pixel from "Cancel," guarantees accidental destruction, refunds, and support tickets. Focus the safe action; separate the dangerous one.
*   **Closing on outside-click mid-form.** A user fills a long form dialog, mis-clicks the backdrop, loses everything. Guard dismissal when there are unsaved changes (the `cancel` event) or make forms non-light-dismiss.
*   **No busy state.** User clicks "Confirm payment," nothing visibly happens, they click again → double charge, or they bounce thinking it broke. Disable + spinner + block dismissal on submit.
*   **Modal for a "success" message.** Interrupting to say "Saved!" with a modal the user must dismiss is friction. Use a toast; save the modal for decisions.
*   **Lost scroll / lost place on close.** Not restoring focus (and, on mobile, not locking body scroll) dumps the user at the top of the page. Feels broken even when the action succeeded.
*   **Stacked dialogs.** A dialog opening a dialog is a focus and cognition trap. Redesign the flow.

**The through-line:** these fail _quietly_ — no crash, just a lower conversion number that's easy to blame on "the market." Audit your dialogs before your ad spend.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A dialog feels free; at scale it isn't.

*   **Prefer native** **`<dialog>`** **over a library.** The top layer, focus trap, inert, and Escape ship in the browser — pulling a modal library for a confirm dialog is hundreds of KB for behavior you already have. Reach for Radix/Headless UI when you need cross-browser polish, controlled portals, or complex composition, not for a yes/no.
*   **Render dialogs lazily.** Don't mount 40 row-level confirm dialogs for a 40-row table. Mount one shared dialog and pass it the target, or mount on open. Forty always-present dialogs are forty subtrees the browser tracks for nothing.
*   **Animate only** **`transform`** **and** **`opacity`****\*\*\*\*.** They're compositor-only (no reflow). Animating `width`/`height`/`top` on a dialog thrashes layout every frame. The enter/exit should scale+fade, not resize.
*   **`content-visibility: auto`** on heavy dialog bodies lets the browser skip rendering off-screen content until needed.
*   **Portal cost:** a dialog rendered via a portal (React) still re-renders with its parent unless memoized. In a big list, memoize the dialog so a parent update doesn't re-render it.
*   **Scroll-lock without layout shift:** when you set `overflow: hidden` on `<body>` to lock scroll, compensate for the vanished scrollbar (`scrollbar-gutter: stable` or a padding offset) so the page doesn't jump behind the modal.

## 15\. Security
A dialog is usually the last thing a user sees before a **state-changing action that is destructive or privileged** — "Delete account," "Transfer funds," "Revoke access." The dialog and its confirm button are UI, and UI is never an authorization boundary: the server must independently authorize and verify the action when the request lands, and must never infer permission from the fact that the user "saw the dialog and clicked confirm." Everything rendered _inside_ the dialog — titles and messages assembled from a user-supplied name, filename, or record — has to be escaped or bound as text, because a dialog that interpolates untrusted input through `innerHTML` is a stored-XSS sink wearing the costume of a warning. A dialog that lazy-loads its body from a remote endpoint inherits that endpoint's authorization and output-escaping obligations in full; a modal is a visual layer painted on top, not an access boundary, so "it's only shown to admins" protects nothing when the underlying fetch is callable directly. Because the confirm click fires a mutation, that request needs the usual state-changing defenses — an anti-CSRF token or `SameSite` cookies with non-GET methods, plus an idempotency key so a double-click or a network retry deletes once and charges once. Destructive confirmations should be audit-logged server-side (who, what, when), and the highest-stakes ones — deleting an account, changing a payout bank — should require re-authentication _inside_ the dialog: the confirmation is intent, the re-auth is proof.

```typescript
// The action the confirm button commits — Node/Express
app.post("/api/items/:id/delete", requireAuth, async (req, res) => {
  // AUTHORIZE FIRST — an idempotency replay must never skip the authz check.
  // The dialog being shown/hidden in the UI is NOT authorization.
  if (!can(req.user, "delete", req.params.id)) return res.sendStatus(403);

  // Scope the idempotency record to the actor + resource, never the bare client key,
  // so one user's key can't replay another user's (or another resource's) result.
  const key = req.header("Idempotency-Key");           // double-confirm = one delete
  const existing = await db.findByIdempotencyKey({ key, userId: req.user.id, itemId: req.params.id });
  if (existing) return res.status(200).json(existing);

  const result = await db.deleteItem(req.params.id, { key, userId: req.user.id });
  await audit.log(req.user.id, "item.delete", req.params.id);  // trace destructive acts
  res.status(200).json(result);
});
```

The backend concerns a confirmation dialog silently depends on:

*   **Authorization re-check.** Hiding or disabling the trigger for non-owners is UX. The endpoint must independently verify this user may act on this resource. "The dialog wasn't shown" must never mean "the action can't happen."
*   **Idempotency.** A user who double-clicks "Confirm," or whose network retries, must not delete twice / charge twice. Idempotency key → the second identical request returns the first result. Scope the key to the actor and resource, and check it _after_ authorization — a replayed key must never return another user's result or skip the authz check. This is _why_ the confirm button also disables while busy: defense in depth.
*   **CSRF.** A state-changing action fired from a dialog is a CSRF target. Anti-CSRF token or `SameSite` cookies, and non-GET methods for mutations.
*   **Output escaping.** Titles and messages built from user data must be escaped or bound as text nodes — never `innerHTML` — or the dialog becomes a stored-XSS delivery surface.
*   **Lazy-loaded content authz.** A dialog that fetches remote HTML on open inherits that endpoint's auth and escaping. The modal wrapper adds no protection; the endpoint must stand on its own.
*   **Audit logging.** Destructive confirmations (delete account, transfer ownership) should log who/what/when server-side so the action is traceable.
*   **Re-authentication for the highest stakes.** For "delete account" or "change payout bank," require re-entering a password _inside_ the dialog — the confirmation is intent, the re-auth is proof.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a dialog is "done":

- [ ] Native `<dialog>` + `showModal()`/`show()` used where possible; hand-rolled modality only with a real focus-trap engine.
- [ ] Correct **mode**: modal (`showModal`) for blocking tasks, non-modal (`show`) for coexisting panels.
- [ ] Correct **role**: `alertdialog` for destructive confirms / blocking errors, `dialog` otherwise.
- [ ] Accessible name via `aria-labelledby` to the title; `aria-describedby` to the message for alertdialogs.
- [ ] Initial focus moves _into_ the dialog — first field for forms, **safe** button for alertdialogs.
- [ ] Focus **trapped** in modal mode; **restored** to the trigger on close.
- [ ] Escape closes a modal dialog; destructive alertdialogs require an explicit choice.
- [ ] Background **inert** in modal mode; page stays live in non-modal mode.
- [ ] Busy state disables confirm, shows progress, and **blocks dismissal** mid-action.
- [ ] Unsaved-changes guard on Escape/backdrop (`cancel` event) where it matters.
- [ ] returnValue/close-event (or equivalent) drives the confirm-vs-cancel branch.
- [ ] Visible close/cancel — never Escape-only.
- [ ] Body scrolls, header/footer pinned; `dvh` height cap; full-screen on small viewports.
- [ ] Contrast ≥ 4.5:1; every control ≥ 44px; visible focus ring.
- [ ] Server re-checks auth, validates, and is idempotent regardless of dialog state.
- [ ] `prefers-reduced-motion` honored on enter/exit.
* * *

**Bottom line:** a dialog is the most disruptive move in your UI, and the modal-vs-non-modal split is the decision everything else hangs on. Get the mode right (`showModal` blocks, `show` coexists), the role right (`alertdialog` for the destructive interrupts), and the three pieces of focus management — move in, trap, restore — and you've cleared the bar that separates a hand-rolled `<div>` overlay from a dialog that's usable, accessible, and trustworthy. When you need the deep mechanics of the trap and inert engine, the **Modal** doc is the sibling that goes there. Everything else is skin.
