## 1\. What a Slider Actually Is
A slider is a control for choosing a **value — or a range of values — along a continuum** by dragging a thumb along a track. That's it. But the word gets stretched over four very different things people lump together, and picking the wrong one is the root of most slider pain:

*   **Slider (value input):** the user _sets_ a number by dragging. Volume, brightness, price ceiling, opacity. Interactive, writes a value. This is what this doc is about.
*   **Range slider (two-value input):** two thumbs on one track defining a _band_ — min price and max price, start date and end date. Same pattern, doubled, plus the thumb-crossing problem.
*   **Progress bar:** looks like a filled track but the user can't touch it. It _reports_ a value the system owns (upload 70% done). `role="progressbar"`, not `slider`. See the **Progress bar** doc — the #1 confusion is styling one as the other.
*   **Carousel / "image slider":** a completely different component that happens to share the marketing word "slider." It rotates content panels. Not this. If a client says "add a slider to the homepage," ask which one they mean before you write a line.

The engineering distinction that matters most: **a slider is for imprecise, continuous, low-stakes values where the** **_relative_** **position carries meaning.** Volume at "about two-thirds" is a perfect slider. A price you need to type exactly — like `$1,247.50` — is a terrible slider and a great number input. Rule of thumb: **if the exact number matters more than the feel of the range, use a number input; if the feel of the range matters more than the exact number, use a slider.** The best designs pair them — a slider _and_ a live numeric field bound to the same state, each editable.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The slider is the primary unit of _adjustment_ in an interface — and it carries outsized weight for its size:

*   **It's the accessibility minefield.** The slider is one of the hardest native-feeling controls to build by hand. The web is littered with `<div>`\-based sliders a mouse user loves and a keyboard or screen-reader user cannot operate _at all_. Get the ARIA slider pattern right and you clear a bar most "senior" engineers fail.
*   **It sells directness.** Dragging a value and watching the result change live (volume, crop, filter strength) feels _tactile_ in a way typing never does — which is why sliders dominate media controls, editors, and configurators.
*   **It hides a precision trap.** Sliders _feel_ great and _measure_ badly; a pixel of drag can equal five dollars or five degrees. Without the value shown as **real text** and a keyboard path to nudge it exactly, you've built something satisfying to play with and useless for anything that matters.
*   **Conversion and filter abandonment live here.** In e-commerce the price-range slider is often the most-used filter on the page. If its thumbs cross, snap wrong, or hide the value, users bail on filtering and leave with a worse impression of the whole catalog.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy of a Slider
Every well-built slider is composed of predictable parts:

*   **Track (rail):** the full line the thumb travels — the entire min→max domain.
*   **Fill (range / active track):** the portion from the minimum to the current thumb — the "how far along" bar. On a range slider it's the segment _between_ the two thumbs.
*   **Thumb (handle):** the draggable grabber the user manipulates; it holds keyboard focus. A range slider has two.
*   **Value label / output:** the current value shown as **text** — inline, in a bubble above the thumb, or in a bound field. Non-negotiable (§12).
*   **Tick marks + labels (optional):** notches marking discrete stops, optionally captioned ("Low / Medium / High," "$0 … $500").
*   **Min/max end labels (optional):** the bounds printed at each end.
*   **Hit target:** the _actual_ draggable area — minimum **44×44px**, even when the visible thumb is a tiny 16px dot. The part juniors forget.
*   **Focus ring:** the outline on the focused thumb. Never removed without a replacement.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes, Scale & Density
Slider dimensions are less about a type ramp and more about **track thickness, thumb diameter, and hit area**. A workable scale:

| Size | Track height | Thumb (visual) | Thumb (hit area) | Tick size | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| SM | 2px | 14px | 44px | 2px | Dense toolbars, inline editor controls |
| MD | 4px | 18px | 44px | 3px | Default for most UI |
| LG | 6px | 24px | 48px | 4px | Touch-first, media players, mobile CTAs |
| XL | 8px | 28px | 48px | 5px | Kiosks, TV/remote, accessibility-forward UIs |

Rules that matter:

*   **The visible thumb can be small; the hit area cannot.** A 16px dot with a transparent 44px pad is correct; a 16px dot that _is_ the target is a mis-tap machine on mobile.
*   **Track thickness is aesthetic; thumb size is functional.** Thin tracks read as premium — never shrink the thumb target to match.
*   **Length carries precision.** A 120px slider over a 0–1000 domain gives ~8 units per pixel — unusable for fine values. Lengthen the track, lower resolution with `step`, or pair a number input.
*   **Drive every dimension from a spacing token,** not magic numbers, so the system scales and themes at once.
* * *

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A production slider has far more states than "sitting there." Missing states are the #1 tell of an amateur build.

1. **Default (rest)** — thumb at its value, fill drawn to it.
2. **Hover (thumb)** — subtle grow/elevation. (No hover on touch — never rely on it alone.) Some designs also **hover the track** to preview where a click would jump.
3. **Focus** — thumb reachable by keyboard, **must** have a visible ring via `:focus-visible`. On a range, exactly one thumb is focused at a time and the ring must make _which_ one obvious.
4. **Active / dragging (grabbing)** — thumb enlarges, value bubble shows, `cursor: grabbing`. Sells tactility; the state where the live readout matters most.
5. **Disabled** — reduced opacity, `cursor: not-allowed`, `aria-disabled`; thumb frozen, keyboard inert.
6. **Readonly** — shown and announced but not editable (rare; still focusable so users can read it).
7. **Invalid / out-of-constraint** — value violates a rule (min budget above max). Signal with color _and_ text, wire `aria-invalid`.
8. **At-limit** — thumb pinned at min or max; a subtle cue tells users they hit the wall, not that it froze.

Range-specific: **thumbs colliding** (decide whether they stop touching or one pushes the other) and **which thumb has focus** — the whole keyboard model depends on this being unambiguous, visually and to a screen reader, with each thumb separately labelled ("Minimum price" / "Maximum price").
* * *

## 6\. Types & Variants
*   **Single-thumb (value):** the baseline. One handle, one value.
*   **Range (two-thumb):** two handles defining a band `[min, max]`. The whole extra complexity of this doc lives here — see §6b.
*   **Stepped / discrete:** movement snaps to `step` increments, usually with tick marks. Good when only certain values are valid (1–5 rating, sizes S/M/L).
*   **Continuous:** any value in the domain, no snapping. Good for volume, opacity, "feel."
*   **Vertical:** track runs top-to-bottom (mixers, EQ, some volume controls). Semantically identical; add `aria-orientation="vertical"` and remember that for most vertical sliders **up = more**.
*   **Fill-from-center / bipolar:** the fill grows outward from a zero midpoint for signed values (balance −100…+100, EQ gain in dB). The "start" of the fill is the center, not the min.
*   **Non-linear scales:** the pixel-to-value mapping is logarithmic or exponential (price, frequency, zoom). The thumb moves linearly in space but the value curves. `aria-valuetext` becomes essential because `aria-valuenow` alone lies about perceived position.
*   **Slider + input (dual-bound):** a slider wired to a number field, both editing one state. The professional default for anything where users sometimes need an exact number.

### 6b. The Range Slider — where it gets hard
Two thumbs on one track sounds like "two sliders." It isn't. The problems that only exist here:

*   **Thumb crossing.** When the user drags the min thumb past the max, pick one strategy and enforce it in _both_ pointer and keyboard paths: **(a) clamp** — they stop at each other (more common, less confusing); **(b) swap** — they cross and re-label. A slider that clamps on drag but lets the keyboard cross is a bug.
*   **Z-order at collision.** When both thumbs sit at one spot, the next drag should grab the one that can move _away_ from the pile-up. Track a "last active thumb" and raise its `z-index`.
*   **Track-click moves the** **_nearer_** **thumb,** not always the same one.
*   **Two labels, not one.** Each thumb is its own `role="slider"` with its own `aria-label` ("Minimum price," "Maximum price") and its own `aria-valuenow`/`aria-valuetext`, so users know which handle they're on. The output is a **pair** — show `$40 – $220`, and each thumb's `aria-valuetext` carries its side ("Minimum $40," "Maximum $220").
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a slider when the value is **continuous, bounded, low-stakes, and better felt than typed** — and ideally when the user sees the effect change live.

Great fits: volume, brightness, opacity, zoom, playback seek, image-filter strength, price/date _range_ filters, "how spicy," font size preview.

Reach for something else when:

*   **The exact number matters** → **number input** or stepper (age, quantity, a specific dollar amount).
*   **Only a few discrete choices** → **radio buttons** or a **segmented control** (3–5 options read better with visible, directly-clickable labels than a stepped slider).
*   **It's binary** → a **Switch**, not a two-stop slider (see the **Switch** doc).
*   **Enormous range needing precision at both ends** → inputs, or a log-scale slider _plus_ inputs. A 0–1,000,000 slider is a pixel lottery.
*   **The value is system-owned and non-interactive** → a **Progress bar**, not a slider.
*   **Fine motor control is a barrier.** Dragging is hard for tremor, low-vision, and switch users — always provide the keyboard path and the numeric field as first-class alternatives.

Placement heuristics: keep the readout adjacent and always visible; give the track enough length for the domain's resolution; put min/max context at the ends when the scale isn't obvious; on mobile give the track vertical breathing room so the thumb isn't fighting scroll gestures.

## 8\. Sliders Across Design Systems
Same component, very different philosophies. Knowing these makes you fluent in any codebase.

*   **Material Design (Google):** Prominent thumb with a **value label** that pops above on drag, a state-layer around the thumb, discrete mode with tick dots, accent-color fill. Material 3 adds a chunkier thumb and springier motion. The value bubble is core, not optional.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** Restrained thin track, round white thumb, light shadow, optional min/max "value images" at the ends (the sun icons on brightness), haptics as the thumb passes ticks. In 2026, thumb and track adopt **Liquid Glass**.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** Compact, subtle depth, accent fill, optional ticks, well-supported vertical variant for creative tools.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** Feature-dense — built-in `range`, `marks` (labeled ticks), `tooltip` formatter, `dots`, `included`, vertical, reverse. Made for dashboards and admin filters.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** No slider primitive — style a native range with utilities and vendor pseudo-elements, or drop in a headless lib. Freedom plus the cross-browser thumb tax (§9.2).
*   **shadcn/Radix (2024+ web standard):** Headless behavior and full ARIA from `@radix-ui/react-slider` (single _and_ range, keyboard, orientation), styled with Tailwind. The best-practice answer for React — §9.7.
*   **Bootstrap:** Ships `.form-range`, a styled native range. Single-thumb only; **no built-in two-thumb range** — reach for noUiSlider. §9.12.
*   **noUiSlider (framework-agnostic):** The long-standing vanilla library for serious range/multi-thumb/non-linear sliders without a framework dependency.
* * *

## 8b. Visual Styles & what's trending in 2026
Design systems are _whose_ rules; visual styles are _which look_. The same semantic slider can wear eleven different skins — Flat, Material, Glassmorphism, Liquid Glass, Neumorphism, Skeuomorphism, Neo-Brutalism, Claymorphism, Aurora/Gradient, Minimal/Swiss, and UJG Brand — and it's **skin only**: the semantics, keyboard model, focus ring, and 44px thumb target never change. Each of the eleven is described slider-specifically in **§17**; full cross-component guidance lives on the [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) doc.

**The dominant force in 2026 is Liquid Glass.** Apple shipped it across iOS 26, iPadOS, and macOS Tahoe in late 2025, and the web is racing to approximate the refractive-thumb look with `backdrop-filter`, layered highlights, and the emerging CSS `::slider-track`/`::slider-fill` proposals. Around it: spatial/depth-aware UI carried over from visionOS, Expressive Material 3 (springier motion, chunkier thumbs), motion-forward-but-restrained micro-interactions with `prefers-reduced-motion` respected by default, and a retro-tactile counter-current bringing skeuomorphic mixer-style sliders back. **For UJG:** lean the house style toward a Liquid Glass thumb accent over the deep Eminence/Night track (the dark, saturated rail makes the refraction sing), keep Flat/Swiss for dense data work, and reserve Brutalism/Aurora for campaign moments — contrast and reduced-motion stay non-negotiable no matter how on-trend the skin.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML (the foundation everyone should master first)
The native `<input type="range">` gets you a keyboard-operable, screen-reader-announced, form-associated slider **for free**. Start here and only leave when you truly can't style it or need two thumbs.

```plain
<!-- Single value: native, accessible, form-bound -->
<label for="volume">Volume</label>
<input type="range" id="volume" name="volume"
       min="0" max="100" step="1" value="60"
       oninput="volOut.value = this.value + '%'" />
<output id="volOut" for="volume">60%</output>

<!-- Stepped with visible ticks (native datalist) -->
<label for="spice">Spice level</label>
<input type="range" id="spice" list="spice-ticks"
       min="0" max="4" step="1" value="2"
       aria-valuetext="Medium" />
<datalist id="spice-ticks">
  <option value="0" label="None"></option>
  <option value="1" label="Mild"></option>
  <option value="2" label="Medium"></option>
  <option value="3" label="Hot"></option>
  <option value="4" label="Fire"></option>
</datalist>

<!-- Vertical (CSS drives orientation; see 9.2) -->
<input type="range" id="fader" min="0" max="11" value="7"
       aria-orientation="vertical" style="writing-mode: vertical-lr; direction: rtl" />

<!-- Disabled -->
<input type="range" min="0" max="100" value="30" disabled />
```

Key HTML rules:

*   **Always give it a label** — `<label for>`, `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby`. An unlabeled slider announces as just "slider," which is useless.
*   **`min`****\*\*\*\*,** **`max`****\*\*\*\*,** **`step`** **are the contract.** They set the domain and resolution _and_ feed the browser's built-in `aria-valuemin`/`max`/`now`. Omit `max` and it silently defaults to 100.
*   **`step="any"`** allows continuous (non-snapping) values; a numeric `step` snaps.
*   **Wire** **`<output>`** **with** **`oninput`****\*\*\*\*,** not `onchange`. `input` fires continuously during drag; `change` fires only on release. Users need the number to move _while_ they drag.
*   **Native range has no two-thumb mode.** A range (min/max band) needs two inputs coordinated, or a custom/headless build (§9.3, §9.7).

### 9.2 CSS (states + the cross-browser thumb pain)
Styling a native range is the notorious part: the track and thumb are **vendor pseudo-elements** that don't share syntax across engines, and you often must reset `appearance` first. This is real and unavoidable — there is no single standard selector yet.

```css
input[type="range"] {
  -webkit-appearance: none;   /* kill default chrome */
  appearance: none;
  width: 100%;
  height: 44px;               /* hit area, even though the track looks thin */
  background: transparent;    /* we draw the track ourselves */
  cursor: pointer;
}

/* --- TRACK: must be written twice, no shared selector --- */
input[type="range"]::-webkit-slider-runnable-track {
  height: 4px;
  border-radius: 999px;
  background: #3a2a4d;        /* rail */
}
input[type="range"]::-moz-range-track {
  height: 4px;
  border-radius: 999px;
  background: #3a2a4d;
}
/* Firefox draws the fill for you; WebKit/Blink do not (see JS below) */
input[type="range"]::-moz-range-progress {
  height: 4px;
  border-radius: 999px;
  background: #DCA424;        /* Goldenrod fill */
}

/* --- THUMB: also written per-engine --- */
input[type="range"]::-webkit-slider-thumb {
  -webkit-appearance: none;
  width: 18px; height: 18px;
  margin-top: -7px;           /* center the 18px thumb on the 4px track */
  border-radius: 50%;
  background: #DCA424;
  border: 2px solid #0A0A0A;
}
input[type="range"]::-moz-range-thumb {
  width: 18px; height: 18px;
  border-radius: 50%;
  background: #DCA424;
  border: 2px solid #0A0A0A;
}

/* --- STATES --- */
input[type="range"]:focus-visible::-webkit-slider-thumb {
  outline: 3px solid #DCA424; outline-offset: 3px;   /* visible focus */
}
input[type="range"]:focus-visible::-moz-range-thumb {
  outline: 3px solid #DCA424; outline-offset: 3px;
}
input[type="range"]:active::-webkit-slider-thumb { transform: scale(1.15); }  /* grabbing */
input[type="range"]:disabled { opacity: .5; cursor: not-allowed; }

/* WebKit/Blink don't fill the track natively — paint it with a gradient
   driven by a --val custom property the JS updates (0–100). */
input[type="range"].filled::-webkit-slider-runnable-track {
  background: linear-gradient(
    to right,
    #DCA424 calc(var(--val) * 1%),
    #3a2a4d calc(var(--val) * 1%)
  );
}

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  input[type="range"]::-webkit-slider-thumb { transition: none; }
}
```

The takeaway juniors miss: **WebKit/Blink do not paint a fill.** Firefox gives you `::-moz-range-progress`; Chrome/Safari give you nothing, so you fake the fill with a `linear-gradient` background whose stop is driven by a CSS variable your JS keeps in sync with the value. That one asymmetry is why "just style the range input" turns into an afternoon.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (a proper headless Slider — single + range, keyboard, aria-valuetext)
The reusable, dependency-free version. It implements the **ARIA slider pattern** by hand: each thumb is a focusable `role="slider"` with the full value ARIA and the complete keyboard model, and it supports both single and range with clamping.

```typescript
import { forwardRef, useRef, useState, useCallback, KeyboardEvent, PointerEvent } from "react";

type Format = (value: number) => string;

interface SliderProps {
  min?: number;
  max?: number;
  step?: number;
  /** number = single thumb; [lo, hi] = range (two thumbs) */
  value: number | [number, number];
  onChange: (value: number | [number, number]) => void;
  /** turns 50 into "$50" / "Medium" for aria-valuetext + the visible label */
  format?: Format;
  label?: string;            // single-thumb label
  minLabel?: string;         // range: label for the low thumb
  maxLabel?: string;         // range: label for the high thumb
  disabled?: boolean;
  orientation?: "horizontal" | "vertical";
  /** large step for PageUp/PageDown; defaults to 10× step */
  largeStep?: number;
}

const clamp = (n: number, lo: number, hi: number) => Math.min(hi, Math.max(lo, n));
const roundToStep = (n: number, step: number, min: number) =>
  Math.round((n - min) / step) * step + min;

export const Slider = forwardRef<HTMLDivElement, SliderProps>(function Slider(
  {
    min = 0, max = 100, step = 1,
    value, onChange, format = String,
    label, minLabel = "Minimum", maxLabel = "Maximum",
    disabled = false, orientation = "horizontal", largeStep,
  },
  ref
) {
  const trackRef = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
  const isRange = Array.isArray(value);
  const big = largeStep ?? step * 10;
  const vertical = orientation === "vertical";
  // Which thumb was last grabbed/focused — raised above its sibling so colliding
  // thumbs don't bury the one the user is trying to move.
  const [activeThumb, setActiveThumb] = useState<0 | 1>(0);

  // Map a value to a 0–100% position along the track.
  const pct = (v: number) => ((v - min) / (max - min)) * 100;

  // Move one thumb, clamping so the low thumb can't pass the high one (and vice versa).
  const setThumb = useCallback(
    (index: 0 | 1, next: number) => {
      const snapped = clamp(roundToStep(next, step, min), min, max);
      if (!isRange) return onChange(snapped);
      const [lo, hi] = value as [number, number];
      if (index === 0) onChange([Math.min(snapped, hi), hi]);       // clamp, don't cross
      else onChange([lo, Math.max(snapped, lo)]);
      // swap strategy would instead sort() the pair here.
    },
    [isRange, max, min, onChange, step, value]
  );

  const keyDelta = (e: KeyboardEvent) => {
    switch (e.key) {
      case "ArrowRight": case "ArrowUp":   return step;
      case "ArrowLeft":  case "ArrowDown": return -step;
      case "PageUp":   return big;
      case "PageDown": return -big;
      case "Home":     return "min" as const;
      case "End":      return "max" as const;
      default:         return null;
    }
  };

  const onThumbKeyDown = (index: 0 | 1) => (e: KeyboardEvent) => {
    if (disabled) return;
    const d = keyDelta(e);
    if (d === null) return;
    e.preventDefault();                                  // stop the page scrolling
    const current = isRange ? (value as number[])[index] : (value as number);
    if (d === "min") return setThumb(index, min);
    if (d === "max") return setThumb(index, max);
    setThumb(index, current + d);
  };

  // Pointer drag: translate cursor position on the track into a value.
  const startDrag = (index: 0 | 1) => (e: PointerEvent) => {
    if (disabled) return;
    setActiveThumb(index);                                     // raise this thumb above its sibling
    (e.target as HTMLElement).setPointerCapture(e.pointerId);  // keep tracking off-thumb
    const move = (ev: globalThis.PointerEvent) => {
      const rect = trackRef.current!.getBoundingClientRect();
      const ratio = vertical
        ? 1 - (ev.clientY - rect.top) / rect.height
        : (ev.clientX - rect.left) / rect.width;
      setThumb(index, min + clamp(ratio, 0, 1) * (max - min));
    };
    // One teardown for every way the gesture can end — pointerup OR pointercancel
    // (touch interruption, browser gesture-takeover) — so we never leak `move`.
    const end = () => {
      window.removeEventListener("pointermove", move);
      window.removeEventListener("pointerup", end);
      window.removeEventListener("pointercancel", end);
    };
    move(e.nativeEvent);
    window.addEventListener("pointermove", move);
    window.addEventListener("pointerup", end);
    window.addEventListener("pointercancel", end);
  };

  const thumbs: 0[] | (0 | 1)[] = isRange ? [0, 1] : [0];

  return (
    <div
      ref={ref}
      className="ui-slider"
      data-orientation={orientation}
      data-disabled={disabled || undefined}
    >
      <div ref={trackRef} className="ui-slider__track">
        <div
          className="ui-slider__fill"
          style={
            isRange
              ? vertical
                ? { bottom: `${pct((value as number[])[0])}%`, top: `${100 - pct((value as number[])[1])}%` }
                : { left: `${pct((value as number[])[0])}%`, right: `${100 - pct((value as number[])[1])}%` }
              : vertical
                ? { bottom: 0, height: `${pct(value as number)}%` }
                : { left: 0, width: `${pct(value as number)}%` }
          }
        />
        {thumbs.map((index) => {
          const v = isRange ? (value as number[])[index] : (value as number);
          const thumbLabel = !isRange ? label : index === 0 ? minLabel : maxLabel;
          // Announce the reachable range, not the track ends: the clamp stops the low
          // thumb at the high one (and vice versa), so the sibling is the real bound.
          const [lo, hi] = isRange ? (value as [number, number]) : [min, max];
          const thumbMin = isRange && index === 1 ? lo : min;
          const thumbMax = isRange && index === 0 ? hi : max;
          return (
            <div
              key={index}
              role="slider"
              tabIndex={disabled ? -1 : 0}
              aria-label={thumbLabel}
              aria-orientation={orientation}
              aria-valuemin={thumbMin}
              aria-valuemax={thumbMax}
              aria-valuenow={v}
              aria-valuetext={format(v)}   /* "$50", "Medium" — the human value */
              aria-disabled={disabled || undefined}
              className="ui-slider__thumb"
              style={{
                ...(vertical ? { bottom: `${pct(v)}%` } : { left: `${pct(v)}%` }),
                zIndex: isRange && activeThumb === index ? 1 : undefined,  // last-moved thumb wins collisions
              }}
              onKeyDown={onThumbKeyDown(index)}
              onFocus={() => setActiveThumb(index as 0 | 1)}
              onPointerDown={startDrag(index)}
            />
          );
        })}
      </div>
      <div className="ui-slider__output" aria-hidden="true">
        {isRange
          ? `${format((value as number[])[0])} – ${format((value as number[])[1])}`
          : format(value as number)}
      </div>
    </div>
  );
});
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:

*   **`role="slider"`** **per thumb** with `aria-valuemin/max/now` — native ranges get this free, but a custom build must supply it or it's invisible to assistive tech.
*   **`aria-valuetext`** **carries the** **_human_** **value** ("$50," "Medium," "2:30 PM") because `aria-valuenow="50"` alone announces "50," not "fifty dollars." The single most-skipped attribute in custom sliders.
*   **The full keyboard map** — arrows step, Page Up/Down large-step, Home/End to the ends, `preventDefault()` so arrows don't scroll the page.
*   **Clamping lives in one place** (`setThumb`), so pointer _and_ keyboard obey the same no-cross rule; switching to "swap" is a one-line `sort()`.
*   **`format`** **does double duty** — one function feeds both `aria-valuetext` and the visible readout, so they can't drift apart.

Usage:

```typescript
// Single
const [vol, setVol] = useState(60);
<Slider label="Volume" value={vol} onChange={(v) => setVol(v as number)}
        format={(v) => `${v}%`} />

// Range with human-formatted currency
const [price, setPrice] = useState<[number, number]>([40, 220]);
<Slider value={price} onChange={(v) => setPrice(v as [number, number])}
        min={0} max={500} step={5}
        minLabel="Minimum price" maxLabel="Maximum price"
        format={(v) => `$${v}`} />
```

### 9.4 Vanilla JavaScript (native range + live output + WebKit fill)
The building block everything else abstracts. Note the fill trick and `input` vs `change`.

```javascript
const slider = document.querySelector("#volume");
const out = document.querySelector("#volOut");

function sync() {
  const pct = ((slider.value - slider.min) / (slider.max - slider.min)) * 100;
  slider.style.setProperty("--val", pct);      // drives the WebKit fill gradient
  out.value = slider.value + "%";              // live text readout
  slider.setAttribute("aria-valuetext", slider.value + " percent");
}

slider.addEventListener("input", sync);   // fires continuously WHILE dragging
slider.addEventListener("change", () => { // fires once on release
  analytics.track("volume_committed", { value: Number(slider.value) });
});
sync();  // set initial fill + label
```

The `input`/`change` split is the whole game: paint the fill and update the label on **`input`** so it tracks the drag; fire the expensive/committing work (network save, analytics) on **`change`** so you don't spam it 60×/second while the thumb moves.

### 9.6 Native range has no two-thumb mode — say it plainly
There is no honest native equivalent for a range (two-thumb) slider — `<input type="range">` is single-value, full stop. Your three real options: **(1) Radix/shadcn** **`Slider`** (§9.7) — headless, accessible, `value={[lo, hi]}` out of the box, the default for React; **(2) a headless library** — Radix, React Aria (`useSlider`/`useSliderThumb`), or noUiSlider for vanilla, all of which solve crossing/focus/ARIA; **(3) hand-roll it** (§9.3), owning the clamping, focus, and ARIA forever.

Do **not** fake a range by absolutely-positioning two native inputs atop each other unless you deeply understand the pointer-events and z-index dance — it's the classic source of "the max thumb won't grab near the min thumb" bugs.

### 9.7 shadcn/ui + Radix (the 2024+ standard)
You copy the component into your repo and own it; Radix supplies behavior and the full ARIA slider pattern (single _and_ range, keyboard, orientation), Tailwind supplies looks.

```typescript
"use client";
import * as React from "react";
import * as SliderPrimitive from "@radix-ui/react-slider";
import { cn } from "@/lib/utils";

const Slider = React.forwardRef<
  React.ElementRef<typeof SliderPrimitive.Root>,
  React.ComponentPropsWithoutRef<typeof SliderPrimitive.Root> & {
    /** turns each thumb's value into human text for aria-valuetext */
    format?: (v: number) => string;
    thumbLabels?: string[];   // one label per thumb, e.g. ["Min price","Max price"]
  }
>(({ className, format, thumbLabels, ...props }, ref) => {
  const values = (props.value ?? props.defaultValue ?? [0]) as number[];
  return (
    <SliderPrimitive.Root
      ref={ref}

        "relative flex w-full touch-none select-none items-center " +

        className
      )}
      {...props}
    >
      <SliderPrimitive.Track className="relative h-1 w-full grow rounded-full bg-purple-900">
        <SliderPrimitive.Range className="absolute h-full rounded-full bg-amber-400" />
      </SliderPrimitive.Track>

        <SliderPrimitive.Thumb

```

Usage: `<Slider defaultValue={[40, 220]} min={0} max={500} step={5} thumbLabels={["Min price","Max price"]} format={(v) => \`$${v}\`} />`. Radix renders one` <Thumb>`per value in the array — pass one value for a single slider, two for a range — and it handles crossing, roving focus between thumbs, and the full keyboard model. The one thing Radix _doesn't_ infer is`aria-valuetext`; you must pass` format\` so "$220" is announced instead of "220."

### 9.8 Next.js (App Router: client vs. server)
A slider is interactive (`onChange`, drag), so the control itself is a **Client Component**. But the _committed_ value flows to the server on release — commonly a filter that re-runs a Server Component query via URL search params, no API route needed.

```typescript
// app/products/PriceFilter.tsx
"use client";                                   // it has state + onChange
import { useRouter, useSearchParams, usePathname } from "next/navigation";
import { useState, useTransition } from "react";
import { Slider } from "@/components/ui/slider";

export function PriceFilter() {
  const router = useRouter();
  const pathname = usePathname();
  const params = useSearchParams();
  const [pending, startTransition] = useTransition();
  const [range, setRange] = useState<[number, number]>([
    Number(params.get("min") ?? 0),
    Number(params.get("max") ?? 500),
  ]);

  // Live drag updates local state; commit to the URL only on release (onValueCommit).
  function commit([lo, hi]: number[]) {
    const q = new URLSearchParams(params);
    q.set("min", String(lo));
    q.set("max", String(hi));
    startTransition(() => router.push(`${pathname}?${q}`));  // server re-queries
  }

  return (
    <fieldset aria-busy={pending}>
      <legend>Price: ${range[0]} – ${range[1]}</legend>
      <Slider
        value={range}
        onValueChange={(v) => setRange(v as [number, number])}   // continuous, local
        onValueCommit={commit}                                   // on release → server
        min={0} max={500} step={5}
        thumbLabels={["Minimum price", "Maximum price"]}
        format={(v) => `$${v}`}
      />
    </fieldset>
  );
}
```

The pattern to internalize: **drag updates local state continuously; only the** **_commit_** **(release) hits the server.** Pushing a new query on every `input` frame would fire dozens of navigations per drag. `onValueCommit` (or a debounced effect) is the difference between a snappy filter and a hammered server.

### 9.9 Node.js (the endpoint behind a range filter)
The slider's committed value reaches the server as query params, and the server must **never trust them**. Clamp, coerce, and validate — a hand-crafted URL can send `min=-999&max=9e9`.

```typescript
import express from "express";
import { z } from "zod";

const router = express.Router();

const PriceQuery = z.object({
  // coerce strings from the URL, clamp to the real domain, enforce min<=max
  min: z.coerce.number().min(0).max(500).default(0),
  max: z.coerce.number().min(0).max(500).default(500),
}).refine((q) => q.min <= q.max, { message: "min must be ≤ max" });

router.get("/api/products", async (req, res) => {
  const parsed = PriceQuery.safeParse(req.query);
  if (!parsed.success) return res.status(422).json(parsed.error.issues);
  const { min, max } = parsed.data;

  const products = await db.products.findMany({
    where: { price: { gte: min, lte: max } },   // parameterized, not string-built
  });
  res.json(products);
});

export default router;
```

Backend concerns a slider silently depends on:

*   **Coerce + clamp:** the value arrives as a string and is fully attacker-controllable. Coerce to number, clamp to the domain, reject inverted ranges.
*   **Parameterized queries:** never interpolate the slider value into SQL. A range filter is a classic injection vector when built with string concatenation.
*   **Bounded work:** a `max` of 9e9 shouldn't scan the universe. The clamp is both a correctness _and_ a denial-of-service defense.

### 9.10 Python (backend + a rendered slider)
**Backend** (FastAPI) receiving the committed range:

```python
from fastapi import FastAPI, Query, HTTPException

app = FastAPI()

@app.get("/api/products")
def products(
    min_price: float = Query(0, ge=0, le=500),     # validated + clamped at the edge
    max_price: float = Query(500, ge=0, le=500),
):
    if min_price > max_price:
        raise HTTPException(422, "min_price must be <= max_price")
    return db.query_products(min_price=min_price, max_price=max_price)  # parameterized
```

**Rendering** server-side (Django/Jinja) is still native HTML — Python just fills the bounds and value: `<input type="range" min="{{ floor }}" max="{{ ceiling }}" step="5" value="{{ budget }}" aria-valuetext="${{ budget }}">`. Python's real job is to render correct bounds and defend the endpoint (coerce, clamp, validate, parameterize) exactly like Node does.

**Unit / component (Vitest + React Testing Library).**

```typescript
  it("steps up with ArrowRight and jumps with Home/End", async () => {

    const s = screen.getByRole("slider");

    expect(onChange).toHaveBeenLastCalledWith(0);
    await userEvent.keyboard("{End}");
    expect(onChange).toHaveBeenLastCalledWith(100);
  });

  it("clamps the low thumb so it can't cross the high thumb", async () => {
    // A stateful wrapper feeds each onChange back in as the next `value`, so the
    // component actually re-renders between key presses. A controlled value that
    // never updates makes this test tautological — it can't observe a crossing bug.
    function Harness() {
      const [val, setVal] = React.useState<[number, number]>([40, 45]);
      return (
        <Slider value={val} min={0} max={100} step={5}
                minLabel="Min" maxLabel="Max"
                onChange={(v) => setVal(v as [number, number])} />
      );
    }
    render(<Harness />);
    const low = screen.getByRole("slider", { name: "Min" });
    low.focus();
    await userEvent.keyboard("{ArrowRight}{ArrowRight}{ArrowRight}"); // tries to pass 45
    // With live re-renders, a broken clamp would let valuenow climb past 45.
    expect(low).toHaveAttribute("aria-valuenow", "45");   // stopped at the high thumb, never crossed
  });

  it("does nothing when disabled", async () => {
    const onChange = vi.fn();
    render(<Slider label="Volume" value={60} disabled onChange={onChange} />);
    screen.getByRole("slider").focus();
    await userEvent.keyboard("{ArrowRight}");
    expect(onChange).not.toHaveBeenCalled();
  });
});
```

### 9.15 Web Components (framework-agnostic)
A slider that works in any framework (or none), wrapping the native input and re-exposing the value ARIA:

```typescript
class UISlider extends HTMLElement {
  static observedAttributes = ["min", "max", "step", "value", "disabled", "label"];
  private input!: HTMLInputElement;
  /** human formatter hook: turn the raw value into "$50" / "Medium" for aria-valuetext.
      Set it in JS (el.format = v => `$${v}`) — a bare number lies about units ("$50 vs 50"). */
  format: (value: string) => string = (v) => v;

  connectedCallback() {
    this.attachShadow({ mode: "open" });
    this.render();
  }
  attributeChangedCallback() { if (this.shadowRoot) this.sync(); }

  private render() {
    this.shadowRoot!.innerHTML = `
      <style>input { width: 100%; height: 44px; accent-color: #DCA424; }</style>
      <input type="range" part="input" aria-label="${this.getAttribute("label") ?? "Value"}" />
      <output part="value"></output>`;
    this.input = this.shadowRoot!.querySelector("input")!;
    this.input.addEventListener("input", () => {
      this.setAttribute("value", this.input.value);
      this.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent("change", { detail: Number(this.input.value) }));
      this.sync();
    });
    this.sync();
  }
  private sync() {
    if (!this.input) return;
    for (const a of ["min", "max", "step", "value"] as const) {
      const v = this.getAttribute(a);
      if (v !== null) this.input[a] = v;
    }
    this.input.disabled = this.hasAttribute("disabled");
    // Keep the accessible name in sync — a later `label` change must reach the input.
    this.input.setAttribute("aria-label", this.getAttribute("label") ?? "Value");
    this.input.setAttribute("aria-valuetext", this.format(this.input.value));
    this.shadowRoot!.querySelector("output")!.textContent = this.input.value;
  }
}
customElements.define("ui-slider", UISlider);
// Usage anywhere: <ui-slider label="Volume" min="0" max="100" value="60"></ui-slider>
```

Note `accent-color: #DCA424` — the one-line modern way to brand a native range/checkbox/radio without touching a single vendor pseudo-element. It won't give you a custom fill or two thumbs, but for a quick single-value slider it's the least-effort correct answer.

### 9.16 The Drag & Value Lifecycle
The engine under the slider. Rendering it is half the job; knowing exactly what fires when — and how a pixel becomes a value — is the other half.

**`input`** **vs** **`change`** **(the whole game).** On a native range, `input` fires **continuously while dragging** and on every keyboard step; `change` fires **once, on release** (or on each keyboard commit). Paint the fill and update the readout on `input`; run committing work (network, analytics, URL push) on `change` or a debounced `input`. Wiring the expensive work to `input` fires it 60×/second during a drag.

**Pointer, not mouse.** Bind `pointerdown` and use `setPointerCapture` so the drag keeps tracking even when the cursor leaves the thumb — the classic "thumb stops following my cursor" bug is missing pointer capture.

```plain
thumb.addEventListener("pointerdown", (e) => {
  thumb.setPointerCapture(e.pointerId);   // keep receiving moves outside the element
});
```

**Pixel → value math (the core transform).** Every custom slider reduces to one formula (shown in §9.3's `startDrag`): take the cursor's offset within the track, divide by track length for a 0–1 ratio, map onto `[min, max]`, then snap to `step` with `Math.round((raw - min) / step) * step + min`.

**Snapping, ticks & non-linear scales.** With a `step`, the raw value is quantized to the nearest increment (above). For non-linear scales, ease _after_ the 0–1 ratio — `value = min + (max - min) * Math.pow(ratio, 2)`, or a `Math.exp`/`Math.log` pair for a true logarithmic price/frequency slider — and keep `aria-valuetext` truthful to the eased value. Finally, **throttle the paint, debounce the commit:** position updates ride `requestAnimationFrame` (one paint per frame, not per event); the committing side-effect debounces so a fast drag doesn't queue a hundred saves.

### 9.17 System & Environment States
Production sliders respond to the user's environment, not just your design. The states tutorials skip and audits catch.

**Reduced motion.** Kill thumb-grow springs and the Liquid-Glass sheen for users who get motion sick:

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .ui-slider__thumb { transition: none; }
  .ui-slider__fill  { transition: none; }
}
```

**Forced colors / Windows High Contrast.** In forced-colors mode your track and fill palettes vanish. Lean on system color keywords so the fill stays distinguishable from the rail and the thumb keeps a real border:

```css
@media (forced-colors: active) {
  .ui-slider__track { background: GrayText; }
  .ui-slider__fill  { background: Highlight; }
  .ui-slider__thumb { background: ButtonText; border: 1px solid ButtonFace; forced-color-adjust: none; }
}
```

**RTL (right-to-left).** For Arabic/Hebrew the value axis flips — the max end moves left and the arrow keys follow the visual direction. Native range respects `dir="rtl"`; custom builds must mirror the pixel→value math, swap the arrow-key deltas, and position with logical properties (`inset-inline-start`).

**Touch vs. pointer.** On touch there's no hover, so a hover-only value bubble is invisible to most users — show it on drag (`:active`) too, or keep the readout always-on. Add `touch-action: none` on the track so dragging the thumb doesn't scroll the page.

### 9.18 Accessibility-forced-by-types (TS)
A discriminated union can _require_ per-thumb labels whenever the slider is a range, and require a `format` for `aria-valuetext` whenever values aren't plain numbers — catching a11y bugs at compile time.

```typescript
type Base = { min?: number; max?: number; step?: number; format?: (v: number) => string };

type SingleSlider = Base & {
  value: number;
  onChange: (v: number) => void;
  label: string;              // single slider MUST have a label
};

type RangeSlider = Base & {
  value: [number, number];
  onChange: (v: [number, number]) => void;
  minLabel: string;           // range MUST label BOTH thumbs
  maxLabel: string;
};

type SliderProps = SingleSlider | RangeSlider;
// Now <Slider value={[0,10]} /> won't compile without minLabel AND maxLabel.
```

The type system enforces the "each thumb is separately labelled" rule so a human doesn't have to remember it under deadline.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
The slider is the ARIA pattern engineers most often get wrong. The bar to clear:

*   **Prefer native** **`<input type="range">`****\*\*\*\*.** It gives you role, keyboard, focus, value announcement, and form association for free. Only leave it for two thumbs or un-stylable requirements — and then replicate _all_ of this.
*   **Roles & values (custom builds):** each thumb is `role="slider"` with `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax`, and `aria-valuenow`. Always add **`aria-valuetext`** when the raw number isn't the meaning — `aria-valuenow="50"` + `aria-valuetext="$50"`, or `aria-valuenow="2"` + `aria-valuetext="Medium"`, or a date, or "2:30 PM." Without it, screen-reader users hear a bare number stripped of units.
*   **The keyboard model (memorize it):**
    *   `Left`/`Down` → decrease by one step; `Right`/`Up` → increase by one step.
    *   `Page Down` → decrease by a large step; `Page Up` → increase by a large step (~10% of the range is a good default).
    *   `Home` → jump to minimum; `End` → jump to maximum.
    *   `preventDefault()` on these so the page doesn't scroll.
*   **Labels:** every slider needs a name — `<label for>`, `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby`. On a **range**, each thumb needs its _own_ label ("Minimum price," "Maximum price") so the user knows which handle they're on.
*   **Focus:** on a range, each thumb is focusable and `Tab` moves between them (the APG multi-thumb pattern — both thumbs are in the tab order, not a single roving one); a clearly visible `:focus-visible` ring; never `outline: none` without a replacement.
*   **Orientation:** vertical sliders get `aria-orientation="vertical"`; the arrow-key mapping should match (up = more).
*   **Show the value as text, always.** A tooltip that only appears on hover/drag is not enough — keep a persistent text readout so touch, low-vision, and cognitive-load users always see the number, not just a thumb position.
*   **Contrast:** the fill vs. the rail, and the thumb vs. both, each ≥ 3:1 (non-text UI contrast, WCAG 1.4.11). Never signal "invalid range" with color alone.
*   **Target size ≥ 44px.** The draggable thumb hit area is a minimum 44×44px even when the visible thumb is a tiny dot — expand it with transparent padding.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The slider looks solved but keeps evolving:

*   **Liquid Glass thumbs (2026):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe material on the handle — it refracts the fill behind it and picks up a moving sheen as you drag; the web approximates with layered highlights + `backdrop-filter`.
*   **Haptic detents:** a subtle vibration each time the thumb crosses a tick, so stepped sliders _feel_ their stops. Limited on web (`navigator.vibrate`), first-class on iOS/Android.
*   **`accent-color`** **everywhere:** the one-line native theming property, now broadly supported, brands a native range without vendor pseudo-elements for simple cases.
*   **Slider + number field as the norm:** the paired control (each editable, one state) is becoming the default wherever precision sometimes matters — plus range presets ("Under $50," "$50–150") that set both thumbs at once.
*   **Scrubbing gestures & fine-drag:** media seek bars that slow the drag ratio when the finger moves _away_ from the track (iOS-style precise scrubbing), giving frame-level control on a short track.
*   **CSS** **`::slider-fill`** **/** **`::slider-track`** **proposals:** the platform is inching toward standardized cross-browser fill styling, so the WebKit-gradient hack (§9.2) eventually dies.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers (mistakes that bleed money silently)
None of these throw an error. They just quietly make the control frustrating, which makes them dangerous. Every one is fixable in an afternoon.

*   **No text value — position only.** A thumb with no number is a guessing game; users can't tell "$180" from "$220" by pixel. Show the value as live text, both ends on a range (`$40 – $220`). Related: **value on hover/drag only** — a bubble that vanishes on release leaves touch users (no hover) with nothing. Keep a persistent readout.
*   **Tiny thumb / tiny hit area.** A 12px dot with a 12px target is a mis-tap machine on mobile. 44px hit area minimum, always.
*   **No keyboard path.** A `<div>`\-based slider that only responds to drag locks out keyboard and switch users entirely — and often fails an audit outright. The single most common slider defect.
*   **Precision mismatch.** A 0–1,000,000 salary slider where one pixel = $4,000 is unusable — and forcing users to hit exactly `$1,247` by drag is cruel. Match `step`/track length to the domain, or pair a number input; use a slider only when "about right" is genuinely fine.
*   **Thumbs that cross wrong.** A range where the min silently passes the max (or two thumbs jam and neither will grab) feels broken. Pick clamp _or_ swap and enforce it in every input path.
*   **`change`****\*\*\*\*-only updates.** If the fill and readout only update on release, the drag feels dead. Update on `input`.
*   **Scroll-jacking on mobile.** A horizontal slider without `touch-action: none` fights page scroll; users scroll when they meant to nudge. Isolate the gesture.

**The through-line:** these all fail _quietly_. No log, no crash — just a filter people abandon and a control that feels cheap. Audit your sliders before you blame the funnel.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
Techniques that lean on the platform and the type system instead of custom JavaScript.

### Slider bound to a number input (both editable, one state)
The professional default. The slider gives feel; the input gives precision; they share state so neither drifts.

```plain
<label for="budget">Budget</label>
<input type="range" id="budget" min="0" max="500" step="5" value="220"
       oninput="budgetNum.value = this.value" />
<input type="number" id="budgetNum" min="0" max="500" step="5" value="220"
       oninput="budget.value = this.value" aria-label="Budget (exact)" />
```

Each control writes the other; clamp both to the same domain so a typed `9999` snaps back to `500`. (For a non-linear/logarithmic price or frequency scale, apply the `Math.exp`/`Math.log` easing from §9.16 to the ratio, and keep `aria-valuetext` honest to the eased value.)

### Design tokens (theme, dark mode, cross-platform)
Drive every slider dimension and color from tokens so a rebrand or dark-mode swap touches one block, not every slider.

```css
:root {
  --slider-track-h: 4px;
  --slider-thumb-size: 18px;
  --slider-hit: 44px;
  --slider-rail: #3a2a4d;
  --slider-fill: var(--color-accent, #DCA424);
  --slider-thumb: var(--color-accent, #DCA424);
  --slider-focus: var(--color-accent, #DCA424);
}
@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) {
  :root { --slider-rail: #241733; }   /* lift the rail so the fill still reads */
}
```

### `aria-live` for range-summary feedback
When both thumbs move a filtered result count, announce the outcome politely so screen-reader users hear "142 products from $40 to $220," not silence:

```plain
<p id="filter-status" aria-live="polite" class="sr-only"></p>
<!-- on commit: status.textContent = `${count} products from $${lo} to $${hi}` -->
```

### The systems view
Treat a slider as a **system component**, not a styled input: clear semantics (`role="slider"` or native), a documented API (min/max/step/value/orientation/format/labels), the full keyboard model, `aria-valuetext` for human values, a persistent text readout, a 44px thumb target, enforced range-crossing rules, and tests that assert the announced value and keyboard — not pixels. That's what separates a one-off slider from a slider _architecture_.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A slider feels free. Dragged 60 times a second across a list of results, it isn't.

*   **Throttle the paint to** **`requestAnimationFrame`****\*\*\*\*.** Thumb position and fill update once per frame, not once per `pointermove` event (which fires far faster). Batch the DOM write in a rAF callback.
*   **Debounce the commit.** The expensive side-effect — network query, URL push, analytics, re-render of a big result list — must not run on every `input` frame. Fire it on `change`/`onValueCommit` or debounce it. This is the #1 slider perf mistake: a price filter that re-queries the server on every pixel of drag.
*   **Animate only** **`transform`****/****`opacity`****\*\*\*\*.** Position the thumb with `transform: translateX()` (compositor-only, no reflow), not `left`. Add `will-change: transform` on `pointerdown` and remove it on `pointerup` — don't leave it on permanently.
*   **Prefer the native input** when you can: zero JS, zero bundle, and the browser's drag handling beats anything hand-written. Radix `Slider` is a few KB and tree-shakes; noUiSlider is heavier but self-contained; for a single value, native + `accent-color` wins on bundle.
* * *

## 15\. Security (range filters reach the server)
Most sliders are client-only (volume, opacity) and carry no security weight. The moment a slider's value hits an endpoint — a price/date range filter, a "set quota" control — the server rules from §9.9 apply:

*   **Never trust the value.** It arrives as a string on a URL or in a body and is fully attacker-controllable. Coerce to number, clamp to the real domain, reject inverted ranges (`min > max`).
*   **Parameterize the query.** A range that builds `WHERE price BETWEEN ${min} AND ${max}` by string concat is an injection hole. Bind parameters.
*   **Bound the work.** An unclamped `max` can turn a filter into a table-scan DoS. The clamp is a performance _and_ security control.
*   **Authorize the action.** A "set spending limit" slider must re-check on the server that _this_ user may set _that_ limit — the UI constraint is a hint, never the boundary.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a slider is "done":

- [ ] Native `<input type="range">` unless two thumbs / un-stylable needs force a custom build.
- [ ] Every slider has an accessible name; each range thumb is labelled separately.
- [ ] `role="slider"` + `aria-valuemin`/`valuemax`/`valuenow` present (native gives these free).
- [ ] `aria-valuetext` set whenever the raw number isn't the meaning ("$50," "Medium," a time).
- [ ] Full keyboard model: arrows step, PageUp/Down large-step, Home/End to the ends, `preventDefault` on all.
- [ ] Current value shown as **persistent text**, not just thumb position (both ends on a range).
- [ ] Thumb hit area ≥ 44×44px even when the visible thumb is small.
- [ ] Visible `:focus-visible` ring; each range thumb focusable with Tab moving between them (one focused at a time).
- [ ] Range crossing rule chosen (clamp or swap) and enforced in pointer **and** keyboard paths.
- [ ] Fill/readout update on `input`; committing work on `change`/commit (debounced).
- [ ] `prefers-reduced-motion`, forced-colors, and RTL handled; `touch-action: none` on the track.
- [ ] Fill-vs-rail and thumb contrast ≥ 3:1; state never signalled by color alone.
- [ ] Server coerces, clamps, validates, and parameterizes any value that reaches it.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the eleven skins, behavior unchanged)
The same semantic slider rendered across the eleven visual languages. This is **skin only** — the `role="slider"`, the keyboard model, the focus ring, the persistent value readout, and the 44px thumb target are identical in every one.

*   **Flat:** solid rail and fill, plain round thumb, no shadow. Fast, legible, honest.
*   **Material:** accent fill, state-layer halo, a value bubble on drag, tick dots in discrete mode; Material 3 chunkier and springier.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent rail and thumb over a vivid background with `backdrop-filter: blur()`. Guard contrast — keep a rim so a glass thumb doesn't vanish on a busy photo.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026):** a refractive glass-pill thumb bending the fill behind it, with a specular rim and a drag-reactive sheen. Web = layered highlights + blur; SwiftUI emits `.glassEffect()`. Best on the deep UJG track; honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Neumorphism:** soft extruded-puck thumb, inset-groove track, dual light/dark shadows on a same-color surface. Low contrast — accent only.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** the physical mixer — beveled metal thumb, inset track with inner shadow, engraved ticks. Tactile and literal.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black-bordered rail, hard offset shadow on a chunky thumb, zero blur, clashing fill. Loud and high-contrast by nature.
*   **Claymorphism:** puffy rounded thumb, inner top-light, soft drop shadow, big radius, pastel fill. Friendly and playful.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue gradient fill on a dark rail; keep the thumb high-contrast against it and honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** hairline rail, small precise thumb, value in clean type doing the talking. Near-zero decoration.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod fill and thumb over a deep Eminence/Night rail, confident radius, warm focus glow — the house default and the ideal dark backdrop for a Liquid Glass thumb accent.

**Rule that never changes:** style is skin, behavior is the skeleton. Guard glass / liquid-glass / neumorphism for contrast (fill vs. rail, thumb vs. both); guard aurora / liquid-glass for `prefers-reduced-motion`. Full guidance on the [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) doc.
* * *

**Bottom line:** a slider is a tiny control with a huge accessibility blast radius. Anyone can drag a thumb; the senior build is the one where the keyboard steps, `aria-valuetext` speaks the _human_ value ("$50," not "50"), the thumb has a 44px target you can't see, two thumbs never cross wrong, the value lives in text and not just position, and the number that reaches the server gets clamped before it touches the database. Get those right and you've cleared a bar most engineers don't even know is there. Cross-link the **Progress bar** (its non-interactive twin), the **Audio** seek bar (a slider in disguise), and the **Switch** (its binary cousin) — together they're the whole family of "pick a value" controls.
