## 1\. What an Overlay Actually Is
An overlay is **content rendered above the normal document flow, on its own layer, usually over a scrim**. It is not a widget — it's the _substrate_ a whole family of widgets is built on. The word gets lumped together with five things that are actually distinct:
*   **Overlay (the primitive):** the layer itself. A portal or top-layer element, an optional scrim, a stacking position, and a dismiss contract. Everything below is an overlay _plus_ a role.
*   **Modal:** an overlay that **blocks** — it traps focus, makes the background `inert`, and demands a decision before you continue. `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"`.
*   **Dialog (non-modal):** an overlay you can interact around. It floats above but doesn't seal off the page. Same `role="dialog"`, no `aria-modal`.
*   **Popover / Menu / Tooltip:** a **non-modal, light-dismiss** overlay anchored to a trigger. Closes on outside click or `Escape`, never traps focus.
*   **Drawer / Sheet:** a modal (usually) overlay that enters from an edge. Mechanically a modal with a slide transition and edge anchoring.
*   **Toast / Snackbar:** an overlay that **never takes focus** and auto-dismisses. Announced via a live region, not trapped.

The single most common architecture mistake: **rebuilding the scrim, the scroll-lock, the focus logic, and the z-index guesswork separately inside each of those five.** That's how you end up with a modal that traps focus correctly and a drawer that doesn't, a popover that locks scroll when it shouldn't, and four different z-index values fighting each other. The senior move is to build **one Overlay primitive** and let Modal, Drawer, Popover, and Toast configure it. Rule of thumb: **if it renders above the page, it's an overlay; what it does up there — block or float, trap or light-dismiss — is a role you pass in, not a component you fork.**
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The overlay is where an interface **interrupts** the user, and interruption is a loaded act. Get the layer wrong and the failures are severe and expensive — because they're structural, not cosmetic.
*   **It's the #1 source of accessibility lawsuits.** Modals that don't trap focus, that leave the background reachable, that can't be closed with `Escape`, that never move focus in or restore it out — these are the exact failures ADA/WCAG audits flag first. A broken overlay layer fails an entire product's compliance in one component.
*   **Conversion lives on it.** Checkout confirmations, upsell modals, cookie/consent sheets, paywalls, "are you sure?" dialogs — the highest-stakes moments in a funnel happen on an overlay. A scrim that reads as "the page froze," a dialog you can't dismiss on mobile, a scroll-jump when it opens — each one bleeds completed purchases.
*   **It sets the trust tone instantly.** When a dialog opens and the page _jumps_ because a scrollbar vanished, or the scrim doesn't block clicks and you fire an action on the page behind it, the app feels broken even when the logic is perfect. Perceived quality of an app is disproportionately "how do its overlays feel."
*   **It's a security surface.** Clickjacking is _literally an overlay attack_ — a hostile page floats an invisible layer over yours. Understanding overlays is understanding the defense.

Build the primitive once, correctly, and every modal, drawer, and popover in the product inherits correct focus, correct scroll-lock, correct dismissal, and correct stacking — for free. That leverage is why this is the keystone doc.

## 3\. Anatomy of an Overlay
Every well-built overlay is composed of predictable parts. Most bugs are a missing part.
*   **Portal / render target:** where the overlay DOM actually mounts. Almost never inline where it's declared — it's teleported to `document.body` or the browser's **top layer** so no ancestor `overflow`, `transform`, or `z-index` can clip or trap it.
*   **Scrim (a.k.a. backdrop / overlay veil):** the dimming layer between the page and the overlay content. Signals "the thing behind is paused," directs the eye, and — for modals — intercepts clicks. Native `<dialog>` gives you `::backdrop` for free.
*   **Surface / panel:** the actual content container — the dialog box, the drawer sheet, the popover card. This is what the _sibling_ docs style; the primitive just positions it.
*   **Stacking context / layer position:** where this overlay sits relative to others. Either a **z-index scale** (old way) or the **top layer** (new way), which orders by insertion, not by number.
*   **Focus scope:** the boundary focus is allowed to move within. A **trap** for modals (focus can't leave), a looser **scope** for non-modal (focus returns to trigger on close, but you can click out).
*   **Dismiss contract:** how it closes. `Escape`, scrim click, outside click, a close button, a timeout — and which of these are enabled defines _modal vs light-dismiss_.
*   **Scroll lock:** freezing the background so the page behind doesn't scroll under the overlay — done _without_ the layout shifting when the scrollbar disappears.
*   **Inert background:** the rest of the page marked `inert` so it's unreachable by pointer, keyboard, and assistive tech while the overlay is open.
*   **The overlay manager / context:** the coordinator that knows what's open, in what order, which one owns `Escape` right now, and when to release the scroll lock. The part that separates a component from an _architecture_.

## 4\. Scale, Depth & the Z-Index Stack
Overlays don't have "sizes" like buttons — they have **stacking depth**. The single most valuable thing you can standardize is a **layer scale**: a named, documented set of z-index tiers so nobody ever writes `z-index: 9999` again. `9999` is the universal tell of a developer who lost a stacking fight.

| Layer | Token | z-index | What lives here |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Base | `--z-base` | 0 | Normal page content |
| Sticky | `--z-sticky` | 100 | Sticky headers, sticky table columns |
| Dropdown | `--z-dropdown` | 1000 | Select menus, autocomplete lists |
| Overlay scrim | `--z-scrim` | 1400 | The dimming backdrop |
| Modal / Drawer | `--z-modal` | 1500 | Dialog and drawer surfaces |
| Popover | `--z-popover` | 1600 | Anchored popovers above modals |
| Toast | `--z-toast` | 1700 | Notifications, always on top |
| Tooltip | `--z-tooltip` | 1800 | The very top — tooltips beat everything |

Rules that matter:
*   **Every overlay reads a token, never a literal.** One scale, one file, one source of truth. A new overlay slots into an existing tier instead of inventing a bigger number.
*   **Leave gaps (100s, not 1s)** so you can insert a layer later without renumbering the world.
*   **The top layer makes most of this obsolete.** Elements promoted to the browser's top layer (native `<dialog>`, Popover API) paint above _all_ z-index regardless of value — a `z-index: 2147483647` element still renders _below_ a top-layer dialog. When you're all-in on the top layer, ordering is by **insertion order**, not number: the last one opened wins. The z-scale still matters for the non-top-layer overlays you can't or won't promote (custom drawers, legacy popovers, toasts).
* * *

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
An overlay has more states than a button because it has a _lifecycle_ — it animates in, lives, and animates out, and each transition is a place to leak a bug.
1. **Closed / unmounted** — not in the DOM at all (the correct default; don't render-then-hide with `display:none`, that's a focus-order and perf trap).
2. **Opening (enter transition)** — mounting + animating in. Focus must _not_ move until it's actually present; the scroll lock engages _now_.
3. **Open (rest)** — fully present. Scrim at target opacity, focus inside, background `inert`, scroll locked.
4. **Focused-within** — the active descendant inside the overlay has focus; `Tab` cycles within the trap (modal) or scope (non-modal).
5. **Closing (exit transition)** — animating out but _still in the DOM_. The #1 overlay bug lives here: unmount too early and the exit animation never plays; unmount too late and focus/scroll-lock leak. This is what Radix `Presence` and Framer `AnimatePresence` exist to solve.
6. **Dismissing** — the moment a close is _requested_ (Escape, scrim click, outside click). A guarded modal can _veto_ this ("You have unsaved changes").
7. **Stacked / behind** — this overlay is open but another opened on top. It should become inert-to-input while the top one owns focus and `Escape`.
8. **Scroll-locked** — background frozen. Sub-state: whether the lock compensated for scrollbar width (no layout shift) or not (the amateur jump).

Component-specific states the sibling widgets add: **loading** (async content inside a dialog), **error/confirm** (destructive "are you sure"), **anchored/repositioned** (a popover flipping when it hits the viewport edge), **auto-dismiss countdown** (a toast).

## 6\. Types & Variants (by behavior, not by look)
The overlay family splits on **two axes**: does it _block_ (modal vs non-modal), and does it _anchor_ (positioned vs centered/edge). Everything else is skin.

| Variant | Blocks? | Traps focus? | Dismiss | Scroll lock | Anchored? |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Modal dialog | Yes | Yes | Escape + explicit | Yes | No (centered) |
| Non-modal dialog | No | No (scoped) | Escape + explicit | No | No |
| Drawer / sheet | Usually yes | Usually yes | Escape + scrim | Yes | Edge |
| Popover / menu | No | No (scoped) | Light (outside/Esc) | No | Yes (to trigger) |
| Tooltip | No | No | Hover/blur | No | Yes |
| Toast / snackbar | No | Never | Timeout + close | No | Corner |

The two behavioral archetypes to burn into memory:
*   **Modal overlays block + trap.** They seal the page: scrim intercepts clicks, background goes `inert`, focus is trapped, scroll is locked, `Escape` closes the top one. Use when the user _must_ deal with this before anything else. Overuse is the sin — most "modals" should be non-modal.
*   **Non-modal / light-dismiss overlays float.** No trap, no inert background, no scroll lock. Focus is _scoped_ (returns to the trigger on close) but you can freely click and scroll the page; doing so _dismisses_ the overlay. Use for menus, popovers, comboboxes — anything auxiliary to the page, not a gate across it.

A single Overlay primitive expresses all of this by taking `modal`, `dismissible`, `trapFocus`, `lockScroll`, and `anchor` as props. Same machine, different switches.
* * *

## 7\. When to Reach for One (and When Not To)
Use an overlay when content must **rise above the page** — either to demand a decision (modal) or to offer contextual, dismissible extra (non-modal). Don't reach for one when:
*   **The content belongs on the page.** A modal that's really just a form section is friction; inline it. The best overlay is often no overlay.
*   **You'd stack a modal on a modal on a modal.** Nesting modals is a smell — usually a wizard that should be a multi-step _single_ surface, or a flow that should be a route.
*   **It's a confirmation you could make undoable.** "Are you sure?" modals interrupt everyone to catch a rare mistake. An action + an undo toast is usually better UX and _less_ overlay.
*   **It's persistent, page-level chrome.** Nav, filters, and side panels that stay open aren't overlays — they're layout. Don't scrim what shouldn't be dismissed.

Placement & behavior heuristics:
*   **Modals center** (or near-top on mobile so the keyboard doesn't shove them off-screen). **Drawers hug an edge.** **Popovers anchor to their trigger and flip when they'd clip.**
*   **One modal at a time.** If a second must open, the first should close or the stack must be a managed, ordered set (see §9.3).
*   **Match the dismiss to the stakes.** Light-dismiss (click-out closes) for low-stakes menus; require an explicit choice for anything destructive or unsaved.

## 8\. Overlays Across Design Systems
Same primitive, very different philosophies. Knowing these makes you fluent in any codebase.
*   **Material Design (Google):** Scrim is a defined token (`scrim` at ~32% black). Dialogs, bottom sheets, and menus are distinct components but share the scrim + elevation model. Elevation (shadow) _and_ the scrim communicate layering. Full-screen dialogs on mobile.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** Sheets are the signature — cards that slide up and can be _detented_ (half/full height), with the parent scaling back behind them. iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe render these in **Liquid Glass**. Popovers have a native arrow anchor. Alerts are deliberately austere and centered.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** Dialogs, `Panel` (their drawer), `Callout` (their popover), and `Teaching Bubble`. Acrylic/mica materials tint the surface; light-dismiss is a first-class configurable on Callouts.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** `Modal`, `Drawer`, `Popover`, `Popconfirm`, `Tooltip` — a dense, explicit family with `maskClosable`, `keyboard` (Escape), and `getContainer` (portal target) props exposed directly. Built for data-heavy admin UIs where overlays are everywhere.
*   **Radix / shadcn (2024+ web standard):** The gold standard for _behavior_. `Dialog`, `AlertDialog`, `Popover`, `DropdownMenu`, `HoverCard`, `Sheet` all share the same primitives underneath — `Portal`, `Overlay` (the scrim), `Presence` (mount/unmount animation), `FocusScope`, `DismissableLayer`, and `RemoveScroll`. This doc is essentially a first-principles rebuild of that layer.
*   **Bootstrap:** `Modal` and `Offcanvas` (drawer) as jQuery-era-descended components with `data-bs-*` attributes and a `.modal-backdrop` element. Manages a `body.modal-open` class and its own scroll-lock padding.
*   **Headless UI (Tailwind):** `Dialog` + `Transition` — unstyled behavior, you bring the skin. Popular pairing with Tailwind projects that don't want Radix.

## 8b. Visual Styles (the same scrim, eleven aesthetics)
The overlay's _behavior_ never changes with the skin — only the scrim treatment and the surface material do. See them all live in the [Design Styles gallery](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551).
*   **Flat:** solid scrim at a fixed opacity (e.g. `rgba(10,10,10,.5)`), crisp-edged surface, no shadow. Reads instantly, cheapest to render.
*   **Material:** scrim token + elevation shadow on the surface; the shadow _and_ the scrim both signal depth.
*   **Glassmorphism:** scrim uses `backdrop-filter: blur()` so the page behind is frosted, not just dimmed. Watch contrast — text on frosted glass fails AA fast.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language. The surface is a refractive translucent material with a specular rim and a sheen that moves with content behind it — glassmorphism grown up. The _scrim_ becomes a graduated blur-plus-tint rather than a flat dim. SwiftUI emits it natively with `.glassEffect()`; the web is an honest approximation with layered `backdrop-filter` + highlights. Guard it for `prefers-reduced-motion` and contrast.
*   **Neumorphism:** soft same-color surface with dual shadows; scrim is a very light tint. Contrast-poor — accent use only.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** the surface looks like a physical card/tray with bevels and inner highlights; scrim is a heavy dim to sell "the real object floating."
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** no scrim blur, a hard flat dim, thick-bordered surface with a hard offset shadow, zero radius. High personality.
*   **Claymorphism:** puffy surface, big radius, soft inner light; scrim is a soft warm dim.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue scrim or surface glow on dark. Premium — honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-invisible scrim (a faint tint), typography and generous whitespace carry the surface; sharp corners.
*   **UJG Brand:** Afro-Futurist house style — a deep tinted scrim toward Eminence/Night (**not** flat black), the surface on `#042D1D`/`#0A0A0A` with a warm Goldenrod-glow rim and a confident radius. On the dark saturated backdrop, a Liquid-Glass scrim _sings_.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin. The overlay's portal target, focus trap, scroll lock, inert background, `Escape` handling, and 44px close-target stay identical across all eleven. And the design lesson buried in the whole list: **never a pure-black scrim.** `#000` at 50% is the tell of a default no one tuned. Tint it toward the brand hue (for UJG, toward Eminence) and it reads intentional. Also non-negotiable regardless of skin: a scrim that blurs must still let the surface's text hit 4.5:1, and any animated scrim collapses to a static dim under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 9\. The Code
The biggest section. We build the primitive four ways — native top-layer first (because it's the future and the least code), then the portal + scrim React primitive (the workhorse), then the overlay-manager context (the architecture), then the Radix reality (what you'll actually ship on) — and finish with testing.

### 9.1 The Native Top Layer (`<dialog>` + `::backdrop` + Popover API)
Start here because in 2026 this is the _right default_ and it eliminates most of the hard parts. The browser's **top layer** is a special painting layer _above_ the entire document, outside the normal stacking context. Elements promoted to it — via `<dialog>.showModal()` or the Popover API — paint above everything regardless of z-index, escape any ancestor `overflow`/`transform` clipping, and get a real `::backdrop` pseudo-element for the scrim. No portal needed. The browser _is_ the portal.

```plain
<!-- MODAL: blocks, traps focus, inert background — ALL from the browser -->
<dialog id="confirm">
  <form method="dialog">
    <h2>Delete workspace?</h2>
    <p>This can't be undone.</p>
    <button value="cancel">Cancel</button>
    <button value="confirm" autofocus>Delete</button>
  </form>
</dialog>
<button type="button" id="open">Delete…</button>

<!-- NON-MODAL / light-dismiss: Popover API, zero JS to wire -->
<button popovertarget="menu">Options</button>
<div id="menu" popover>
  <button>Rename</button>
  <button>Duplicate</button>
</div>
```

```js
const dlg = document.getElementById("confirm");
document.getElementById("open").addEventListener("click", () => dlg.showModal());
// .showModal() (not .show()) is what promotes to the top layer AND gives you:
//   - focus trapped inside the dialog
//   - the rest of the page made inert automatically
//   - Escape closes it, for free
//   - a ::backdrop element you can style as the scrim
dlg.addEventListener("close", () => {
  console.log("returnValue:", dlg.returnValue); // "confirm" | "cancel"
});
```

Style the scrim through the pseudo-element — no separate scrim `<div>` exists:

```css
#confirm::backdrop {
  /* tinted toward Eminence, NOT flat black */
  background: color-mix(in oklch, #5F2C82 30%, #0A0A0A);
  opacity: .55;
  backdrop-filter: blur(2px);
}
#confirm[open]::backdrop { animation: fade .2s ease; }
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  #confirm[open]::backdrop { animation: none; }
}
/* Light-dismiss the popover for free; :popover-open is the styling hook */
#menu:popover-open { /* enter styles */ }
```

What `showModal()` hands you for free — the exact list juniors reimplement badly:
*   **Top-layer promotion** — beats all z-index, escapes `overflow:hidden` ancestors.
*   **Focus trap** — `Tab` cycles inside the dialog, can't reach the page.
*   **Inert background** — the rest of the document is non-interactive automatically.
*   **`Escape`** **to close** — wired in.
*   **`::backdrop`** — a real scrim element to style.

What it _doesn't_ solve, so you still own it: **scroll-locking the background** (`<dialog>` does _not_ lock body scroll — see §9.5), **enter/exit animation** (needs `@starting-style` + `transition-behavior: allow-discrete` to animate a top-layer element in and out), and **stacking your own non-top-layer overlays** around it. The Popover API adds `popovertarget` for declarative light-dismiss menus/tooltips with no JS at all — `popover="auto"` light-dismisses, `popover="manual"` doesn't. This is the direction the whole platform is moving; build new work on it and reach for the portal approach below only where you need to support older engines or need behavior the native elements don't yet give you.

### 9.2 The Portal + Scrim React Overlay Primitive (the workhorse)
The reusable component the rest of the family is built from. This is the long block — read the comments, they're the lesson. It portals to `document.body`, renders a tinted scrim, traps _or_ scopes focus, locks scroll, marks the background `inert`, and wires the full dismiss contract. Modal, Drawer, and Popover all render _this_ with different props.

```typescript
import {
  useEffect, useRef, useCallback, useId, type ReactNode,
} from "react";
import { createPortal } from "react-dom";

type OverlayProps = {
  open: boolean;
  onClose: () => void;
  /** modal = block + trap + inert + scroll-lock. non-modal = float + light-dismiss. */
  modal?: boolean;
  /** clicking the scrim requests close (guardable by the caller) */
  dismissOnScrimClick?: boolean;
  /** Escape requests close */
  dismissOnEscape?: boolean;
  /** lock background scroll while open (defaults to `modal`) */
  lockScroll?: boolean;
  labelledBy?: string;   // id of the surface's heading
  describedBy?: string;
  children: ReactNode;
  className?: string;
};

export function Overlay({
  open,
  onClose,
  modal = true,
  dismissOnScrimClick = true,
  dismissOnEscape = true,
  lockScroll = modal,
  labelledBy,
  describedBy,
  children,
  className = "",
}: OverlayProps) {
  const surfaceRef = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
  const lastFocused = useRef<HTMLElement | null>(null);

  // 1. FOCUS MANAGEMENT — remember where we came from, move focus in,
  //    restore it on close. Skipping restore is the silent a11y failure.
  useEffect(() => {
    if (!open) return;
    lastFocused.current = document.activeElement as HTMLElement;
    const surface = surfaceRef.current;
    // move focus to the first focusable, or the surface itself
    const first = surface?.querySelector<HTMLElement>(
      '[autofocus], button, [href], input, select, textarea, [tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"])'
    );
    (first ?? surface)?.focus();
    return () => lastFocused.current?.focus(); // RESTORE — non-negotiable
  }, [open]);

  // 2. FOCUS TRAP (modal only). Non-modal overlays scope focus but let it leave.
  const onKeyDown = useCallback(
    (e: React.KeyboardEvent) => {
      if (e.key === "Escape" && dismissOnEscape) {
        e.stopPropagation();
        onClose();
        return;
      }
      if (e.key !== "Tab" || !modal) return;
      const focusables = surfaceRef.current?.querySelectorAll<HTMLElement>(
        'button, [href], input, select, textarea, [tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"])'
      );
      if (!focusables?.length) return;
      const list = Array.from(focusables).filter((el) => !el.hasAttribute("disabled"));
      const first = list[0];
      const last = list[list.length - 1];
      // wrap the tab cycle so focus can never escape the modal
      if (e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === first) {
        e.preventDefault();
        last.focus();
      } else if (!e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === last) {
        e.preventDefault();
        first.focus();
      }
    },
    [modal, dismissOnEscape, onClose]
  );

  // 3. SCROLL LOCK WITHOUT LAYOUT SHIFT — compensate for the scrollbar width
  //    so the page behind doesn't jump when the scrollbar disappears.
  useEffect(() => {
    if (!open || !lockScroll) return;
    const scrollbar = window.innerWidth - document.documentElement.clientWidth;
    const { overflow, paddingRight } = document.body.style;
    document.body.style.overflow = "hidden";
    if (scrollbar > 0) document.body.style.paddingRight = `${scrollbar}px`;
    return () => {
      document.body.style.overflow = overflow;
      document.body.style.paddingRight = paddingRight;
    };
  }, [open, lockScroll]);

  // 4. INERT THE BACKGROUND (modal only) — the app root becomes unreachable
  //    by pointer, keyboard, and assistive tech. This is the modern replacement
  //    for the old aria-hidden-everything-else dance.
  useEffect(() => {
    if (!open || !modal) return;
    const root = document.getElementById("app-root");
    root?.setAttribute("inert", "");
    return () => root?.removeAttribute("inert");
  }, [open, modal]);

  if (!open) return null; // unmount when closed — no display:none ghost

  return createPortal(
    <div
      className={`overlay ${className}`}
      onKeyDown={onKeyDown}
      // scrim click: only the scrim itself, not clicks bubbling from the surface
      onMouseDown={(e) => {
        if (dismissOnScrimClick && e.target === e.currentTarget) onClose();
      }}
    >
      <div
        ref={surfaceRef}
        className="overlay__surface"
        role="dialog"
        aria-modal={modal || undefined}
        aria-labelledby={labelledBy}
        aria-describedby={describedBy}
        tabIndex={-1}
      >
        {children}
      </div>
    </div>,
    document.body
  );
}
```


Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:
*   **`modal`** **is one prop that flips five behaviors** — trap, inert, scroll-lock, `aria-modal`, and scrim-click default all key off it. That's the primitive earning its keep.
*   **Focus is** **_restored_****, not just moved.** The cleanup `return` puts focus back on the trigger. Forgetting this dumps screen-reader users at the top of the page.
*   **Scroll lock compensates for scrollbar width** so the background never jumps. `scrollbar-gutter: stable` is the even-better modern fix — reserve the gutter permanently and there's nothing to compensate.
*   **`inert`** **on the app root** is the correct 2026 way to seal the background — one attribute vs. the old, leaky "set `aria-hidden` and `tabindex=-1` on every sibling."
*   **Scrim click uses** **`e.target === e.currentTarget`** so a click _inside_ the surface that bubbles up doesn't accidentally close it — the classic "my modal closes when I select text" bug.
*   **It unmounts when closed** — no `display:none` corpse sitting in the DOM stealing tab order and paint.

### 9.3 The Overlay Manager / Context (the architecture)
One `<Overlay>` is a component. A _stack_ of them, coordinated, is an architecture. The manager answers the questions a single overlay can't: **which overlay owns** **`Escape`** **right now** (only the topmost should close), **when to actually release the scroll lock** (only when the _last_ overlay closes), and **what stacking order** everything is in. This is the pattern Radix, React-Aria, and every serious design system implement.

```typescript
import {
  createContext, useContext, useState, useCallback, useMemo,
  useEffect, type ReactNode,
} from "react";

type OverlayEntry = { id: string; modal: boolean };
type OverlayCtx = {
  stack: OverlayEntry[];
  open: (entry: OverlayEntry) => void;
  close: (id: string) => void;
  isTop: (id: string) => boolean;
};

const Ctx = createContext<OverlayCtx | null>(null);

export function OverlayProvider({ children }: { children: ReactNode }) {
  const [stack, setStack] = useState<OverlayEntry[]>([]);

  const open = useCallback((entry: OverlayEntry) => {
    setStack((s) => [...s, entry]); // push
  }, []);
  const close = useCallback((id: string) => {
    setStack((s) => s.filter((e) => e.id !== id)); // pop by id
  }, []);
  const isTop = useCallback(
    (id: string) => stack.length > 0 && stack[stack.length - 1].id === id,
    [stack]
  );

  // ONE global Escape listener; only the topmost overlay reacts.
  // Without this, Escape inside three nested overlays closes all three.
  useEffect(() => {
    function onEsc(e: KeyboardEvent) {
      if (e.key !== "Escape" || stack.length === 0) return;
      const top = stack[stack.length - 1];
      window.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent("overlay:dismiss", { detail: top.id }));
    }
    window.addEventListener("keydown", onEsc);
    return () => window.removeEventListener("keydown", onEsc);
  }, [stack]);

  // Scroll lock keys off "is ANY modal open", released only on the last close.
  useEffect(() => {
    const anyModal = stack.some((e) => e.modal);
    if (!anyModal) return;
    const sb = window.innerWidth - document.documentElement.clientWidth;
    const prev = document.body.style.cssText;
    document.body.style.overflow = "hidden";
    if (sb > 0) document.body.style.paddingRight = `${sb}px`;
    return () => { document.body.style.cssText = prev; };
  }, [stack]);

  const value = useMemo(
    () => ({ stack, open, close, isTop }),
    [stack, open, close, isTop]
  );
  return <Ctx.Provider value={value}>{children}</Ctx.Provider>;
}

export function useOverlay() {
  const ctx = useContext(Ctx);
  if (!ctx) throw new Error("useOverlay must be used within <OverlayProvider>");
  return ctx;
}
```

A managed overlay registers itself on open, reads its stacking depth from its position, and only closes on `Escape` when it's the top of the stack:

```typescript
export function ManagedModal({ id, open, onClose, children }: {
  id: string; open: boolean; onClose: () => void; children: ReactNode;
}) {
  const { open: push, close: pop, stack, isTop } = useOverlay();

  useEffect(() => {
    if (open) push({ id, modal: true });
    return () => pop(id);
  }, [open, id, push, pop]);

  // only the topmost overlay obeys the global Escape
  useEffect(() => {
    function onDismiss(e: Event) {
      if ((e as CustomEvent).detail === id && isTop(id)) onClose();
    }
    window.addEventListener("overlay:dismiss", onDismiss);
    return () => window.removeEventListener("overlay:dismiss", onDismiss);
  }, [id, isTop, onClose]);

  const depth = stack.findIndex((e) => e.id === id);
  return (
    <Overlay open={open} onClose={onClose} modal
      className="managed"
      // stack each overlay 10 above the last, on top of the base modal tier
      // (style prop shown inline for clarity)
    >
      <div style={{ zIndex: 1500 + depth * 10 }}>{children}</div>
    </Overlay>
  );
}
```

The three problems the manager solves that a lone overlay can't:
*   **Escape only closes the top.** Three nested overlays, one `Escape` press → one closes, not all three. The global listener + `isTop` check enforces it.
*   **Scroll lock is reference-counted.** Locking on every open and unlocking on every close double-frees; the manager releases only when the _last_ modal closes. (For UJG's typical single-modal flows this is overkill — but the moment a confirm dialog can open over a drawer, you need it.)
*   **Stacking is ordered, not guessed.** Depth comes from stack position, so overlay N always sits above overlay N-1 without anyone hand-picking z-index numbers.

The one piece worth understanding deeply is **`Presence`** — the reason Radix overlays can animate _out_. React normally unmounts a component the instant its `open` flips to false, which kills any exit transition. `Presence` keeps the node mounted, flips a `data-state="closed"` attribute, waits for the CSS animation/transition to finish (`animationend`), _then_ unmounts. That's the fix for state 5 in §5 — the "closing" state. Wire your CSS to the data attribute:

```css
.overlay[data-state="open"]    { animation: overlay-in .2s ease; }
.overlay[data-state="closed"]  { animation: overlay-out .15s ease forwards; }
.overlay__surface[data-state="closed"] { animation: surface-out .15s ease forwards; }
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .overlay[data-state], .overlay__surface[data-state] { animation: none; }
}
```

Radix also ships `DismissableLayer` (the light-dismiss logic — outside pointerdown + Escape, stack-aware) and `RemoveScroll` (the battle-tested scroll-lock that survives iOS Safari's rubber-banding). If you build your own primitive, steal those two concepts specifically; they're where naïve implementations break.

### 9.5 The Scroll-Lock Problem, Solved Three Ways
This deserves its own subsection because it's the most-botched part of the whole component. When an overlay opens and you set `body { overflow: hidden }`, the vertical scrollbar disappears — and on desktop that's ~15px of width suddenly freed, so the whole page **jumps right**. The fix, cheapest to best:

```css
/* BEST (modern): reserve the scrollbar gutter permanently. Zero JS, zero jump. */
html { scrollbar-gutter: stable; }
```

```js
/* GOOD (compat): measure the scrollbar and pad the body by that width */
function lockScroll() {
  const sb = window.innerWidth - document.documentElement.clientWidth;
  document.body.style.overflow = "hidden";
  document.body.style.paddingRight = `${sb}px`; // exactly replaces the lost scrollbar
}
function unlockScroll() {
  document.body.style.overflow = "";
  document.body.style.paddingRight = "";
}
```

```js
/* iOS caveat: overflow:hidden on <body> does NOT stop touch scroll in Safari.
   You need position:fixed on the body (and to restore scrollY on unlock),
   which is exactly why teams reach for `react-remove-scroll` / Radix's RemoveScroll
   instead of hand-rolling. Know this gotcha exists before it costs you a day. */
```

Note that native `<dialog>.showModal()` gives you the top layer and the focus trap but does **not** lock body scroll — so even on the native path, this subsection still applies.

### 9.6 Vanilla JS (no framework)
The primitive without React, so you understand what the frameworks abstract. Same five jobs: portal (append to body), scrim, focus trap + restore, scroll lock, inert.

```javascript
function createOverlay({ modal = true } = {}) {
  const lastFocused = document.activeElement;
  const root = document.getElementById("app-root");

  const scrim = document.createElement("div");
  scrim.className = "overlay";
  const surface = document.createElement("div");
  surface.className = "overlay__surface";
  surface.setAttribute("role", "dialog");
  if (modal) surface.setAttribute("aria-modal", "true");
  surface.tabIndex = -1;
  scrim.appendChild(surface);
  document.body.appendChild(scrim); // "portal"

  if (modal) {
    root?.setAttribute("inert", "");                          // seal background
    const sb = window.innerWidth - document.documentElement.clientWidth;
    document.body.style.overflow = "hidden";                 // scroll lock
    if (sb > 0) document.body.style.paddingRight = sb + "px"; // no jump
  }
  surface.focus();

  function onKey(e) {
    if (e.key === "Escape") return close();
    if (e.key !== "Tab" || !modal) return;
    const f = surface.querySelectorAll(
      'button,[href],input,select,textarea,[tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"])');
    if (!f.length) return;
    const first = f[0], last = f[f.length - 1];
    if (e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === first) { e.preventDefault(); last.focus(); }
    else if (!e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === last) { e.preventDefault(); first.focus(); }
  }
  scrim.addEventListener("keydown", onKey);
  scrim.addEventListener("mousedown", (e) => { if (e.target === scrim) close(); });

  function close() {
    scrim.remove();
    root?.removeAttribute("inert");
    document.body.style.overflow = "";
    document.body.style.paddingRight = "";
    lastFocused?.focus(); // RESTORE
  }
  return { surface, close };
}
```

### 9.12 Web Component (framework-agnostic)
A portal is awkward in a shadow DOM (the whole point is encapsulation), so a native `<dialog>` inside the component gets you the top layer without fighting the shadow boundary:

*   **Bonus:** test the _stack_ — open two overlays, press `Escape` once, assert only the top closed.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
The overlay is where accessibility is won or lost, because _four_ systems have to cooperate: focus, keyboard, screen-reader semantics, and the inert background. Get all four or the component is broken for someone.

**Semantics & roles:**
*   **Modal:** `role="dialog"` + `aria-modal="true"` on the surface. `aria-modal` tells assistive tech the rest of the page is unavailable — but it does _not_ actually make it inert, so you _also_ set `inert` on the background. Both.
*   **Alert-style confirm:** `role="alertdialog"` for interruptions that need an immediate response (destructive confirms), with `aria-describedby` pointing at the consequence text.
*   **Non-modal popover/menu:** `role="menu"`/`role="listbox"`/`role="dialog"` _without_ `aria-modal`, and the trigger gets `aria-expanded` + `aria-controls`.
*   **Label the surface:** `aria-labelledby` → the heading's id, `aria-describedby` → the body text. An unlabeled dialog is announced as just "dialog."
*   **Toast:** _not_ a dialog — an `aria-live="polite"` (or `role="status"`) region so it's announced without stealing focus. Assertive only for genuine errors.

**Keyboard map:**

| Key | Modal | Non-modal (popover/menu) |
| ---| ---| --- |
| `Tab` / `Shift+Tab` | Cycles within (trapped) | Moves through, leaving closes it |
| `Escape` | Closes the topmost | Closes / returns focus to trigger |
| `Enter` / `Space` | Activates focused control | Activates / selects |
| Arrow keys | (n/a unless it's a listbox) | Move between menu items |
| Outside click | Blocked by scrim | Light-dismisses |

**Focus management — the four rules that fail audits:**
1. **Move focus in** on open (to the first focusable, or the surface, or the primary/least-destructive action — _never_ leave it on the trigger behind the scrim).
2. **Trap it** for modals — `Tab` from the last element wraps to the first, never to the page.
3. **Restore it** on close — back to the element that opened the overlay. Forgetting this dumps keyboard/SR users at the top of the document.
4. **Seal the background** with `inert` so pointer, keyboard, _and_ screen-reader virtual cursor can't reach it. This is the single most-skipped rule.

**Focus-scope vs focus-trap — which overlays do which:** _modal_ overlays **trap** (focus physically cannot leave until close). _Non-modal_ overlays use a _scope_ — focus can leave freely (that's the whole point of a light-dismiss menu), but on close it _returns_ to the trigger. Trapping a popover is a bug; not scoping a modal is a worse one. Match the containment to the archetype.

**Contrast & targets:** the scrim must not drop the surface text below **4.5:1** (blurred/glass scrims are the usual offender — test the _worst-case_ background behind the blur). The close button and any dismiss control keep a **≥44px** tap target and a visible `:focus-visible` ring. Never signal "destructive" by color alone.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The overlay looks solved but the platform is actively reinventing its foundation:
*   **The top layer as default.** Native `<dialog>` + the **Popover API** (`popover`, `popovertarget`) are now broadly shipped, moving focus-trap, inert, and `::backdrop` from your JS into the browser. New standards on the way: the **`interesttarget`**/invoker-commands work (`command`/`commandfor`) to open dialogs and popovers with zero JS, and **anchor positioning** (`anchor-name` / `position-anchor`) to place popovers relative to a trigger in pure CSS — killing Popper.js/Floating-UI for the common cases.
*   **`@starting-style`** **+** **`allow-discrete`** finally let you animate top-layer elements _in and out_ with pure CSS, no `Presence` component — transitioning `display` and `overlay` as discrete properties.
*   **Scroll-driven & view-transition overlays.** The **View Transitions API** morphs a trigger _into_ its overlay (a card expanding into a dialog) natively, and sheets that track a drag gesture with scroll-driven animations feel native-app-smooth on the web.
*   **Liquid Glass scrims.** Apple's 2026 material makes the scrim itself a refractive, content-aware surface rather than a flat dim — coming to the web as layered `backdrop-filter` approximations.
*   **AI/agentic overlays.** "Do it for me" surfaces that stream an agent's progress in a non-modal dock overlay, and command-palette overlays (⌘K) becoming the primary navigation layer of a whole class of apps.
*   **Detented sheets on the web.** The iOS half-sheet/full-sheet pattern (`presentationDetents`) is arriving as a web convention — drawers that snap to defined heights and can be dragged between them.

**For UJG:** build the overlay family on the native top layer now (less code, better a11y for free), reserve the Liquid-Glass scrim for hero/marketing moments over the deep Eminence backdrop where refraction reads richest, and keep a Flat tinted scrim for dense product work. Accessibility caveats hold no matter how on-trend: contrast and reduced-motion are non-negotiable.

## 12\. Conversion & UX Killers (mistakes that bleed money silently)
None of these throw an error. They just quietly drop numbers and rack up support tickets.
*   **The layout jump on open.** Scrollbar disappears, page lurches right, the user's click lands on the wrong thing. Reeks of amateur. Fix: `scrollbar-gutter: stable` or scrollbar-width compensation (§9.5).
*   **No way out.** A modal with no `Escape`, no scrim-dismiss, and a close button below the fold on mobile = a trapped user = a bounce. Every overlay needs an obvious, reachable exit (unless it's a genuinely blocking legal gate).
*   **Modal-on-modal-on-modal.** Stacked interruptions read as a broken app. Collapse wizards into one surface; make confirmations undoable toasts instead.
*   **Focus never moves in.** Sighted mouse users don't notice; keyboard and screen-reader users are stranded behind the scrim, tabbing through the page they can't see. Silent for most, total failure for some.
*   **Focus never restored.** Close the modal, focus is back at `<body>` top, the keyboard user re-tabs through the whole page to get back. Death by a thousand tabs.
*   **Pure-black 50% scrim.** The universal "nobody tuned this" tell. Tint toward brand, pick an intentional opacity.
*   **Scrim that doesn't block.** Clicks bleed through to the page behind and fire actions the user didn't mean. `pointer-events` and z-index misconfigured — the scrim _looks_ right and does nothing.
*   **Aggressive overlays.** The newsletter modal 2 seconds after landing, the exit-intent popup on mobile (where exit-intent doesn't even exist), the cookie wall covering the content. Each one is a measurable bounce. Overlay sparingly; earn the interruption.
*   **The unclosable mobile drawer.** Off-canvas nav with no visible close and a scrim too thin to look tappable. Users force-quit.

**The through-line:** these all fail _quietly_ — no crash, just a slightly worse funnel and a slightly-more-broken feel that's easy to blame on "the market." Audit your overlays before your ad spend.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns (native-platform tricks & TS-enforced safety)
The senior-move versions of everything above.

**Declarative overlays with zero JS.** Modern HTML wires triggers to overlays without a single event listener:

```plain
<!-- Popover: open + light-dismiss, no JS -->
<button popovertarget="filters">Filters</button>
<div id="filters" popover="auto">…</div>

<!-- Command a dialog declaratively (invoker commands) -->
<button command="show-modal" commandfor="editDlg">Edit</button>
<dialog id="editDlg">…</dialog>

<!-- CSS-only anchored positioning, no Floating-UI -->
<style>
  #filters { position-anchor: --filterBtn; inset-area: bottom span-right; }
  button[popovertarget="filters"] { anchor-name: --filterBtn; }
</style>
```

**TypeScript: make the modal contract un-misusable.** A discriminated union can _require_ the pieces each archetype needs — a modal must have a label, a non-modal must not trap:

```typescript
type Base = { open: boolean; onClose: () => void; children: React.ReactNode };
type ModalOverlay = Base & {
  modal: true;
  /** modal MUST be labelled — enforced at compile time */
  "aria-labelledby": string;
  lockScroll?: true;
};
type NonModalOverlay = Base & {
  modal?: false;
  trapFocus?: false;   // trapping a non-modal is a bug; the type forbids `true`
  anchor: React.RefObject<HTMLElement>; // non-modals anchor to a trigger
};
type OverlayProps = ModalOverlay | NonModalOverlay;
// Now <Overlay modal> won't compile without aria-labelledby, and you can't
// pass trapFocus to a non-modal. The type system enforces the a11y contract.
```

**Design tokens for the whole layer system.** The z-scale, scrim, and surface all as tokens so a rebrand touches one file:

```css
:root {
  /* layer scale (primitive) */
  --z-dropdown: 1000; --z-scrim: 1400; --z-modal: 1500;
  --z-popover: 1600;  --z-toast: 1700; --z-tooltip: 1800;
  /* scrim (semantic) — tinted, not black */
  --scrim-bg: color-mix(in oklch, #5F2C82 22%, #0A0A0A);
  --scrim-opacity: .55;
  --scrim-blur: 2px;
  /* surface (component) */
  --overlay-surface-bg: #042D1D;
  --overlay-surface-radius: 16px;
  --overlay-focus-ring: #DCA424;
}
```

**Nested light-dismiss done right.** A popover opened _inside_ a modal must dismiss on its own outside-click without closing the modal underneath. This is precisely what Radix's `DismissableLayer` stack solves — each layer registers, and an outside-click dismisses only the topmost. Rolling your own means a layer registry (the manager in §9.3) that pops one level per outside-click/Escape, never all.

**aria-live status paired with an overlay.** For async work inside a dialog, a polite live region announces results to screen readers that a spinner alone won't:

```plain
<div role="dialog" aria-modal="true" aria-labelledby="t">
  <h2 id="t">Importing…</h2>
  <p aria-live="polite" id="status"></p>  <!-- "Imported 240 rows" / "Import failed" -->
</div>
```

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
An overlay feels free; at scale it isn't.
*   **Don't render closed overlays.** The #1 overlay perf sin is mounting every modal in the tree and hiding them with `display:none`. Fifty hidden modals = fifty subtrees the browser still parses, styles, and keeps in memory, plus a polluted tab order. Render on open, unmount on close (use `Presence` only to defer unmount for the exit animation).
*   **`backdrop-filter: blur()`** **is expensive.** It forces the compositor to sample everything behind the scrim every frame. On a big or animated background it can tank frame rate on mid-range devices. Use a modest blur (≤4px), or drop to a flat tinted scrim on low-end devices via a `@media (update: slow)` or a capability check. Never animate the blur _radius_.
*   **Animate only** **`transform`** **and** **`opacity`****.** Scrim fade = `opacity`; surface entrance = `transform`. Animating `width`/`height`/`top` on a drawer forces layout reflow every frame. `contain: layout paint` on the surface isolates its internals from the page's layout.
*   **Portal churn.** Creating and destroying a portal container on every open/close thrashes the DOM. Reuse a single persistent portal root (`<div id="overlay-root">` in the layout) that overlays mount into, rather than `document.createElement` each time.
*   **Lazy-load heavy overlay content.** A settings modal or a media lightbox shouldn't ship in the initial bundle. `React.lazy` the _content_, keep the lightweight primitive eager, and the overlay costs nothing until it opens.
*   **Scroll-lock reflow.** Toggling `body { overflow:hidden; padding-right }` triggers one reflow on open and one on close — acceptable. `scrollbar-gutter: stable` avoids even that by never changing the body's box.

## 15\. Security (the overlay is literally an attack surface)
The overlay is unique among components: **it is the vector for a named attack class.**

**Clickjacking is an overlay attack.** A hostile site loads _your_ page in a transparent `<iframe>` and floats its own decoy UI over it, tricking the user into clicking your real "Confirm transfer" / "Grant access" button through the invisible layer. The defense is HTTP headers, not markup:
*   `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'` (modern) and/or `X-Frame-Options: DENY` so your page refuses to be framed at all.
*   For high-stakes confirmations rendered in an overlay, re-authenticate or require a typed confirmation server-side — don't trust that a click on your confirm button was intentional.

**The scrim is not a security boundary.** `inert`, `aria-hidden`, and a scrim that "blocks" the page are UX affordances. They're trivially removed in devtools, and the endpoint behind the overlay's confirm button can be hit directly with `curl`. Every state-changing action an overlay fires must independently re-check **auth, authorization, CSRF, and validation** server-side (see the Button doc §9.24). The overlay never being shown must never be the only thing preventing the action.

**Don't leak into the top layer.** A `popover="manual"` or a stray top-layer element left mounted can float invisibly over the page and intercept clicks — an accidental self-clickjack. Always unmount overlays fully on close and audit for orphaned top-layer nodes.

**Untrusted content in an overlay = XSS surface.** Overlays often render server- or user-supplied HTML (a preview, a rich confirmation). Sanitize it. An overlay is a high-attention layer; injected script here is especially effective.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before an overlay — or the primitive the whole family sits on — is "done":
- [ ] Renders via a portal or the native top layer; escapes ancestor `overflow`/`transform`/`z-index`.
- [ ] Reads a z-index token from the documented layer scale — no magic `9999`.
- [ ] Scrim is tinted (not pure black), meets contrast behind any blur, and blocks pointer events.
- [ ] Modal: focus moves _in_ on open, is _trapped_, and is _restored_ to the trigger on close.
- [ ] Non-modal: focus is _scoped_ (returns to trigger) but not trapped; light-dismisses on outside click / Escape.
- [ ] Background is `inert` while a modal is open (not just `aria-hidden`).
- [ ] `Escape` closes only the _topmost_ overlay; the stack is managed, not guessed.
- [ ] Scroll is locked without layout shift (`scrollbar-gutter: stable` or scrollbar-width compensation); iOS handled.
- [ ] Correct roles: `dialog`+`aria-modal` (modal), `alertdialog` (destructive), live region (toast); `aria-labelledby`/`describedby` set.
- [ ] Enter/exit animation plays fully (unmount deferred via `Presence`), and collapses under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- [ ] Close controls ≥44px with a visible focus ring.
- [ ] Every action the overlay fires is re-validated and re-authorized server-side; page is `frame-ancestors 'none'`.
- [ ] Closed overlays are unmounted, not `display:none`; heavy content is lazy-loaded.
- [ ] Tested: role, focus-in, focus-restore, trap, Escape, scrim-vs-surface click, inert background, `axe` clean.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the eleven skins, behavior held constant)
The overlay rendered across all eleven visual languages. **Skin only** — the portal target, focus trap/scope, scroll lock, inert background, `Escape` handling, dismiss contract, and 44px close-target are _identical_ in every one. What changes is the scrim treatment and the surface material.

*   **Flat:** solid tinted scrim at fixed opacity, crisp-edged surface, no shadow, flat close button. Fastest to render, reads instantly. The safe default for dense product UI.
*   **Material:** scrim token (~32–55% dim) + an elevation shadow on the surface; both the shadow and the scrim signal depth. Rounded corners, a subtle scale-in.
*   **Glassmorphism:** scrim uses `backdrop-filter: blur()` so the page frosts rather than just dims; translucent surface. Guard contrast hard — test the worst-case background behind the blur.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026):** scrim becomes a graduated blur-plus-tint; surface is a refractive translucent material with a specular Goldenrod rim and a sheen that shifts with the content behind it. Web = layered `backdrop-filter` + highlight approximation; SwiftUI = native `.glassEffect()`. Guard for reduced-motion and contrast.
*   **Neumorphism:** very light scrim tint; same-color surface with soft dual shadows. Contrast-poor — accent use only, never for text-heavy dialogs.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** heavy dim scrim to sell "a real tray floating up"; surface has bevels, inner highlights, a gradient fill.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** hard flat dim (no blur), thick-bordered surface, hard offset shadow, zero radius, clashing accent. Maximum personality.
*   **Claymorphism:** soft warm-dim scrim; puffy surface, big radius, inner top-light + bottom-shadow, gentle glow. Friendly.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue scrim or a gradient glow around the surface on dark. Premium — collapse the animation under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-invisible faint-tint scrim; typography and generous whitespace carry the surface; sharp corners, near-zero decoration.
*   **UJG Brand:** the house default — scrim tinted deep toward **Eminence** **`#5F2C82`** **over Night** **`#0A0A0A`** (never flat black), surface on **Dark Green** **`#042D1D`**/Night with a warm **Goldenrod** **`#DCA424`** glow rim and a confident 16px radius. On the dark, saturated backdrop a Liquid-Glass scrim reads richest — this is the one place UJG leans into the 2026 material. Body text in **Platinum** **`#E8E6E1`**, headings in Methanerse, and the ≥44px close-target keeps its Goldenrod `:focus-visible` ring.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is the skin; the overlay's behavior is the skeleton. A brutalist scrim and a liquid-glass scrim trap focus, seal the background, lock scroll, and answer `Escape` in exactly the same way. Full guidance in the [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) doc.
* * *

**Bottom line:** the overlay is the one component where building the _primitive_ right pays off across the entire product — every modal, drawer, dialog, popover, and toast inherits its focus trap, its scroll lock, its inert background, its dismiss contract, and its stacking. Master the top layer vs. portal tradeoff, the modal-vs-non-modal split (block+trap vs float+light-dismiss), and the manager that coordinates the stack, and you haven't just built a modal — you've built the layer the whole family stands on. That's the difference between shipping a dialog and shipping an overlay _architecture_.
