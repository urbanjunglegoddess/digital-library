# Countdown Timer

# Countdown Timer

A live display counting down to a fixed moment in time, rendered days/hours/minutes/seconds. The engineering is all in the parts you can't see: drift-free timing, a UTC-anchored deadline, an accessibility contract that doesn't scream every second, and a hard rule that anything that _matters_ is settled on the server, never on the visitor's clock.
* * *

## 1\. What It Actually Is

A countdown timer is a component that computes and displays the remaining time between _now_ and a fixed **target timestamp**, refreshing on an interval and firing a defined behavior when it reaches zero. The load-bearing idea is that the display is _derived_ — it is `target − now` recomputed each tick, never a set of numbers you decrement by hand. That single distinction separates a correct timer from the buggy majority.

The 2-4 things people confuse it with, named and distinguished:

*   **Stopwatch / count-up timer.** Counts _up_ from a start point with no fixed end. Measures elapsed time (a workout, a call duration). A countdown counts _down_ to a known end. Different sign, different anchor.
*   **Elapsed-time / "time ago" label.** "Posted 3 minutes ago" is a relative-time display that also derives from a timestamp, but it updates lazily, has no urgency semantics, and never hits zero. Use `<relative-time>` / `Intl.RelativeTimeFormat`, not a countdown.
*   **Progress bar / meter.** Shows completion of a _task_ (upload 60%), not remaining _time_ to a deadline. A countdown can drive a progress ring, but the meter answers "how much is done," the countdown answers "how long until the deadline."
*   **Scheduler / interval timer (****`setInterval`****).** The raw browser primitive. A countdown _uses_ a timer under the hood, but a `setInterval` that decrements a number is exactly the drift-prone anti-pattern this component exists to replace.

**Rule of thumb:** if there is a **fixed future moment** the user is waiting for and you want to show **how much time is left** — and that moment is real — it's a countdown timer. If there's no fixed end, it's a stopwatch. If the "deadline" resets on refresh or is invented per-visitor, it's not a countdown, it's a dark pattern (see §12).
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters

*   **Conversion — and the trust that makes conversion repeatable.** An honest countdown to a real deadline ("Early-bird pricing ends Friday 11:59pm ET") is one of the highest-lift elements on a landing or checkout-adjacent page, because it converts an abstract "later" into a concrete loss the visitor can feel. But the same mechanic run dishonestly (a fake or resetting timer) is a documented conversion _killer_ the moment a returning visitor notices the number reset — and regulators have fined companies for exactly this. The stakes cut both ways: real deadline = lift, fake deadline = churn, refunds, and legal exposure.
*   **Accessibility — timers are a screen-reader minefield.** A naive `aria-live="polite"` region that updates every second will fire ~86,400 announcements a day, burying the user in "fifty-nine… fifty-eight…" and making the page unusable with a screen reader on. Get the live-region contract wrong and you don't just degrade the experience — you actively break it for assistive-tech users. This is the rare component where the _default_ accessible-looking implementation is the wrong one.
*   **Correctness — clock drift and timezones silently corrupt the number.** Decrementing on `setInterval` accumulates error every tick, and background tabs get throttled so the count falls minutes behind real time. Store the deadline in local time and every visitor outside your timezone sees the sale end at the wrong moment. These aren't edge cases; they're the _default_ outcome unless you engineer against them. A timer that lies about how much time is left is worse than no timer.
*   **Legal / commercial integrity.** For anything with money attached — a sale ending, an auction closing, a limited drop, a payment window — the deadline is a commercial commitment. If the client's clock is wrong (or attacker-adjusted) and you trusted it, you either let a sale through after it closed or reject a valid order a second early. Deadlines that matter are **server-authoritative** or they're a liability.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy

A countdown is simple on screen and layered underneath. The named parts:

*   **Target timestamp (the anchor).** The single source of truth: a fixed future instant, stored and transmitted as **UTC / ISO-8601** (`2026-08-14T03:59:00Z`). Everything displayed is computed from this. Not part of the visible UI, but the most important "part" of the component.
*   **Time source (****`Date.now()`** **/ server offset).** What "now" is measured against. In a correct build this is the client clock _corrected_ by a server-provided offset, so the timer counts against real time, not the visitor's possibly-wrong clock.
*   **Unit segments.** The visible blocks — **Days**, **Hours**, **Minutes**, **Seconds** — each a zero-padded number. Which units show is configurable (a 40-minute webinar countdown drops Days).
*   **Unit labels.** The caption under/beside each number ("Days", "Hrs", "Min", "Sec"). Must pluralize correctly ("1 Day" vs "2 Days") and localize.
*   **Separators.** Colons or dividers between segments. Decorative — must be `aria-hidden` so a screen reader doesn't read "colon."
*   **Live region (visually-hidden text equivalent).** An off-screen `aria-live` node that announces the remaining time at _meaningful intervals_ (not every second), giving screen-reader users a usable spoken equivalent of the visual ticking.
*   **Flip / slide animation layer (optional).** The per-digit flip-card or slide transition. Pure decoration; must vanish under `prefers-reduced-motion` while the numbers keep updating.
*   **Expired / completion slot.** What renders at zero: `00:00:00`, or (better) a replacement — a message, a disabled CTA, or a redirect. Backed by a completion callback/state.
*   **Container.** Optional card/banner wrapper carrying padding, background, urgency styling (e.g. a color shift under one hour).
* * *

## 4\. Sizes / Scale / Density

Countdowns span from a one-line inline banner string to a hero centerpiece. The scale is driven by the **digit font size**; everything else (labels, gaps, padding) derives from tokens so the whole unit scales as a block.

| Tier | Token | Digit size | Label size | Segment gap | Min tap target\* | Use case |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Inline | `--cd-inline` | 14px / `0.875rem` | 11px | 4px | n/a (static) | Inside a banner sentence: "Ends in 02:14:33" |
| SM | `--cd-sm` | 24px / `1.5rem` | 11px | 8px | 44px | Sidebar, cart drawer, compact card |
| MD | `--cd-md` | 40px / `2.5rem` | 13px | 12px | 44px | Default — most landing sections |
| LG | `--cd-lg` | 56px / `3.5rem` | 14px | 16px | 44px | Primary sale block, event page |
| XL | `--cd-xl` | 88px / `5.5rem` | 16px | 24px | 44px | Hero / full-bleed launch page |

\*Tap target applies only when a segment is interactive (rare — e.g. tapping to add the deadline to a calendar). A pure-display countdown has no tap target requirement, but any control near it (a "Buy now" CTA, an "Add to calendar" button) obeys the 44px minimum.

Rules that matter:

*   **Digits need** **`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`** **(or a fixed digit width).** Proportional digits make the number _jitter_ horizontally every second as glyph widths change (`1` is narrower than `8`). Tabular figures lock the width; the countdown stops "dancing."
*   **Reserve width for the largest value, not the current one.** A timer that shrinks from `10` to `9` reflows if you don't fix segment width — animate/size for two digits from the start.
*   **Responsive unit shedding.** On narrow viewports, drop the least-significant unit or wrap to two rows rather than shrinking digits below legibility. A hero XL timer should step down to LG/MD on mobile via `clamp()`.
*   **Drive digit size from one token** (`--cd-digit`) and derive label size, gap, and padding from it, so a single change rescales the block coherently.
* * *

## 5\. States

A countdown's states are less about pointer interaction and more about _time_ and _environment_. Each described, not just named:

*   **Running (default).** The live state: numbers update on a self-correcting tick, deriving from `target − now`. The 99% case.
*   **Idle / not-yet-started.** For countdowns that only begin at a start time (a scheduled drop that opens Friday and _then_ counts to close Sunday). Before start, it shows a "starts in" message or a placeholder, not the running count.
*   **Urgent (threshold-crossed).** A running sub-state triggered when remaining time drops below a threshold (e.g. < 1 hour): color shifts to a warning hue, optional emphasis. Must be signaled by _more than color alone_ (an icon, a label like "Ending soon") for color-blind users.
*   **Paused.** Rare and usually wrong for a real-deadline countdown (real time doesn't pause), but valid for a _pausable_ interval (a quiz question timer that pauses on tab-blur). When paused, the display freezes and the live region announces "paused."
*   **Expired / completed.** Reached zero. Shows the terminal display (`00:00:00`) or, better, swaps to a replacement slot (message, disabled CTA, redirect). The completion callback fires exactly once. This state must be _announced_ to screen readers.
*   **Reconciling / stale (after tab wake).** When a backgrounded tab wakes, throttling means the last painted value is stale. The correct state on `visibilitychange` → visible is an immediate recompute-and-repaint (and re-check of expiry) before resuming ticks. A well-built timer is briefly in this state on every wake.
*   **Error / unavailable.** The deadline couldn't be resolved (server fetch for the authoritative deadline failed, or the passed target is invalid/`NaN`). Show a graceful fallback ("Offer ending soon") rather than `NaN:NaN:NaN`, and don't gate content behind a timer you can't trust.
*   **Reduced-motion.** Environment sub-state: `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is active. Numbers still update, but the flip/slide animation is replaced by an instant swap. Behaviorally identical, visually calm.
* * *

## 6\. Types / Variants

Every functional variant named and described. (Interactive-adjacent, so ≥4; here are the working set.)

*   **Segmented (Days/Hours/Minutes/Seconds blocks).** The canonical form: four labeled numeric blocks with separators. Configurable which units appear.
*   **Flip-clock.** Each digit is a flip card that rotates as it changes, mimicking a split-flap or mechanical clock. High personality; the animation is the whole point and must degrade cleanly under reduced-motion.
*   **Inline / sentence.** A compact single-line string embedded in copy or a banner: "Sale ends in **2d 04h 33m**." No blocks, no labels beyond the abbreviations. Lowest visual weight.
*   **Circular / ring (progress-dial).** One or more radial progress rings that deplete as time runs out, often with the number in the center. Pairs the countdown with a _proportion_ (how much of the window remains), driven by `stroke-dashoffset` or `conic-gradient`.
*   **Minimal (digits only).** Numbers and separators, no unit labels — relies on the `HH:MM:SS` convention being self-evident. Only safe when the format is unambiguous (never for multi-day spans without labels).
*   **Card / boxed.** Each unit sits in its own elevated card with the label inside — the "event page" look. A styling variant of Segmented.
*   **Evergreen (deadline-per-user).** Each visitor gets their own N-hour window anchored to _their_ first visit (stored server-side, e.g. "24h from signup"). Legitimate _only_ when the deadline is genuinely personal and persisted — the moment it resets on refresh or is faked, it becomes the dark pattern in §12. Distinct from a fixed campaign deadline everyone shares.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use (and When Not To)

**Use a countdown when:**

*   There is a **real, fixed deadline** the user benefits from knowing: a sale/promo end, a product launch or drop, an event/webinar start, an auction close, a booking-hold expiry, a payment/OTP window.
*   The remaining time is **decision-relevant** — knowing "2 hours left" changes what the user does now.
*   The deadline is **verifiable and honest** — you can point to the same end time in the fine print, the email, and the server.

**Don't use one (use something else) when:**

*   **The deadline is fake, arbitrary, or resets.** That's not a countdown, it's a manipulation. Use honest scarcity ("only 4 left" _if true_) or nothing.
*   **You're showing task progress, not time-to-deadline.** Use a **Progress Bar / Meter**.
*   **You're showing how long** **_ago_** **something happened.** Use a **relative-time label** (`Intl.RelativeTimeFormat` / `<relative-time>`).
*   **You're measuring elapsed time with no fixed end.** Use a **stopwatch / count-up timer**.
*   **On checkout/payment steps where the "deadline" is really a session TTL.** A visible ticking timer here manufactures anxiety and abandons carts; if you must guard a session, warn _near expiry_ with a dialog and an "extend" action, not an always-on countdown.
*   **The window is so long the seconds are meaningless** ("launching in 47 days 06:14:22"). Drop to days/hours; a ticking seconds field on a multi-week timer is noise.

Placement heuristics: put the countdown **adjacent to the action it pressures** (next to the CTA it's motivating), not floating unattached. Keep it **above the fold** for campaign pages. For urgency, one honest timer per view — competing timers dilute the signal exactly like competing primary buttons.

## 8\. Across Design Systems

Most formal design systems don't ship a "Countdown" primitive — it's a _composed_ pattern built from typography, layout, and a live region. What the big systems give you is the raw material and the timekeeping/announcement guidance:

*   **Material Design (Google).** No countdown component, but Material 3's motion and type scales apply directly: use the display/headline type roles for digits, `tabular-nums`, and Material's emphasized-easing tokens for a flip. Material's guidance on _reduced motion_ and live regions governs the announcement layer. Chips/cards host the segments.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS/SwiftUI).** First-class support: `Text(timerInterval:countsDown:)` renders a self-updating, drift-free countdown the system drives, and there's a dedicated **countdown** style. HIG stresses monospaced digits (`.monospacedDigit()`) so the layout doesn't jitter, and Live Activities / Dynamic Island expose countdowns on the lock screen — the OS owns the ticking, you own the deadline.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft).** No countdown widget; you compose from Fluent's Text + tokens. Fluent's emphasis on `aria-live` discipline and its reduced-motion tokens shape the accessible build. Often paired with a ProgressBar for the proportional view.
*   **Ant Design.** Ships **`Statistic.Countdown`** directly — `<Statistic.Countdown value={deadline} format="D:HH:mm:ss" onFinish={...} />`. Takes a target timestamp (not a duration), which is exactly the drift-free anchor pattern, and exposes an `onFinish` completion callback. The closest thing to a canonical countdown in a major system.
*   **Tailwind CSS.** No component (utility-first), but the ergonomics are ideal: `tabular-nums`, `font-mono`, grid for segments, `motion-reduce:` variants to strip the flip animation, and `min-w-*` to lock digit width. You bring the JS engine; Tailwind styles it.
*   **shadcn/ui + Radix.** No countdown primitive, but the _pattern_ is a headless hook (drift-free `useCountdown`) + a Radix-style presentational component, styled with cva variants. Community `shadcn`\-flavored countdowns follow exactly this split: behavior in the hook, skin in the variants.
*   **Bootstrap 5.** No countdown component; the canonical approach is Bootstrap's official _"countdown"_ JS example composed from grid + display utilities, or a card row. You add a small script; Bootstrap supplies layout and type scale.

### 8b. Visual Styles / Trends — What's trending in 2026

The 2026 currents specific to countdowns:

*   **Liquid Glass countdowns.** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language applied to the timer: translucent, refractive segment cards over a saturated backdrop, with a specular rim — the digits appear to float on bent light. Gorgeous on a dark hero; watch digit contrast against the moving sheen.
*   **Live Activities / ambient countdowns.** The trend is _off the page_: the same deadline surfaced on the lock screen, Dynamic Island, home-screen widget, or a smartwatch complication, all driven by one server-authoritative deadline. The countdown is becoming a cross-surface object, not a web widget.
*   **Motion-forward flip, reduced-motion by default.** Springy, physical flip/slide digit transitions are in — but 2026 best practice ships them _behind_ `prefers-reduced-motion`, with an instant swap as the honored default.
*   **Fluid/****`clamp()`** **typography.** Hero timers that scale continuously from mobile to desktop with `clamp()` and container queries, so one timer serves every breakpoint without unit-shedding hacks.
*   **Honesty-forward design (post-regulation).** After enforcement actions against fake urgency, the trend is _provable_ deadlines: the end time shown with an explicit timezone, matching the fine print, sometimes with a "why this deadline" tooltip. Trust as an aesthetic.

If Visual Styles matter for a given build, §17 carries the full 11-skin breakdown.

## 9\. The Code

The through-line of every block below: **derive from a target timestamp, never decrement a counter; tick with a self-correcting timer, not a naive** **`setInterval`****; recompute on tab-wake; and treat a deadline that matters as server-authoritative.**

### 9.1 HTML (semantic foundation with ARIA)

```html
<!-- The deadline lives in a machine-readable <time>. Digits are decorative
     mirrors of it; the visually-hidden live region carries the real a11y text. -->
<section class="countdown" aria-label="Time remaining until early-bird pricing ends">
  <!-- Source of truth: ISO-8601 in UTC. JS reads this, not the visible digits. -->
  <time id="cd-target" datetime="2026-08-14T03:59:00Z" class="visually-hidden">
    August 14, 2026 at 3:59 AM UTC
  </time>

  <div class="countdown__row" aria-hidden="true"><!-- visual only; screen readers use the live region -->
    <div class="countdown__seg"><span class="countdown__num" data-unit="days">02</span><span class="countdown__label">Days</span></div>
    <span class="countdown__sep">:</span>
    <div class="countdown__seg"><span class="countdown__num" data-unit="hours">04</span><span class="countdown__label">Hrs</span></div>
    <span class="countdown__sep">:</span>
    <div class="countdown__seg"><span class="countdown__num" data-unit="minutes">33</span><span class="countdown__label">Min</span></div>
    <span class="countdown__sep">:</span>
    <div class="countdown__seg"><span class="countdown__num" data-unit="seconds">18</span><span class="countdown__label">Sec</span></div>
  </div>

  <!-- Polite, throttled announcements (~every 60s / on threshold), NOT every second. -->
  <p id="cd-status" class="visually-hidden" aria-live="polite" role="status">
    2 days, 4 hours, 33 minutes remaining
  </p>
</section>
```

Key HTML rules:

*   **The deadline is a** **`<time datetime>`** **in UTC.** It's the one durable, machine-readable anchor. The visible digits are `aria-hidden` because they'd otherwise fire a live update every second.
*   **Separators are decorative** — inside the `aria-hidden` row, so a screen reader never reads "colon."
*   **`role="status"`** **+** **`aria-live="polite"`** on the hidden text node, updated at meaningful intervals only (see §10). Never put the live region _on_ the ticking seconds.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (drift-free hook + component)

```tsx
import { useEffect, useRef, useState, useCallback } from "react";

/** Remaining time broken into units, plus flags. */
export interface Remaining {
  total: number;            // ms remaining (clamped ≥ 0)
  days: number; hours: number; minutes: number; seconds: number;
  expired: boolean;
}

function breakdown(ms: number): Remaining {
  const total = Math.max(0, ms);
  const sec = Math.floor(total / 1000);
  return {
    total,
    days: Math.floor(sec / 86400),
    hours: Math.floor((sec % 86400) / 3600),
    minutes: Math.floor((sec % 3600) / 60),
    seconds: sec % 60,
    expired: total <= 0,
  };
}

/**
 * Drift-free countdown. Computes from `target − now` every tick and schedules
 * the NEXT tick to the top of the next second via self-correcting setTimeout,
 * so error never accumulates. Recomputes immediately on tab wake.
 * `serverOffsetMs` corrects the client clock against a trusted server time.
 */
export function useCountdown(
  target: Date | string | number,
  opts: { serverOffsetMs?: number; onExpire?: () => void } = {}
): Remaining {
  const targetMs =
    target instanceof Date ? target.getTime()
    : typeof target === "string" ? Date.parse(target)   // ISO-8601 → ms (UTC-safe)
    : target;

  const { serverOffsetMs = 0, onExpire } = opts;
  const now = () => Date.now() + serverOffsetMs;
  const [rem, setRem] = useState<Remaining>(() => breakdown(targetMs - now()));
  const timer = useRef<ReturnType<typeof setTimeout>>();
  const fired = useRef(false);

  const tick = useCallback(() => {
    const next = breakdown(targetMs - now());
    setRem(next);
    if (next.expired) {
      if (!fired.current) { fired.current = true; onExpire?.(); }
      return; // stop scheduling
    }
    // schedule to the next whole-second boundary, correcting for drift
    const delay = 1000 - ((now()) % 1000);
    timer.current = setTimeout(tick, delay);
  }, [targetMs, serverOffsetMs, onExpire]);

  useEffect(() => {
    if (Number.isNaN(targetMs)) return;   // invalid deadline → render fallback upstream
    tick();
    const onVisible = () => {
      if (document.visibilityState === "visible") {
        clearTimeout(timer.current);      // stale after throttling
        tick();                           // recompute + repaint immediately
      }
    };
    document.addEventListener("visibilitychange", onVisible);
    return () => { clearTimeout(timer.current); document.removeEventListener("visibilitychange", onVisible); };
  }, [tick, targetMs]);

  return rem;
}

const pad = (n: number) => String(n).padStart(2, "0");

export function Countdown({ target, serverOffsetMs, onExpire, urgentUnderMs = 3_600_000 }:
  { target: string; serverOffsetMs?: number; onExpire?: () => void; urgentUnderMs?: number }) {

  const r = useCountdown(target, { serverOffsetMs, onExpire });
  if (Number.isNaN(Date.parse(target))) return <p>Offer ending soon.</p>;
  const urgent = !r.expired && r.total < urgentUnderMs;

  // Text equivalent, updated by React but only announced at meaningful cadence:
  const label = r.expired
    ? "Offer has ended"
    : `${r.days} ${r.days === 1 ? "day" : "days"}, ${r.hours} ${r.hours === 1 ? "hour" : "hours"}, ` +
      `${r.minutes} ${r.minutes === 1 ? "minute" : "minutes"} remaining`;

  return (
    <section className={`countdown${urgent ? " is-urgent" : ""}${r.expired ? " is-expired" : ""}`}
             aria-label="Time remaining">
      {r.expired ? (
        <p role="status">Offer has ended.</p>
      ) : (
        <div className="countdown__row" aria-hidden="true">
          <Seg n={r.days} label="Days" /><span className="countdown__sep">:</span>
          <Seg n={r.hours} label="Hrs" /><span className="countdown__sep">:</span>
          <Seg n={r.minutes} label="Min" /><span className="countdown__sep">:</span>
          <Seg n={r.seconds} label="Sec" />
        </div>
      )}
      {/* announced ~every minute via key change; see §10 for the throttle */}
      <p className="visually-hidden" role="status" aria-live="polite"
         key={`${r.days}-${r.hours}-${r.minutes}${urgent ? `-${r.seconds}` : ""}`}>
        {label}
      </p>
    </section>
  );
}

function Seg({ n, label }: { n: number; label: string }) {
  return (
    <span className="countdown__seg">
      <span className="countdown__num">{pad(n)}</span>
      <span className="countdown__label">{label}</span>
    </span>
  );
}
```

### 9.4 Vanilla JavaScript (self-correcting `setTimeout`, no framework)

```js
function createCountdown(root) {
  const targetMs = Date.parse(root.querySelector("time").dateTime); // ISO in UTC
  const status  = root.querySelector("[aria-live]");
  const nums = {
    days:    root.querySelector('[data-unit="days"]'),
    hours:   root.querySelector('[data-unit="hours"]'),
    minutes: root.querySelector('[data-unit="minutes"]'),
    seconds: root.querySelector('[data-unit="seconds"]'),
  };
  let timer, expiredFired = false, lastAnnounceMin = -1;
  const pad = (n) => String(n).padStart(2, "0");

  if (Number.isNaN(targetMs)) { root.textContent = "Offer ending soon."; return; }

  function paint() {
    const total = Math.max(0, targetMs - Date.now());   // derive, never decrement
    const sec = Math.floor(total / 1000);
    const d = Math.floor(sec / 86400), h = Math.floor((sec % 86400) / 3600);
    const m = Math.floor((sec % 3600) / 60), s = sec % 60;
    nums.days.textContent = pad(d); nums.hours.textContent = pad(h);
    nums.minutes.textContent = pad(m); nums.seconds.textContent = pad(s);
    root.classList.toggle("is-urgent", total > 0 && total < 3_600_000);

    if (total <= 0) {
      root.classList.add("is-expired");
      if (!expiredFired) { expiredFired = true; status.textContent = "Offer has ended";
        root.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent("countdown:expire", { bubbles: true })); }
      return; // stop the loop
    }
    // Announce at most once per minute (or per 10s in the final urgent minute)
    if (m !== lastAnnounceMin || total < 60_000) {
      lastAnnounceMin = m;
      if (total >= 60_000 || s % 10 === 0)
        status.textContent = `${d} days, ${h} hours, ${m} minutes remaining`;
    }
    timer = setTimeout(paint, 1000 - (Date.now() % 1000)); // correct to the next second
  }

  paint();
```

```kotlin
import androidx.compose.foundation.layout.*
import androidx.compose.material3.Text
import androidx.compose.runtime.*
import androidx.compose.ui.Modifier
import androidx.compose.ui.semantics.*
import androidx.compose.ui.text.font.FontWeight

import kotlinx.coroutines.delay
import kotlin.math.max

@Composable
fun Countdown(deadlineEpochMs: Long, onExpire: () -> Unit = {}) {
    // Derive remaining from the clock each tick; never decrement a stored counter.
    var remaining by remember { mutableStateOf(max(0L, deadlineEpochMs - System.currentTimeMillis())) }
    var fired by remember { mutableStateOf(false) }

    LaunchedEffect(deadlineEpochMs) {
        while (true) {
            remaining = max(0L, deadlineEpochMs - System.currentTimeMillis())
            if (remaining <= 0L) { if (!fired) { fired = true; onExpire() }; break }
            delay(1000L - System.currentTimeMillis() % 1000L)   // self-correcting to the next second
        }
    }

    val s = remaining / 1000
    val d = s / 86400; val h = (s % 86400) / 3600; val m = (s % 3600) / 60; val sec = s % 60
    val spoken = "$d days, $h hours, $m minutes remaining"

    Row(
        horizontalArrangement = Arrangement.spacedBy(12.dp),
        modifier = Modifier.semantics(mergeDescendants = true) {
            liveRegion = LiveRegionMode.Polite            // Compose throttles; not every second
            contentDescription = spoken
        }
    ) {
        listOf("Days" to d, "Hrs" to h, "Min" to m, "Sec" to sec).forEach { (label, v) ->
            Column {
                Text("%02d".format(v), fontSize = 40.sp, fontWeight = FontWeight.Bold)
                Text(label, fontSize = 12.sp)
            }
        }
    }
}
```

### 9.16 Flutter (Dart)

```dart
import 'dart:async';
import 'package:flutter/material.dart';

class Countdown extends StatefulWidget {
  final DateTime deadlineUtc;          // absolute instant, stored as UTC
  final VoidCallback? onExpire;
  const Countdown({super.key, required this.deadlineUtc, this.onExpire});
  @override
  State<Countdown> createState() => _CountdownState();
}

class _CountdownState extends State<Countdown> {
  Timer? _timer;
  bool _fired = false;
  Duration _remaining = Duration.zero;

  @override
  void initState() {
    super.initState();
    _recompute();
    _schedule();
  }

  void _recompute() {
    // Derive from wall-clock difference; do not subtract a fixed amount each tick.
    final ms = widget.deadlineUtc.difference(DateTime.now().toUtc()).inMilliseconds;
    _remaining = Duration(milliseconds: ms < 0 ? 0 : ms);
    if (_remaining == Duration.zero && !_fired) { _fired = true; widget.onExpire?.call(); }
  }

  void _schedule() {
    final msToNextSecond = 1000 - DateTime.now().millisecond;
    _timer = Timer(Duration(milliseconds: msToNextSecond), () {
      setState(_recompute);
      if (_remaining > Duration.zero) _schedule();
    });
  }

  @override
  void dispose() { _timer?.cancel(); super.dispose(); }

  String _two(int n) => n.toString().padLeft(2, '0');

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final d = _remaining.inDays;
    final h = _remaining.inHours % 24;
    final m = _remaining.inMinutes % 60;
    final s = _remaining.inSeconds % 60;
    final spoken = '$d days, $h hours, $m minutes remaining';

    return Semantics(
      liveRegion: true,                // OS decides cadence; not every second
      label: spoken,
      child: Row(
        mainAxisSize: MainAxisSize.min,
        children: [
          for (final e in [['Days', d], ['Hrs', h], ['Min', m], ['Sec', s]])
            Padding(
              padding: const EdgeInsets.symmetric(horizontal: 6),
              child: Column(children: [
                Text(_two(e[1] as int),
                    style: const TextStyle(fontSize: 40, fontWeight: FontWeight.bold,
                        fontFeatures: [FontFeature.tabularFigures()])),
                Text(e[0] as String, style: const TextStyle(fontSize: 12)),
              ]),
            ),
        ],
      ),
    );
  }
}
```

## 10\. Accessibility

Standalone. This is the component where the naive accessible-looking build is _wrong_, so the contract is exact.

**Roles & ARIA.**

*   Wrap the timer in a labeled container: `aria-label="Time remaining until early-bird pricing ends"`. This gives context the raw numbers lack.
*   The visible ticking digits go inside an `aria-hidden="true"` element. If they were exposed and live, they'd fire ~86,400 changes/day.
*   Provide a **visually-hidden text equivalent** in a `role="status"` / `aria-live="polite"` region, updated at **meaningful intervals** — once per minute for a multi-hour timer, and only in the final minute (every ~10s) as it gets urgent. Never announce every second.
*   `role="timer"` exists but is **poorly and inconsistently supported**; don't rely on it as your only mechanism. The polite live-region-with-throttled-text pattern is the durable one. Use `role="timer"` only additively, never as the sole announcement path.
*   Store the deadline in a `<time datetime="…Z">` so the underlying instant is machine-readable regardless of styling.

**Keyboard map.** A pure-display countdown has _no_ interactive parts, so it takes no keyboard focus and needs no key handling — a screen reader reads the live region as it updates. It becomes interactive only when you attach controls; those get the standard contract:

| Key | Action (only if controls are present) |
| ---| --- |
| `Tab` | Move to an adjacent control ("Add to calendar", the CTA the timer motivates). The digits themselves are not a tab stop. |
| `Enter` / `Space` | Activate a focused control (e.g. "Add to calendar", "Extend session"). |
| `Esc` | Dismiss an "expiring soon" session dialog, if one is used instead of an always-on timer. |

**Focus management.** The timer never _steals_ focus on tick or on expiry — moving focus every second would be catastrophic. On expiry, if content changes (a CTA disables, a replacement appears), announce it via the live region; only move focus if the user's current task is invalidated, and then to a sensible landmark, never abruptly.

**Contrast.** Digits and labels must clear **WCAG AA 4.5:1** against their background (large digits ≥ 24px can use 3:1 as large text, but hold 4.5:1 for safety). The **urgent** state must not rely on color alone — pair the color shift with a text/icon change ("Ending soon") so color-blind and low-vision users get the signal. Separators are decorative; their contrast is cosmetic.

**Target size.** No interactive target in the display case. Any adjacent control (CTA, "Add to calendar", "Extend") is **≥ 44×44px**.

**`prefers-reduced-motion`****.** Flip/slide/odometer digit animations are **disabled** under reduced-motion — replaced by an instant value swap. The numbers keep updating; only the transition goes. A countdown must be fully usable and truthful with zero animation.

**Common accessibility failures specific to countdowns:**

*   **Live region on the seconds** → a wall of "fifty-nine, fifty-eight…" that makes the page unusable. The #1 countdown a11y bug.
*   **Digits exposed, no text equivalent** → a screen reader reads "0 2 colon 0 4" with no meaning.
*   **Urgency by color only** → invisible to color-blind users.
*   **Animation with no reduced-motion fallback** → motion-sickness trigger, and flip cards can leave assistive tech reading a half-flipped value.
*   **No expiry announcement** → the sale ends and the screen-reader user never hears it.
*   **`NaN:NaN:NaN`** from an unparseable/local-time deadline → announced literally as "NaN."

## 11\. Innovative / Emerging Ideas

*   **`Intl.DurationFormat`** **for zero-effort localized, pluralized output (2025/2026-era API).** Now shipping across modern engines, it formats a duration into a correctly pluralized, localized string (`2 days, 4 hours` / `2 Tage, 4 Stunden`) without hand-rolling plural rules — the countdown's text equivalent becomes a one-liner that's correct in every locale.
*   **`Temporal`** **for correct deadline math.** The `Temporal` API (`Temporal.ZonedDateTime`, `Temporal.Instant`) makes UTC-anchored deadlines and "ends at 11:59pm _in the user's timezone_" math correct by construction, retiring a decade of `Date` timezone bugs.
*   **Live Activities / Dynamic Island / lock-screen widgets.** The same server deadline surfaced _off_ the page — iOS Live Activities and watch complications render an OS-driven, drift-free countdown the user sees without opening the app. The countdown becomes a cross-surface object.
*   **`View Transitions API`** **for digit morphs.** Native browser morphing between digit values (or between running → expired states) with no animation library, and it composites cleanly, honoring reduced-motion.
*   **Server-driven / edge-computed deadlines.** Edge functions inject the authoritative remaining time at request time (and a signed deadline token), so the first paint is already correct and the client only fine-ticks — no flash of a wrong number, no trusting the client clock for anything that matters.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers

Each is a specific, silent failure — why it fails and the fix implied.

*   **Fake or resetting urgency (the cardinal sin).** A timer that restarts to "10:00" on every page load, or that regenerates a "24-hour" deadline per visit, is a manipulation. It _works_ until a returning visitor watches it reset — then trust collapses and it converts _negatively_, plus it's drawn regulatory fines. Fix: tie the timer to one real, persisted deadline; if there's no real deadline, don't show a timer.
*   **Client-clock-trusted deadlines on things that matter.** Anchoring a sale/auction/payment window to the visitor's device clock means a wrong or hand-set clock lets late orders through or blocks valid ones. Fix: server-authoritative deadline; the visible timer is decoration, the server is the gate.
*   **Timezone stored/shown in local time.** "Ends at midnight" stored without a zone shows the wrong end time to everyone outside your timezone — a visitor thinks they have hours when the sale already closed. Fix: store UTC/ISO, render in the user's zone, and _state the zone_ explicitly.
*   **Drift from decrementing on** **`setInterval`****.** Subtracting one second per tick accumulates error, and a backgrounded (throttled) tab falls minutes behind. The number visibly disagrees with reality on return. Fix: derive from `target − now` every tick; recompute on `visibilitychange`.
*   **No expired/replacement state.** A timer frozen at `00:00:00` next to a still-clickable "Buy at sale price" button either lies or breaks at checkout when the server rejects it. Fix: a defined expired state that swaps the CTA and matches the server's decision.
*   **Manufactured anxiety in the wrong place.** An always-ticking timer on a checkout/payment step (really a session TTL) spikes abandonment. Fix: no ambient timer; warn _near_ expiry with a dialog + "extend," or extend silently.
*   **Jittery, reflowing digits.** Proportional figures make the number twitch and the layout jump every second, reading as "broken/cheap." Fix: `tabular-nums` + reserved digit width.
* * *

## 13\. Advanced Patterns

*   **TypeScript-enforced accessibility via discriminated union.** Force the text-equivalent contract at compile time: a config that hides labels _must_ supply an accessible label, and an "announce every second" mode is simply unrepresentable.

```typescript
type Announce =
  | { announce: "minute" }                                  // default, safe
  | { announce: "tenSecondsInFinalMinute" }                 // urgent ramp
  | { announce: "custom"; getLabel: (r: Remaining) => string };

type CountdownConfig =
  | ({ showLabels: true;  target: string } & Announce)
  | ({ showLabels: false; target: string; "aria-label": string } & Announce);
//     ^ hiding visible labels REQUIRES an aria-label; the compiler enforces it.

// There is deliberately no `announce: "second"` — the a11y-hostile mode can't be typed.
```

*   **Design-token tiers for the timer skin.** Three-level tokens so a rebrand or an urgent-state theme is one line, and the timer never hardcodes a hex:

```css
:root {
  /* primitive */
  --gold-500: oklch(78% 0.135 82);
  --orange-500: oklch(68% 0.19 45);
  --platinum: oklch(92% 0.008 90);
  /* semantic */
  --color-countdown-fg: var(--platinum);
  --color-countdown-accent: var(--gold-500);
  --color-countdown-urgent: var(--orange-500);
  /* component */
  --cd-digit-color: var(--color-countdown-fg);
  --cd-sep-color: var(--color-countdown-accent);
  --cd-urgent-color: var(--color-countdown-urgent);
}
.countdown__num { color: var(--cd-digit-color); }
.countdown.is-urgent .countdown__num { color: var(--cd-urgent-color); }
```

*   **Signed-deadline token (trust without a round-trip per tick).** The server issues the deadline plus an HMAC signature and its own timestamp; the client computes a clock offset once and ticks locally, but any _action_ (buy, bid) re-presents the signed token and the server re-verifies. The client gets a smooth local tick; the server keeps authority. Defeats client-clock tampering without a network call every second.
*   **Web Worker tick for background accuracy.** Main-thread timers are throttled hard in background tabs; a `Worker` (or `postMessage` heartbeat) keeps a more accurate cadence and posts corrections back, so the number is already right the instant the tab regains focus — combined with the `visibilitychange` recompute as the belt-and-suspenders.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost

*   **Animate compositor-only properties.** A flip/odometer must animate `transform` (and `opacity`), never `width`/`height`/`top` — animating layout properties forces a reflow _every second_, which is uniquely bad for a component that updates once per second forever. Keep `tabular-nums` + fixed digit width so text changes never trigger layout at all.
*   **One self-correcting** **`setTimeout`****, not** **`requestAnimationFrame`** **for the tick.** A seconds countdown needs 1 update/second, not 60 fps. Driving it off `rAF` burns the compositor 60× more than needed and keeps the CPU awake. Use rAF _only_ for a smooth sub-second progress ring, and gate it behind reduced-motion. Stop the timer entirely at expiry — a dead timer that keeps scheduling is a leak.
*   **Tree-shake formatting and time libs.** Reach for the platform (`Intl.DurationFormat`, `Intl.RelativeTimeFormat`, `Temporal`) before pulling `moment`/`date-fns`; if you use `date-fns`, import the single function (`import { differenceInSeconds } from "date-fns"`), never the namespace. A countdown that ships 200KB of `moment` for one subtraction is the classic bloat.
*   **Virtualize / consolidate many timers.** A list of auction rows each with its own timer shouldn't spin N independent loops. Run **one** shared ticker that fans out to all visible rows (and only visible ones — pause off-screen timers via `IntersectionObserver`), so a 500-row page has one interval, not 500. Off-screen countdowns don't need to paint.
*   **Avoid per-tick React re-renders of the whole tree.** Keep the ticking state in a leaf component (or a hook that only re-renders the digits), so the surrounding page doesn't reconcile once a second. Memoize the segment components; the parent shouldn't re-render because a second passed.
* * *

## 15\. Security

Standalone. A countdown's display has a _small_ surface, but the **deadline it represents is a commercial control**, and that's where the real security lives.

*   **Attack surface.** The visible timer is client-side and fully attacker-controllable: the DOM, the JS, and — critically — **the device clock**. Anyone can set their clock back, freeze the timer in devtools, or edit the rendered numbers. So the timer's _display_ proves nothing. The exposure isn't XSS-shaped; it's **trust-shaped**: any decision made because "the client's timer still showed time left" is exploitable.
*   **Server-authoritative deadlines (the core control).** For anything that matters — a sale price, an auction bid, a limited-drop purchase, a payment/OTP window, a booking hold — the server must independently check `now > deadline` at the moment of the action, using its own clock and its own stored deadline. Never accept a client-sent "remaining time" or "deadline" as the basis for granting the action. The Next.js/FastAPI examples above return `410 Gone` after expiry regardless of what the client's timer showed.
*   **Signed deadlines against tampering.** If the deadline travels to the client (to render), sign it (HMAC/JWT) so a tampered deadline is detected, and still re-verify server-side on the action. The signature stops a client from _claiming_ a later deadline; the server re-check stops a client from _acting_ after the real one.
*   **XSS in labels/messages.** The one injection vector is dynamic text — a localized label, a campaign name, or an expiry message pulled from a CMS/URL param and dropped into the DOM. Render it as text (`textContent`, framework escaping), never `innerHTML`. The numbers themselves are computed integers and safe, but "Sale: `${campaignName}` ends in…" is XSS if `campaignName` is unescaped user/CMS input.
*   **Authorization, not just timing.** A timer often gates access ("offer available for 24h"). The gate is authorization: the server checks _this user_ is entitled _and_ within the window. Hiding/showing the CTA client-side is UX; the entitlement check is security.

**Bottom line:** the countdown is a UI affordance with essentially no XSS surface of its own beyond unescaped labels — but the _deadline_ is a security boundary. Enforce it on the server, sign it if it travels, and never let the client's clock decide what the client is allowed to do.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist

Ship-ready gate — every item non-negotiable:

- [ ] **Derived, not decremented:** every tick computes `target − now`; nothing subtracts a fixed amount per interval.
- [ ] **Self-correcting timer:** `setTimeout` scheduled to the next whole-second boundary (or an rAF/Worker equivalent); no naive fixed-1000ms `setInterval`.
- [ ] **Tab-wake recompute:** `visibilitychange` → immediate recompute + repaint + expiry re-check; the number is correct the instant the tab returns.
- [ ] **UTC/ISO deadline:** stored and transmitted as UTC/ISO-8601; rendered in the user's timezone with the zone stated.
- [ ] **Server-authoritative for anything that matters:** the server independently enforces the deadline on the action; the client clock is never trusted for sales/auctions/payments.
- [ ] **Accessible announcement contract:** ticking digits `aria-hidden`; a polite `role="status"` text equivalent announced at meaningful intervals (per-minute, ramping in the final minute), **never every second**.
- [ ] **Text equivalent + expiry announced:** screen readers get a readable "X days, Y hours remaining" and hear the expiry.
- [ ] **Reduced-motion honored:** flip/slide animation disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`; numbers still update via instant swap.
- [ ] **Contrast + non-color urgency:** digits/labels ≥ 4.5:1; the urgent state pairs color with text/icon, never color alone.
- [ ] **No jitter/reflow:** `tabular-nums` + reserved digit width; animations use `transform`/`opacity` only.
- [ ] **Defined expired state:** swaps to a replacement/disabled CTA that matches the server's decision; completion callback fires exactly once.
- [ ] **Graceful invalid-deadline fallback:** unparseable/`NaN` target renders honest fallback copy, never `NaN:NaN:NaN`.
- [ ] **No fake/resetting urgency:** the deadline is real and persisted; it does not reset on refresh.
- [ ] **Performance at scale:** many timers share one ticker; off-screen timers pause; time/format libs tree-shaken; the timer stops scheduling at expiry.

## 17\. Visual Styles

The same drift-free, accessible countdown skeleton, rendered across the 11 core visual languages. Each note is specific to _this_ component — the digit segments, separators, labels, and urgent state.

1. **Flat.** Solid-fill segment blocks, crisp edges, no shadow; digits in a bold sans, a flat accent color on the separators, and the urgent state a single flat color swap. Reads instantly and scales to any size — the safe default for dense pages.
2. **Material.** Each unit sits on an elevated card (dp shadow), digits in a Material display type role, an emphasized-easing flip on change, and the urgent state raises elevation + switches to the error color. Ripple has no place here (nothing's tapped), but the motion tokens drive the digit transitions.
3. **Glassmorphism.** Translucent, `backdrop-filter: blur()` segment cards over a vivid image/gradient, thin light borders, digits in near-white. Watch digit contrast against the busy backdrop — keep a solid-color fallback so the numbers never wash out.
4. **Liquid Glass (2026).** Refractive, pill-shaped segment capsules that bend the background with a specular rim and a moving sheen; the digits appear to float on light, and the separator is a bright refracted glint. Stunning on a dark hero — hold reduced-motion (kill the sheen) and verify contrast against the shifting refraction.
5. **Neumorphism.** Digit segments as soft-extruded tiles on a same-color surface, dual light/dark shadows, the separator an inset groove. Pretty but contrast-poor — add a subtle border and reserve for a calm, low-stakes countdown, never the primary sale timer.
6. **Skeuomorphism.** The full split-flap / mechanical flip-clock: beveled cards with a center hinge line, inner highlight, gradient faces, and a physical flip as each digit rolls over. The most "real object" the countdown can be; the flip is the whole aesthetic, so its reduced-motion fallback (instant swap) must be clean.
7. **Neo-Brutalism.** Thick black borders, hard offset drop-shadow, zero radius, clashing high-saturation blocks per unit, monospaced digits. The urgent state can flip to a jarring red block with the same hard shadow. Loud, campaign-only — the thick borders actually _help_ focus/legibility.
8. **Claymorphism.** Puffy, big-radius segment blobs with an inner top-light and soft outer shadow, rounded chunky digits, a friendly pill separator. Reads playful and approachable — good for a fun launch or a kids/education product; the urgent state warms the clay color rather than shouting.
9. **Aurora / Gradient.** Animated multi-hue gradient behind the digits (or a gradient text fill on the numbers) over a dark field, with a soft glow. Premium and eye-catching; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (freeze the gradient) and verify 4.5:1 at _every_ gradient stop the digits cross, not the average.
10. **Minimal / Swiss.** No boxes, no separators-as-decoration — just precisely-set tabular digits and small uppercase labels on a strict grid, generous whitespace, one hairline rule. Typography and alignment do all the work; the urgent state is a single weight or color shift, nothing more.
11. **UJG Brand (house default).** Goldenrod digits (or Goldenrod separators + Platinum digits) on deep Eminence/Night surfaces, Methanerse for the numerals, a confident radius on the segment cards, and a warm glow; the urgent state shifts to Spanish Orange with an "Ending soon" label so it's never color-alone. Warm, editorial, unmistakably UJG.

**The rule that never changes:** style is skin, behavior is the skeleton. Across all 11 skins the countdown derives from the same UTC deadline, ticks drift-free, recomputes on tab-wake, announces politely at meaningful intervals (never every second), honors reduced-motion, and defers to the server for any deadline that matters. Semantics, the text equivalent, focus behavior of adjacent controls, and the 44px target for those controls are identical no matter which skin is on top.
