## 1\. What a Card Is
A card is a **container**, not an action. It bundles a chunk of content (an article preview, a product, a person, a metric) so it reads as one unit and sits comfortably in a grid or list. Unlike a button, its default job is to _present_, not to _do_. A card becomes interactive only when the whole thing is a single link/button (see §6).

**Not a card:** a section with a background (that's just a section). Not a modal (that's an overlay). Not a tile (tiles are usually fixed-size grid cells; cards are fluid).

Rule of thumb: reach for a card when content is **distinct, comparable, and self-contained**. If you're just stacking text, spacing and dividers do the job with less visual noise. Cards are overused; use them when the boundary earns its keep.

* * *
## 2\. Why It Matters
Cards directly impact scannability, conversion, and trust:
*   **Scannability:** Cards chunk information so users can compare options (plans, products, articles) without reading everything. The boundary says "this is one unit, decide on it."
*   **Conversion:** Product cards with clear image + price + CTA convert better than text lists. Pricing cards with a "recommended" highlight steer decisions.
*   **Trust:** Professional card grids signal competence. Broken cards (misaligned footers, missing images, layout shift) signal amateur hour.
*   **Mobile:** Cards are the native unit of touch interfaces. Swipeable, tappable, thumb-friendly. Every feed you scroll is cards.

* * *
## 3\. Anatomy
*   **Container:** background, border or shadow, radius, padding. The frame that says “one unit.”
*   **Media slot (optional):** image/video/gradient header, usually bleeding to the card edges. Aspect ratio: 16:9 or 4:3.
*   **Eyebrow / tag:** small category label (overline token, 11px, uppercase). Your content pillars fit here.
*   **Title:** the scannable headline (h3 or h4 token).
*   **Body:** supporting text, capped at 2-3 lines (line-clamp) so cards in a row stay balanced.
*   **Footer / actions:** buttons or links, pinned to the bottom so ragged body lengths don't misalign them.
*   **Metadata:** author avatar, date, read time, stats. Usually in footer or below title.
*   **Badge (optional):** “New”, “Sale”, “Featured” — positioned top-right overlapping the media.

* * *
## 4\. Sizes / Scale
*   **S:** 200px W, padding 16px, radius radius-md (8px). Dense grids, sidebar widgets.
*   **M:** 300px W, padding 20px, radius radius-lg (12px). Default. Blog grids, product listings.
*   **L:** 400px W, padding 24px, radius radius-lg (12px). Featured cards, hero product.
*   **Full-width:** 100% W on mobile, stacked single column.

Grid behavior: 3-col (desktop) → 2-col (tablet) → 1-col (mobile). Gap: space-6 (24px) between cards.
## 5\. States
*   **Default:** resting elevation-1, normal appearance.
*   **Hover (interactive cards):** elevation-1 → elevation-2 (200ms ease), slight translate-y -2px. Cursor pointer.
*   **Active/Pressed:** scale 0.98, elevation drops back to 1 (50ms).
*   **Focus:** 2px outline offset 2px, brand color. Visible on keyboard navigation.
*   **Selected:** border-brand (2px), optional checkmark badge top-right.
*   **Loading/Skeleton:** shimmer blocks matching media, title, body, and footer shapes. Prevents layout shift.
*   **Empty:** "No items yet" illustration + CTA button centered.
*   **Error:** contained error message + retry button within card frame.
*   **Expanded:** card grows to reveal more content (accordion-style or modal-like detail view).

* * *
## 6\. Types / Variants
*   **Content card:** media + title + body + actions. Blog posts, articles.
*   **Pricing card:** plan name, price, features, one CTA. Highlighted tier gets border-brand.
*   **Profile card:** avatar, name, role, stats row.
*   **Stat / metric card:** one big number with trend indicator.
*   **Product card:** image, title, price, add-to-cart button.
*   **Interactive card:** the whole card is one clickable target (see §10 for rules).
*   **Horizontal card:** media left, content right (list layouts, featured).
*   **Skeleton card:** the loading placeholder matching target card dimensions.
*   **Expandable card:** reveals hidden content on click/tap (detail preview).

* * *
## 7\. When to Use (and When Not To)
**Use cards when:**
*   Content is distinct, comparable, and self-contained (products, articles, people, plans)
*   Users need to scan and choose between items
*   You have media + text + action as a repeating unit
*   Grid/masonry layouts where visual boundaries aid scanning

**Don't use cards when:**
*   Content is continuous prose (use sections with headings)
*   You have a single item (just show it, no frame needed)
*   Items are text-only lists (use a List component, much lighter)
*   You're nesting cards inside cards (always wrong)
*   Every section of a page is a card (card fatigue, everything looks the same)

* * *
## 8\. Across Design Systems
*   **Material Design (Google):** "Contained" and "Elevated" variants. Uses strict elevation scale (1dp, 4dp, 8dp). Rounded corners 12px default. Supports "Draggable" cards.
*   **Apple HIG:** Doesn't name "card" explicitly but uses grouped content cells in Lists. In visionOS, cards float with depth (glass material).
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** "Card" component with "filled", "outline", and "subtle" appearances. Supports "selectable" and "interactive" behaviors.
*   **Ant Design:** Card with title, extra, cover, actions, tabs. Supports loading state natively. Grid.Card for responsive layouts.
*   **Chakra UI:** Card with CardHeader, CardBody, CardFooter compound pattern. Variants: elevated, outline, filled, unstyled.
*   **shadcn/ui:** Card, CardHeader, CardTitle, CardDescription, CardContent, CardFooter. Minimal, composable, unstyled by default.

* * *
## 6\. Interactive Cards (the accessibility trap)
When the whole card is clickable, do it right or you break keyboard and screen-reader users:
*   Make the title a real `<a>` and stretch its `::after` pseudo-element to cover the entire card. This gives one accessible target without nesting interactives.
*   Give the card `tabindex="0"` only if it's not already focusable via the stretched link.
*   **Never nest interactive elements.** A clickable card with a button _inside_ it is invalid and traps focus. If you need multiple actions, the card is a container, not a link, and the actions are the interactive parts.
*   Hover should lift (transform + shadow transition 200ms), never animate layout properties (causes reflow).

* * *
## 7\. States
*   **Default / hover / focus** (for interactive cards).
*   **Loading:** a **skeleton** that mirrors the card's shape (shimmer blocks where media, title, and text will be). Feels faster than a spinner and prevents layout shift when content arrives.
*   **Empty:** teach the interface (“No projects yet, add your first”), don't show a blank frame.
*   **Error:** contained, with a retry action.

* * *
## 8\. Common Mistakes (Conversion / UX Killers)
*   **Nested cards.** A card inside a card is always wrong; use spacing and dividers.
*   **Identical card grids** repeated endlessly with icon + heading + text: the AI-slop tell. Vary size, or use a real list.
*   **Unbalanced footers** from not pinning actions to the bottom.
*   **Whole-card links with buttons inside**, breaking focus.
*   **Layout-shift on load** from not reserving space (use skeletons).
*   **Card fatigue:** when every section is a card, nothing stands out. Use sparingly where boundaries earn their keep.

## 10\. Accessibility
**Interactive cards (whole card is clickable):**
*   Wrap the title in an `<a>` and use `::after` pseudo-element stretched to cover the card (the "pseudo-content trick"). This gives one accessible link without nesting interactive elements.
*   **Never nest interactive elements.** A clickable card with a button inside is invalid HTML and creates a focus trap. If you need multiple actions, the card is a container (not a link) and actions are separate interactive elements.
*   Provide a visible `:focus-visible` ring on the stretched link (2px outline, offset 2px).
*   `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` pointing to the card title.

**General:**
*   Card as `<article>` element (or `role="article"` on a div) when it represents a self-contained composition.
*   Images: always have alt text. Decorative images: `alt=""`.
*   Media lazy-loading: `loading="lazy"` for below-fold images.
*   Skeleton states: `aria-hidden="true"` on skeleton content; announce loading via `aria-busy="true"` on container.
*   Color contrast: all text meets 4.5:1 against card background.
*   Touch target: if card has a footer button, it must be 44×44px minimum.

## 11\. Innovative / Emerging Ideas
*   **Bento grids (2024-26):** Apple-inspired irregular card grids where cards span different row/column sizes. Creates visual hierarchy without size variants.
*   **Scroll-driven animations:** cards that parallax, scale, or reveal content as they enter the viewport (CSS scroll-timeline).
*   **View Transitions API:** card-to-detail page transitions where the card image morphs into the hero image seamlessly.
*   **Container queries:** cards that adapt their internal layout based on their own width (not viewport), enabling true component-level responsive design.
*   **Hover 3D tilt:** subtle perspective rotation on hover following cursor position (popular in portfolios, 2025).
*   **Live data cards:** real-time WebSocket updates (stats cards, stock tickers) with animated count transitions.

* * *
## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers
*   **Identical grids of 3 cards** with icon + heading + text, repeated endlessly. The AI-slop tell. Vary size or use a real list.
*   **Unbalanced footers** from not pinning actions to bottom (`margin-top: auto` on footer). Users can't find the CTA.
*   **No hover feedback** on interactive cards. Users don't realize it's clickable.
*   **Layout shift on load** from not reserving space with skeletons. Destroys trust.
*   **Whole-card links with nested buttons.** Screen readers announce the entire card content as one giant link label. Nightmare.
*   **Too many cards.** If everything is a card, nothing is. Use cards for distinct items, not every section.
*   **Missing loading states.** Blank cards → skeleton → content. Users who see a blank frame think it's broken.
*   **Truncation without access to full content.** If body is line-clamped, user must have a path to the full text.

* * *
## 13\. Advanced Patterns
*   **Compound component API (React):** `Card`, `Card.Media`, `Card.Title`, `Card.Body`, `Card.Footer`, `Card.Skeleton`, `Card.Badge`. Context shares variant/size downward. No prop explosion.
*   **CSS subgrid for card grids:** align internal card elements (titles, footers) across cards in a row, even when content lengths differ.
*   **Intersection Observer for lazy cards:** defer rendering card content (not just images) until approaching viewport. Critical for feeds with 100+ cards.
*   **Token-driven elevation:** shadow values from CSS custom properties, making dark-mode inversion a single token swap.
*   **Aspect ratio for media slot:** `aspect-ratio: 16/9` on the media container prevents layout shift without JS.
*   **TS-enforced accessibility:** TypeScript interface requires `alt` on [Card.Media](http://Card.Media), requires `href` on interactive cards, errors on nested interactive children.

* * *
## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
*   **CSS only (no JS):** pure card styling is ~0.5-1KB gzipped. The hover/focus states are all CSS transitions.
*   **JS (interactive/skeleton):** React compound component adds ~2-3KB. Intersection Observer for lazy loading: ~1KB.
*   **Images are the real cost.** Use `srcset`, `loading="lazy"`, `decoding="async"`, and modern formats (WebP/AVIF). A card grid with 12 unoptimized images is a 5MB page.
*   **Skeleton vs spinner:** skeletons prevent CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift) which directly impacts Core Web Vitals. Worth the extra markup.
*   **Virtualize for 50+ cards:** if you're rendering more than 50 cards, use a virtualized list/grid to keep DOM nodes under 100.
*   **CSS containment:** `contain: layout style paint` on each card enables browser paint optimizations for large grids.

* * *
## 15\. Security
Cards are display-only containers, so security concerns are minimal:
*   **User-generated content in cards** (social feeds, comments): sanitize HTML to prevent XSS. Never render raw `innerHTML` from untrusted sources.
*   **Image URLs from users:** validate and proxy through your own CDN to prevent tracking pixels and SSRF.
*   **External links in cards:** add `rel="noopener noreferrer"` to `target="_blank"` links to prevent tab-nabbing.
*   **Pricing cards:** if prices are dynamic, validate server-side. Client-rendered prices are suggestions, not truth.

* * *
## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist
Before shipping, confirm:
- [ ] Footer actions align across cards of different body lengths (margin-top: auto)
- [ ] Skeleton matches exact card dimensions (no layout shift on load)
- [ ] Interactive card has exactly one accessible target (no nested interactives)
- [ ] Focus ring visible on keyboard navigation
- [ ] Images have alt text; lazy-loaded below fold
- [ ] Card grid responsive: 3→2→1 columns across breakpoints
- [ ] Dark mode: elevation inverts (lighter surface = higher), shadows reduced
- [ ] No horizontal scroll on any breakpoint
- [ ] Line-clamp on body text with path to full content
- [ ] Tested: screen reader announces card as article with meaningful label
- [ ] Tested: keyboard Tab moves between cards, Enter activates interactive cards
- [ ] Performance: no CLS from image/content loading (skeleton or aspect-ratio placeholder)

1. **Flat:** border only, no shadow. Dense dashboards, data-heavy UIs.
2. **Material:** elevation shadow scale (1dp, 4dp, 8dp), the classic app card. Ripple optional on interactive.
3. **Glassmorphism:** translucent blur (`backdrop-filter: blur(16px)`) over vivid backgrounds + 1px light border. Watch text contrast (often fails WCAG).
4. **Liquid Glass (2026):** Refractive translucent material with specular rim highlight + moving sheen on scroll/tilt. Glassmorphism's successor (Apple iOS 26). Web: layered gradients + backdrop-filter + animated pseudo-element for sheen. Guard for `prefers-reduced-motion` and contrast.
5. **Neumorphism:** dual light/dark shadows on same-color surface, soft extruded plastic. Decorative/accent only (contrast-poor). Inner shadow variant for "pressed" cards.
6. **Skeuomorphism:** beveled edges, inner highlight, gradient fills, thick drop shadow. The pre-flat iOS look returning for tactile/retro projects.
7. **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black border (2-3px), hard offset shadow (4px 4px 0 black), zero radius, clashing bg color. High-personality, indie, loud.
8. **Claymorphism:** big radius (20px+), puffy inner top-light + bottom-shadow, soft outer glow. Friendly, playful, consumer/kids.
9. **Aurora / Gradient:** animated gradient border or fill on dark background. Energetic, premium. Always honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (fallback to static gradient).
10. **Minimal / Swiss:** hairline border (1px neutral-200) or none. Sharp corners (0px radius). Typography and whitespace carry it. Restraint as the statement.
11. **UJG Brand:** bg-surface on bg-page (Platinum), radius-lg (12px), warm subtle glow (`0 4px 12px rgba(92,44,130,0.08)`), Goldenrod accent on hover/selected border. The house style.

**Rule that never changes:** style is skin. The card's structure (pinned footer, media bleed, one accessible target if interactive, no nested interactives, skeleton loading) stays identical across all eleven.
