## 1\. What a Modal Actually Is
A modal is a UI surface that appears **on top of everything else and blocks interaction with the rest of the page until it's dealt with.** That last clause — _blocks interaction_ — is the entire definition. Take it away and you don't have a modal; you have a floating panel.

The word "modal" describes a **mode**: the app enters a state where the only thing you can do is engage with the overlay or dismiss it. That's why a modal traps focus, dims the page, and swallows clicks to the background. Everything else — the box, the shadow, the animation — is decoration on top of that one behavioral contract.

Four things get lumped together constantly, and the differences are the whole game:

*   **Modal (blocking):** overlays the page, **traps focus**, makes the background inert, and demands you finish or dismiss before continuing. "Confirm delete," "Sign in to continue," "Your session expired." The rest of the UI is unreachable — by mouse, keyboard, _and_ screen reader — until it closes.
*   **Dialog (the semantic superset):** a `<dialog>` or `role="dialog"` container that presents content and asks for a response. **A modal is a dialog; not every dialog is modal.** A `<dialog>` opened with `.show()` (not `showModal()`) is a _non-modal_ dialog — it floats, but you can still click the page behind it. In practice most people say "dialog" and mean "modal dialog." Know that the native element does both.
*   **Popover:** a small, **non-blocking** overlay tied to a trigger — a menu, a tooltip-on-click, a date picker. It does **not** trap focus and does **not** inert the page; clicking outside just closes it (light-dismiss). The browser now ships this natively as the `popover` attribute + Popover API. Reaching for a modal when you want a popover is the single most common overlay mistake — you've stopped the world to show a color picker.
*   **Drawer / Sheet:** a panel that slides in from an edge (side drawer, bottom sheet). It can be **either** modal (blocking, with a scrim — think a mobile nav that dims the page) **or** non-modal (a persistent side panel you can click past). "Drawer" describes _where it comes from and how it moves_; "modal" describes _whether it blocks_. They're orthogonal axes, and a bottom sheet on mobile is very often just a modal that animates from the bottom.

The rule of thumb: **if the user must resolve it before doing anything else, it's a modal. If they can ignore it and keep working, it's a popover, a non-modal dialog, or a toast.** Modality is expensive — you've seized the user's entire attention — so spend it only when the task genuinely can't coexist with the page behind it.

One more distinction juniors miss: a modal is **not** a notification. A toast/snackbar informs without blocking; a modal blocks. If your "modal" doesn't require a decision or an input, it probably shouldn't be a modal.

## 2\. Why It Matters
The modal is the most _interruptive_ component you own. That power cuts both ways.

*   **It's the accessibility cliff.** More overlays fail WCAG than any other component, and modals fail hardest. A modal that doesn't trap focus lets a keyboard or screen-reader user Tab straight out of the "blocking" dialog and into the page they can't see — they're now typing into fields underneath a visual overlay, completely lost. A modal that doesn't return focus on close dumps them at the top of the document. Get the modal right and you've solved the hardest keyboard-management problem in the app; get it wrong and you've locked out everyone who doesn't use a mouse.
*   **It's a conversion pressure point.** Modals gate the highest-intent moments: checkout confirmation, sign-in, "are you sure you want to cancel your subscription?" A confusing or un-dismissable modal at those moments is pure abandonment. And the _uninvited_ modal — the newsletter popup that hits three seconds after landing — is one of the most reliable ways to make a user bounce and to earn a Google interstitial penalty on mobile.
*   **It sets the trust tone louder than any other component.** A modal is the app grabbing you by the collar. If it animates janky, shifts the page when it opens (scrollbar jump), can't be closed with Esc, or re-appears the instant you dismiss it, the app feels hostile. A modal that opens smoothly, focuses the right thing, closes on Esc _and_ backdrop click, and never traps you feels engineered. Users don't consciously notice good modal behavior — they only notice its absence, as friction.
*   **It's where destructive actions live.** Delete, transfer, pay, cancel. The confirmation modal is the last line of defense against an irreversible mistake — and, done lazily, it's the "click OK to make the annoying box go away" reflex that defends nothing (see §12).

## 3\. Anatomy of a Modal
Every well-built modal is composed of predictable parts. The ones marked **behavioral** are invisible but non-negotiable.

*   **Trigger:** the button (or action) that opens it. It must receive focus back when the modal closes — hold a reference to it.
*   **Backdrop / scrim / overlay:** the dimmed layer behind the dialog. It signals "the page is inert" and is usually the click-to-dismiss target. Native `<dialog>` gives you this for free as `::backdrop`.
*   **Dialog container:** the actual box. Has the role (`dialog` or `alertdialog`), the label, the max-width, the radius, the shadow.
*   **Header:** title (the accessible name via `aria-labelledby`) and usually the close button.
*   **Close button (the "X"):** an icon button, top-right by convention. Needs `aria-label="Close"`. Not the _only_ way to close — Esc and (usually) backdrop click also close.
*   **Body / content:** the scrollable region. When content overflows, **the body scrolls, not the page** — and it needs its own overflow handling.
*   **Footer / action bar:** the primary and secondary buttons ("Confirm" / "Cancel"). Primary action follows your button hierarchy (see the [Button](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200231) doc).
*   **Focus trap (behavioral):** the engine that keeps Tab/Shift+Tab cycling _inside_ the dialog. The heart of "modal."
*   **Inert background (behavioral):** everything outside the dialog is made non-interactive and hidden from assistive tech (`inert` / `aria-hidden`).
*   **Scroll lock (behavioral):** the page behind cannot scroll while the modal is open — ideally without the layout shifting.
*   **Initial focus target (behavioral):** the element that receives focus when it opens. **Focus return target (behavioral):** the trigger it goes back to on close.

If you can name and place the first eight and you _forgot_ the last four, you've built a pretty box, not a modal.

## 4\. Sizes & Scale
Modal size communicates the weight of the task. A confirmation is small; a multi-step form is large. A typical scale:

| Size | Max width | Use |
| ---| ---| --- |
| XS | 320px | Tight confirmations, single-input prompts |
| SM | 400px | Confirm dialogs, "are you sure?", simple alerts |
| MD | 512px | Default. Sign-in, short forms, most content |
| LG | 640px | Longer forms, settings panels, rich content |
| XL | 800px+ | Multi-column, tables, dense editors |
| Full-screen | 100vw/100vh | Mobile modals, immersive flows, image lightboxes |

Rules that matter:

*   **Cap the width, never the height blindly.** Use `max-width` for the box and `max-height: min(90vh, ...)` so the modal never exceeds the viewport. When content is taller than that, the **body** scrolls internally — header and footer stay pinned.
*   **On mobile, most modals should go full-screen** (or a bottom sheet). A 512px box centered on a 375px phone with tiny margins is worse than owning the whole screen. Below ~640px viewport width, promote to full-screen.
*   **Center vertically with flexbox/grid, not magic margins,** and let the box shrink on short viewports. A modal taller than the screen with no internal scroll is a trap — the footer buttons are unreachable.
*   **Respect the safe area** on notched devices: `padding: max(16px, env(safe-area-inset-bottom))` on the footer so "Confirm" isn't under the home indicator.

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A modal has more states than people expect, and several are _transitional_ — the moments between open and closed are where the bugs live.

1. **Closed (unmounted / hidden):** the default. Ideally not in the DOM at all, or `display:none` / not `[open]`. Nothing inside it is focusable or announced.
2. **Opening (enter transition):** backdrop fades in, dialog scales/slides in. Focus is being moved. With the top layer + `@starting-style`, this animates from nothing without JS.
3. **Open (idle):** focus is trapped inside, background is inert, page scroll is locked. The stable state.
4. **Focused-within:** which control inside holds focus. First-focus lands on a sensible target (first input, or the least-destructive action — _not_ the "Delete" button).
5. **Closing (exit transition):** dialog animates out. The tricky part: the element must stay visible _during_ the exit even though it's logically closing — this is what `transition-behavior: allow-discrete` + `@starting-style` solve on the top layer.
6. **Loading:** content or an async submit is in flight. The footer's primary button shows a spinner and disables; the modal usually should **not** be dismissable mid-submit (or must handle cancellation cleanly).
7. **Error:** the action inside failed. Surface it _inside_ the modal (inline, near the action) with an `aria-live` region — never a second modal on top.
8. **Success:** transient confirmation, then auto-close or a "Done" state.
9. **Nested / stacked:** a second modal opened from the first (a confirm-on-top-of-a-form). Avoid it, but when it happens, only the topmost traps focus and each has its own backdrop (see §13).
10. **Scroll-overflow:** body content exceeds max-height; internal scroll engages, header/footer stay pinned, and ideally a shadow/fade hints there's more.

Missing the transitional states (2 and 5) is the tell of a modal bolted on with `display:none` toggling — it pops in and out with no grace and often breaks the exit animation entirely.

## 6\. Types & Variants
By _function_, not just size:

*   **Alert dialog (****`role="alertdialog"`****):** interrupts for a critical decision or error that needs acknowledgment. "Delete this account?" Uses `alertdialog` (not `dialog`) so screen readers treat it more urgently, and focus goes to the safest action. Esc still closes (= cancel).
*   **Confirmation modal:** the "are you sure?" gate before a destructive or irreversible action. The single most abused modal type — see the anti-pattern in §12.
*   **Form modal:** collects input (sign-in, "create new project," edit settings). Has real validation, real submit, real error states. Should almost never close on accidental backdrop click if the user has typed something (see §7).
*   **Content / detail modal:** shows expanded content — an image lightbox, a record's detail, a preview. Often light-dismiss-friendly (backdrop click closes) because nothing is lost.
*   **Full-screen modal / takeover:** owns the whole viewport for a focused, multi-step task (mobile flows, onboarding, an editor). Usually has an explicit "Close/Cancel" and "Done" rather than a backdrop.
*   **Drawer / sheet (modal variant):** slides from an edge with a scrim. Behaviorally a modal; visually anchored to a side. Bottom sheets are the mobile-native modal.
*   **Command palette:** a specialized modal (⌘K) — a focus-trapped overlay with a search input and a results list. Modal mechanics, search-box soul.
*   **Non-modal dialog:** the odd one out — a `<dialog>` opened with `.show()`. Floats above content but does _not_ block. Useful for a persistent "find in page" bar. Included here so you know the native element does both.

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a modal when the task **genuinely cannot coexist with the page behind it** — it needs a decision, a focused input, or an acknowledgment before anything else proceeds. Reach for one when:

*   A **destructive or irreversible action** needs confirmation (delete, pay, cancel subscription).
*   You need **input that blocks the flow** (sign in to continue, complete required info).
*   You must show **critical information that must not be missed** (session expired, terms changed).
*   A **focused sub-task** benefits from removing all other distractions (a short create/edit form).

Do **not** use a modal when:

*   **The content is non-blocking.** A menu, tooltip, or date picker is a **popover**, not a modal — don't stop the world to pick a date.
*   **It's a notification.** Success/info messages are **toasts/snackbars** — they inform without seizing the page.
*   **The content is long or primary.** If users need to read/scroll a lot or refer back to the page, it belongs on **its own page or a side panel**, not trapped in a box.
*   **You're interrupting uninvited.** The instant-newsletter-popup on mobile is a Google interstitial penalty _and_ a bounce machine. Delay it, gate it on intent, or use an inline banner.
*   **You'd stack modals.** A modal opening another modal is a smell (§13). Rethink the flow.

**The Esc / backdrop-click decision — when NOT to auto-dismiss:**
Default behavior is that **Esc closes** and **backdrop click closes**. But there are cases where backdrop-click-to-close is actively harmful:

*   **A form with unsaved input.** One stray click on the dim area and the user loses everything they typed. Either disable backdrop-close for dirty forms, or intercept and show a "Discard changes?" confirm.
*   **A critical** **`alertdialog`****\*\*\*\*.** A destructive-confirm shouldn't be dismissable by a careless click _outside_ — require an explicit "Cancel." (Esc-as-cancel is fine because it's deliberate.)
*   **A required step** (accept terms, complete payment) with no valid "skip." Here even Esc may be disabled — but if you disable Esc, you **must** provide an obvious visible way out, or you've built a trap.

The safe default: **Esc closes, backdrop closes, but a dirty form or an alertdialog opts out of backdrop-close and warns before discarding.**

## 8\. Modals Across Design Systems
Same blocking dialog, very different house rules. Fluency here means you can drop into any codebase.

*   **Material Design (Google):** Dialogs are surfaces with elevation and a scrim. Distinguishes **basic dialogs**, **full-screen dialogs** (mobile, with a top app bar + Cancel/Save), and **bottom sheets** (standard vs. modal). Motion is a container transform. Material 3 leans into larger radius and expressive motion.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** Calls them **sheets** and **alerts**. Sheets slide up and can be dragged to dismiss; iOS 15+ supports **detents** (half-sheet / full-sheet). Alerts are small, centered, and reserved for critical choices with 1–3 buttons. Strong opinion that alerts are interruptive — use sparingly.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** **Dialog** for blocking decisions, **Panel** for a side drawer that slides from the edge. Subtle depth, acrylic/mica materials on the surface.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** `Modal` component with rich props — `confirm()`, `Modal.confirm/info/warning/error` static helpers, `maskClosable`, `keyboard` (Esc), draggable modals, and a confirm-before-close pattern baked in for data-heavy admin flows.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** No prescribed component; you compose it (or use Headless UI's `Dialog`, which brings the behavior and leaves the looks to you).
*   **Radix / shadcn (the 2024+ web standard):** `Dialog` (and `AlertDialog`) are headless primitives — focus trap, Esc, scroll lock, `aria-*`, and portal all handled; you style the parts. shadcn copies these into your repo so you own the markup. This is the closest thing to a modern default.
*   **Bootstrap:** The classic `.modal` component with `data-bs-toggle="modal"`, a JS-driven backdrop, and `.modal-dialog-scrollable` / `.modal-fullscreen` helpers. Ubiquitous, but its focus management and stacking have historically been the weak spots.

**The 2026 throughline:** everyone is converging on the **native** **`<dialog>`** **element + the top layer** as the foundation, then layering their styling on top. The behavior is finally standardizing in the platform; the design systems increasingly just skin it.

## 9\. The Code
This is the big one. The order is deliberate: master the **native** **`<dialog>`** first — in 2026 it's the correct default and it hands you the backdrop, the top layer, focus trapping, and Esc-to-close almost for free — then learn the framework wrappers and the headless engine for when you need full control.

### 9.1 HTML — the native `<dialog>` element (start here)
The platform now has a real modal primitive. Learn it cold before you reach for a library.

```plain
<!-- The trigger -->
<button type="button" id="open">Delete account</button>

<!-- The dialog. Note: it's inert and hidden until opened. -->
<dialog id="dlg" aria-labelledby="dlg-title" aria-describedby="dlg-desc">
  <form method="dialog">
    <h2 id="dlg-title">Delete account?</h2>
    <p id="dlg-desc">This permanently removes your account and all data. This can't be undone.</p>

    <menu>
      <!-- method="dialog" submit closes the dialog and reports this value -->
      <button value="cancel" autofocus>Cancel</button>
      <button value="confirm" class="danger" formmethod="dialog">Delete</button>
    </menu>
  </form>
</dialog>
```

```javascript
const dlg = document.getElementById("dlg");

document.getElementById("open").addEventListener("click", () => {
  dlg.showModal();               // THE magic method — see below
});

dlg.addEventListener("close", () => {
  // dlg.returnValue is "cancel" or "confirm" — set by the button's value
  if (dlg.returnValue === "confirm") deleteAccount();
});
```

What `showModal()` gives you **for free** — and why it's the 2026 default:

*   **Top layer.** The dialog is promoted to the browser's top layer — it renders above _everything_ regardless of `z-index` or `overflow:hidden` ancestors. The `z-index` war is over; there is no stacking-context escape hatch to fight. This alone kills a whole category of "my modal is behind the header" bugs.
*   **`::backdrop`****\*\*\*\*.** A real pseudo-element behind the dialog you can style directly (`dialog::backdrop { background: rgb(0 0 0 / .5); }`). No extra div.
*   **Focus trapping.** The browser traps Tab/Shift+Tab inside the dialog automatically. You do not write a focus-trap engine for a native modal — the platform does it. (§9.6 covers writing one by hand for when you can't use `<dialog>`.)
*   **Inert background.** Everything outside the dialog is automatically made inert and hidden from assistive tech while it's open. No manual `inert` toggling.
*   **Esc to close.** `showModal()` wires Escape → `close()` (and fires a `cancel` event you can `preventDefault()` to block). `.show()` (non-modal) does not.
*   **`autofocus`****\*\*\*\*.** The element with `autofocus` gets initial focus; otherwise the first focusable element does. Put `autofocus` on the _safe_ action.
*   **Focus return.** On close, focus returns to the element that had it before opening (usually your trigger) — automatically.
*   **`method="dialog"`** **forms.** A `<form method="dialog">` closes the dialog on submit _without_ navigating or reloading, and sets `dialog.returnValue` to the submitting button's `value`. Zero-JS close with a returned answer.

The gaps to know: `.showModal()` requires JS to open (there's a declarative `command="show-modal"` / `commandfor` path now landing, but coverage is still uneven in 2026 — feature-detect). Older Safari needs the polyfill for `::backdrop` styling niceties. And the default centering/reset styling is minimal — you _will_ write CSS.

### 9.2 CSS — styling the dialog, the backdrop, and the animation
The native element needs a reset and real styling. This is also where the **top-layer animation** trick lives.

```css
dialog {
  /* Reset the UA defaults */
  margin: auto;                       /* centers in the viewport */
  padding: 24px;
  border: none;
  border-radius: 16px;
  max-width: min(512px, calc(100vw - 32px));
  max-height: min(90vh, 720px);
  box-shadow: 0 24px 64px rgb(0 0 0 / .4);
  color: #E8E6E1;
  background: #1a1420;                 /* Eminence-tinted dark surface */

  /* Enter/exit animation, top-layer aware */
  opacity: 0;
  transform: translateY(8px) scale(.98);
  transition:
    opacity .2s ease,
    transform .2s ease,
    overlay .2s ease allow-discrete,   /* the top-layer property */
    display .2s ease allow-discrete;   /* keep it displayed during exit */
}

/* Open state */
dialog[open] {
  opacity: 1;
  transform: translateY(0) scale(1);
}

/* Starting state for the ENTER animation (from the top layer) */
@starting-style {
  dialog[open] {
    opacity: 0;
    transform: translateY(8px) scale(.98);
  }
}

/* The backdrop is a real pseudo-element — no extra div */
dialog::backdrop {
  background: rgb(4 5 8 / 0);
  backdrop-filter: blur(0px);
  transition:
    background .2s ease,
    backdrop-filter .2s ease,
    overlay .2s ease allow-discrete,
    display .2s ease allow-discrete;
}
dialog[open]::backdrop {
  background: rgb(4 5 8 / .55);
  backdrop-filter: blur(2px);
}
@starting-style {
  dialog[open]::backdrop { background: rgb(4 5 8 / 0); backdrop-filter: blur(0); }
}

/* Body scrolls internally; header/footer stay pinned */
dialog > form { display: flex; flex-direction: column; max-height: inherit; }
dialog .modal-body { overflow-y: auto; overscroll-behavior: contain; }

/* Respect users who don't want motion */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  dialog, dialog::backdrop { transition: none; }
}
```

The three properties doing the heavy lifting for a modal that animates _both directions_ on the top layer:

*   **`@starting-style`** defines the "before-open" values so the browser has something to animate _from_ when the element enters the top layer (elements can't otherwise transition from `display:none`).
*   **`transition-behavior: allow-discrete`** (here written inline in the shorthand) lets normally un-animatable discrete properties — `display` and the internal `overlay` property — participate, so the element stays rendered through the _exit_ transition instead of vanishing instantly.
*   **`overlay`** is the property that represents "is this in the top layer." Transitioning it with `allow-discrete` is what keeps the exit smooth.

Without these three, native `<dialog>` animates _in_ acceptably but pops _out_ instantly — the classic "why won't my modal fade out" bug.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript — a proper headless modal (the long one)
The reusable component most teams actually own. This wraps native `<dialog>` where possible, portals to `document.body`, manages the return-focus, and locks scroll. It's the "I need full control but correct behavior" version.

```typescript
import {
  useRef, useEffect, useId, type ReactNode, type MouseEvent,
} from "react";
import { createPortal } from "react-dom";

interface ModalProps {
  open: boolean;
  onClose: () => void;
  title: string;
  children: ReactNode;
  /** role="alertdialog" for destructive confirms; blocks backdrop-close. */
  variant?: "dialog" | "alertdialog";
  /** allow closing by clicking the backdrop (default true, off for dirty forms) */
  dismissOnBackdrop?: boolean;
  /** allow Esc to close (default true) */
  dismissOnEsc?: boolean;
  describedById?: string;
}

export function Modal({
  open, onClose, title, children,
  variant = "dialog",
  dismissOnBackdrop = true,
  dismissOnEsc = true,
  describedById,
}: ModalProps) {
  const dlgRef = useRef<HTMLDialogElement>(null);
  const titleId = useId();
  const triggerRef = useRef<Element | null>(null);

  // Open/close the NATIVE dialog so we inherit focus-trap + inert + top layer.
  useEffect(() => {
    const dlg = dlgRef.current;
    if (!dlg) return;
    if (open && !dlg.open) {
      triggerRef.current = document.activeElement;   // remember who to return focus to
      dlg.showModal();
      lockScroll(true);
    } else if (!open && dlg.open) {
      dlg.close();
    }
  }, [open]);

  // Native 'close' fires on Esc, form submit, or dlg.close(). Sync + restore.
  useEffect(() => {
    const dlg = dlgRef.current;
    if (!dlg) return;
    const handleClose = () => {
      lockScroll(false);
      onClose();
      (triggerRef.current as HTMLElement | null)?.focus?.();   // return focus
    };
    // Esc fires 'cancel' first; block it if the caller opted out.
    const handleCancel = (e: Event) => { if (!dismissOnEsc) e.preventDefault(); };
    dlg.addEventListener("close", handleClose);
    dlg.addEventListener("cancel", handleCancel);
    return () => {
      dlg.removeEventListener("close", handleClose);
      dlg.removeEventListener("cancel", handleCancel);
    };
  }, [onClose, dismissOnEsc]);

  // Mount-scoped safety net. If this modal is conditionally mounted and gets
  // UNMOUNTED while still open, the native 'close' event never fires, so the
  // scroll lock above is never released and the page is left frozen with
  // body{overflow:hidden}. Releasing it on unmount guarantees the lock (and the
  // native dialog's background inert, torn down automatically when the element
  // leaves the DOM) can never get stuck. Runs once on unmount only.
  useEffect(() => () => lockScroll(false), []);

  function onBackdropClick(e: MouseEvent<HTMLDialogElement>) {
    if (!dismissOnBackdrop || variant === "alertdialog") return;
    // A click whose target is the dialog element itself = the backdrop area.
    if (e.target === dlgRef.current) dlgRef.current?.close();
  }

  return createPortal(
    <dialog
      ref={dlgRef}
      role={variant}
      aria-labelledby={titleId}
      aria-describedby={describedById}
      onClick={onBackdropClick}
      className="ujg-modal"
    >
      {/* Inner wrapper stops backdrop-click from firing on content clicks */}
      <div className="ujg-modal__panel" onClick={(e) => e.stopPropagation()}>
        <header className="ujg-modal__header">
          <h2 id={titleId}>{title}</h2>
          {/* An alertdialog demands an explicit decision — no always-on "X" that
              silently dismisses it. Render the close affordance for standard
              dialogs only; alertdialogs exit through Cancel/confirm in the footer. */}
          {variant !== "alertdialog" && (
            <button type="button" aria-label="Close" onClick={() => dlgRef.current?.close()}>
              &times;
            </button>
          )}
        </header>
        <div className="ujg-modal__body">{children}</div>
      </div>
    </dialog>,
    document.body
  );
}

// Scroll-lock WITHOUT layout shift — pad for the scrollbar we're removing.
function lockScroll(on: boolean) {
  const sbw = window.innerWidth - document.documentElement.clientWidth;
  document.body.style.overflow = on ? "hidden" : "";
  document.body.style.paddingRight = on ? `${sbw}px` : "";
}
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:

*   **It delegates to native** **`<dialog>`** — so the focus trap, background `inert`, top layer, and Esc handling are the browser's job, not a 200-line hand-rolled trap that breaks on the next portal.
*   **It captures** **`document.activeElement`** **before opening and restores it on close** — focus return to the trigger, the thing everyone forgets. (Native does this too, but capturing explicitly survives portals and re-renders.)
*   **`variant="alertdialog"`** **blocks backdrop-close** and switches the ARIA role, so destructive confirms can't be dismissed by a stray click.
*   **`dismissOnEsc={false}`** **cancels the native** **`cancel`** **event** — the correct way to disable Esc, and it forces you to provide another way out.
*   **Scroll lock pads for the scrollbar width**, so opening the modal doesn't shift the page sideways (the jump-on-open bug in §12).
*   **It portals to** **`document.body`** so no ancestor's `overflow:hidden` or `transform` can clip it — belt-and-suspenders even though the top layer already escapes.

### 9.6 Vanilla JS — a hand-rolled focus trap (for when you can't use `<dialog>`)
Sometimes you're stuck with a `<div role="dialog">` (legacy app, custom overlay). Then the focus trap is _yours_ to build — and this is the engine the whole overlay family shares. Get it right once.

```javascript
const FOCUSABLE = [
  'a[href]', 'button:not([disabled])', 'input:not([disabled])',
  'select:not([disabled])', 'textarea:not([disabled])',
  '[tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"])',
].join(",");

function openModal(dialogEl) {
  const previouslyFocused = document.activeElement;   // 1. remember the trigger
  const backdrop = document.querySelector(".backdrop");

  // 2. inert the rest of the page (everything that isn't the dialog)
  [...document.body.children].forEach((el) => {
    if (el !== dialogEl && el !== backdrop) el.inert = true;
  });

  dialogEl.hidden = false;
  lockScroll(true);

  // 3. first focus: the element with [autofocus], else first focusable, else the dialog
  const focusables = () => [...dialogEl.querySelectorAll(FOCUSABLE)]
    // Visible only. Use checkVisibility() (falling back to getClientRects) rather
    // than offsetParent — offsetParent is null for position:fixed elements, which
    // would wrongly drop a legitimately visible fixed focusable from the trap.
    .filter((el) => el.checkVisibility?.() ?? el.getClientRects().length > 0);
  const first = dialogEl.querySelector("[autofocus]") || focusables()[0] || dialogEl;
  first.focus();

  function onKeydown(e) {
    if (e.key === "Escape") { close(); return; }
    if (e.key !== "Tab") return;

    // 4. the trap: cycle Tab / Shift+Tab within the dialog
    const items = focusables();
    if (items.length === 0) { e.preventDefault(); return; }
    const firstEl = items[0];
    const lastEl = items[items.length - 1];

    if (e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === firstEl) {
      e.preventDefault(); lastEl.focus();               // wrap to end
    } else if (!e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === lastEl) {
      e.preventDefault(); firstEl.focus();              // wrap to start
    }
  }

  function onBackdrop(e) { if (e.target === backdrop) close(); }

  function close() {
    document.removeEventListener("keydown", onKeydown);
    backdrop?.removeEventListener("click", onBackdrop);
    [...document.body.children].forEach((el) => (el.inert = false));  // un-inert
    dialogEl.hidden = true;
    lockScroll(false);
    previouslyFocused?.focus();                          // 5. RETURN focus to trigger
  }

  document.addEventListener("keydown", onKeydown);
  backdrop?.addEventListener("click", onBackdrop);
  return close;
}
```

The five things a correct trap must do, all present above: **(1)** remember the trigger, **(2)** inert everything else, **(3)** set first-focus sensibly, **(4)** cycle Tab _and_ Shift+Tab with wraparound, **(5)** return focus on close. Miss any one and you have a keyboard bug. Note the two subtle correctnesses: `focusables()` is re-queried on every keypress (so a modal whose content changes doesn't trap on a stale list), and it filters to _visible_ elements (`checkVisibility()`, not `offsetParent` — the latter is null for `position:fixed` focusables and would wrongly exclude them) so a hidden input doesn't swallow focus. Honestly, though — **this is why** **`<dialog>`** **exists.** Prefer §9.1; keep this for legacy.

### 9.8 Node.js — the endpoint behind a confirm modal
A confirmation modal exists to gate a state-changing request. The server must still defend that action as if the modal didn't exist — the modal is UX, never security.

```typescript
import express from "express";
import { z } from "zod";

const router = express.Router();
const seen = new Map<string, unknown>();   // real apps: Redis/DB

// The modal's "Delete" button ultimately hits this.
router.delete("/api/accounts/:id", requireAuth, rateLimit, async (req, res) => {
  const key = req.header("Idempotency-Key");

  // Authorize BEFORE serving any cached response. If the cache is checked first,
  // a replayed Idempotency-Key skips authorization entirely — re-check every time.
  if (!req.user.can("delete", "account", req.params.id)) return res.sendStatus(403);

  // Scope the dedupe key to the ACTOR and the RESOURCE, never the raw client key
  // alone — otherwise one user's key can collide with (and replay) another user's
  // cached result across a different account.
  const cacheKey = key ? `${req.user.id}:${req.params.id}:${key}` : null;
  if (cacheKey && seen.has(cacheKey)) return res.status(200).json(seen.get(cacheKey));  // dedupe

  // High-stakes destructive action → require a re-typed confirmation from the body
  const parsed = z.object({ confirmText: z.literal("DELETE") }).safeParse(req.body);
  if (!parsed.success) return res.status(422).json({ error: "Confirmation text required" });

  await db.accounts.softDelete(req.params.id, { by: req.user.id });
  await audit.log("account.delete", { actor: req.user.id, target: req.params.id });  // trail
  const result = { deleted: true };
  if (cacheKey) seen.set(cacheKey, result);
  return res.status(200).json(result);
});

export default router;
```

Backend concerns a confirm modal silently depends on: **the confirmation is not a security control.** A user can call `DELETE /api/accounts/:id` with `curl` and never see your beautiful modal. So the server independently re-checks **authentication**, **authorization** (can _this_ user delete _that_ account), **validation** (for type-to-confirm, verify the token server-side too), **idempotency** (a double-clicked "Delete" must not run twice — but run authorization _before_ serving the cached result, and key the cache on the actor and resource, not the client-supplied key alone, so a replayed key can't skip authz or collide across users), **rate limiting**, and writes an **audit event**. The modal reduces _accidental_ clicks; the server defends against _everything else_.

### 9.9 Python (backend defense + a server-rendered dialog)
**Backend** (FastAPI) receiving the confirm modal's request:

```python
from fastapi import FastAPI, Header, HTTPException, Depends
from pydantic import BaseModel

app = FastAPI()
_seen: dict[str, dict] = {}

class Confirm(BaseModel):
    confirm_text: str

@app.delete("/api/accounts/{account_id}")
def delete_account(
    account_id: str,
    body: Confirm,
    user=Depends(require_auth),
    idempotency_key: str | None = Header(default=None),
):
    # Authorize BEFORE serving any cached response — a replayed Idempotency-Key
    # must never skip the per-resource authorization check.
    if not user.can("delete", "account", account_id):
        raise HTTPException(403, "Not allowed")
    # Scope the dedupe key to the ACTOR and the RESOURCE, never the raw client
    # key alone, so one user's key can't replay another's cached result.
    cache_key = f"{user.id}:{account_id}:{idempotency_key}" if idempotency_key else None
    if cache_key and cache_key in _seen:
        return _seen[cache_key]
    if body.confirm_text != "DELETE":                 # re-verify the modal's gate
        raise HTTPException(422, "Confirmation text required")
    db.soft_delete_account(account_id, by=user.id)
    audit.log("account.delete", actor=user.id, target=account_id)
    result = {"deleted": True}
    if cache_key:
        _seen[cache_key] = result
    return result
```

**Rendering** a native dialog server-side (Django/Jinja) — the markup ships in the HTML; a sprinkle of JS calls `showModal()`:

### 9.12 Web Component (framework-agnostic)
A modal that works in any framework (or none), wrapping native `<dialog>` in a shadow root:

```typescript
class UIModal extends HTMLElement {
  #dlg!: HTMLDialogElement;
  connectedCallback() {
    this.attachShadow({ mode: "open" });
    this.shadowRoot!.innerHTML = `
      <style>
        dialog { margin:auto; border:none; border-radius:16px; padding:24px;
                 max-width:min(512px,90vw); background:#1a1420; color:#E8E6E1; }
        dialog::backdrop { background:rgb(4 5 8 / .55); backdrop-filter:blur(2px); }
      </style>
      <dialog part="dialog" aria-labelledby="t">
        <slot name="title"></slot>
        <slot></slot>
      </dialog>`;
    this.#dlg = this.shadowRoot!.querySelector("dialog")!;
    this.#dlg.addEventListener("close", () =>
      this.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent("close")));
    this.#dlg.addEventListener("click", (e) => {
      if (e.target === this.#dlg) this.#dlg.close();       // backdrop click
    });
  }
  open() { this.#dlg.showModal(); }        // inherits focus trap + inert + top layer
  close() { this.#dlg.close(); }
}
customElements.define("ui-modal", UIModal);
// Usage: const m = document.querySelector("ui-modal"); m.open();

  await expect(dialog).toBeVisible();

  // Background is inert: a button behind the modal should not be reachable
  await page.keyboard.press("Tab");
  await page.keyboard.press("Tab");
  await expect(dialog).toContainText("This can't be undone.");

  await page.keyboard.press("Escape");
  await expect(dialog).toBeHidden();
  await expect(trigger).toBeFocused();          // focus returned to the trigger
});
```

What to actually assert (and what to skip):

*   **Do test:** it's found `byRole("dialog"/"alertdialog")` with an accessible name; initial focus lands inside; **Tab and Shift+Tab are trapped**; Esc closes (when allowed); backdrop-close is on/off per variant; **focus returns to the trigger**; background is inert; `axe` is clean.
*   **Don't test:** exact class names, z-index values, animation durations, hex colors. Implementation details that break tests without catching real bugs.
*   The focus-trap and return-focus tests are the ones that catch the bugs users actually hit. Write them first.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
The modal is the accessibility component. There is no "we'll add a11y later" — an inaccessible modal is a broken modal for a huge population. The full contract, per the ARIA Authoring Practices "Dialog (Modal)" pattern:

*   **Role.** The container is `role="dialog"` (or `role="alertdialog"` for a critical confirm/error that needs immediate acknowledgment). Native `<dialog>` + `showModal()` supplies this. `alertdialog` makes screen readers announce more assertively and expects a description.
*   **`aria-modal="true"`****\*\*\*\*.** Tells assistive tech the rest of the page is inert. Native `showModal()` sets the equivalent; on a hand-rolled `role="dialog"` you set it yourself — **but** **`aria-modal`** **alone is not enough**, you still must make the background actually `inert`.
*   **Accessible name.** `aria-labelledby` points at the visible title (preferred), or `aria-label` if there's no visible heading. Without it, screen readers announce "dialog" with no context.
*   **Accessible description.** `aria-describedby` points at the body text so the purpose is announced with the dialog — essential for `alertdialog`.
*   **Focus goes in on open.** Move focus into the dialog when it opens: to the first focusable element, or a specific input, or — for destructive confirms — the **safest** action (Cancel), never the destructive one. If there's nothing interactive, focus the dialog container (`tabindex="-1"`).
*   **Focus is trapped while open.** Tab and Shift+Tab cycle within the dialog and never reach the page behind. This is the defining behavior; §9.6 is the engine.
*   **Focus returns on close.** When the modal closes, focus goes back to the element that opened it. Dumping the user at the top of the document is a classic, disorienting failure.
*   **Background is inert.** Everything outside the dialog gets `inert` (which also removes it from the accessibility tree) or `aria-hidden="true"` on the siblings. Native `showModal()` does this automatically; hand-rolled modals must do it explicitly — and remember to undo it on close.
*   **Escape closes** (unless a deliberate, clearly-signposted exception). Esc is the universal "get me out" and must map to cancel/dismiss.
*   **Keyboard map:**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| `Tab` | Move to next focusable element within the dialog; wraps from last to first |
| `Shift + Tab` | Move to previous focusable within the dialog; wraps from first to last |
| `Esc` | Close the dialog (cancel), unless deliberately disabled with an alternate exit |
| `Enter` | Activate the focused control; in a form, submit via the default button |

*   **Contrast.** Dialog text ≥ 4.5:1 against the dialog surface; the backdrop must provide enough separation that the dialog reads as "on top." Don't rely on the dim alone to convey "blocked."
*   **Target size.** The close "X" and all action buttons keep a ≥ 44px hit target — small-looking icon buttons still need big tappable areas.
*   **Don't trap forever.** A focus trap with **no way out** (Esc disabled _and_ no visible close/cancel) is itself a WCAG failure (2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap). If you block Esc, you must provide an obvious visible exit.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The modal looks solved but the platform is actively reshaping it in 2026:

*   **Native** **`<dialog>`** **\+ top layer as the default.** The biggest shift: you no longer hand-roll focus traps, `z-index` stacks, or portals for the common case. The platform owns the hard parts, and the ecosystem is migrating onto it.
*   **`@starting-style`** **+** **`allow-discrete`** **+** **`overlay`****\*\*\*\*.** Entry/exit animations for top-layer elements _without JavaScript_ — the modal fades in and out declaratively, something that required a state-machine and `onAnimationEnd` juggling a year ago.
*   **Declarative invokers:** **`command`** **/** **`commandfor`****\*\*\*\*.** `<button command="show-modal" commandfor="dlg">` opens a dialog with **zero JavaScript** — the modal equivalent of the popover attribute. Landing across browsers through 2026; feature-detect for now.
*   **The Popover API as the non-modal sibling.** `popover` + `popovertarget` gives light-dismiss, top-layer popovers natively — which finally makes it easy to reach for the _right_ overlay (popover vs. modal) instead of defaulting to a modal for everything.
*   **`::backdrop`** **+** **`backdrop-filter`****\*\*\*\*.** Real, styleable backdrops with blur/refraction, no overlay div — and the hook for Liquid-Glass scrims (§17).
*   **URL-addressable modals** (Next.js intercepting routes, and framework equivalents): modals with their own URL, shareable and back-button-native. The modal stops being ephemeral UI state and becomes a real, linkable place.
*   **Scroll-driven and gesture-driven sheets on the web:** drag-to-dismiss bottom sheets with detents, powered by CSS scroll-snap and the emerging CSS anchor/scroll APIs, closing the gap with native mobile.
*   **AI-native confirm surfaces:** "explain what this will do" modals where an agent previews the consequence of a destructive action before you confirm — the confirmation modal grows a brain.

## 12\. Conversion & UX Killers (mistakes that bleed money and trust)
None of these throw an error. They quietly tank your numbers and your accessibility score.

*   **The focus trap that doesn't trap.** Keyboard/screen-reader users Tab straight out of the "modal" into the hidden page behind it. The single most common overlay a11y failure — and invisible to mouse-using QA.
*   **No focus return.** Closing the modal dumps focus at the top of the document. The user was on "row 47," now they're lost. Silent for sighted mouse users; brutal for everyone else.
*   **Backdrop-click nukes a filled-in form.** One stray click on the dim area, and everything typed is gone with no warning. Disable backdrop-close for dirty forms, or confirm before discarding.
*   **The scrollbar jump on open.** Locking scroll by setting `overflow:hidden` removes the scrollbar, the page gets ~15px wider, and everything shifts sideways as the modal opens. Pad the body for the scrollbar width (§9.3). Cheap to fix, instantly makes the app feel amateur if you don't.
*   **The uninvited pop-up.** A newsletter/discount modal three seconds after landing — especially on mobile — is a bounce machine and a Google interstitial penalty. Gate it on exit-intent or scroll depth, or use an inline banner.
*   **Can't close it.** Esc disabled, no visible X, backdrop-click off, and the only button is "Continue." You've built a trap. There must always be an obvious way out.
*   **The confirmation-modal-as-security theater.** "Are you sure?" on _every_ trivial action trains users to click "Yes" without reading — so when the one that matters appears, they reflex-confirm and delete the wrong thing. Reserve confirmation for genuinely destructive/irreversible actions; for the rest, prefer **undo** over **confirm** (an "Undo" toast beats an "Are you sure?" gate for reversible actions).
*   **Modal-on-modal stacking.** A second modal on top of the first (a confirm over a form-modal) doubles the scrim, confuses focus, and signals a flow you didn't finish designing. Rethink it (§13).
*   **Body doesn't scroll, so the footer is unreachable.** Content taller than the viewport with no internal scroll region means "Confirm" is below the fold with no way to reach it. Cap height, scroll the body, pin the footer.
*   **Content shifts as it animates in.** Animating `height`/`width`/`top` reflows every frame and jitters. Animate only `transform` and `opacity` (§14).

**The through-line:** modal failures are quiet. No crash, just a keyboard user who can't escape, a form that lost its data, or a bounce you'll blame on "the market." Audit your modals before you audit your funnel.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
### Scroll-lock without layout shift
The correct lock preserves the scrollbar gutter so the page never jumps. Measure the scrollbar, hide overflow, pad the difference:

```css
/* Modern one-liner where supported — reserves the gutter always */
html { scrollbar-gutter: stable; }
```

```javascript
// Robust cross-browser version (mirrors §9.3)
function lockScroll(on) {
  const sbw = window.innerWidth - document.documentElement.clientWidth;
  document.body.style.overflow = on ? "hidden" : "";
  document.body.style.paddingRight = on ? `${sbw}px` : "";
}
```

On iOS Safari, `overflow:hidden` on `body` famously doesn't fully lock touch-scroll; the bullet-proof approach adds `position:fixed; top:-scrollY` on the body and restores the scroll position on close, or uses `overscroll-behavior: contain` on the modal body to at least stop scroll-chaining.

### Nested / stacked modals (avoid — but handle)
Stacking is a smell, but a "Discard changes?" confirm over an edit-modal is sometimes unavoidable. Rules if you must:

*   **Only the topmost modal traps focus and receives Esc.** Maintain a stack; the active trap is always the last-pushed.
*   **Each layer gets its own backdrop,** but consider dimming _more_ per layer so depth reads.
*   **Esc pops one layer at a time** (closes the top confirm, not the whole stack).
*   **Return focus down the stack** — closing the top modal returns focus into the one beneath, not to the page.
*   Native `<dialog>` stacks correctly in the top layer (each `showModal()` pushes above the last), which is a strong reason to prefer it for the rare legitimate case.
*   **Better:** redesign so the confirm is inline within the first modal (swap the footer to "Discard / Keep editing") instead of layering a second dialog.

### The confirmation modal, done right (and when to skip it)
Prefer **undo over confirm** for anything reversible: perform the action immediately, show an "Undone? Undo" toast. It's faster and less annoying. Reserve the modal for the irreversible. When you do confirm a destructive action:

*   Use `role="alertdialog"`, focus **Cancel**, and name the specific consequence ("Delete _3 projects and 47 files_?", not "Are you sure?").
*   For the highest stakes, require **type-to-confirm** (type the resource name) or **re-authentication** — and verify it server-side (§9.8), because the modal is not the security control.
*   Make the destructive button visually distinct (danger color) and separated from Cancel so it's not a reflex click.

### `inert` — the whole background, one attribute
Modern browsers ship the `inert` attribute: an inert subtree can't be focused, clicked, or reached by assistive tech. For a hand-rolled modal, inerting the app root is far cleaner than toggling `aria-hidden` + `tabindex="-1"` on dozens of elements:

```javascript
const appRoot = document.getElementById("app");
appRoot.inert = true;    // on open: the entire app behind the modal goes dead
appRoot.inert = false;   // on close: bring it back
```

(Native `showModal()` does the equivalent automatically. You only reach for `inert` by hand when you can't use `<dialog>`.)

### TypeScript: force a label at compile time
A modal with no accessible name is the most common a11y bug. A discriminated union can _require_ one of `title` / `aria-label` / `aria-labelledby`:

```typescript
type Labelled =
  | { title: string }                     // renders a visible <h2> + wires aria-labelledby
  | { "aria-label": string }              // no visible title
  | { "aria-labelledby": string };        // points at an existing element

type ModalProps = Labelled & {
  open: boolean;
  onClose: () => void;
  children: React.ReactNode;
};
// <Modal open onClose={x}>…</Modal>  ❌ won't compile — no accessible name
```

Now a nameless modal won't build. The type system enforces the a11y rule so a human doesn't have to remember it.

### aria-live for in-modal async results
When a submit inside the modal succeeds or fails, screen-reader users need it _announced_, not just shown. Pair the action with a polite live region inside the dialog:

```plain
<div class="modal-body">
  <p id="modal-status" aria-live="polite"></p>
  <!-- On error: status.textContent = "Couldn't save. Check the highlighted fields." -->
</div>
```

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A modal feels free. At scale it isn't — and the wrong choices cost jank at the worst moment (the user is _waiting_ on this overlay).

*   **Don't mount every modal upfront.** A page with twenty "row detail" modals rendered and hidden with `display:none` is twenty subtrees of DOM and state you're paying for on every render. **Render the modal only when open** (conditional mount) or lazy-load its contents. The trigger stays cheap; the heavy modal body loads on demand.
*   **Code-split heavy modal bodies.** A modal containing a rich editor, a chart, or a map should `React.lazy()` its contents so that weight isn't in the initial bundle — the user pays for the editor only when they open it.
*   **Animate only** **`transform`** **and** **`opacity`****\*\*\*\*.** Animating `width`/`height`/`top`/`margin` on open forces layout reflow every frame and jitters the entrance. Compositor-only properties keep it at 60fps. This is also why the loading state should keep the modal's size stable.
*   **Prefer the top layer over a portal +** **`z-index`****\*\*\*\*.** Native `<dialog>`/`showModal()` promotes to the top layer with no stacking-context cost and no giant `z-index`. A portal still works, but the top layer is cheaper and bug-free by construction.
*   **One delegated backdrop listener,** not a listener per interactive element to detect "outside" clicks. And clean up every listener on close — a modal that adds a `keydown` listener on open and forgets to remove it on close leaks a listener per open/close cycle.
*   **`backdrop-filter: blur()`** **is not free.** It's a real GPU cost, and stacking several blurred layers (nested modals, blurred backdrop + blurred card) can tank scroll performance on low-end devices. Use it deliberately; drop it under `prefers-reduced-transparency` or for perf.
*   **Unlock scroll and un-inert on close, always.** The nastiest modal bug is a close path that throws before restoring `body` overflow or clearing `inert` — the page is now permanently frozen or dead. Restore in a `finally`, not the happy path. And if the modal is conditionally mounted, release the lock in an **unmount-scoped cleanup** too (§9.3) — a modal unmounted _while still open_ never fires its close handler, so without that safety net the lock is left stuck.

## 15\. Security (the confirm button reaches the server)
Covered in depth in §9.8, distilled here because it's the part that gets skipped: **a modal is never a security boundary.**

*   **The confirmation is UX, not a control.** The endpoint behind "Delete" can be called directly with `curl` — the modal was never shown. Re-check auth, authorization, and validation server-side, every time (see the [Button](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200231) doc §9.24).
*   **Type-to-confirm / re-auth must be verified server-side.** If the modal makes the user type "DELETE" or re-enter a password, the server re-checks that token/credential too — a client that skips the modal skips the check otherwise.
*   **Idempotency.** A double-clicked "Confirm" must not run the action twice — idempotency key on the request, dedupe on the server (§9.8). Authorize _before_ the dedupe lookup, and scope the cache key to the actor and resource (not the raw client key), so a replayed key can't bypass per-resource authorization or return another user's result.
*   **CSRF & clickjacking.** A destructive endpoint fronted by a modal is still a state-changing endpoint: anti-CSRF token + `SameSite` cookies, and `frame-ancestors 'none'` so an attacker can't iframe your page and trick a click through a decoy modal.
*   **Audit the destructive action** server-side (who, what, when) so a confirmed deletion is traceable.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a modal is "done":

- [ ] Uses native `<dialog>` + `showModal()` where possible (top layer, free focus trap + inert).
- [ ] `role="dialog"` (or `alertdialog` for destructive/critical) with an accessible **name** (`aria-labelledby`/`aria-label`) and, for alertdialog, a **description** (`aria-describedby`).
- [ ] Focus moves **into** the dialog on open — to a safe target, never the destructive button.
- [ ] Focus is **trapped**: Tab and Shift+Tab cycle within and wrap; nothing behind is reachable.
- [ ] Focus **returns to the trigger** on close.
- [ ] Background is **inert** while open (and un-inerted on close).
- [ ] **Esc closes** (or, if deliberately disabled, an obvious visible exit exists — no keyboard trap).
- [ ] **Backdrop click** closes for content modals; **disabled** for dirty forms and alertdialogs (warn before discarding).
- [ ] **Scroll is locked** while open, **without layout shift** (scrollbar gutter padded).
- [ ] Body scrolls internally; header/footer stay pinned; footer reachable on short viewports.
- [ ] Enter/exit animate only `transform`/`opacity`; `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; exit actually animates (top-layer `@starting-style` + `allow-discrete`).
- [ ] Contrast ≥ 4.5:1; close "X" and actions ≥ 44px tap target.
- [ ] Heavy modal bodies are conditionally mounted / code-split; listeners cleaned up on close.
- [ ] The confirmed action is re-checked server-side (auth, validation, idempotency, audit) — the modal is not security.
- [ ] No stacked modals (or, if truly unavoidable, only the top one traps focus and Esc pops one layer).

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same modal, eleven aesthetics)
Design systems are _whose_ rules; visual styles are _which look_. The same semantic modal — same role, same focus trap, same Esc, same 44px targets — can wear any of these skins. **Style is skin; behavior is the skeleton.** Never let a skin change the semantics, the keyboard map, the focus management, or the target size. Full guidance lives in the [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) reference.

*   **Flat:** solid surface, crisp edge, a flat semi-opaque scrim, no shadow theatrics. Reads instantly, cheapest to render.
*   **Material:** elevated surface (shadow denotes the top layer), a scrim at a defined opacity token, container-transform motion on open. Full-screen dialog variant on mobile.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent dialog with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over the dimmed page. Watch text contrast — the blurred content behind can wreck legibility; keep a solid content band behind the text.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language. The `::backdrop` refracts and blurs the page beneath, the dialog has a specular rim and a subtle moving sheen, pill-soft corners, and the surface reacts to motion. On the web it's an honest approximation with layered `backdrop-filter`, gradients, and highlight borders; SwiftUI emits a native `.glassEffect()` sheet. Guard it hard for contrast **and** `prefers-reduced-motion` / `prefers-reduced-transparency`.
*   **Neumorphism:** dialog is the same color as a (light) page with dual inner/outer soft shadows. Contrast-poor by nature — fine for a soft settings sheet, wrong for an alert. Accent use only.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** the "real object" dialog — beveled edges, inner highlight, a paper/card texture, a heavier drop shadow so it reads as a physical card laid on the page.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** hard 4–6px offset shadow, thick black border, zero radius, a stark flat scrim, high-contrast clashing colors. Loud, confident, on-brand for campaign moments.
*   **Claymorphism:** big radius, puffy surface with inner top-light and bottom-shadow, soft colored glow around the dialog. Friendly, great for onboarding/celebration modals.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue gradient border or backdrop on a dark dialog. Premium feel; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (freeze the animation) and keep body text on a solid band.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** typography and generous whitespace do the work; near-invisible border, a light flat scrim, tight grid, sharp or barely-rounded corners. The dialog says less and means more.
*   **UJG Brand:** Afro-Futurist house style — a deep **Eminence** (`#5F2C82`) / **Night** (`#0A0A0A`) dialog surface, **Goldenrod** (`#DCA424`) primary action and focus ring, a `::backdrop` of Night at ~55% with a whisper of blur, confident 16px radius, and a warm gold glow on the elevated surface. Liquid-Glass accents sing over the dark Eminence backdrop — lean into it for hero moments, stay Flat/Swiss for dense/utility modals.

**The rule that never changes:** the modal's role, its focus trap, its return-focus, its Esc handling, its inert background, and its 44px targets are **identical across all eleven skins.** Guard glass / liquid-glass / neumorphism for contrast; guard aurora / liquid-glass for reduced-motion and reduced-transparency. A gorgeous modal that a keyboard user can't escape is a broken modal in a nice outfit.
