# News feed

# The News Feed: A Senior Engineer's Complete Breakdown
The infinite, heterogeneous, real-time stream that ate the internet. Here's everything from first principles to production code — feed semantics, virtualization, optimistic UI, and the cursor-paginated endpoint behind it.

**Try it live:** the companion playground will let you tune item density, media ratio, load-more vs. infinite scroll, the "new posts" pill, and the skeleton shimmer, then watch the feed re-render with `role=feed` wired up and a live keyboard-nav trace. It also outputs the component code across targets with one-click copy. (Built in a later pass — no live URL yet.)

**Audit a feed:** the companion audit angle will paste any feed's markup and run live checks — is it a real `role=feed`? do items carry `aria-posinset`/`aria-setsize`? is there a keyboard model? does new content yank scroll? — then export a client-ready report.

A news feed is not one component — it's an orchestration of many. It composes the [Card](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200471), Avatar, Image, Video, List, and Pagination into a single vertically-scrolling stream. Get the composition right and you have Instagram, LinkedIn, a Slack channel, an activity log. Get it wrong and you have a janky, inaccessible, memory-leaking scroll trap. This doc is the difference.
* * *

## 1\. What a News Feed Actually Is
A news feed is a **vertically-scrolling stream of heterogeneous content items, ordered by time or relevance, that grows as the user scrolls.** That's the whole idea. But four words in that sentence hide the entire discipline, and people conflate the feed with things it isn't:

*   **Feed vs. List.** A [List](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211) is homogeneous rows of the same shape (a settings list, a to-do list). A feed is _heterogeneous_ — a text post, then a photo card, then a shared link, then a "3 people you may know" carousel, then a video — all in one stream. The ARIA pattern is different (`role=list`/`listitem` vs. `role=feed`/`article`), the keyboard model is different, and the rendering strategy is different. Calling a feed "just a list" is the first mistake.
*   **Feed vs. Table/Data Grid.** A table is a 2-D grid of cells with row/column relationships and its own keyboard grid navigation. A feed is 1-D — a column of self-contained articles. If your items have columns that align across rows, you want a table, not a feed.
*   **Feed vs. Timeline.** "Timeline" is a _product_ word (Twitter/X timeline, Facebook timeline) usually implemented _as_ a feed. "Feed" is the _engineering_ pattern. They're the same skeleton.
*   **Feed vs. Pagination.** Classic [Pagination](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211) chops content into numbered pages you jump between. A feed _appends_ — page 2 stacks under page 1 in one continuous scroll. Both fetch in pages under the hood (cursor pagination, §9.7); the difference is purely how they present it. A feed is pagination that never makes you click "next" — which is exactly what creates its accessibility problems.
*   **The item is not the feed.** Each entry is a "feed item," "post," "story," "card," or "activity." The _feed_ is the container that manages ordering, loading, virtualization, and real-time updates. Keep the two mentally separate — they have different responsibilities and different tests.

**The one-line definition to remember:** a feed is an _append-only, heterogeneous, time/relevance-ordered stream with lazy loading and (usually) real-time updates._ Every hard part of building one falls out of those five properties.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The feed is the single most engagement-dense surface in modern software. It is where people _live_ — the home screen of every social product, the pulse of every SaaS activity log, the notification center of every app.

*   **Engagement and revenue live here.** Feed dwell time is the metric social companies build their business around. Every millisecond of scroll jank and every "why did my position jump" moment is measured in retention. The feed _is_ the product for a huge class of apps.
*   **It's the hardest accessibility surface on the web.** Naive infinite scroll is a documented a11y disaster: keyboard users can't reach the footer, screen-reader users have no idea how many items exist or where they are, focus vanishes when content loads. The ARIA `feed` pattern (§10) exists _specifically_ because this problem was so common and so bad.
*   **It's where performance goes to die.** A feed that renders 10,000 DOM nodes after a ten-minute scroll locks the main thread and crashes mobile Safari. Virtualization (§9, §14) is the price of admission at scale.
*   **It's a trust and safety surface.** A feed leaks private posts if authorization is wrong (§9.8), shows stale data if real-time is wrong, double-posts if idempotency is wrong. The blast radius of a feed bug is _everyone's data at once_.

Building a feed well means solving loading, ordering, virtualization, accessibility, real-time sync, optimistic UI, and authorization _simultaneously_ — which is why it's the composite component that separates senior engineers from the rest.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy
A feed decomposes into two nested anatomies: the **feed container** and the **feed item**.

**The feed container:**
*   **Scroll region:** the vertical viewport the stream lives in (page-level or a bounded `overflow` container).
*   **Item stream:** the ordered set of rendered items — the `role=feed` element.
*   **New-items indicator:** the "5 new posts" pill that appears when fresh content arrives above the current scroll position (§9, §11).
*   **Load trigger:** the sentinel/"Load more" button at the bottom that fetches the next page.
*   **Loading affordance:** skeletons on first load, a spinner or shimmering placeholders on append.
*   **Empty state:** what shows when there are zero items ("Nothing here yet — follow some people").
*   **Error state:** what shows when a fetch fails, with a retry.
*   **Pull-to-refresh:** the mobile gesture affordance at the top (§9, §11).

**The feed item (the composed card):**
*   **Author block:** [Avatar](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211) + display name + handle + optional verified badge + "• Follow" affordance.
*   **Timestamp / meta:** relative time ("2h"), often with an absolute time in `<time datetime>` for machines and hover.
*   **Content body:** the text — with entity linking (mentions, hashtags, URLs), truncation ("…more"), and RTL awareness.
*   **Media:** an [Image](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211), image grid, Video, link preview card, poll, or embedded quote. This is what makes the stream _heterogeneous_.
*   **Action bar:** like, comment, share/repost, save/bookmark — each with a count and a pressed state.
*   **Meta footer:** view count, "liked by X and 3 others," edited indicator.
*   **Overflow menu:** the "•••" for mute/report/hide/copy-link.

Notice: the item _is_ a [Card](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211) — everything the Card doc says about media ratios, padding, and elevation applies. The feed's job is to _sequence and manage_ these cards, not to redefine them.
* * *

## 4\. Density & Scale
Feeds ship at three densities. The density is a product decision that cascades into padding, avatar size, media treatment, and how many items fit above the fold.

| Density | Item padding | Avatar | Line spacing | Media | Example |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Comfortable | 16–20px | 48px | 1.5 | Full-bleed edge-to-edge | Instagram, Pinterest |
| Cozy (default) | 12–16px | 40px | 1.45 | Rounded, inset 12px | Twitter/X, LinkedIn |
| Compact | 8–12px | 32px | 1.35 | Small thumb or none | Slack, activity logs, admin |

Values that actually matter:
*   **Item vertical rhythm** is driven by a spacing token, never a magic number — the whole feed scales from one variable.
*   **Media aspect ratios** should be _constrained_ (`aspect-ratio: 16/9` / `4/5` / `1/1`), never left to the natural image size, or every image load shifts the layout below it (see CLS, §12).
*   **Max content width** on desktop is ~600px even on a 1440px screen. Feeds are single-column by design — line length beyond ~70ch destroys readability, and the eye needs a fixed scan column.
*   **Above-the-fold count:** at cozy density on a phone you get ~2–3 full items visible. That's your entire first impression budget — the first item's skeleton-to-content time is the number that matters.

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A feed is a _stateful machine_, and the amateur tell is shipping only the happy path. A production feed has a state for every one of these:

**Container-level states:**
1. **Initial load** — nothing fetched yet. Show **skeletons** (shaped placeholders), not a bare spinner. Skeletons preserve layout and lower perceived latency (§12).
2. **Loaded / idle** — items rendered, user scrolling.
3. **Loading more (append)** — next page in flight. Spinner/skeleton _at the bottom_, existing items untouched.
4. **All caught up / end of feed** — no more pages. Say so explicitly ("You're all caught up") instead of an endless spinner that implies more is coming.
5. **New items available** — fresh content arrived above the scroll. Show the **"5 new posts" pill**; do _not_ auto-inject and shove the user's reading position (§11).
6. **Empty** — zero items ever. A real empty state with a next action, not a blank void.
7. **Error (initial)** — first fetch failed. Full-panel error + retry.
8. **Error (append)** — a page fetch failed mid-scroll. Inline "Couldn't load more — retry" at the bottom, keep everything above.
9. **Offline / stale** — connection lost; show a banner and serve cached items.
10. **Refreshing** — pull-to-refresh in progress (mobile).

**Item-level states:** loading (skeleton), loaded, **focused** (keyboard ring on the article), hovered, **read/unread** (activity feeds), **optimistic** (shown before the server confirms, §9.6), **failed** (rolled back — "Couldn't post, retry"), **deleting** (fading before removal), **highlighted** (deep-linked "jump to this post").

The single hardest state is **new-items-above during real-time updates** — injecting content above the viewport without preserving scroll anchor is the #1 way to make a feed feel hostile. §11 covers the fix.
* * *

## 6\. Types & Variants
"Feed" is a family. The engineering is shared; the ordering, item mix, and update model differ.

*   **Reverse-chronological feed:** newest first, pure time order. Predictable, no ranking. Slack, a Twitter/X "Following" tab, activity logs. Easiest to reason about and to paginate (cursor = timestamp/id).
*   **Ranked / algorithmic feed:** ordered by a relevance score, not time. The "For You" feed. Requires a ranking service (§9.8 note) and makes cursor pagination harder because the order isn't a stable sort key.
*   **Aggregated / activity feed:** system-generated events ("Omegea liked your post," "3 new followers"), often _collapsed_ ("X and 12 others liked…"). Notification centers, GitHub's activity feed, audit logs.
*   **Media-first feed:** the grid/masonry variant where media dominates and text is secondary. Pinterest, Instagram Explore. Virtualization is brutal here because of variable tile heights (§14).
*   **Conversational feed:** a chat/thread stream — technically a feed with `role=feed` or `role=log`, ordered oldest→newest, that sticks to the bottom and prepends history when you scroll _up_. Slack, iMessage, comment threads. The scroll-anchor problem is inverted (new content at the _bottom_, history loads at the _top_).
*   **Hybrid injected feed:** organic items interleaved with ads, suggestions, "who to follow," and prompts. The injection logic is a product concern but a real engineering constraint — injected items break naive index math and must carry their own stable keys.

By update model: **static** (fetched once), **paginated** ("load more"/infinite scroll), **polled** (refetch on an interval), **real-time** (websocket/SSE push). Most real feeds are paginated _and_ real-time at once.

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a feed when content is **open-ended, time/relevance-ordered, heterogeneous, and consumed by scrolling.** Reach for something else when:

*   **The set is small and bounded** (< ~20 stable items) → a plain [List](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211). Virtualization and infinite scroll are pure overhead here.
*   **Users need to find a** **_specific_** **item or jump to page 40** → classic numbered [Pagination](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211). Infinite scroll makes "get me to the middle" impossible and destroys deep-linking and back-button behavior.
*   **The content is a table of comparable records** (invoices, users, transactions) → a Data Grid with sortable columns.
*   **There's a task the footer must always reach** (site footer links, a form's submit) → do _not_ trap it behind an infinite scroll. Either use "Load more" (footer stays reachable) or move the critical action out of the scroll region.
*   **SEO matters for every item** → infinite scroll hides content from crawlers unless you pair it with real paginated URLs (§12). E-commerce category pages famously fight this.

**The infinite-scroll-vs-"Load more" decision** is the central UX call:
*   **Infinite scroll** maximizes passive engagement (social feeds, discovery) but breaks footer access, harms deep-linking, disorients ("where am I?"), and is hostile to keyboard/screen-reader users unless `role=feed` is done right.
*   **"Load more" button** keeps the footer reachable, gives the user a controllable stopping point, is trivially keyboard-accessible, and preserves a sense of place. It costs one click per page. For most _utility_ feeds (dashboards, notifications, results), "Load more" is the more respectful default. `role=feed` is designed to make _either_ accessible — but "Load more" is accessible almost for free.

**Rule of thumb:** entertainment/discovery → infinite scroll with a rock-solid `role=feed`. Utility/task → "Load more." Never infinite-scroll a page that has a footer users need.

## 8\. Feeds Across Design Systems
Same stream, different house rules. Knowing these makes you fluent in any codebase.

*   **Material Design (Google):** a stream of elevated Cards on an 8dp grid, with the "list vs. feed" distinction baked into the guidance. Pull-to-refresh is first-class (`SwipeRefreshLayout` → `PullRefresh` in Compose).
*   **Apple HIG (iOS):** `UICollectionView` with compositional layout and _diffable data sources_ — cell reuse (virtualization) is the default, not an add-on. Pull-to-refresh (`UIRefreshControl`) is system-provided; SwiftUI's `List`/`LazyVStack` lazily render, and iOS 26 brings Liquid Glass to the chrome over the feed.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** virtualized lists for dense enterprise activity feeds — subtle materials, compact density, heavy keyboard-operability emphasis for power users.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** ships a `List` with a built-in `loadMore` footer and an `InfiniteScroll` component; explicit timeline/activity variant. Dense, pagination-first — the "utility feed" philosophy.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** no prescribed feed; you compose it. Community standard is TanStack Virtual (headless windowing) + Intersection Observer, wrapped into a `Feed` component.
*   **shadcn/Radix (2024+ web standard):** no dedicated Feed primitive, but the composition is idiomatic — cards/avatars/skeletons from the kit, TanStack Virtual for windowing, a Radix `ScrollArea`, and your own `role=feed` semantics. You own the code.
*   **Brand/expressive (social apps):** Instagram, TikTok, X roll bespoke engines. Common thread: aggressive cell reuse, media prefetching, and scroll-position preservation are non-negotiable at their scale.

The through-line: **every mature platform gives you a** **_virtualized, reusable-cell_** **scroller with pull-to-refresh and a load trigger.** Learn the concept once (windowing + lazy fetch + scroll anchoring), remap the API per platform.

### 9.1 HTML (the accessible foundation)
Before any framework, the semantics. A feed is a `role=feed` whose children are `role=article`, each labelled and positioned.

```plain
<!-- The stream: role=feed makes this an accessible feed region -->
<section role="feed" aria-busy="false" aria-label="Home feed">

  <!-- Each item is an article that knows its position in the set -->
  <article role="article"
           aria-posinset="1"
           aria-setsize="-1"                <!-- -1 = total unknown (infinite) -->
           aria-labelledby="post-42-author post-42-time"
           tabindex="0">                    <!-- articles are focusable, see §10 -->
    <header>
      <img src="/a/omegea.jpg" alt="" class="avatar" />   <!-- decorative: name is in text -->
      <span id="post-42-author">Omegea Hunter</span>
      <a href="/@omegea">@urbanjunglegoddess</a>
      <time id="post-42-time" datetime="2026-07-22T14:03:00Z">2h</time>
    </header>

    <div class="post-body">
      Shipping the new feed component today. role=feed is doing a lot of work here.
    </div>

    <img class="post-media" src="/m/ship.jpg" width="600" height="338"
         alt="A screenshot of the feed component with skeleton loaders" />

    <footer class="actions">
      <button type="button" aria-pressed="false" aria-label="Like, 128 likes">♡ 128</button>
      <button type="button" aria-label="Comment, 12 comments">💬 12</button>
      <button type="button" aria-label="Share">↗</button>
      <button type="button" aria-pressed="false" aria-label="Save">🔖</button>
    </footer>
  </article>

  <!-- more articles… -->
</section>

<!-- The load trigger: a REAL button, keyboard-reachable, not just a scroll sentinel -->
<button type="button" class="load-more">Load more posts</button>

<!-- Announce load activity to screen readers without stealing focus -->
<p class="sr-only" aria-live="polite" id="feed-status">Loaded 20 more posts</p>
```

Key HTML rules:
*   **`role=feed`** **is the container;** **`role=article`** **is each item.** This is the WAI-ARIA "Feed" pattern — it's what tells assistive tech "this is a dynamically-loading stream," enabling the special keyboard model in §10.
*   **`aria-setsize="-1"`** honestly signals "total is unknown" for an infinite feed. If you _do_ know the total, set the real number so screen readers can say "3 of 240."
*   **`aria-posinset`** on each article gives its 1-based position — essential when items load in and out and the DOM index isn't the logical index.
*   **`aria-labelledby`** points at the author + timestamp so the article announces as "Omegea Hunter, 2h, article" instead of reading the entire post on focus.
*   The **avatar** **`alt=""`** because the name is already live text beside it — a redundant "Omegea Hunter's avatar" is screen-reader noise. (See the Avatar doc.)
*   The **load trigger is a** **`<button>`**, not merely an Intersection Observer sentinel — keyboard and screen-reader users can't scroll a sentinel into view reliably.

Two senior details hide here: **`content-visibility: auto`** **+** **`contain-intrinsic-size`** is the browser-native "poor man's virtualization" — the engine skips layout/paint for off-screen items while still reserving their space so the scrollbar doesn't lie. And **`overflow-anchor: none`** turns _off_ the browser's automatic scroll anchoring on the container so _we_ control it when we prepend real-time items (§11) — otherwise the browser and your JS fight over the scroll position.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (a virtualized, accessible feed)
The reusable core. Windowed rendering (only visible items in the DOM), `role=feed` semantics, a "Load more" trigger, and an `aria-live` status. This is the longest block because it's the component.

```typescript
import {
  useRef, useState, useCallback, useEffect, forwardRef, type ReactNode,
} from "react";
import { useVirtualizer } from "@tanstack/react-virtual";

export interface FeedItemData {
  id: string;
  author: { name: string; handle: string; avatarUrl: string };
  createdAt: string;          // ISO
  body: string;
  media?: { type: "image" | "video"; url: string; width: number; height: number };
  likeCount: number;
  likedByMe: boolean;
  commentCount: number;
  kind: "post" | "share" | "suggestion" | "ad"; // heterogeneous item types
}

interface FeedProps {
  items: FeedItemData[];
  hasNextPage: boolean;
  isFetchingNextPage: boolean;
  onLoadMore: () => void;
  renderItem: (item: FeedItemData, index: number, total: number) => ReactNode;
  /** total set size if known; -1 signals "unknown / infinite" to AT */
  totalCount?: number;
}

export function Feed({
  items, hasNextPage, isFetchingNextPage, onLoadMore, renderItem, totalCount = -1,
}: FeedProps) {
  const scrollRef = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
  const [status, setStatus] = useState("");

  // Windowing: render only what's visible (+overscan). measureElement handles
  // DYNAMIC / variable item heights — the hard part of feed virtualization (§14).
  const virtualizer = useVirtualizer({
    count: items.length,
    getScrollElement: () => scrollRef.current,
    estimateSize: () => 320,        // best guess; corrected by real measurement
    overscan: 6,                    // keep a few off-screen for smooth scroll + focus
    measureElement: (el) => el.getBoundingClientRect().height,
  });

  // Load-more trigger: Intersection Observer for scroll + a real button for keyboard.
  const sentinelRef = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
  useEffect(() => {
    const el = sentinelRef.current;
    if (!el || !hasNextPage) return;
    const io = new IntersectionObserver(
      ([entry]) => { if (entry.isIntersecting && !isFetchingNextPage) onLoadMore(); },
      { rootMargin: "600px" }        // prefetch before the user hits the bottom
    );
    io.observe(el);
    return () => io.disconnect();
  }, [hasNextPage, isFetchingNextPage, onLoadMore]);

  // Announce append completion politely (no focus theft)
  useEffect(() => {
    if (!isFetchingNextPage && items.length) setStatus(`${items.length} posts loaded`);
  }, [isFetchingNextPage, items.length]);

  // Keyboard model for role=feed: PageDown/PageUp move between articles,
  // Ctrl+Home/Ctrl+End jump to the first / last loaded article (§10)
  const onKeyDown = useCallback((e: React.KeyboardEvent) => {
    // Ctrl+Home / Ctrl+End: the target article may be windowed OUT of the DOM,
    // so scroll it into view first, then focus once it has rendered — otherwise
    // focus would fall to <body> and strand the keyboard user (§10).
    if (e.ctrlKey && (e.key === "Home" || e.key === "End")) {
      const index = e.key === "Home" ? 0 : items.length - 1;
      if (index < 0) return;
      e.preventDefault();
      virtualizer.scrollToIndex(index, { align: e.key === "Home" ? "start" : "end" });
      requestAnimationFrame(() => {
        scrollRef.current
          ?.querySelector<HTMLElement>(`[data-index="${index}"] [role="article"]`)
          ?.focus();
      });
      return;
    }
    if (e.key !== "PageDown" && e.key !== "PageUp") return;
    const articles = Array.from(
      scrollRef.current?.querySelectorAll<HTMLElement>('[role="article"]') ?? []
    );
    const active = document.activeElement as HTMLElement;
    const i = articles.indexOf(active.closest('[role="article"]') as HTMLElement);
    const next = e.key === "PageDown" ? i + 1 : i - 1;
    if (articles[next]) { e.preventDefault(); articles[next].focus(); }
  }, [items.length, virtualizer]);

  return (
    <div ref={scrollRef} style={{ overflowY: "auto", height: "100%" }}>
      <div
        role="feed"
        aria-busy={isFetchingNextPage}
        aria-label="Home feed"
        onKeyDown={onKeyDown}
        style={{ height: virtualizer.getTotalSize(), position: "relative" }}
      >
        {virtualizer.getVirtualItems().map((v) => {
          const item = items[v.index];
          return (
            <div
              key={item.id}                         // STABLE key = item id, never index
              role="presentation"                   // transparent wrapper: feed still owns the article child
              data-index={v.index}
              ref={virtualizer.measureElement}      // re-measure real height
              style={{
                position: "absolute", top: 0, left: 0, width: "100%",
                transform: `translateY(${v.start}px)`,
              }}
            >
              {renderItem(item, v.index, totalCount)}
            </div>
          );
        })}
      </div>

      {hasNextPage && (
        <>
          <div ref={sentinelRef} aria-hidden="true" style={{ height: 1 }} />
          <button type="button" className="load-more"
                  onClick={onLoadMore} disabled={isFetchingNextPage}>
            {isFetchingNextPage ? "Loading…" : "Load more posts"}
          </button>
        </>
      )}
      {!hasNextPage && <p className="feed-end">You're all caught up ✳</p>}

      <p className="sr-only" role="status" aria-live="polite">{status}</p>
    </div>
  );
}
```

Why this is the senior version:
*   **Windowing with** **`measureElement`** solves the _variable-height_ problem — the reason feed virtualization is hard (§14). Items get an _estimated_ height, then their _real_ height is measured after render and the offsets corrected, so a short text post and a tall photo post coexist without the scrollbar jumping.
*   **Stable** **`key={item.id}`****\*\*\*\*, never** **`key={index}`** — index keys destroy React's reconciliation the instant an item is prepended (real-time) or removed (delete), causing wrong DOM reuse, lost focus, and mis-rendered optimistic state.
*   **Dual load trigger** — Intersection Observer for mouse scrollers, a real `<button>` for keyboard/AT. `rootMargin: 600px` prefetches the next page _before_ the user hits the bottom so the scroll never stalls.
*   **`aria-busy`** **\+ polite** **`role=status`** tell screen readers "loading" and "done" without yanking focus.
*   **`renderItem`** **injection** keeps the feed container agnostic to item _type_ — the heterogeneity (post vs. ad vs. suggestion) lives in the render prop, not the scroller.

### 9.4 The Feed Item (the composed card)
The item is where composition happens — Avatar + Image/Video + action bar. Kept as a pure, memoized component so a parent re-render (new item prepended) doesn't re-render every visible card.

```typescript
import { memo, useState } from "react";

// Prefer Intl.RelativeTimeFormat over a hand-rolled "2h ago" — it's i18n-correct
function relativeTime(iso: string): string {
  const diff = Date.now() - new Date(iso).getTime();
  const rtf = new Intl.RelativeTimeFormat(undefined, { numeric: "auto" });
  const min = Math.round(diff / 60000);
  if (Math.abs(min) < 60) return rtf.format(-min, "minute");
  const hr = Math.round(min / 60);
  if (Math.abs(hr) < 24) return rtf.format(-hr, "hour");
  return rtf.format(-Math.round(hr / 24), "day");
}
```

### 9.6 Optimistic Like (instant feedback, safe rollback)
The action a user does most in a feed is _like_. It must feel instant — update the UI _before_ the server confirms, and roll back if it fails. This is the canonical optimistic-UI pattern, shown with TanStack Query.

```typescript
import { useMutation, useQueryClient, type InfiniteData } from "@tanstack/react-query";
import type { FeedItemData } from "./Feed";

type Page = { items: FeedItemData[]; nextCursor: string | null };

export function useLike() {
  const qc = useQueryClient();
  return useMutation({
    mutationFn: ({ id, next }: { id: string; next: boolean }) =>
      fetch(`/api/posts/${id}/like`, {
        method: next ? "POST" : "DELETE",
        headers: { "Idempotency-Key": crypto.randomUUID() }, // guard double-fire
      }).then((r) => { if (!r.ok) throw new Error("like failed"); }),

    // 1. Optimistically flip the UI, snapshot for rollback
    onMutate: async ({ id, next }) => {
      await qc.cancelQueries({ queryKey: ["feed"] });
      const prev = qc.getQueryData<InfiniteData<Page>>(["feed"]);
      qc.setQueryData<InfiniteData<Page>>(["feed"], (data) =>
        !data ? data : {
          ...data,
          pages: data.pages.map((p) => ({
            ...p,
            items: p.items.map((it) =>
              it.id === id
                ? { ...it, likedByMe: next, likeCount: it.likeCount + (next ? 1 : -1) }
                : it),
          })),
        });
      return { prev };
    },

    // 2. Server rejected → restore the snapshot
    onError: (_e, _vars, ctx) => {
      if (ctx?.prev) qc.setQueryData(["feed"], ctx.prev);
      // and surface a toast: "Couldn't like — try again"
    },

    // 3. Reconcile with server truth (real count may differ from our +1)
    onSettled: () => qc.invalidateQueries({ queryKey: ["feed"] }),
  });
}
```

The three-beat rhythm — **optimistic apply → rollback on error → reconcile on settle** — is the whole discipline. The `Idempotency-Key` means a fast double-tap can't create two likes server-side (§9.8). And `onSettled` invalidation is what stops your optimistic `+1` from drifting from reality when someone _else_ liked the same post at the same moment.

### 9.7 Node.js — the cursor-paginated feed endpoint
Feeds **never** use `OFFSET`/`LIMIT` pagination. Offset pagination is broken for feeds: as new items arrive at the top, page 2's offset shifts and the user sees duplicates or skips. **Cursor (keyset) pagination** is the only correct approach — the cursor points at "where I left off," stable regardless of insertions above.

```typescript
import express from "express";
import { z } from "zod";

const router = express.Router();
const Query = z.object({
  cursor: z.string().optional(),          // opaque: encodes (createdAt, id)
  limit: z.coerce.number().int().min(1).max(50).default(20),
});

router.get("/api/feed", requireAuth, async (req, res) => {
  const parsed = Query.safeParse(req.query);
  if (!parsed.success) return res.status(422).json(parsed.error.issues);
  const { cursor, limit } = parsed.data;

  // Decode the opaque cursor → the (createdAt, id) of the last item seen.
  // The cursor is attacker-controllable (§15): reject anything that doesn't
  // decode to a sane (timestamp, id) before it reaches the query.
  let after: { createdAt: string; id: string } | null;
  try {
    after = cursor ? decodeCursor(cursor) : null;
  } catch {
    return res.status(400).json({ error: "invalid cursor" });
  }

  // Keyset pagination: fetch the NEXT `limit`+1 rows strictly "older" than the cursor.
  // The composite (created_at, id) ordering makes the sort key UNIQUE and stable.
  const rows = await db.query(
    `SELECT p.*
       FROM posts p
       JOIN follows f ON f.followee_id = p.author_id
      WHERE f.follower_id = $1
        AND p.visibility = 'public'                       -- AUTHZ, see below
        AND ($2::timestamptz IS NULL
             OR (p.created_at, p.id) < ($2, $3))          -- the keyset predicate
      ORDER BY p.created_at DESC, p.id DESC
      LIMIT $4`,
    [req.user.id, after?.createdAt ?? null, after?.id ?? null, limit + 1]
  );

  // Fetch limit+1 to know if there's a next page without a second COUNT query
  const hasMore = rows.length > limit;
  const items = rows.slice(0, limit);
  const nextCursor = hasMore
    ? encodeCursor({ createdAt: items.at(-1)!.created_at, id: items.at(-1)!.id })
    : null;

  res.json({ items: items.map(toDTO), nextCursor });
});

function encodeCursor(c: { createdAt: string; id: string }) {
  return Buffer.from(`${c.createdAt}|${c.id}`).toString("base64url");
}
function decodeCursor(s: string) {
  const [createdAt, id] = Buffer.from(s, "base64url").toString().split("|");
  // Never trust the client cursor: it must decode to a real (timestamp, id) (§15)
  if (!createdAt || !id || Number.isNaN(Date.parse(createdAt))) {
    throw new Error("malformed cursor");
  }
  return { createdAt, id };
}

export default router;
```

The details that make this correct:
*   **Keyset, not offset.** `(created_at, id) < (cursor)` walks strictly older rows. Inserting 50 new posts at the top does _nothing_ to this query — the user's page 2 is exactly what it was. Offset pagination would shift and show duplicates.
*   **Composite** **`(created_at, id)`** **sort key.** Timestamps collide (two posts in the same millisecond). Adding the unique `id` as a tiebreaker makes the ordering _total_, so no row is ever skipped or repeated at a page boundary. Back it with a composite index `(created_at DESC, id DESC)`.
*   **`limit + 1`** **trick.** Fetch one extra row to compute `hasMore` without a separate, expensive `COUNT(*)`.
*   **Opaque base64 cursor.** The client treats it as a blob. You can change the internal encoding (add a ranking score) without breaking clients.

**On ranking:** a chronological feed's cursor _is_ the sort key. A _ranked_ ("For You") feed can't keyset on a recomputed relevance score — the score changes between requests. Production ranked feeds pre-compute a materialized, ranked list per user (a "feed table" fanned out on write, or a scored snapshot cached per session), then cursor-paginate _that stable snapshot_ by rank+id. Ranking itself (engagement prediction, recency decay, diversity rules) is a separate service; the endpoint just paginates its output.

**On authorization (the part that leaks private data):** the `WHERE` clause above enforces two things — the viewer only sees posts from people they _follow_ (the `JOIN follows`), and only `public` posts. That is not enough on its own. You must **filter every item by the viewer's permission to see it**: blocked users, private/followers-only accounts, muted authors, deleted/soft-deleted posts, and per-post audience settings. The cardinal feed security sin is trusting the _feed generation_ to have filtered correctly and skipping a re-check at read time. Re-authorize at the read boundary. A single missing predicate here leaks someone's private posts into a stranger's feed — the highest-severity bug a feed can have.

### 9.8 Python — the same endpoint (FastAPI)

```python
from fastapi import FastAPI, Depends, Query, HTTPException
from pydantic import BaseModel
from datetime import datetime
import base64, binascii

app = FastAPI()

class FeedItem(BaseModel):
    id: str
    author_name: str
    body: str
    created_at: str
    like_count: int
    liked_by_me: bool

class FeedPage(BaseModel):
    items: list[FeedItem]
    next_cursor: str | None

def _encode(created_at: str, id_: str) -> str:
    return base64.urlsafe_b64encode(f"{created_at}|{id_}".encode()).decode()

def _decode(cursor: str) -> tuple[str, str]:
    # The cursor is attacker-controllable (§15): validate it decodes to a sane
    # (timestamp, id) and reject anything malformed with a 400, never a 500.
    try:
        created_at, id_ = base64.urlsafe_b64decode(cursor).decode().split("|")
        datetime.fromisoformat(created_at)          # must be a real timestamp
    except (ValueError, binascii.Error):
        raise HTTPException(status_code=400, detail="invalid cursor")
    if not created_at or not id_:
        raise HTTPException(status_code=400, detail="invalid cursor")
    return created_at, id_

@app.get("/api/feed", response_model=FeedPage)
def get_feed(
    cursor: str | None = Query(default=None),
    limit: int = Query(default=20, ge=1, le=50),
    user=Depends(require_auth),
):
    after = _decode(cursor) if cursor else None

    # Keyset pagination + read-time authorization in one query.
    rows = db.fetch(
        """
        SELECT p.* FROM posts p
        JOIN follows f ON f.followee_id = p.author_id
        WHERE f.follower_id = %(uid)s
          AND p.visibility = 'public'
          AND p.author_id NOT IN (SELECT blocked_id FROM blocks WHERE blocker_id = %(uid)s)
          AND (%(after)s IS NULL OR (p.created_at, p.id) < (%(ca)s, %(id)s))
        ORDER BY p.created_at DESC, p.id DESC
        LIMIT %(lim)s
        """,
        uid=user.id, after=after, ca=after and after[0], id=after and after[1],
        lim=limit + 1,
    )

    has_more = len(rows) > limit
    items = rows[:limit]
    next_cursor = _encode(items[-1]["created_at"], items[-1]["id"]) if has_more else None
    return FeedPage(items=[to_dto(r, user) for r in items], next_cursor=next_cursor)
```

Same shape, same rules: keyset predicate, `limit + 1`, opaque cursor, and the block-list re-check woven into the `WHERE` so no muted/blocked author leaks through. Python's job is identical to Node's — paginate stably and defend the read boundary.

### 9.9 Real-time updates (websocket / SSE, without yanking scroll)
New posts arrive while the user reads. The rule: **buffer, don't inject.** Never shove content into the viewport under the user's eyes — hold it and let them opt in via the "N new posts" pill.

```typescript
// Subscribe to new-item events; buffer them, surface a count.
function useRealtimeFeed(feedId: string) {
  const [buffered, setBuffered] = useState<FeedItemData[]>([]);

  useEffect(() => {
    const es = new EventSource(`/api/feed/${feedId}/stream`); // SSE; WS is analogous
    es.addEventListener("post", (e) => {
      const post: FeedItemData = JSON.parse((e as MessageEvent).data);
      setBuffered((b) => [post, ...b]);         // stash above, DON'T render inline
    });
    es.onerror = () => {/* backoff + reconnect; SSE auto-retries by default */};
    return () => es.close();
  }, [feedId]);

  return { newCount: buffered.length, buffered, clear: () => setBuffered([]) };
}
```

When the user taps the pill, _then_ you prepend — and you preserve their scroll position so the content they were reading doesn't jump (§11). For _polling_ instead of push, the same buffer applies: `setInterval` a fetch of "items newer than my newest cursor," stash them, show the pill. Poll on a backoff, pause when the tab is hidden (`document.visibilityState`), and never poll a background tab into the ground.

```typescript
"use client";
import { useInfiniteQuery } from "@tanstack/react-query";
import { Feed } from "@/components/Feed";

export function FeedClient({ initialItems, initialCursor }: Props) {
  const q = useInfiniteQuery({
    queryKey: ["feed"],
    queryFn: ({ pageParam }) =>
      fetch(`/api/feed?cursor=${pageParam ?? ""}`).then((r) => r.json()),
    initialPageParam: null as string | null,
    getNextPageParam: (last) => last.nextCursor,          // null = no more pages
    initialData: { pages: [{ items: initialItems, nextCursor: initialCursor }], pageParams: [null] },
  });

  const items = q.data?.pages.flatMap((p) => p.items) ?? [];
  return (
    <Feed items={items} hasNextPage={!!q.hasNextPage}
          isFetchingNextPage={q.isFetchingNextPage}
          onLoadMore={() => q.fetchNextPage()}
          renderItem={(it, i, total) => <FeedItem item={it} posInSet={i + 1} setSize={total} onLike={like} />} />
```

Server-rendering page 1 gives you a real first paint and crawlable content; the client takes over for the infinite part. `initialData` seeds TanStack Query with the server's page so there's no duplicate fetch on hydration.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
The feed is the hardest a11y surface on the web, and the ARIA **Feed pattern** exists specifically to tame it. This is where seniors are separated from juniors.

**The** **`role=feed`** **pattern — what it is and why it exists.** A feed is _not_ a list and _not_ a grid. It's a dynamically-loading stream where the total is often unknown and content appears/disappears as you scroll. `role=feed` tells assistive tech "expect this," and unlocks a keyboard model the browser can't provide for a plain `<div>`.

*   **Container:** `role="feed"`. Set `aria-busy="true"` on it _while_ loading a batch, back to `false` when the DOM is stable — so a screen reader doesn't try to read half-inserted items.
*   **Items:** each is `role="article"`, **focusable** (`tabindex="0"`), and carries:
    *   `aria-posinset` — its 1-based position in the logical set.
    *   `aria-setsize` — the total, or **`-1`** when unknown (infinite feed).
    *   `aria-labelledby` — pointing at the author + timestamp, so the article announces concisely on focus instead of dumping the whole post.

**The keyboard model** (this is the payoff — it's what makes an infinite feed _navigable_):
*   **Page Down** → move focus to the next article. **Page Up** → previous article.
*   **Ctrl + Home** → focus the first article; **Ctrl + End** → focus the last loaded article.
*   Within an article, **Tab** reaches the interactive controls (like, comment, links) normally.
*   Because articles are focusable landmarks, a screen-reader user can _jump between whole posts_ instead of tabbing through every like button in the stream. That's the entire point.

**Focus management as items load** — the make-or-break detail:
*   **Never move focus when new content loads.** When "Load more" appends items, focus stays exactly where the user put it. Yanking focus to the top (or to the new content) is disorienting and a WCAG failure.
*   **Announce, don't interrupt.** Use an `aria-live="polite"` status region ("20 more posts loaded") so the user _knows_ content arrived without being teleported.
*   **When an item is removed** (delete/hide), move focus to a sensible neighbor (the next article, or the feed container) — never let focus fall onto `document.body`, which strands keyboard users.
*   **Preserve the focused article across virtualization.** If a focused article scrolls out of the window and gets unmounted, focus is lost. Keep the focused item mounted (overscan) or restore focus to its replacement.

**The infinite-scroll accessibility problem (and how** **`role=feed`** **answers it):**
*   **No footer access.** Infinite scroll means the footer _never_ arrives — keyboard and screen-reader users can never reach "Privacy," "Settings," or "Contact." The fix is either "Load more" (footer reachable after the button) or moving critical links out of the infinite region entirely. This alone is why utility feeds should prefer "Load more."
*   **No sense of place.** Without `aria-setsize`/`aria-posinset`, a screen-reader user has no idea if they're at post 3 or post 3,000. The feed pattern restores "3 of many."
*   **Content vanishing.** Virtualization removes off-screen items from the DOM — a screen reader reviewing by arrow key hits a wall. Overscan and honest `aria-busy` mitigate; never virtualize so aggressively that the _currently-announced_ item is unmounted.

**The rest of the a11y checklist:**
*   **Media alt text:** every content image needs real `alt` describing it (not `alt=""` — that's only for the _decorative_ avatar beside live-text names). User-posted images should prompt the author for alt text.
*   **Action buttons:** icon-only like/comment/share need `aria-label` _with the count_ ("Like, 128 likes"), and toggles (like, save) need `aria-pressed`.
*   **Relative timestamps:** wrap in `<time datetime>` so the machine-readable absolute time is available; "2h" alone is ambiguous to AT.
*   **Contrast ≥ 4.5:1** for body text; don't signal "unread" with color alone.
*   **Touch targets ≥ 44px** for every action button, even when the icon looks small.
*   **`prefers-reduced-motion`****\*\*\*\*:** kill skeleton shimmer, auto-play, and scroll animations for users who ask.

## 11\. New-Items Indicator & Scroll Preservation
The single most-botched feed interaction: fresh content arrives, and the feed **shoves the user's reading position down** as items inject above. This is the "I was reading and the page jumped" rage-trigger. Two problems, two fixes.

**Problem 1 — don't auto-inject.** When real-time/polled items arrive, **buffer them** (§9.9) and show a **"5 new posts" pill** pinned at the top. The user opts in. Exception: if the user is _already scrolled to the very top_, injecting is fine (they're watching the live edge) — many feeds auto-prepend only when `scrollTop === 0`.

**Problem 2 — preserve scroll when you** **_do_** **prepend.** When the user taps the pill and you insert N items above the viewport, the content they were reading shifts down by the height of those items. Anchor it:

```typescript
function prependPreservingScroll(scrollEl: HTMLElement, insert: () => void) {
  const before = scrollEl.scrollHeight;
  insert();                                   // React commits the new items
  requestAnimationFrame(() => {               // after layout
    const after = scrollEl.scrollHeight;
    scrollEl.scrollTop += after - before;     // nudge down by exactly the added height
  });                                         // net effect: the user's post stays put
}
```

The idea: measure `scrollHeight` before and after inserting, then add the delta to `scrollTop`. The user's current post stays visually pinned while new content stacks above it, off-screen, ready when they scroll up. Modern browsers can do a version of this automatically with **CSS** **`overflow-anchor`** — but for a virtualized feed you almost always manage it manually (which is why §9.2 sets `overflow-anchor: none` on the container). The **conversational feed** (chat) is the inverse: it _sticks to the bottom_ and preserves position when _history_ prepends at the top — same delta trick, applied while loading older messages.

**Pull-to-refresh (mobile)** is the manual sibling: the user drags down at the top to fetch the newest items. On native it's one call (§9.12). On the web it's a touch-gesture handler (`touchstart`/`touchmove`/`touchend`) that shows a spinner past a drag threshold — but **don't hijack the browser's native pull-to-refresh** unless you fully replace it, and always give a non-gesture alternative (a refresh button) for accessibility.

## 12\. Conversion & UX Killers
None of these throw an error. They quietly bleed engagement, and they're all fixable.

*   **Layout shift on image load (CLS).** Media without a reserved aspect ratio makes everything below it jump as each image loads — the user taps the wrong thing, loses their place, and rages. _Always_ set `width`/`height` or `aspect-ratio` so the space is reserved before the pixels arrive. This is the #1 feed quality bug and it's measured directly by Core Web Vitals.
*   **Scroll-position yank.** New items injected above the viewport (§11). The single fastest way to make a feed feel hostile. Buffer + pill + anchor.
*   **No skeletons — just a spinner or blank.** A centered spinner tells the user nothing about what's coming and feels slower. Shaped skeletons preserve layout and lower _perceived_ latency by ~30% in study after study.
*   **Endless spinner at the end.** A feed that spins forever at the bottom (instead of "You're all caught up") makes users wait for content that will never come. Say when it's over.
*   **Offset pagination duplicates/skips.** Using `OFFSET`/`LIMIT` on a feed that receives new items means page 2 overlaps page 1 (dupes) or skips rows. Users see the same post twice and trust erodes. Cursor pagination (§9.7) is the fix.
*   **Index keys on items.** `key={index}` in a stream that prepends/removes → React reuses the wrong DOM, optimistic state lands on the wrong card, focus jumps. Always `key={item.id}`.
*   **Re-fetching from scratch on refresh.** Blowing away the whole feed and re-rendering on every poll = flicker, lost scroll, wasted data. Merge deltas; keep what's unchanged.
*   **Infinite scroll on a page with a footer.** The footer becomes unreachable for everyone, keyboard users especially. Use "Load more" when the footer matters.
*   **Autoplay everything.** Every video playing at once destroys battery, data, and focus (and is a WCAG 2.2 issue). Autoplay muted, one at a time, honoring `prefers-reduced-motion` and data-saver.
*   **SEO invisibility.** Pure client-side infinite scroll hides every item past page 1 from crawlers. Pair infinite scroll with real, crawlable paginated URLs (`/feed?page=2`) or server-render the first page (§9.10).

**The through-line:** feeds fail _quietly_ — a slightly lower dwell time, a slightly higher bounce, blamed on "the algorithm" when it's actually CLS and scroll yank. Audit the mechanics before you blame the content.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
The senior-move versions of everything above.

**Bidirectional (windowed) virtualization.** For feeds a user can scroll _both_ ways (jump-to-permalink then scroll up into history), you window in both directions and load older _and_ newer pages, keeping only a sliding window in the DOM. TanStack Virtual and `react-window` both support this; the trick is stable keys + scroll anchoring on _both_ ends.

**Feed as a normalized store, not an array.** At scale, don't keep the feed as one big array of full post objects. Keep an **ordered array of ids** plus a **normalized entity cache** (`{ [id]: post }`). A like updates _one entity_; every feed referencing that post updates for free, no array surgery. This is what Redux Toolkit's `createEntityAdapter` / Relay / Apollo normalization buy you — and it's why the same post liked in two places stays in sync.

**Fan-out on write vs. read.** The architectural fork for social feeds. _Fan-out on write_ (push): when Omegea posts, copy the post id into every follower's precomputed feed list — fast reads, expensive writes, the "celebrity problem" (a 10M-follower post writes 10M rows). _Fan-out on read_ (pull): assemble each user's feed at request time by querying who they follow — cheap writes, expensive reads. Real systems do a **hybrid**: push for normal users, pull for celebrities, merged at read time. The §9.7 endpoint is a pull model; a push model paginates a precomputed `feed_items` table instead.

**View-tracking / seen-state.** "Mark as read" needs to know what the user _saw_: IntersectionObserver fires a "viewed" event when an item is ≥50% visible for ≥1s, debounced and batched — never one request per item.

**Prefetch on intent.** Fetch the next page when the user is 600px from the bottom (§9.3 `rootMargin`), and prefetch below-the-fold media so it's decoded before it scrolls in. Density/avatar/media-radius all drive off tokens, so "comfortable/cozy/compact" (§4) is a token swap, not a rewrite.

**TS-enforced item variance.** Model the heterogeneous item as a **discriminated union** on `kind` so the renderer _must_ handle every case and can't render an ad as a post:

```typescript
type FeedEntry =
  | { kind: "post"; id: string; body: string; media?: Media }
  | { kind: "share"; id: string; sharedPost: FeedEntry; comment: string }
  | { kind: "suggestion"; id: string; users: User[] }
  | { kind: "ad"; id: string; creative: AdCreative };
// A switch on entry.kind is now exhaustively type-checked — forget a case, TS complains.
```

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A feed is the most performance-sensitive component you'll build, because it's unbounded — it grows forever as the user scrolls.

*   **Virtualization is the whole game.** Without it, a 10-minute scroll session builds 5,000+ DOM nodes, each with images and event listeners; the main thread chokes, memory balloons, mobile Safari kills the tab. Windowing keeps the DOM at a constant ~20–40 nodes no matter how far the user scrolls. This is non-negotiable past a few hundred items.
*   **Variable item heights are the hard part.** Fixed-height virtualization is easy (position = index × height). Feeds have _variable_ heights — a one-line post vs. a photo vs. a five-image gallery. You must **estimate then measure**: render with an estimated height, measure the real height after paint (`measureElement`/`ResizeObserver`), and correct the offsets. Get this wrong and the scrollbar jitters or "jumps" as items resize. This is why `react-window`'s `VariableSizeList`, TanStack Virtual's `measureElement`, and CDK's `autosize` exist — and why native `List`/`LazyColumn` (which measure automatically) feel smoother.
*   **`content-visibility: auto`** (§9.2) is the zero-dependency partial answer — the browser skips rendering off-screen items while reserving their space. Cheaper than JS virtualization, but keeps the nodes in the DOM (memory) — good to a few hundred items, not tens of thousands.
*   **Image discipline.** `loading="lazy"` + `decoding="async"` + responsive `srcset` + a reserved aspect ratio. Serve modern formats (AVIF/WebP). Media is 90% of a feed's byte weight — this is where the bandwidth budget lives.
*   **Memoize items** (§9.4). A prepend re-renders the container; without `memo` every visible card re-renders. Memo + stable keys = only the changed card re-renders.
*   **Delegated events / normalized store.** One listener on the container beats one per card; a normalized entity cache means a like touches one object, not an array walk.
*   **The virtualization library costs ~3–10KB** — trivial next to the DOM/memory it saves. Don't hand-roll it unless you enjoy scroll-jump bugs.

## 15\. Security (the feed reaches the server, so it's an attack surface)
Covered inline in §9.7–9.8; the concentrated version:

*   **Read-time authorization is the cardinal rule.** Every item must be filtered by _this viewer's_ permission to see it — follows, blocks, mutes, private accounts, per-post audience, soft-deletes. Never trust feed _generation_ to have filtered; re-authorize at the read boundary. A single missing predicate leaks private posts. This is the highest-severity feed bug.
*   **Cursor tampering.** The cursor is attacker-controllable. Keep it opaque, validate it decodes to a sane `(timestamp, id)`, and — critically — never let a crafted cursor bypass the authorization `WHERE` clause. The authz filter must apply regardless of cursor value.
*   **Idempotent actions.** Like/share/post endpoints need idempotency keys (§9.6) so a double-tap or retry can't double-post or double-like.
*   **Rate limiting.** Feed fetches, posting, and likes are abuse/scrape targets. Rate-limit per user and per IP; a public feed is a scraping magnet.
*   **Injection & XSS in user content.** Post bodies are user-generated. Never `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` raw content — sanitize server-side, render as text, and linkify mentions/hashtags/URLs through a safe parser. A stored-XSS in a feed item runs in _every viewer's_ session.
*   **Media validation.** User-uploaded images/videos: validate type and size server-side, strip EXIF (location leaks!), re-encode, and serve from a separate origin/CDN with its own CSP so a malicious upload can't script your app origin.
*   **The UI is not a security boundary.** Hiding a "delete" or "report" affordance is UX; the endpoint must re-check that this user may act on that post.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a feed is "done":
- [ ] `role="feed"` container; each item `role="article"`, focusable, with `aria-posinset` + `aria-setsize` (`-1` if unknown) + `aria-labelledby`.
- [ ] Keyboard model: Page Down/Up move between articles; focus never stolen on load; removed items hand focus to a neighbor.
- [ ] `aria-live` status announces "N more loaded" without moving focus.
- [ ] Virtualized with **variable-height** support (estimate → measure → correct); DOM node count stays constant while scrolling.
- [ ] Stable `key={item.id}` everywhere — never index keys.
- [ ] Media has reserved aspect ratio (no CLS); `loading="lazy"` + `decoding="async"` + responsive sources.
- [ ] Cursor (keyset) pagination — never offset; `(created_at, id)` composite sort key; `limit+1` for `hasMore`.
- [ ] Read-time authorization filters follows/blocks/mutes/private/soft-deletes; verified with a "no private leak" test.
- [ ] Real-time/polled items **buffered**, surfaced via "N new posts" pill; scroll position preserved on prepend.
- [ ] Optimistic like/action with rollback on error + reconcile on settle; idempotency key on the request.
- [ ] Skeletons on first load; "You're all caught up" at the end; real empty + error(retry) states.
- [ ] Infinite scroll only where the footer isn't needed; otherwise "Load more" (keyboard-reachable button, not just a sentinel).
- [ ] Pull-to-refresh (mobile) with a non-gesture alternative.
- [ ] `prefers-reduced-motion` kills shimmer/autoplay/scroll animation; autoplay muted + one-at-a-time.
- [ ] User content sanitized (no raw HTML injection); media EXIF-stripped and served cross-origin.
- [ ] Tests: `role=feed`/positions, Page Down focus, load-more, optimistic+rollback, pagination has no gaps/dupes, no private leak, axe clean.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same feed, eleven skins)
The feed's _behavior_ — `role=feed`, the keyboard model, focus management, 44px targets, virtualization — is the skeleton and **never changes**. These are skins on the item cards and chrome only.

*   **Flat:** solid card fills, crisp 1px separators or subtle gaps, no shadow. Reads instantly, scales to thousands of items cheaply. The default for dense/utility feeds.
*   **Material:** each item is an elevated Card with a resting shadow, ripple on tap, and Material's 8dp rhythm. Pull-to-refresh and the FAB ("new post") are native Material feed furniture.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent item cards with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over a vivid backdrop. Gorgeous for a hero feed, risky for a long one — guard text contrast hard and watch the blur's GPU cost when 30 cards stack.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language — item chrome and the sticky "new posts" pill become refractive translucent material with a specular rim and a sheen that shifts as the feed scrolls. On a deep Eminence/Night backdrop the refraction _sings_. Web = honest approximation with layered highlights; SwiftUI emits native `.glassEffect()`. Guard contrast and `prefers-reduced-motion` (the sheen is motion).
*   **Neumorphism:** cards as same-color extrusions with dual light/dark shadows. Pretty in isolation, contrast-poor across a long stream — reserve for a single hero card, not the whole feed.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** the "paper card" / physical-timeline look — bevels, inner highlights, texture. Nostalgic (the old Path/Paper apps); heavy for infinite scroll.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** hard offset shadows, thick borders, zero radius, clashing color per card. Huge personality for a zine/culture feed; exhausting at length — use for short, curated streams.
*   **Claymorphism:** big radius, soft inner light + drop shadow, puffy friendly cards. Warm and approachable — good for community/wellness feeds.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue gradient behind or bordering cards. Premium and moody; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (kill the animation) and keep it to accents, not every card, or the stream vibrates.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** typography and generous whitespace do all the work — hairline separators, no card chrome at all, the content _is_ the design. Ages best; ideal for a reading/editorial feed.
*   **UJG Brand:** Afro-Futurist house style — Goldenrod accents (like counts, the active pill, focus rings) on deep Eminence/Night item surfaces, confident 12px radius, a warm glow on hover, Platinum body text at ≥4.5:1. The "N new posts" pill is Eminence with a Goldenrod ring; the caught-up marker gets a small gold flourish. Full guidance on the 🎨 [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551).

**Rule that never changes:** style is skin, behavior is the skeleton. Every skin keeps `role=feed`, the article positions, the Page Down/Up keyboard model, the focus-never-stolen-on-load contract, the reserved media ratio, and the 44px action targets. Guard glass/liquid-glass/neumorphism for contrast; guard aurora/liquid-glass for `prefers-reduced-motion`.
