# Testimonial

# Testimonial
A curated customer quote, presented with real attribution, that turns a stranger's hesitation into someone else's proof. The most persuasion-heavy display component on a marketing site — and the one most often built as a semantically empty `<div>` soup.
* * *

## 1\. What it actually is
A testimonial is a **display component** that presents a specific person's endorsement of a product, service, or brand: a quote, plus real attribution (name, and usually role + company), plus optional avatar, star rating, company logo, or outcome metric. It is _curated_ social proof — you selected it, you're vouching for it, and it carries the credibility of a named human, not an anonymous crowd.

The word gets stretched over four different things people lump together:
*   **Testimonial (curated endorsement):** you chose it, it's attributed to a named person, it's marketing you stand behind. Semantics: `<figure>` + `<blockquote>` + `<figcaption>`.
*   **Review (user-generated rating):** the _public_ submits it, it's tied to a numeric rating and moderation queue, it's UGC governed by platform rules and its own aggregate schema (`AggregateRating`). A review widget is a different component with a submission form, sort/filter, and a report button.
*   **Blockquote (generic quotation):** the semantic element for _any_ extended quote — a cited paragraph in an article, a pulled sentence, an epigraph. A testimonial _uses_ `<blockquote>`, but a bare Blockquote component is content-neutral: no avatar, no rating, no persuasion job. Don't collapse the two; the Testimonial is a Blockquote wearing an attribution and a conversion goal.
*   **Case study / logo wall:** longer-form proof (a full narrative) or lower-fidelity proof (a grid of client logos, no quote). Testimonials sit between them.

**Rule of thumb:** if a _named person_ is _vouching_ for you and you _chose to publish it_, it's a Testimonial — build it as `<figure><blockquote>…</blockquote><figcaption>— Real Name, Role</figcaption></figure>`. If the _public submits and rates_ it, it's a Review. If it's _any quote with no endorsement job_, it's a plain Blockquote.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
Testimonials carry disproportionate weight because they're doing a _trust transfer_ — borrowing a real person's credibility to lower a stranger's risk. That makes the stakes unusually concrete:
*   **Conversion.** Social proof is one of the highest-leverage on-page levers there is. A testimonial placed beside a pricing table or CTA answers the silent objection ("does this actually work for someone like me?") at the exact moment of hesitation. The lift comes from _specificity_ — a named person with a photo and a measurable outcome ("cut our onboarding from 3 weeks to 4 days") converts far better than "Great product! — J.S." The component's whole job is to make that specificity legible and believable.
*   **Trust & credibility.** Real name + real photo + real company is the difference between proof and decoration. Anonymous or obviously stock-photo testimonials actively _lower_ trust — users have pattern-matched fake proof for a decade and it reads as desperation. The component either builds credibility or quietly burns it; there is no neutral.
*   **Legal / ethical (FTC).** In the US, testimonials are advertising. The FTC's Endorsement Guides require that any **material connection** between the endorser and the brand — payment, free product, employee/affiliate relationship, an incentive to post — be **clearly and conspicuously disclosed**. Fabricated testimonials, or presenting an atypical result as if it were typical without a disclaimer, are deceptive practices with real penalties (the FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews and testimonials adds civil penalties). The component must have a _place to put the disclosure_ and the process behind it must feed real, permissioned quotes.
*   **Accessibility.** The persuasion only lands if it's perceivable. A quote trapped in `<div>`s with a star rating conveyed as five orange glyphs and an avatar with `alt=""` gives a screen-reader user a decorative smear where the sighted user gets a compelling story. Correct semantics (`blockquote`/`cite`), a text alternative for the rating, and sensible avatar alt are what make the proof reach everyone.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy
A testimonial is display-only, but a well-built one still has many named parts (a carousel variant adds interactive ones):
*   **Figure container** (`<figure>`): the outer grouping element that binds the quote to its attribution as one unit. This is the correct semantic wrapper — _not_ a bare `<div class="card">`.
*   **Quote / body** (`<blockquote>`): the testimonial text itself, 1–3 sentences typically. Holds _only_ the endorser's words — never the attribution.
*   **Attribution / caption** (`<figcaption>`): the credit line, which lives **outside** the `<blockquote>`. Contains the name and role/company. This placement is a spec rule, not a style choice (see §10).
*   **Cite / source name** (`<cite>`): the person or organization being credited, wrapped in `<cite>` inside the figcaption. Semantically marks _who_ said it.
*   **Avatar** (`<img>`): 40–72px photo of the endorser. Its `alt` behavior is conditional (see §3 note + §10): decorative when the name is already in text, descriptive only when it carries unique info.
*   **Rating** (optional): a star rating with a **required text alternative** ("Rated 5 out of 5") — reusing the Rating component's number-first semantics, never stars-only.
*   **Company logo** (optional): the endorser's employer mark, adding institutional weight. Decorative or labeled depending on whether the company name is already in text.
*   **Outcome metric** (optional): a highlighted number ("+40% revenue," "3 weeks → 4 days") that makes the result concrete.
*   **Decorative quote glyph** (optional): the oversized "❝" ornament. Purely decorative — must be `aria-hidden` and never the _only_ signal that this is a quote.
*   **Material-connection disclosure** (conditional): a short "Paid partnership" / "Received a free trial" line when a material connection exists (FTC).
*   **Carousel controls** (carousel variant only): previous/next buttons, slide dots/tabs, and a **pause/play** toggle for auto-rotation.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes / Scale / Density
Testimonials scale by layout role, not by a single height dial. The table gives real values plus the design tokens they should read from:

| Tier | Container W | Quote font | Avatar | Padding | Token set | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact (grid cell) | 300–360px | 15px / 1.5 | 40px | 20px | `--tst-pad-sm` | 2–3 up card grid, sidebars |
| Default (card) | 360–480px | 16px / 1.6 | 48–56px | 24px | `--tst-pad-md` | Standard card, most pages |
| Featured (single) | 560–720px | 20–24px / 1.5 | 64–72px | 40px | `--tst-pad-lg` | Hero social proof, one at a time |
| Banner (full-bleed) | 100% (max 1100px inner) | 24–32px / 1.4 | 72px+ | 48–64px | `--tst-pad-xl` | Section-width feature strip |
| Pull-quote (inline) | max 600px, centered | 22–28px / 1.4 | none | 0 (rule/indent) | `--tst-pull` | Inline within long-form content |

Rules that matter:
*   **Line length over font size.** Quote body should sit at ~45–75 characters per line for readability; on featured tiers cap width rather than letting the quote run edge to edge.
*   **Avatar is a token, not a magic number.** Drive it from `--tst-avatar-size` so the whole system rescales; keep it a perfect square and `object-fit: cover` so non-square photos don't distort.
*   **Interactive controls stay ≥44px** even when the visual dot is 8px — expand the carousel dot/arrow hit area with padding (see §10).
*   **Responsive collapse:** a 3-up grid becomes a 1-up stack or a swipeable carousel under ~640px; don't shrink three cards to unreadable widths.

## 5\. States
A static testimonial has fewer states than a button, but the carousel and submission variants add real ones. Each described, not just named:
*   **Default (rest):** the quote, attribution, and any avatar/rating rendered statically. The baseline for a card or grid cell.
*   **Hover (card):** subtle elevation or border shift when the whole card is a link (e.g., to a full case study). If the card is _not_ a link, don't fake interactivity with hover — it implies a click that does nothing.
*   **Focus (interactive parts):** any focusable element (carousel arrows, dots, a "read full story" link, a video play button) shows a visible `:focus-visible` ring. Static text gets no focus.
*   **Active (carousel slide):** the currently-visible slide in a carousel; its corresponding dot/tab is marked selected (`aria-selected` / current) and visually distinct.
*   **Auto-rotating vs. paused (carousel):** the carousel is either advancing on a timer or halted. The **paused** state must be reachable and _persistent_ once the user pauses — don't silently resume. Auto-rotation must not start at all under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Loading (async / lazy):** avatar images and video thumbnails load lazily; reserve their box (fixed aspect ratio) so the card doesn't reflow when they arrive. A skeleton shimmer is acceptable for content fetched at runtime.
*   **Empty / no-data:** when a testimonials feed returns nothing, render nothing (or a graceful fallback) — never a card with placeholder lorem or "Testimonial goes here."
*   **Error (submission variant):** a user-submitted testimonial form shows inline validation errors (missing consent checkbox, over-length quote) tied to the field with `aria-describedby`.
*   **Pending moderation (submission variant):** a just-submitted testimonial is held, not shown live; the submitter sees a "thanks, we'll review it" confirmation, never their raw text rendered back into the page unsanitized.
*   **Video: idle / playing / captions-on:** the video variant has a poster/idle state with a play affordance, a playing state, and captions that default to available (see §6, §10).
* * *

## 6\. Types / Variants
More than four functional variants, each with a real reason to exist:
*   **Single card:** one quote, attribution, avatar. The atomic unit; drops into any grid.
*   **Card grid (2–3 up):** several single cards side by side. Best when you have multiple strong, comparably-weighted quotes and want breadth of proof. Each card is an independent `<figure>`.
*   **Carousel / slider:** rotating single testimonials with dots/arrows, optionally auto-advancing. Use when space is tight and you have many quotes; costs accessibility complexity (pause, keyboard, reduced-motion — see §10) and hides all-but-one quote from crawlers and skimmers, so never bury your _strongest_ proof in a non-first slide.
*   **Single featured:** one large, high-emphasis testimonial — big quote, big photo, often a logo and metric. Your best proof, given the spotlight near a CTA.
*   **With rating:** any variant plus a star rating, rendered number-first for accessibility. Pulls double duty as endorsement + score.
*   **With metric / outcome:** foregrounds a quantified result ("+40% MRR," "cut support tickets in half"). The most persuasive B2B format — pairs a number with a face.
*   **Video testimonial:** a poster thumbnail + play control that expands to a captioned video. Highest-trust format (hard to fake a talking human) but **captions are mandatory** and it carries bandwidth/privacy cost (see §14, §15).
*   **Logo wall + quote:** a strip of client logos with one rotating or featured quote — institutional proof plus a human voice.
*   **Pull-quote (inline):** a large decorative quote set _within_ long-form content (a blog post, case study). No card chrome; typographic emphasis does the work.
*   **User-submitted (interactive):** a form that collects testimonials from real customers, with consent capture, sanitization, and a moderation queue before anything renders publicly.

## 7\. When to Use (and When Not To)
Use a testimonial when a _specific, named human vouching for you_ answers a real objection:
*   **Landing pages**, immediately below or beside the primary CTA — proof at the decision point.
*   **Pricing pages**, next to the plan you want people to pick — reduces the "is it worth it" objection.
*   **Case-study sections** and service pages, where a quote anchors a longer narrative.
*   **Homepage social-proof band**, mixing a logo wall with one or two featured quotes.
*   **Checkout reassurance** — _one_ small, trust-focused quote (security/refund themed) can reduce abandonment; more than that is distraction.

Don't use one — reach for a different component — when:
*   **You don't have real, permissioned testimonials.** Never fabricate. Use a **logo wall**, aggregate stats, or third-party **Review** widget (real UGC) instead. Fake testimonials are an FTC violation and a trust bomb.
*   **You need public, ratable feedback at scale.** That's a **Review** component (submission + moderation + `AggregateRating`), not a curated Testimonial.
*   **The quote is generic** ("Great service, highly recommend!"). Vague praise _lowers_ credibility — cut it or swap for a specific one. A weak testimonial is worse than none.
*   **Mid-flow, high-focus screens** (onboarding steps, form fields, the actual payment step) where any proof beyond a single reassurance line is noise. Use inline microcopy, not a carousel.
*   **You just need to quote a source** (an article citation, an epigraph) with no endorsement job — that's a plain **Blockquote**.

Placement heuristics: strongest proof _above the fold_ or beside the CTA; never bury your best quote as carousel slide #4; match the testimonial's _persona_ to the visitor (a CFO quote on the enterprise page, a solo-founder quote on the starter plan).

## 8\. Across Design Systems
No major design system ships a "Testimonial" primitive — it's a _composed_ pattern built from Card, Avatar, and typography tokens. How each system's building blocks shape it:
*   **Material Design (Google):** Composed from a `Card` (elevated/filled/outlined) + `Avatar` + type scale (`headline`/`body`). Material 3's larger corner radii and tonal surfaces give testimonials a soft, friendly card; the quote uses `body-large`, attribution `label-large`. Carousel maps to Material's `Carousel` with its hero/multi-browse layouts.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** No card obsession — testimonials read as grouped content with generous whitespace, SF Pro type, and a subtle separator or grouped-list background. On iOS 26 the featured card leans into **Liquid Glass** material. Avatars are circular by platform convention.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** `Card` + `Persona` (Fluent's avatar-plus-name-plus-role primitive — practically a pre-built attribution block) + `Text` ramp. Acrylic/mica materials give depth; dense enough for enterprise proof grids.
*   **Ant Design:** `Card` + `Avatar` + `Rate` (Ant's star-rating component, which already exposes an accessible value) + `Comment`/`List` for stacked layouts. Enterprise-flavored, data-dense; the `Carousel` component handles rotation with built-in dots.
*   **Tailwind CSS / Tailwind UI:** No component — you compose utilities, but Tailwind UI _ships_ named "Testimonials" section patterns (single centered, side-by-side grid, with-logo, dark). Freedom plus a real risk of skipping semantics, so wrap the utilities around a proper `<figure>/<blockquote>/<figcaption>`.
*   **shadcn/ui + Radix:** No testimonial primitive, but `Card`, `Avatar`, and `AspectRatio` compose one, and Radix/Embla power the carousel (`shadcn` `Carousel` is Embla-based with keyboard + roving focus baked in). You own the code, so you own the semantics.
*   **Bootstrap 5:** `.card` + `.blockquote` (Bootstrap has a dedicated blockquote + `.blockquote-footer` for the attribution — the closest thing to a native testimonial) + `.carousel` component with built-in controls, indicators, `data-bs-ride`, and pause-on-hover.

### 8b. Visual Styles / Trends — What's trending in 2026
Testimonials in 2026 are moving toward _higher-fidelity human proof_:
*   **Video-first and clip testimonials:** short, captioned, autoplay-muted clips of real customers, replacing static quotes as the premium format (tools like Vocal Video/Senja normalized this). Captions and reduced-motion discipline are the cost of entry.
*   **Liquid Glass featured cards:** the featured single testimonial rendered as Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe translucent, light-refracting material over a saturated backdrop — the quote floats, the avatar sits on frosted depth.
*   **AI-authenticity backlash → verified proof:** as AI makes fake quotes and synthetic faces trivial, "verified customer" badges, LinkedIn-linked attribution, and platform-imported (G2/Trustpilot) testimonials rise as trust signals. Provenance beats polish.
*   **Metric-forward B2B cards:** the outcome number is the hero, the quote is support. Springy Material-Expressive motion on the number counting up.
*   **Wall-of-love grids:** dense masonry walls of many short real testimonials (Senja/Testimonial.to style) as a single high-density trust artifact.

For UJG: reserve Liquid Glass for the _one_ featured client quote over deep Eminence; use a clean Swiss/Minimal grid for the "wall of love"; and treat verified, permissioned, FTC-clean attribution as the non-negotiable regardless of skin.

## 9\. The Code
Every block is real and runnable for **this** component. The through-line: `figure > blockquote + figcaption`, attribution outside the quote in a `<cite>`, avatar alt conditional, rating with a number-first text alternative, and any carousel accessible + reduced-motion-safe.

### 9.1 HTML (semantic foundation)

```html
<!-- Single testimonial: the correct semantic skeleton -->
<figure class="tst">
  <!-- decorative glyph, hidden from AT -->
  <span class="tst__glyph" aria-hidden="true">&#10077;</span>

  <blockquote class="tst__quote" cite="https://acme.example/case-studies/ujg">
    <p>Urban Jungle Goddess rebuilt our intake flow and cut onboarding
       from three weeks to four days. It just works.</p>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- attribution lives OUTSIDE the blockquote -->
  <figcaption class="tst__by">
    <img class="tst__avatar" src="/img/lena.jpg" alt="" width="56" height="56" />
    <span class="tst__who">
      <cite class="tst__name">Lena Okafor</cite>
      <span class="tst__role">VP Operations, Acme Co.</span>
    </span>
  </figcaption>

  <!-- rating: NUMBER first, stars are decorative -->
  <p class="tst__rating">
    <span class="tst__stars" aria-hidden="true">★★★★★</span>
    <span class="tst__rating-text">Rated 5 out of 5</span>
  </p>

  <!-- FTC disclosure when a material connection exists -->
  <p class="tst__disclosure">Acme received a complimentary pilot.</p>
</figure>
```

Key HTML rules for a testimonial:
*   **Quote inside** **`<blockquote>`****, credit outside it.** Per HTML spec, the attribution is _not_ part of the quotation, so it belongs in `<figcaption>`, not inside `<blockquote>`. Wrap the source name in `<cite>`.
*   **`cite`** **attribute (a URL) ≠** **`<cite>`** **element (the source name).** Use both correctly: the attribute points to the source document, the element names the human/org.
*   **Avatar** **`alt=""`** when the name is already in adjacent text (the photo is decorative duplication). Only give it descriptive alt if it conveys something the text doesn't.
*   **Decorative quote glyph is** **`aria-hidden`** and never the sole cue that this is a quote — the `<blockquote>` role carries that.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (reusable component)

```tsx
import { forwardRef } from "react";

export interface TestimonialProps
  extends React.HTMLAttributes<HTMLElement> {
  quote: string;
  name: string;
  role?: string;
  /** URL of the source document (goes on blockquote cite attr) */
  sourceUrl?: string;
  avatarSrc?: string;
  /** rating 0–5; renders a number-first text alternative */
  rating?: number;
  /** FTC material-connection note, e.g. "Paid partnership" */
  disclosure?: string;
}

export const Testimonial = forwardRef<HTMLElement, TestimonialProps>(
  ({ quote, name, role, sourceUrl, avatarSrc, rating, disclosure, className = "", ...rest }, ref) => {
    return (
      <figure ref={ref} className={`tst ${className}`} {...rest}>
        <span className="tst__glyph" aria-hidden="true">&#10077;</span>
        <blockquote className="tst__quote" cite={sourceUrl}>
          <p>{quote}</p>
        </blockquote>
        <figcaption className="tst__by">
          {avatarSrc && (
            <img className="tst__avatar" src={avatarSrc} alt="" width={56} height={56} loading="lazy" />
          )}
          <span className="tst__who">
            <cite className="tst__name">{name}</cite>
            {role && <span className="tst__role">{role}</span>}
          </span>
        </figcaption>
        {typeof rating === "number" && (
          <p className="tst__rating">
            <span className="tst__stars" aria-hidden="true">
              {"★".repeat(Math.round(rating)) + "☆".repeat(5 - Math.round(rating))}
            </span>
            <span className="tst__rating-text">Rated {rating} out of 5</span>
          </p>
        )}
        {disclosure && <p className="tst__disclosure">{disclosure}</p>}
      </figure>
    );
  }
);
Testimonial.displayName = "Testimonial";
```

Why this is the senior version: attribution is structurally separate from the quote; `rating` renders a _number_ ("Rated 5 out of 5") that screen readers announce, with stars as `aria-hidden` decoration; `disclosure` gives FTC copy a first-class home; `forwardRef` + native `HTMLAttributes` spread let it drop into grids, links, and animation wrappers.

### 9.4 Vanilla JavaScript (accessible carousel, full keyboard + pause)

```javascript
class TestimonialCarousel {
  constructor(root) {
    this.root = root;
    this.slides = [...root.querySelectorAll('[role="tabpanel"]')];
    this.dots = [...root.querySelectorAll('[role="tab"]')];
    this.pauseBtn = root.querySelector(".tst-carousel__pause");
    this.index = 0;
    this.timer = null;
    this.reduced = window.matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)").matches;

    this.dots.forEach((d, i) => d.addEventListener("click", () => this.go(i)));
    root.addEventListener("keydown", (e) => this.onKey(e));
    this.pauseBtn.addEventListener("click", () => this.toggle());
    root.addEventListener("mouseenter", () => this.stop());
    root.addEventListener("focusin", () => this.stop());

    this.go(0, false);
    if (!this.reduced) this.play();      // never auto-rotate under reduced-motion
  }
  go(i, focus = true) {
    this.index = (i + this.slides.length) % this.slides.length;
    this.slides.forEach((s, n) => (s.hidden = n !== this.index));
    this.dots.forEach((d, n) => {
      const on = n === this.index;
      d.setAttribute("aria-selected", String(on));
      d.tabIndex = on ? 0 : -1;
    });
    if (focus) this.dots[this.index].focus();
  }
  onKey(e) {
    if (e.key === "ArrowRight") { e.preventDefault(); this.go(this.index + 1); }
    if (e.key === "ArrowLeft")  { e.preventDefault(); this.go(this.index - 1); }
  }
  play()  { this.stop(); this.playing = true;  this.pauseBtn.setAttribute("aria-pressed", "true");
            this.timer = setInterval(() => this.go(this.index + 1, false), 6000); }
  stop()  { clearInterval(this.timer); this.timer = null; }
  toggle(){ this.playing ? (this.stop(), this.pauseBtn.setAttribute("aria-pressed","false"), this.playing=false)
                         : this.play(); }
}
document.querySelectorAll(".tst-carousel").forEach((el) => new TestimonialCarousel(el));
```

```tsx
import { getTestimonials } from "@/lib/cms";

        ))}
      </TestimonialCarousel>
    </section>
  );
}
```

```python
from fastapi import FastAPI, Depends, HTTPException
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field, field_validator
import bleach  # allowlist-based sanitizer

app = FastAPI()

class TestimonialIn(BaseModel):
    quote: str = Field(min_length=10, max_length=500)
    name: str = Field(min_length=2, max_length=80)
    role: str | None = Field(default=None, max_length=120)
    rating: int | None = Field(default=None, ge=1, le=5)
    consent: bool                      # explicit publish permission (FTC + privacy)

    @field_validator("quote", "name", "role")
    @classmethod
    def strip_html(cls, v):
        if v is None:
            return v
        # strip ALL tags — testimonials are plain text, never markup
        return bleach.clean(v, tags=[], strip=True).strip()

@app.post("/api/testimonials", status_code=202)
def submit(body: TestimonialIn, user=Depends(require_auth)):
    if not body.consent:
        raise HTTPException(422, "Consent to publish is required")
    # HELD for moderation — never rendered live from raw submission
    db.testimonials.create(**body.model_dump(), status="pending",
                           submitter_id=user.id)
    return {"status": "pending_review"}   # 202 Accepted, not shown yet
```

## 10\. Accessibility
Standalone. The persuasion only counts if everyone perceives it.

**Roles & structure**
*   Wrap each testimonial in `<figure>`; put the quote in `<blockquote>` and the credit in `<figcaption>`. The attribution must be **outside** the `<blockquote>` — per the HTML spec the citation is not part of the quotation, and screen readers should not read the name as if the endorser said their own name.
*   Name the source with `<cite>` (reset its default italic in CSS if needed). Use the `cite` **attribute** (a URL) on `<blockquote>` for the source document — distinct from the `<cite>` element.
*   A carousel container gets `aria-roledescription="carousel"` and an `aria-label` ("Client testimonials"); each slide gets `aria-roledescription="slide"`. Use a tabs pattern (`role="tablist"`/`tab`/`tabpanel`) for the dots, or `role="group"`.

**Rating (reuse Rating semantics, number-first)**
*   Never convey a rating with stars alone. Render a real text alternative — "Rated 5 out of 5" — either visible or visually-hidden, with the star glyphs `aria-hidden="true"`. This mirrors the Rating component: the _number_ is the accessible value, stars are decoration.

**Avatar image alt / decorative rules**
*   If the person's name is already in adjacent text (it almost always is), the avatar is decorative duplication → `alt=""` (or `aria-hidden`/`ExcludeSemantics` on native). Giving it `alt="Lena Okafor"` makes a screen reader announce the name twice.
*   Only use descriptive alt when the image carries unique, non-redundant meaning (rare for testimonials). A company logo follows the same rule: `alt=""` if the company name is in text, else `alt="Acme Co."`.

**Keyboard map (carousel / interactive variants)**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| Tab | Move into the carousel (one stop), then to prev/next/pause controls |
| Arrow Right / Left | Move between slides (when dots use the tabs pattern) |
| Enter / Space | Activate the focused control (dot, prev/next, pause/play, "read full story") |
| Home / End | Jump to first / last slide (tabs pattern) |
| Esc | (Video variant) close an expanded/modal video |

**Focus management**
*   Auto-rotation must **stop on focus-in and hover**, and stay stopped after a manual pause. When a user activates a dot, move focus to the corresponding slide/panel or keep it on the dot — don't drop focus to the top of the page.
*   Never move focus _because_ the carousel auto-advanced — that would yank a keyboard user mid-read.

**Contrast & target size**
*   Quote and attribution text ≥ **4.5:1**; the role/company line often goes muted-gray — verify it still passes, don't let "secondary" mean "fails AA."
*   Star glyphs and any non-text UI ≥ **3:1** against their background.
*   Carousel arrows, dots, pause, and the video play button need a **≥44px** hit target even when the visible mark is tiny — pad the hit area.

**Reduced motion**
*   Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: **do not auto-rotate at all** (or replace sliding with an instant cross-fade/no transition), kill any counting-number animation, and never autoplay video. The user must opt into motion.

**Common failures specific to testimonials**
*   Attribution placed _inside_ `<blockquote>` (screen reader reads "…it just works, Lena Okafor" as one utterance).
*   Rating as five orange stars with no text → announced as nothing, or as "image image image."
*   Avatar with `alt="avatar"` or the filename → noise; or `alt="Lena Okafor"` → duplicate announcement.
*   Auto-rotating carousel with no pause, no keyboard, resuming after the user paused.
*   The whole card is a `<div onClick>` link with no role — not reachable or announced.
*   Decorative "❝" glyph read aloud as a character because it isn't `aria-hidden`.

## 11\. Innovative / Emerging Ideas
*   **Verified-proof provenance (2026):** as generative AI makes fake quotes and synthetic faces trivial, testimonials gain machine-checkable provenance — imported directly from G2/Trustpilot/LinkedIn via API, stamped "Verified customer," or signed with C2PA content credentials on the avatar/video so viewers can confirm the human is real. Provenance becomes a first-class UI element.
*   **View Transitions API for carousels:** native browser morphing between slides (`document.startViewTransition`) replaces heavy JS animation libraries, giving smooth cross-fades that automatically no-op under reduced-motion.
*   **Scroll-driven, JS-free reveals:** CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: view()`) fade testimonials in as they enter the viewport with zero JavaScript and compositor-only work.
*   **Interactive/expandable proof:** a compact quote that expands in place (`popover` + `command`/`commandfor` declarative HTML) into the full case study, or a wall-of-love with client-side filtering by industry/role so a visitor sees proof from _their_ segment.
*   **Captioned short-form video as default:** auto-transcribed, auto-captioned customer clips (Whisper-class transcription) become the standard "premium" testimonial, with the transcript also feeding SEO and accessibility for free.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers
Each fails quietly — no error, just lost trust or lower conversion:
*   **Anonymous or "J.S." attribution.** No real name, no company, no face reads as fabricated. Trust _drops_ below having no testimonial at all. Fix: real name + role + company + photo, or don't publish it.
*   **Stock-photo avatars.** Users reverse-image-recognize stock faces instantly, and one fake tell poisons every other quote on the page. Fix: real customer photos, or an initial/monogram avatar rather than a fake person.
*   **Generic, unspecific quotes.** "Great product, highly recommend!" proves nothing and signals you couldn't get a real one. Fix: specific, outcome-bearing quotes ("cut onboarding from 3 weeks to 4 days").
*   **Best proof buried in carousel slide #4.** Auto-rotating carousels hide all but one quote from skimmers and often from crawlers; your strongest testimonial never gets seen. Fix: feature the best quote statically; use the carousel for the long tail.
*   **Auto-rotating too fast to read.** A 3-second rotation means nobody finishes a quote; it also fails accessibility. Fix: ≥6s, pause on hover/focus, and a real pause button — or don't auto-rotate.
*   **No FTC disclosure on incentivized quotes.** A paid or free-product endorsement with no "material connection" disclosure is both a legal violation and a credibility risk if discovered. Fix: label paid/incentivized testimonials clearly and conspicuously.
*   **Atypical results shown as typical.** "I made $40k in a week!" with no context implies a normal outcome and is deceptive. Fix: use representative results, or disclose that results vary.
*   **Testimonial that doesn't match the visitor.** A solo-founder quote on the enterprise page (or vice-versa) fails to answer _this_ buyer's objection. Fix: segment proof to the page's audience.
*   **Layout shift on load.** Avatars and video posters loading without reserved space bounce the CTA around and hurt CLS. Fix: fixed aspect-ratio boxes and `width`/`height` on images.

*   **aria-live announcement on manual slide change:** when a user advances the carousel, announce the new slide's context to screen-reader users with a polite live region ("Testimonial 2 of 5, Lena Okafor") without stealing focus, so the change is perceivable but not disruptive.
*   **Progressive enhancement:** render the full stack of testimonials as plain, crawlable, no-JS `<figure>` elements; the carousel JS _collapses_ them into a slider only when it loads. If JS fails, the user still sees every quote — the opposite of a JS-only carousel that shows nothing.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
*   **Lazy-load avatars and video posters** with `loading="lazy"` and fixed `width`/`height` (or `aspect-ratio`) so images below the fold don't block render and don't cause layout shift (CLS) when they arrive.
*   **Never eager-load testimonial video.** Use a poster image + click-to-load; instantiate the player (and any YouTube/Vimeo iframe) only on interaction. A wall of autoplaying video destroys bandwidth, battery, and Core Web Vitals.
*   **Animate compositor-only properties.** Carousel slide transitions should use `transform: translateX()` and `opacity`, never `left`/`margin`/`width` — the latter force layout reflow every frame. Keep the featured-quote counting-metric animation on `transform` too.
*   **Tree-shake the carousel engine.** Import only what you use (`import Autoplay from "embla-carousel-autoplay"`, not a whole slider suite), and code-split the carousel so a page with a _static_ testimonial never ships slider JS. Embla is ~4–5KB gzipped vs. heavyweight alternatives at 40KB+.
*   **Virtualize / paginate a wall of love.** A masonry wall of 200 real testimonials should render a windowed subset (IntersectionObserver "load more" or virtual list), not 200 avatars and quotes in the DOM at once.
*   **Optimize avatar delivery.** Serve 2x-DPR-capped, `object-fit: cover`, WebP/AVIF avatars at the _displayed_ size (56px, not a 1000px original) — a grid of 12 full-res headshots is a hidden multi-MB payload.
* * *

## 15\. Security
Standalone. A _curated_ testimonial you author is near-zero surface — it's your own trusted content, the only rule being "don't inject raw HTML you didn't write." The real attack surface appears the moment testimonials become **user-submitted**, and it's significant:
*   **XSS (the primary sink).** Any testimonial text, name, role, or avatar URL that comes from a user is untrusted input. Rendered into the DOM without escaping, `"<img src=x onerror=alert(document.cookie)>"` in a "quote" becomes stored XSS that runs for every visitor. **Sanitize on input and escape on output.** Testimonials are plain text — strip _all_ HTML (allowlist of zero tags via DOMPurify/`bleach`), never `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` / Jinja `|safe` / `v-html` on submitted content. Validate avatar URLs against an allowlist scheme (`https:` only, no `javascript:`/`data:`).
*   **Server-side validation.** Client-side length/consent checks are UX, not security — the endpoint must independently enforce length caps, required consent, rating range (1–5), and sanitization. Never trust that the form limited anything.
*   **Moderation queue / authorization.** Submitted testimonials must be **held as** **`pending`** and never rendered live from raw input; a human (or classifier) approves before publication. This blocks XSS, spam, profanity, defamation, and impersonation. Gate the approve/publish action behind an authenticated admin role — publishing is a privileged operation, and the server must re-check the actor's permission, not trust a hidden field.
*   **Consent, provenance & FTC integrity.** Store explicit publish consent and the real source with each testimonial; fabricating or editing a quote to change its meaning is both a trust and a legal (FTC) problem. Capture material-connection status at submission so the disclosure requirement can be enforced downstream.
*   **Abuse controls.** Rate-limit and CAPTCHA/spam-filter the submission endpoint; testimonial forms are a favorite vector for link-spam and SEO injection. Strip/neutralize URLs in submitted text unless explicitly allowed.

If your page only renders your own hand-picked quotes from a trusted CMS, the honest summary is: low surface, one rule — escape on output and never mark author-trusted-only content as `|safe`. The instant a stranger can submit, treat it as a full XSS + moderation problem.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist
Ship-ready gate — every item non-negotiable:
- [ ] Semantics: `<figure>` + `<blockquote>` for the quote, `<figcaption>` for the credit, `<cite>` for the name — attribution is **outside** the blockquote.
- [ ] Attribution is real: named person + role/company (and photo where possible); nothing anonymous or stock-faked.
- [ ] Rating (if present) has a number-first text alternative ("Rated 5 out of 5"); star glyphs are `aria-hidden`.
- [ ] Avatar alt follows the decorative rule: `alt=""` when the name is in text, descriptive only if it adds unique info.
- [ ] Carousel (if present): one Tab stop, arrow-key navigation, visible focus ring, a real pause/play control, stops on hover/focus, and **does not auto-rotate under** **`prefers-reduced-motion`**.
- [ ] Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for text (including the muted role line), ≥ 3:1 for stars/controls; interactive targets ≥ 44px.
- [ ] Video variant: captions available by default, no autoplay-with-sound, click-to-load, keyboard-operable, Esc closes.
- [ ] FTC: material connections (paid/free-product/employee/affiliate) clearly disclosed; results are representative or disclaimed.
- [ ] User-submitted content is sanitized (strip all HTML), server-validated, and held in a moderation queue before publishing.
- [ ] Performance: avatars/posters lazy-loaded with reserved dimensions (no CLS); carousel engine tree-shaken and code-split; best proof rendered statically for crawlers.
- [ ] Empty state renders nothing/graceful fallback — never placeholder lorem or "Testimonial goes here."

## 17\. Visual Styles
The same semantic `figure > blockquote + figcaption` wears any skin. How each applies to **this** component:
*   **Flat:** solid card, crisp 1px hairline or none, the quote in plain weight with a single accent-color glyph; avatar a clean circle, no shadow. Reads instantly, scales to a dense grid.
*   **Material:** an elevated `Card` with a tonal surface and rounded corners; the avatar sits on a subtle shadow, the rating stars in the accent tone, a ripple if the card links to a full story.
*   **Glassmorphism:** the testimonial card as frosted translucent glass over a vivid photo/gradient, `backdrop-filter: blur()`; the avatar rim catches light — watch quote-text contrast against the blurred backdrop.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026):** the featured single quote on Apple's iOS 26 material — the card _refracts_ the Eminence backdrop behind it with a specular rim and a moving sheen; the avatar and stars float on real depth, pill-radius corners.
*   **Neumorphism:** the card as a soft same-color extrusion, the avatar in an inset "pressed" well and the quote glyph subtly embossed — pretty but contrast-poor, so keep it for accent, never the primary proof block.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** the testimonial as a physical "index card" or torn paper note — beveled edges, a paperclip holding a Polaroid-style avatar, faint paper texture behind the quote.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black border, hard offset drop-shadow, zero radius, a loud flat accent block behind the quote glyph; the attribution in mono, unapologetically raw. High personality for campaign pages.
*   **Claymorphism:** a puffy, big-radius card with inner top-light and bottom-shadow; the avatar a soft 3D blob, the stars rounded and candy-bright. Friendly, approachable proof.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** an animated multi-hue gradient border or backdrop on dark, the quote in light type, the metric number glowing — premium feel; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` on the animation.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** no card at all — a hairline rule or a bold hanging quotation mark, the quote in a strong serif/grotesk, attribution small and set on a strict grid. Typography does all the work; ideal for a wall of love.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod quote glyph and stars on deep Eminence/Night, Platinum quote text, Methanerse display for a featured pull-quote, warm glow on the featured card — the house default.

**The rule that never changes:** style is skin, behavior is the skeleton. Across all eleven skins the semantics (`figure`/`blockquote`/`figcaption`/`cite`), the attribution-outside-the-quote rule, the number-first rating alternative, the avatar alt logic, the carousel keyboard + pause + reduced-motion behavior, the focus ring, and the 44px targets are identical. The full 31 styles live on the Design Styles page; these 11 are the core.
