## 1\. What a Time Picker Actually Is
A time picker is a control for selecting a **time of day** — an hour, a minute, sometimes seconds — that a human reads on a clock. That's the whole job. But the word "time" hides four very different things people jam into the same widget:

*   **Time of day (wall-clock time):** "3:30 PM." This is what a time picker is _for_. It's a point on the 24-hour dial with no inherent date and — this is the trap — no inherent timezone.
*   **Duration / elapsed time:** "45 minutes," "1:30:00 of runtime." This is a _quantity_, not a clock reading. 90 minutes is valid; 25 o'clock is not. Do **not** use a time-of-day picker for a duration — the wrap-around at 24:00 and the AM/PM framing are wrong. Use a number input, a stepper, or a duration-specific control.
*   **Datetime:** a date _and_ a time together ("2026-08-14 3:30 PM"). This is a Date Picker and a Time Picker composed, or a single `datetime-local` control. The time half is ambiguous until the date half pins it to a calendar day.
*   **Timestamp / instant:** an absolute moment in the physical universe (an epoch value like `1723655400`). Unambiguous everywhere on Earth. A time picker never produces this directly — it produces a wall-clock reading that your code must _combine with a date and a timezone_ to resolve into an instant.

The single most common conceptual mistake: treating a picked time as if it were an instant. **A time with no date and no timezone is ambiguous** — "9:00" could be nine different moments across nine offices. The second most common: reaching for a time-of-day picker when the user actually means a duration. Rule of thumb: **if it reads on a clock, it's a time-of-day picker; if it's an amount of time, it's a duration input; and a lonely time is not a moment until a date and a zone say so.**
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
Time input feels like a rounding error in a form. It isn't — it's one of the highest-leverage, highest-liability fields you'll build.

*   **Bookings are money, and they hinge on the time field.** Appointment scheduling, restaurant reservations, calendar invites, delivery windows, interview slots — the entire transaction is a time. A picker that lets someone book a slot that's already taken, or shows 2:00 PM to a customer in a different zone than the business, is a lost booking or an angry no-show. Conversion and trust live in this field.
*   **It's a localization frontline.** Half the planet reads 24-hour time; the US, UK-casual, and a handful of others read 12-hour with AM/PM. Hardcode one and you've quietly told half your users the product wasn't built for them. Worse, you'll get _wrong data_ — someone types "6" meaning 18:00 and your 12h control records 6:00 AM.
*   **It's an accessibility cliff.** The pretty analog clock-dial (Material's signature) is one of the least accessible common patterns on the web. Keyboard and screen-reader users can be completely locked out of a booking flow by a dial with no text-entry fallback. Get the semantics right and you keep the door open; get them wrong and you've built a wall.
*   **It's a data-integrity contract.** The time the user sees, the time you store, and the time you show back must agree. Every timezone bug is a silent one — no error, just a meeting at the wrong hour and a support ticket that blames "the calendar."
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy of a Time Picker
The parts vary by pattern, but the vocabulary is shared:

*   **Trigger / field:** the visible input the user clicks or types into. Often shows the current value formatted for the locale ("3:30 PM" / "15:30").
*   **Segments:** the editable sub-fields — **hour**, **minute**, optionally **second**, and the **AM/PM (day-period) toggle**. In a good custom picker each is independently focusable and arrow-key editable (`role="spinbutton"`).
*   **Separator:** the colon(s) between segments. Decorative, `aria-hidden`, never focusable.
*   **Day-period control:** the AM/PM selector — only present in 12-hour mode. A toggle, a two-option segmented control, or a spinbutton that cycles AM↔PM.
*   **Popup surface (optional):** where a dropdown/listbox of times, a column-of-columns spinner, or a clock dial lives. Opened from the trigger.
*   **Slot list:** in the listbox variant, the scrollable list of selectable times at a fixed interval (every 15/30 min), with unavailable slots disabled.
*   **Clock face / dial:** in the Material variant, the analog circle with an hour ring, a minute ring, and a draggable hand.
*   **Now / clear affordances:** "Now" jumps to the current time; "Clear" empties the field.
*   **Helper + error text:** the min/max hint ("Choose a time between 9 AM and 5 PM"), the timezone label ("all times ET"), and validation messages.
*   **Hidden canonical value:** the machine-readable value your form actually submits — ideally `HH:mm` (24-hour, `ISO 8601`\-style) regardless of what the user _sees_. The display is locale; the value is canonical.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes, Density & Interval Scale
Two different "scales" matter for a time picker: the **physical size** of the control and the **granularity** of the time itself.

Physical density (match your form controls):

| Size | Field height | Font | Segment tap target | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| SM | 32px | 13px | pad to ≥44px | Dense admin tables, filters |
| MD | 40px | 14px | pad to ≥44px | Default form field |
| LG | 48px | 16px | ≥48px | Mobile-first, booking flows |

Interval / granularity (the `step`, in seconds for the native input):

| Granularity | `step` value | Meaning | Typical use |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Hour | `3600` | on the hour only | Coarse scheduling |
| 30 min | `1800` | :00 / :30 | Reservations, calendars |
| 15 min | `900` | :00 / :15 / :30 / :45 | The booking default |
| 5 min | `300` | fine slots | Logistics, dispatch |
| 1 min | `60` (default) | any minute | General input |
| 1 sec | `1` | seconds segment appears | Timers, precise logs |
| Sub-second | `0.001` | milliseconds | Scientific / media tooling |

Rules that matter:

*   **`step`** **is a data constraint, not just UI.** On `<input type="time">`, `step` both changes the spinner increment _and_ controls whether the **seconds segment even appears** — any `step` under 60 makes the browser render seconds. Set it deliberately.
*   **The interval defines your slots.** A 15-minute booking grid isn't a cosmetic choice; it's the unit your availability engine reasons about. Keep the front-end `step` and the back-end slot size in lockstep or you'll offer times you can't honor.
*   **Segments must hit ≥44px targets** even when the field looks compact — expand hit area with padding, don't shrink the tap zone to match a 13px font.
* * *

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A production time picker has far more than "empty" and "filled." Missing states are the amateur tell.

1. **Empty / placeholder** — no value yet. Show the _format_, not a fake time: `--:-- --` or `HH:MM`, so the expected shape is obvious.
2. **Focused (field)** — the whole control is focused; a visible ring wraps it.
3. **Segment-focused** — one segment (hour/minute/period) is active and arrow-editable. It gets its own highlight so the user knows what Up/Down will change.
4. **Filled / valid** — a complete, in-range value.
5. **Partial** — hour set, minute not yet. The control must not submit a half-value or silently coerce to `:00` without telling the user.
6. **Hover** — pointer over the field or over a slot in the list.
7. **Active / pressing** — a slot or arrow being pressed.
8. **Open** — the popup (list/dial/spinner) is visible; the trigger reflects `aria-expanded="true"`.
9. **Selected** — the currently chosen slot in the list/dial, visually and via `aria-selected` / `aria-activedescendant`.
10. **Disabled (whole control)** — not editable now. Reduced opacity, `aria-disabled`, no focus.
11. **Disabled slot** — an individual unavailable time (already booked, outside hours). Dimmed, `aria-disabled`, skipped by keyboard.
12. **Out-of-range / invalid** — value below `min`, above `max`, off the `step` grid, or unparseable. Red border, `aria-invalid`, an explicit message.
13. **Loading** — availability is being fetched (slots depend on a server round-trip). Show a skeleton list, keep the field width stable.
14. **Error** — server rejected the pick (the slot got taken between load and submit — the classic race).
15. **Success** — the time was accepted (often folded into the parent form's success).
16. **Read-only** — shows a value, focusable for copy, not editable.

The two states people skip and audits catch: **partial** (a half-entered time) and **disabled slot** (availability). Both are where booking bugs breed.
* * *

## 6\. Types & Variants
There are exactly two families — native and custom — and the custom family has three canonical patterns. Choosing correctly is 80% of doing this well.

**Native —** **`<input type="time">`****\*\*\*\*.** The browser's built-in. Free localization (renders 12h/24h per the user's OS/locale), free keyboard, free accessibility, free mobile UX (iOS spins a wheel, Android pops a clock). _This is often the right answer_ and juniors skip it to build something worse. Its cost: you cannot style the internal segments much, you can't disable individual slots, and the popup UI isn't yours.

The three **custom** patterns, when native isn't enough:

*   **Segmented spinbutton (HH : MM : AM/PM).** The gold standard for _typed_ time entry. Each segment is a `role="spinbutton"`; Up/Down increments, typing digits fills, Left/Right moves between segments, and the AM/PM segment cycles. This is what React Aria, the HTML spec's own model, and every serious design system converge on. Most accessible custom option; most code.
*   **Dropdown / listbox of slots.** A `role="listbox"` of times at a fixed interval ("9:00, 9:15, 9:30…"). Perfect when the valid times are a _finite, known set_ — booking a haircut, a table, a demo. Trivially supports disabling taken slots. Bad for "any time to the minute" (a 1,440-item list is absurd).
*   **Clock-face dial (Material).** The analog circle: tap/drag an hour ring, then a minute ring. Beautiful, touch-native, _and the least accessible common pattern in UI._ A raw dial is a drag interaction with no obvious keyboard model and poor screen-reader mapping. **Never ship a dial without a text-entry fallback** — Material's own spec pairs the dial with a keyboard/typed mode toggle for exactly this reason. Use the dial as the _phone_ affordance, the segments as the _keyboard/desktop_ affordance, over one shared value.

A fourth, common on mobile: the **wheel / column spinner** (iOS-style rotating drums, one column per segment). It's really the segmented model wearing a touch skin.

**12-hour vs. 24-hour (and AM/PM).** This is a _variant axis that cuts across every pattern_, and mishandling it is the #1 time-picker mistake:

*   **12-hour** shows 1–12 plus an AM/PM day-period. US default, common in en-GB casual.
*   **24-hour** shows 0–23, no day period. Most of the world, all of aviation/military/medical, and every sane data format.
*   **The rule: never hardcode one.** Derive the hour cycle from the user's locale (`Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().hourCycle`, or `hour12` from a formatter), let it be overridable, and — critically — **store 24-hour regardless of what you display.** The display is a localization concern; the stored value is `HH:mm`, always. Hardcoding "12h with AM/PM" is the tell of a picker built by someone who only tested in one country.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a time picker when the user must choose a **specific clock time** and precision matters. Match the _pattern_ to the shape of the valid set:

*   **Finite, known slots (bookings)** → **listbox of slots.** You control the set, you can disable what's taken, users pick in one tap. This is the reservation/appointment default.
*   **Any exact time, typed fast (power users, forms)** → **segmented spinbutton** (or native `<input type="time">` if you don't need custom styling/slot-disabling).
*   **Casual mobile, approximate time** → **native input** (free wheel/dial) or a **clock dial with a typed fallback.**
*   **You genuinely don't need custom anything** → **native** **`<input type="time">`****\*\*\*\*.** Start here. Earn your way to custom.

Don't use a time picker when:

*   **You mean a duration.** "How long is the session?" is a number/stepper, not a clock.
*   **The time is derivable.** "Now," "end of business," "in 30 minutes" — offer a shortcut instead of making them dial it in.
*   **You only need coarse buckets.** "Morning / Afternoon / Evening" is a radio group, not a minute-precise picker.
*   **A date is also required** — then reach for a datetime pattern (Date Picker + Time Picker, or `datetime-local`), and make the _timezone_ explicit.

Placement heuristics: pair the time field immediately after its date field (people read date-then-time); show the **timezone label right next to the field** in any cross-zone context; and put "Now"/"Clear" shortcuts within thumb reach on mobile.

## 8\. Time Pickers Across Design Systems
Same job, very different philosophies. Fluency here means you can drop into any codebase.

*   **Material Design (Google):** the archetypal **clock dial**, paired with a **keyboard/text-input toggle** (the little keyboard icon) so users can type instead of drag — Material _requires_ that fallback for accessibility. Time picker is distinct from date picker; supports 12h/24h by locale.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** the **wheel/drum** picker (`UIDatePicker` in `.time` mode) and a compact field that expands to it. Deeply locale-aware — respects the device's 24-Hour Time setting automatically. On macOS, a stepper-style segmented field.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** a **`TimePicker`** with dropdown columns (hour / minute / period), driven by a configurable increment (`minuteIncrement`) and `clock` (12/24). Compact, keyboard-first, enterprise-dense.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** `TimePicker` as a panel of scrollable columns, with `format`, `use12Hours`, `minuteStep`/`secondStep`, and `disabledHours`/`disabledMinutes`/`disabledSeconds` predicates for exactly the "disable taken slots" job. Composes with `DatePicker` and `RangePicker`.
*   **React Aria (Adobe):** the reference implementation of the **segmented spinbutton** done right — `useTimeField`/`useDateSegment`, per-segment `spinbutton` semantics, full arrow-key model, locale via `@internationalized/date`. If you build custom, copy this mental model.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** no prescribed component — you compose native `<input type="time">` with utilities, or wrap a headless library. Freedom plus the responsibility to get a11y right yourself.
*   **shadcn/Radix:** no first-party time picker; the community pattern is native `<input type="time">` styled with the shadcn `Input`, or a segmented build on Radix primitives. Honest gap — say so rather than pretending there's a `<TimePicker/>` to import.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML — native `<input type="time">` (start here)
The most underrated time control on the web. Localized, keyboard-accessible, mobile-friendly, free.

```plain
<!-- Minute precision (default). Renders 12h or 24h per the user's locale. -->
<label for="appt">Appointment time</label>
<input type="time" id="appt" name="appt" value="14:30" required>

<!-- 15-minute grid: step is in SECONDS. 900 = 15 min. -->
<input type="time" name="slot" step="900" min="09:00" max="17:00">

<!-- Seconds segment appears automatically when step < 60 -->
<input type="time" name="precise" step="1" value="14:30:15">

<!-- Millisecond precision for timers/media -->
<input type="time" name="ms" step="0.001" value="14:30:15.250">
```

The rules that trip people up:

*   **The** **`value`** **is ALWAYS 24-hour** **`HH:mm`** **(or** **`HH:mm:ss`****\*\*\*\*), regardless of what's displayed.** The browser shows the user 12h with AM/PM in `en-US` and 24h in `de-DE`, but your form always receives `14:30`. This is the single best reason to use native: canonical value, localized display, no work.
*   **`step`** **is seconds and it's load-bearing:** `900` = 15-min increments; any value `< 60` reveals the seconds segment; `0.001` reveals milliseconds. Default is `60`.
*   **`min`****/****`max`** **clamp the range** but note the wrap-around: if `min` is greater than `max` (e.g. `min="22:00" max="06:00"`), the browser treats it as a _reversed_ window that spans midnight.
*   **You cannot disable individual interior slots** (e.g. "10:15 is taken") with native — `min`/`max`/`step` only. When you need per-slot availability, that's your signal to go custom (§9.3 / §9.5).
*   **Styling is limited.** You can size the field and, in modern browsers, reach some pseudo-elements (`::-webkit-datetime-edit`, `::-webkit-calendar-picker-indicator`), but the popup itself isn't yours.

`tabular-nums` matters more than it looks: proportional digits make the segments jump width as they change (a `1` is narrower than an `8`), which is visually jarring during arrow-key spinning. Tabular figures lock the columns.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript — a segmented spinbutton TimePicker
The senior build: three `role="spinbutton"` segments over one canonical `HH:mm` value, full keyboard model, 12h/24h driven by locale. This is the longest block on purpose — it's the pattern worth owning.

```typescript
import {
  forwardRef, useId, useMemo, useState, useCallback, KeyboardEvent,
} from "react";

type HourCycle = "h12" | "h23";

export interface TimePickerProps {
  /** Canonical value, ALWAYS 24-hour "HH:mm". Locale only affects display. */
  value: string | null;
  onChange: (next: string | null) => void;
  /** Omit to derive from the runtime locale. */
  hourCycle?: HourCycle;
  minuteStep?: number;         // e.g. 5, 15
  min?: string;                // "09:00"
  max?: string;                // "17:00"
  disabled?: boolean;
  label: string;               // required — never an unlabeled time field
}

/** Resolve 12h vs 24h from the environment unless explicitly told. */
function resolveHourCycle(explicit?: HourCycle): HourCycle {
  if (explicit) return explicit;
  const hc = new Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().hourCycle;
  return hc === "h11" || hc === "h12" ? "h12" : "h23";
}

const clamp = (n: number, lo: number, hi: number) => Math.min(hi, Math.max(lo, n));
const pad = (n: number) => String(n).padStart(2, "0");

function parse(v: string | null) {
  if (!v) return { h: null as number | null, m: null as number | null };
  const [h, m] = v.split(":").map(Number);
  return { h, m };
}

export const TimePicker = forwardRef<HTMLDivElement, TimePickerProps>(
  function TimePicker(
    { value, onChange, hourCycle, minuteStep = 1, min, max, disabled, label },
    ref
  ) {
    const cycle = useMemo(() => resolveHourCycle(hourCycle), [hourCycle]);
    const is12 = cycle === "h12";
    const { h, m } = parse(value);
    const groupId = useId();

    // Derived display for the hour segment (12h shows 1-12).
    const displayHour =
      h == null ? null : is12 ? ((h % 12) || 12) : h;
    const period = h == null ? "AM" : h < 12 ? "AM" : "PM";

    const commit = useCallback(
      (nextH: number | null, nextM: number | null) => {
        if (nextH == null || nextM == null) return onChange(null);
        let v = `${pad(nextH)}:${pad(nextM)}`;
        if (min && v < min) v = min;       // string compare works on HH:mm
        if (max && v > max) v = max;
        onChange(v);
      },
      [onChange, min, max]
    );

    // 24h internal hour from a 12h display hour + period.
    const to24 = (disp: number, per: "AM" | "PM") =>
      per === "PM" ? (disp % 12) + 12 : disp % 12;

    function bumpHour(delta: number) {
      const base = h ?? 0;
      const next = (base + delta + 24) % 24;   // wrap 23 -> 0
      commit(next, m ?? 0);
    }
    function bumpMinute(delta: number) {
      const stepped = delta * minuteStep;
      const base = m ?? 0;
      let total = (h ?? 0) * 60 + base + stepped;
      total = (total + 1440) % 1440;           // wrap across the hour/day
      commit(Math.floor(total / 60), total % 60);
    }
    function togglePeriod() {
      if (h == null) return commit(12, m ?? 0);
      commit((h + 12) % 24, m ?? 0);
    }

    const onSegKey =
      (kind: "h" | "m" | "p") => (e: KeyboardEvent<HTMLSpanElement>) => {
        switch (e.key) {
          case "ArrowUp":
            e.preventDefault();
            kind === "h" ? bumpHour(1) : kind === "m" ? bumpMinute(1) : togglePeriod();
            break;
          case "ArrowDown":
            e.preventDefault();
            kind === "h" ? bumpHour(-1) : kind === "m" ? bumpMinute(-1) : togglePeriod();
            break;
          case "ArrowLeft":
          case "ArrowRight": {
            // Roving focus between segments (←/→), per the §10 keyboard map.
            e.preventDefault();
            const segs = Array.from(
              e.currentTarget.parentElement?.querySelectorAll<HTMLElement>(
                '[role="spinbutton"]'
              ) ?? []
            );
            const from = segs.indexOf(e.currentTarget);
            const to =
              e.key === "ArrowLeft"
                ? Math.max(0, from - 1)
                : Math.min(segs.length - 1, from + 1);
            segs[to]?.focus();
            break;
          }
          default:
            if (/^[0-9]$/.test(e.key)) {
              const d = Number(e.key);
              if (kind === "h") {
                const disp = is12 ? clamp(d === 0 ? 12 : d, 1, 12) : clamp(d, 0, 23);
                commit(is12 ? to24(disp, period) : disp, m ?? 0);
              } else if (kind === "m") {
                commit(h ?? 0, clamp(d, 0, 59));
              }
            }
        }
      };

    const seg = (
      kind: "h" | "m" | "p",
      now: number | string | null,
      lo: number, hi: number, text: string
    ) => (
      <span
        role="spinbutton"
        tabIndex={disabled ? -1 : 0}
        aria-label={kind === "h" ? "Hour" : kind === "m" ? "Minute" : "AM or PM"}
        aria-valuenow={typeof now === "number" ? now : undefined}
        aria-valuemin={kind === "p" ? undefined : lo}
        aria-valuemax={kind === "p" ? undefined : hi}
        aria-valuetext={text}
        className="seg"
        onKeyDown={onSegKey(kind)}
      >
        {text}
      </span>
    );

    return (
      <div
        ref={ref}
        role="group"
        aria-label={label}
        aria-disabled={disabled || undefined}
        className="time-field"
        id={groupId}
      >
        {seg("h", displayHour, is12 ? 1 : 0, is12 ? 12 : 23,
          displayHour == null ? "HH" : pad(displayHour))}
        <span aria-hidden="true">:</span>
        {seg("m", m, 0, 59, m == null ? "MM" : pad(m))}
        {is12 && (
          <>
            {" "}
            {seg("p", period, 0, 0, h == null ? "--" : period)}
          </>
        )}
      </div>
    );
  }
);
```

Why this is the senior version:

*   **One canonical value (****`HH:mm`****, 24h) is the source of truth.** 12h/AM-PM is a _view_ computed from it (`displayHour`, `period`). You never store "3 PM" — you store `15:00`. This is what keeps the data clean while the display stays localized.
*   **Each segment is a real** **`role="spinbutton"`** with `aria-valuenow/min/max/valuetext`, so a screen reader announces "Hour, 3" and Up/Down works exactly like the native stepper spec. (The `aria-valuetext` carries the human string like "PM" where a number is meaningless.)
*   **Arithmetic wraps correctly:** hours wrap 23→0, minutes wrap across the hour and the day, and `minuteStep` snaps to the grid. Wrap-around is the bug generator in every naive build.
*   **`min`****/****`max`** **clamp on commit** using plain string comparison — valid because zero-padded `HH:mm` sorts lexicographically the same as chronologically.
*   **Locale drives the cycle** via `Intl…resolvedOptions().hourCycle`, overridable by prop. No hardcoded 12h.

### 9.4 Parsing & formatting with locale (the part that actually breaks)
Never build strings by hand. Let `Intl` localize the _display_ and keep `HH:mm` as the wire format.

```typescript
// Canonical -> localized display. Same value, different render per locale.
function formatTime(hhmm: string, locale = navigator.language): string {
  const [h, m] = hhmm.split(":").map(Number);
  const d = new Date(2000, 0, 1, h, m);        // arbitrary date; we only show time
  return new Intl.DateTimeFormat(locale, {
    hour: "numeric",
    minute: "2-digit",
    // hour12 omitted on purpose -> Intl decides from the locale
  }).format(d);
}
formatTime("14:30", "en-US"); // "2:30 PM"
formatTime("14:30", "de-DE"); // "14:30"
formatTime("14:30", "ja-JP"); // "14:30"

// Localized text -> canonical. Be liberal in what you accept.
function parseTime(input: string): string | null {
  const s = input.trim().toLowerCase();
  const m = s.match(/^(\d{1,2})[:.\s]?(\d{2})?\s*(am|pm|a|p)?$/);
  if (!m) return null;
  let h = Number(m[1]);
  const min = m[2] ? Number(m[2]) : 0;
  const mer = m[3]?.[0];                        // 'a' | 'p' | undefined
  if (mer === "p" && h < 12) h += 12;
  if (mer === "a" && h === 12) h = 0;
  if (h > 23 || min > 59) return null;
  return `${String(h).padStart(2, "0")}:${String(min).padStart(2, "0")}`;
}
parseTime("2:30 pm"); // "14:30"
parseTime("1430");    // "14:30"
parseTime("9a");      // "09:00"
```

The lesson: **format with** **`Intl`****\*\*\*\*, store** **`HH:mm`****\*\*\*\*, and parse forgivingly.** Hand-rolled AM/PM string math (`h > 12 ? h - 12 + " PM"`) is where the 12:00-noon-vs-midnight bugs come from — `12 AM` is `00:00` and `12 PM` is `12:00`, the two cases every naive parser gets wrong.

### 9.5 The listbox-of-slots variant (bookings)
When valid times are a finite set and some are taken, a `role="listbox"` beats every other pattern. This is the reservation workhorse.

```typescript
import { useId, useState, KeyboardEvent } from "react";

interface Slot { value: string; label: string; disabled: boolean; }

function buildSlots(
  start: string, end: string, stepMin: number, taken: Set<string>
): Slot[] {
  const toMin = (t: string) => { const [h, m] = t.split(":").map(Number); return h * 60 + m; };
  const out: Slot[] = [];
  for (let t = toMin(start); t <= toMin(end); t += stepMin) {
    const v = `${String(Math.floor(t / 60)).padStart(2, "0")}:${String(t % 60).padStart(2, "0")}`;
    out.push({
      value: v,
      label: new Intl.DateTimeFormat(undefined, { hour: "numeric", minute: "2-digit" })
        .format(new Date(2000, 0, 1, Math.floor(t / 60), t % 60)),
      disabled: taken.has(v),
    });
  }
  return out;
}

export function SlotPicker({
  slots, value, onChange,
}: { slots: Slot[]; value: string | null; onChange: (v: string) => void }) {
  const listId = useId();
  const [active, setActive] = useState(() =>
    Math.max(0, slots.findIndex((s) => s.value === value))
  );

  function move(dir: 1 | -1) {
    let i = active;
    do { i = (i + dir + slots.length) % slots.length; }
    while (slots[i].disabled && i !== active);   // skip taken slots
    setActive(i);
  }

  function onKeyDown(e: KeyboardEvent<HTMLUListElement>) {
    switch (e.key) {
      case "ArrowDown": e.preventDefault(); move(1); break;
      case "ArrowUp":   e.preventDefault(); move(-1); break;
      case "Home":      e.preventDefault(); setActive(0); break;
      case "End":       e.preventDefault(); setActive(slots.length - 1); break;
      case "Enter":
      case " ":
        e.preventDefault();
        if (!slots[active].disabled) onChange(slots[active].value);
        break;
    }
  }

  return (
    <ul
      role="listbox"
      aria-label="Available times"
      aria-activedescendant={`${listId}-${active}`}
      tabIndex={0}
      className="time-popup"
      onKeyDown={onKeyDown}
    >
      {slots.map((s, i) => (
        <li
          key={s.value}
          id={`${listId}-${i}`}
          role="option"
          aria-selected={s.value === value}
          aria-disabled={s.disabled || undefined}
          className="slot"
          onClick={() => !s.disabled && onChange(s.value)}
        >
          {s.label}
        </li>
      ))}
    </ul>
  );
}
```

The a11y-critical bits: **`aria-activedescendant`** keeps DOM focus on the list while the _visual_ active option moves (the correct listbox pattern — you don't move real focus onto each `<li>`); keyboard navigation **skips disabled slots** so a keyboard user never lands on a time they can't pick; and each option carries `aria-selected` and `aria-disabled` so the screen reader announces both the choice and the unavailability.

### 9.6 The clock dial (Material) — and its accessibility cost
The dial is the prettiest and the most dangerous pattern. Ship it **only** with a text-entry fallback.

```plain
Structure of an accessible dial (Material's own model):
  [ 03 : 30 ]  <- editable text segments, ALWAYS present, keyboard-first
  [🕐 dial]     <- the analog face, a POINTER enhancement
  [⌨ toggle]   <- switch between dial mode and keyboard/typed mode

The dial itself:
  - Hour ring (1-12, plus inner 13-24 ring in 24h mode) and a minute ring.
  - A draggable hand; tap or drag to set.
  - Under the hood it is a pointer/drag interaction with NO natural keyboard verb.
```

Why the raw dial fails accessibility, and how the real ones fix it:

*   **No keyboard model exists for "drag a hand around a circle."** Assistive tech can't operate a bare dial. Material solves this by making the **text segments the primary control** and the dial a pointer-only _enhancement_ reachable via the toggle. If you build a dial, the segmented spinbutton from §9.3 _is_ your keyboard mode; the dial just writes to the same value.
*   **Screen-reader mapping is poor** — a circle of 12 numbers with a hand doesn't announce as anything meaningful; the typed segments announce cleanly. And **touch precision on the minute ring is rough** — snap to the `step` grid to help.
*   **Bottom line on the dial:** treat it as decoration over a real control. The value, the keyboard, and the semantics live in the segments; the dial is a mouse/finger nicety on top. A dial _alone_ is an accessibility defect, full stop.

```typescript
export async function book(formData: FormData) {
  const time = String(formData.get("time"));         // "14:30" — wall-clock, business tz
  const date = String(formData.get("date"));         // "2026-08-14"
  const tz = "America/New_York";                      // the BUSINESS timezone, explicit
```

### 9.10 Node.js — the endpoint behind the picker
The booking handler. The time is wall-clock; the server is where it becomes a real, guarded instant.

```typescript
import express from "express";
import { z } from "zod";
import { DateTime } from "luxon";

const router = express.Router();
const Book = z.object({
  date: z.string().regex(/^\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2}$/),
  time: z.string().regex(/^([01]\d|2[0-3]):[0-5]\d$/),   // HH:mm, 24h, validated
  tz: z.string(),                                        // IANA zone, e.g. "America/New_York"
});

router.post("/api/bookings", requireAuth, rateLimit, async (req, res) => {
  const parsed = Book.safeParse(req.body);
  if (!parsed.success) return res.status(422).json(parsed.error.issues);
  const { date, time, tz } = parsed.data;

  // Compose wall-clock + date + zone into an absolute instant (UTC) for storage/comparison.
  const start = DateTime.fromISO(`${date}T${time}`, { zone: tz });
  if (!start.isValid) return res.status(422).json({ error: "Invalid time in zone" });
  const utc = start.toUTC().toISO();

  // Enforce the business window and the step grid server-side (UI is a hint, not a gate).
  const minutes = start.hour * 60 + start.minute;
  if (minutes < 9 * 60 || minutes > 17 * 60) return res.status(422).json({ error: "Outside hours" });
  if (minutes % 15 !== 0) return res.status(422).json({ error: "Off the 15-min grid" });

  // Atomic insert guards the double-book race (unique index on slot).
  try {
    const booking = await db.bookings.create({ startUtc: utc, tz, userId: req.user.id });
    return res.status(201).json(booking);
  } catch (e) {
    if (isUniqueViolation(e)) return res.status(409).json({ error: "Slot already taken" });
    throw e;
  }
});

export default router;
```

Backend concerns every time picker silently depends on:

*   **Store the instant, not just the wall-clock.** Persist a UTC instant _and_ the originating IANA zone (`America/New_York`, never a raw `-04:00` offset — offsets change with DST). From those two you can reconstruct any display; from a naked "14:30" you can reconstruct nothing.
*   **Validate the format server-side:** `HH:mm` 24-hour regex, in-range, on the step grid. The client's picker is a convenience, not a security boundary.
*   **The double-book race is real:** two users load the same free slot and both submit. A unique index / atomic transaction turns the second one into a clean `409`, not a duplicate booking.
*   **DST is a landmine:** "2:30 AM" happens twice on fall-back day and _not at all_ on spring-forward day. A real datetime library (Luxon, `date-fns-tz`, `Temporal`) resolves these; string math does not.

### 9.11 Python (backend + a rendered field)
**Backend** (FastAPI) receiving the pick and resolving the zone:

```python
from fastapi import FastAPI, HTTPException, Depends
from pydantic import BaseModel, field_validator
from zoneinfo import ZoneInfo
from datetime import datetime
import re

app = FastAPI()
_HHMM = re.compile(r"^([01]\d|2[0-3]):[0-5]\d$")

class Booking(BaseModel):
    date: str          # "2026-08-14"
    time: str          # "14:30" — 24h wall-clock
    tz: str            # "America/New_York"

    @field_validator("time")
    @classmethod
    def valid_time(cls, v: str) -> str:
        if not _HHMM.match(v):
            raise ValueError("time must be HH:MM 24-hour")
        return v

@app.post("/api/bookings", status_code=201)
def create_booking(b: Booking, user=Depends(require_auth)):
    try:
        local = datetime.fromisoformat(f"{b.date}T{b.time}").replace(tzinfo=ZoneInfo(b.tz))
    except Exception:
        raise HTTPException(422, "Invalid time or zone")
    minutes = local.hour * 60 + local.minute
    if not (9 * 60 <= minutes <= 17 * 60) or minutes % 15:
        raise HTTPException(422, "Outside business hours or off-grid")
    utc = local.astimezone(ZoneInfo("UTC"))
    return db.create_booking(start_utc=utc, tz=b.tz, user_id=user.id)  # store instant + zone
```

Python note: even on a Python stack the picker still lives in HTML/JS (a native `<input type="time" step="900" min="09:00" max="17:00">` in your Django/Jinja template). Python's job is to _render_ it and to _defend the endpoint_ — validate `HH:mm`, resolve the IANA zone with `zoneinfo`, store UTC + zone, and enforce the grid — exactly like Node does.

### 9.15 Web Component (framework-agnostic)
A time picker that works in any framework or none, wrapping the native input with a canonical-value contract:

```typescript
class UITimePicker extends HTMLElement {
  static observedAttributes = ["value", "step", "min", "max", "disabled"];
  private input!: HTMLInputElement;

  connectedCallback() {
    this.attachShadow({ mode: "open" });
    this.shadowRoot!.innerHTML = `
      <style>input{font:inherit;padding:6px 10px;border-radius:8px}</style>
      <input type="time" part="field">`;
    this.input = this.shadowRoot!.querySelector("input")!;
    this.sync();
    this.input.addEventListener("change", () => {
      // Re-emit the canonical HH:mm value for host frameworks.
      this.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent("timechange", { detail: this.input.value }));
    });
  }
  attributeChangedCallback() { if (this.input) this.sync(); }
  private sync() {
    for (const a of ["value", "step", "min", "max"]) {
      const v = this.getAttribute(a);
      if (v != null) this.input.setAttribute(a, v);
    }
    this.input.disabled = this.hasAttribute("disabled");
  }
}
customElements.define("ui-time-picker", UITimePicker);
// Usage anywhere: <ui-time-picker value="14:30" step="900"></ui-time-picker>
```

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
The time picker is where accessibility is most often quietly broken. The rules, by pattern:

*   **Native** **`<input type="time">`** **is the accessibility floor** — it comes with a role, per-segment keyboard, locale, and screen-reader support for free. If you don't need slot-disabling or custom styling, using it _is_ the a11y strategy.
*   **Segmented custom picker:** each segment is `role="spinbutton"` with `aria-label` ("Hour"/"Minute"/"AM or PM"), `aria-valuenow/valuemin/valuemax`, and `aria-valuetext` for non-numeric segments (AM/PM). Wrap the group in `role="group"` with an `aria-label`. Left/Right move between segments; Up/Down change the focused one; digits type into it.
*   **Keyboard map (segmented):** `Tab` → focus the group / next segment; `←/→` → move between segments; `↑/↓` → increment/decrement the focused segment (with wrap); `0–9` → type the value; `Home/End` → min/max of the segment (optional but nice).
*   **Keyboard map (listbox):** `Tab` → focus the list; `↑/↓` → move active option (skipping disabled); `Home/End` → first/last; `Enter/Space` → select; `Esc` → close the popup. Use `aria-activedescendant`, not roving real focus onto each `<li>`.
*   **The clock dial MUST have a keyboard/text fallback.** A bare dial is unusable by keyboard and screen-reader users. Always pair it with the segmented input and a mode toggle (Material's pattern). This is the single most important a11y rule for time input.
*   **Label the field and the timezone.** Programmatic label via `<label>`/`aria-label`, and surface the timezone in text ("all times ET") so a screen-reader user isn't guessing which zone the times are in.
*   **Announce validity:** `aria-invalid="true"` on out-of-range values, with the error text tied via `aria-describedby` and echoed to an `aria-live="polite"` region.
*   **Contrast ≥ 4.5:1** for segment text; **don't signal a disabled slot by color alone** (add strikethrough / an icon). **Tap targets ≥ 44px** for every segment and slot, even when the digits are small.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
Time input looks solved but keeps moving:

*   **The** **`Temporal`** **API.** JavaScript's long-awaited replacement for `Date` ships `Temporal.PlainTime` (a wall-clock time with no date/zone — _exactly_ what a picker produces) and `Temporal.ZonedDateTime` (the fully-resolved instant). It makes the "time is ambiguous without a zone" distinction a first-class type instead of a footgun. Expect pickers to emit `PlainTime` natively.
*   **Natural-language time entry.** "in 20 min," "half past 2," "noon" parsed inline (Chrono-style), with the picker confirming the interpretation. Lowers friction dramatically for typed input.
*   **Availability-aware pickers.** The list only ever shows _bookable_ slots, computed server-side against a live calendar, streamed in as a skeleton resolves — the picker and the scheduling engine as one surface.
*   **Timezone-transparent scheduling.** Show the same meeting in _both_ parties' zones simultaneously ("3 PM your time / 8 PM theirs"), killing the cross-zone mistake at the UI layer instead of hoping the user does the math.
*   **`useOptimistic`** **slot booking.** Reserve the slot in the UI instantly, confirm against the server in the background, roll back cleanly on the `409` race.
*   **Liquid-glass wheels.** The iOS 26 drum picker rendered as refractive glass — pretty, and still just the segmented model underneath.

## 12\. Conversion & UX Killers (the silent ones)
None of these throw an error. They just quietly lose bookings and trust.

*   **Hardcoding 12h or 24h.** The signature mistake. Show a US user 24h or a German user AM/PM and the product feels foreign — or worse, they misread and book the wrong hour. Derive from locale, store 24h.
*   **No timezone shown in a cross-zone flow.** "Book 3:00 PM" with no zone label is a no-show generator. The customer assumes their zone; the business meant theirs. Always label the zone, and ideally show both.
*   **The 12 AM / 12 PM trap.** Midnight and noon are the two values naive AM/PM math gets backwards. `12 AM = 00:00`, `12 PM = 12:00`. One flipped booking at "12" and you've taught the user not to trust the field.
*   **A dial with no keyboard path.** Beautiful, and it locks out every keyboard and screen-reader user from your booking flow. Always pair with typed entry.
*   **Offering slots you can't honor.** Front-end `step` says 15 min, the back end books on the hour — now you're selling times that don't exist. Keep the grid in lockstep and re-validate server-side.
*   **Silent coercion of partial input.** User types "3" and tabs away; the field quietly records 3:00 without the minute they meant to add. Show the partial state; don't guess.
*   **Free-typing minutes into a coarse grid.** A 15-min booking that accepts "3:07" and rounds invisibly frustrates and misleads. Constrain to the grid _visibly_.
*   **Width jitter while spinning.** Proportional digits make segments jump as you arrow through them. `tabular-nums` — cheap fix, real polish.
*   **Using a time-of-day picker for a duration.** "Session length: 1:30" with an AM/PM toggle is nonsense. Different control entirely.

The through-line: every one fails _quietly_ — no crash, just a wrong hour, a missed appointment, or a locked-out user. Audit the time field before you blame the funnel.
* * *

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
The senior-move versions of the patterns above.

*   **Canonical value, localized view (the core discipline).** Store `HH:mm` 24-hour; compute every display with `Intl.DateTimeFormat`. This one rule dissolves most time bugs: data is stable, display is localized, and the two never drift.
*   **Store instant + IANA zone, never an offset.** `America/New_York` survives DST; `-04:00` doesn't (it's `-05:00` half the year). Reconstruct any display from `{UTC instant, IANA zone}`.
*   **TypeScript-enforced canonical format.** A branded type stops raw strings from masquerading as valid times:

```typescript
type Time24 = string & { readonly __brand: "HH:mm" };
const isTime24 = (s: string): s is Time24 => /^([01]\d|2[0-3]):[0-5]\d$/.test(s);
function toTime24(s: string): Time24 {
  if (!isTime24(s)) throw new Error(`Not HH:mm: ${s}`);
  return s;
}
// Now a function that takes Time24 can't be handed "3:30 PM" or "25:00".
```

*   **Design tokens for the picker:**

```css
:root {
  --time-field-h: 40px;
  --time-radius: 10px;
  --time-seg-focus-bg: var(--color-eminence, #5F2C82);
  --time-slot-selected-bg: var(--color-gold, #DCA424);
  --time-ring: var(--color-gold, #DCA424);
  --time-step-min: 15;                 /* keep FE + BE grid in one place */
}
```

*   **Snap-on-blur, free-type-while-focused.** Let power users type "0307" fast, then snap to the nearest valid grid step when the segment blurs — fast entry without invalid data.
*   **Round-trip to** **`Temporal`****\*\*\*\*.** Parse the picker's `HH:mm` into `Temporal.PlainTime`, combine with a `PlainDate` and a `TimeZone` to get a `ZonedDateTime`, store its `epochMilliseconds`. The types make the date/zone requirement impossible to forget.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A time field feels free; at booking scale it isn't.

*   **Don't ship a datetime mega-library for** **`HH:mm`****\*\*\*\*.** Moment.js is ~70KB gzipped and retired; a full `luxon`/`date-fns-tz` import can dwarf your picker. For pure wall-clock formatting, `Intl.DateTimeFormat` is _built in_ and free — reach for a tz library only where you actually resolve zones (usually the server). Import named functions (`import { formatInTimeZone } from "date-fns-tz"`), never `import * as`.
*   **Memoize the** **`Intl.DateTimeFormat`** **instance.** Constructing a formatter is comparatively expensive; build it once per (locale, options) and reuse it, especially when rendering a long slot list.
*   **Virtualize huge slot lists.** A day of 1-minute slots is 1,440 DOM nodes. If you truly need fine granularity, virtualize (render only what's visible); better, don't offer 1,440 slots — offer a segmented input instead.
*   **Animate only** **`transform`****/****`opacity`** on the dial hand and popup, never `width`/`top` — compositor-only, no reflow. Keep the field width fixed (`tabular-nums`) so spinning doesn't relayout.
*   **Debounce availability fetches.** If the slot list depends on a date change, debounce the server call so rapid date-stepping doesn't fire a request per keystroke.
* * *

## 15\. Security (the picker reaches the server)
A booking time is attacker-controllable input like any other.

*   **The UI grid is not a gate.** `min`/`max`/`step` and disabled slots are UX; the endpoint must independently re-validate the `HH:mm` format, the business window, and the step grid. `curl` can post `03:07` to a 15-minute API all day.
*   **Re-check availability and authorization server-side.** "The slot wasn't shown as available" must never mean "it can't be booked." Verify _this_ user may book _that_ resource, and that the slot is still free at commit time (atomic/unique-index — see §9.10).
*   **Guard the double-book race** with a transaction or unique constraint; return `409`, not a duplicate.
*   **Rate-limit booking/reset endpoints.** Slot-scraping and reservation-flooding are real abuse vectors; limit per user and per IP.
*   **Never trust client-supplied timezone blindly.** Validate it's a real IANA zone before feeding it to `zoneinfo`/Luxon; an unvalidated zone string is an injection/error vector.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same picker, eleven skins)
Style is skin; behavior is the skeleton. Every skin below keeps the identical semantics (`spinbutton`/`listbox`), keyboard model, focus ring, ≥44px targets, and 12h/24h locale logic — only the surface changes.

*   **Flat:** solid field, 1px border, crisp colon separators, flat popup. Fastest to read, scales anywhere.
*   **Material:** filled field with a bottom activation line, elevated popup, the ripple-on-tap dial. Bold locale-driven 12/24h.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent popup with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over a vivid backdrop; guard segment/slot contrast hard or the digits vanish.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026):** the popup and iOS-style drums refract content behind them with a specular rim and moving sheen; SwiftUI `.glassEffect()` native, web an honest approximation. Deep Eminence backdrop makes the refraction sing.
*   **Neumorphism:** soft dual-shadow field on a same-color surface; pretty but contrast-poor — pair with a real border, never rely on it alone for the focused segment.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** a literal beveled clock face with a metallic hand and inner highlights; the dial at its most tactile.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick border, hard offset shadow, zero radius, mono numerals — the segmented look with attitude.
*   **Claymorphism:** puffy field, large radius, soft inner glow; friendly for consumer booking flows.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue selected-slot highlight on dark; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (freeze the animation).
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** hairline underline, typographic segments, near-zero chrome; the numerals carry the design.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod segments and selected-slot fill on deep Eminence/Night, confident 10px radius, a warm Goldenrod focus glow. Lean Liquid-Glass accents over the dark backdrop for hero booking surfaces; keep Flat/Swiss for dense admin grids. Contrast and reduced-motion stay non-negotiable no matter how on-trend the skin.
* * *

**Bottom line:** a time picker is a two-digit control with a shocking blast radius. The hard part was never rendering "3:30" — it's that the value is _ambiguous by nature_. Master the four distinctions (time-of-day vs. duration vs. datetime vs. instant), start from native and earn your way to custom, drive 12h/24h from locale while storing 24-hour, make the dial keyboard-reachable, and treat every picked time as a wall-clock reading that isn't a real moment until a date and an IANA zone say so. Get that chain right and you've mastered the field that quietly runs every booking on the internet.
