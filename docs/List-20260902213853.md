## 1\. What a List Actually Is
A list presents a **collection of similar items stacked in a single direction** — usually vertical — where each item is a small composite (leading icon or avatar, a primary line, an optional secondary line, a trailing action or piece of meta). Settings rows, a contacts roster, a chat inbox, a notification feed, a nav rail: all lists.

That's the easy part. The word "list" gets lumped together with three other things that are genuinely different, and picking the wrong one is the #1 architecture mistake:

**List vs. Table.** A **table** is two-dimensional: rows _and_ columns, where columns are a shared schema and the relationships _across_ a row matter (name, email, role, last-seen — you scan down a column to compare). A **list** is one-dimensional: each item is a self-contained unit you read as a whole, not a row you compare cell-by-cell. If your users sort by column or compare values across items, you want a table. If they scan a stack of "things," you want a list. _Rule: columns you compare → table; units you scan → list._
**List vs. Data Grid.** A **data grid** is a table with an application on top of it: inline editing, column resize/reorder, virtualization, row selection, frozen columns, keyboard cell navigation like a spreadsheet. It has `role="grid"` and a two-dimensional keyboard model (arrows move by cell). A list never does that. If you're building Excel-in-the-browser, that's a data grid, not a list. (See the **Data Grid** reference for the full breakdown.)
**List vs. Menu.** A **menu** (`role="menu"`) is a _transient_ set of **commands** that pops open, does one thing, and closes — the right-click menu, the "⋯" overflow. Its keyboard model is menu-specific (arrows + typeahead, Escape closes, focus returns to the trigger). A **list** is _persistent content_ you live inside. A settings screen is a list; the gear-icon dropdown is a menu. Don't put `role="menu"` on your inbox.
**List vs. Listbox.** This is the subtle one, and it's an **accessibility fork** (see §10). A plain list of content or links is a `<ul>` of `<li>`. The moment the list becomes a **single- or multi-select control** — "pick one of these," "select these three" — it stops being a content list and becomes a **listbox** (`role="listbox"` with `role="option"` children), with its own roving-focus keyboard model. Same pixels, completely different semantics and keyboard contract.

**The through-line:** a list is the simplest of the collection components, which is exactly why people reach for `<div>` soup and skip the semantics. The markup is four tags; the correctness is all in _which_ four tags and _which_ ARIA the interactivity demands.

## 2\. Why It Matters
Lists are the connective tissue of an app. Most screens that aren't a single form or a single article are, structurally, a list. That gives them outsized weight:

**They're where scanning happens.** Users don't read lists, they _scan_ them — F-pattern down the left edge, locking onto the primary line and the leading icon. A list whose primary text isn't visually dominant, or whose items are inconsistent in height and rhythm, forces reading instead of scanning, and slow scanning is felt as a slow app.
**They're an accessibility multiplier.** A screen-reader user hears "list, 24 items" and instantly knows the shape of what's ahead; they can jump item to item, skip the whole list, or hear their position ("item 6 of 24"). Strip the list semantics — the single most common list bug, courtesy of `list-style: none` in Safari (§10) — and that user is now swimming through undifferentiated text with no map.
**They carry the app's density and rhythm.** List density (comfortable vs. compact) is one of the biggest levers on how "serious" or "roomy" a product feels. Gmail's compact rows vs. a wellness app's airy cards are the _same component_ tuned differently, and that tuning is brand.
**They're where performance dies quietly.** A 12-item list is free. A 50,000-row list rendered naively will jank the scroll, blow the memory budget, and freeze the main thread on mount. Virtualization (§9.5) is the difference between "instant" and "the tab crashed," and it's invisible until the data grows.

## 3\. Anatomy of a List
Two layers: the **list container** and the **list item**. Get both vocabularies straight.

**The container:**
**List root** — the `<ul>`/`<ol>` (or `role="list"` / `role="listbox"`). Owns the semantics and, for interactive lists, the keyboard model.
**Section headers** — group labels ("A", "B", "C" in contacts; "Today", "Yesterday" in a feed). Often **sticky** (§5).
**Dividers** — the hairlines _between_ items. Decorative — never a real `<hr>` between `<li>`s (that breaks list semantics); use a border on the item instead.
**Empty / loading / error slots** — the container's non-happy states (§5). A list is not "the items"; it's also what shows when there are zero items, when they're loading, and when the fetch failed.

**The list item** — the composite unit. A rich item has up to five regions:
**Leading** — an icon, avatar, checkbox, radio, drag handle, or thumbnail. Signals type/identity at a glance.
**Primary text** — the one line that matters. The name, the subject, the setting label. Visually dominant.
**Secondary text (supporting)** — subtitle, preview snippet, timestamp, status. Muted, one or two lines, often truncated with ellipsis.
**Trailing** — the action or meta on the right: a chevron (navigates), a toggle/switch (settings), a badge/count, a timestamp, an overflow "⋯" menu, or a value.
**Whole-item affordance** — is the _entire row_ clickable, or only the controls inside it? This decision drives the semantics (§10) more than anything else: a row that navigates is an `<a>` wrapping the content; a row with a toggle on the right is a `<div>`/`<li>` with a real `<button>`/`<switch>` inside; a row that's both (tap row to open, tap toggle to flip) is the hard case and needs a nested-interactive layout, not a `<button>` inside an `<a>`.

## 4\. Density & Scale
List sizing is mostly **row height + the vertical rhythm of the item's internals**. Three canonical densities, and the values that make them:

| Density | Row height | Item padding (V) | Item padding (H) | Primary font | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact | 32–40px | 4–6px | 12px | 13px | Data-dense: file lists, dev tools, admin, Gmail |
| Comfortable (default) | 48–56px | 8–12px | 16px | 14–15px | Most UI: settings, contacts, menus |
| Spacious / two-line | 64–72px | 12–16px | 16px | 15–16px | Feeds, inboxes with preview text, mobile |
| Three-line | 88px+ | 16px | 16px | 16px | Rich previews, email with snippet + meta |

Rules that matter:
**Row height should snap to a spacing scale** (4px or 8px grid), not magic numbers, so density is a token flip, not a rewrite (§13).
**Tap target ≥ 44px even in compact.** A 32px-tall compact row is fine _visually_, but the interactive target should still reach 44px on touch — expand it with padding, not by making everyone squint. This is the compact-list trap: it looks great on desktop and is unusable on a phone.
**Leading element sizes track density:** 20–24px icons for compact, 32–40px avatars for comfortable, 40–56px thumbnails for spacious. Keep the leading column a fixed width so primary text left-edges align into a clean vertical line — that alignment is what makes a list _scannable_.
**Secondary text truncates, it doesn't wrap forever.** One line with `text-overflow: ellipsis`, or a hard 2-line clamp. A list item that grows to five lines because someone's bio is long has broken the list's rhythm.

## 5\. States (the container has as many as the item)
Two state machines running at once: **each item** has interaction states, and **the list as a whole** has data states. Juniors style the item's hover and ship. Seniors handle the empty/loading/error trio and the selection model.

**Item-level states:**
**Default (rest)** — baseline.
**Hover** — pointer over an interactive row. Subtle bg tint. (Doesn't exist on touch — never the only affordance.)
**Focus** — keyboard-reachable. Visible ring via `:focus-visible`. In a listbox, focus is managed (roving tabindex, §10), not native per-item.
**Active (pressed)** — the tap moment. Slight darken/scale.
**Selected** — persistent chosen state (checked in multi-select, the active row in a master-detail nav). `aria-selected` (listbox) or `aria-current` (nav). Must be more than color — a check, a left accent bar, a bg shift.
**Disabled** — an option that can't be chosen. Reduced opacity, `aria-disabled`, skipped by keyboard nav (but often still focusable so SR users know it exists).
**Dragging / drop-target** — during reorder (§9.6): the lifted item, and the gap/indicator showing where it'll land.
**Swiped** (mobile) — partially revealed to expose actions (§8).

**List-level (container) states — the part that separates a real build:**
**Empty state.** Zero items. _Never_ show a blank rectangle. Show an illustration/icon, a one-line explanation, and ideally a primary action ("No contacts yet — Import contacts"). The empty state is a first-run experience, not an error.
**Loading state.** The initial fetch. Use a **skeleton** — greyed placeholder rows shaped like real items (leading circle + two text bars) — not a centered spinner. Skeletons preserve layout, communicate "content shaped like _this_ is coming," and feel faster. Mark the region `aria-busy="true"`.
**Error state.** Fetch failed. Show what happened and a **Retry** button, not a dead empty box. Don't blow away already-loaded items on a background-refresh failure — degrade, don't nuke.
**Loading-more state.** For infinite scroll / "Load more" (§9.7): a spinner or skeleton _appended_ at the bottom while the next page loads, with the count announced to SR users via `aria-live`.
**Partial / stale** — optimistic inserts and background refresh: an item shown before the server confirms (dim it slightly), rolled back on failure.

## 6\. Types & Variants
Lists come in a handful of functional shapes. The shape decides the semantics (§10), so name it before you build it.

**Content / read-only list** — a stack of information, nothing clickable (a spec sheet, a summary). Plain `<ul>`/`<li>`.
**Navigation list** — each item goes somewhere. A list of `<a>`s. The row is a link; the current one gets `aria-current="page"`. Sidebars, contact rosters that open a detail view, settings groups that drill in.
**Action list** — each item _does something_ on this screen (not navigate). A list of `<button>`s, or one delegated handler. "Recent commands," a share sheet.
**Selection list (listbox)** — pick one (single-select) or several (multi-select) from the set. `role="listbox"` + `role="option"`, roving focus, `aria-selected`. This is the a11y fork — it's a _control_, not content.
**Description list** — term/definition pairs (`<dl>`/`<dt>`/`<dd>`). Key-value metadata, glossaries, spec tables that aren't tabular. The forgotten semantic element; the right tool for "Label: value" stacks.
**Ordered list** — sequence matters (steps, rankings, a leaderboard). `<ol>` so the numbering is real and announced, not faked with a `::before`.
**Grouped / sectioned list** — items under sticky headers (contacts A–Z, a feed by day). Semantically nested lists or a flat list with header rows.
**Multi-line / rich list** — two or three lines per item plus leading media and trailing meta. Inboxes, feeds, notifications.
**Interactive-content list** — the hard hybrid: the row navigates _and_ contains its own controls (a toggle, a "⋯" menu, a checkbox). Needs careful nesting so the inner controls don't get swallowed by the outer link/button (§9 and §12).

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a list when you have **a collection of similar items scanned as units in one direction**. Reach for something else when:

**You need to compare across a shared schema** → **Table**. The moment users want to sort by "last active" or line up "price" across items, columns beat a stack. (See the Table reference.)
**You need inline editing, column ops, frozen headers, spreadsheet keys** → **Data Grid**. (See the Data Grid reference.)
**It's a transient set of commands off a trigger** → **Menu / dropdown**, not a persistent list.
**There are 2–5 mutually exclusive options and they all fit** → a **radio group** or **segmented control** reads better than a select-list.
**Each item is a large, visually rich card with its own media and multiple actions** → a **card grid** (see the Cards reference). Cards are lists' heavier cousin; use them when the item _is_ the content, not a pointer to it.

Placement & structure heuristics:
**Most important / most recent at the top.** Users scan from the top and stop early. Sort order is a UX decision, not a data-layer afterthought.
**Group when the set exceeds ~15–20 items** and has a natural key (alpha, date, category). Sticky headers keep the current group's label on screen while scrolling.
**Keep item height consistent within a list.** Ragged heights kill scan speed and break virtualization's math.
**Put the primary action on the row; hide secondary actions** behind hover-reveal (desktop), swipe (mobile), or a "⋯" overflow. Don't stack four buttons on every row.

## 8\. Lists Across Design Systems
Same stack of items, different philosophies. Fluency here means reading any codebase.

**Material Design (Google):** the `List` + `ListItem` with formal one/two/three-line specs, a leading "graphic" slot and trailing "meta" slot, 56px default rows, dividers as an explicit component, and ripple on press. Material 3 leans into larger touch targets and tonal selected states.
**Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** the defining list system. **Table views** and SwiftUI `List` with grouped vs. plain styles, inset-grouped rounded sections on iOS, the disclosure chevron convention, **leading/trailing swipe actions** as a first-class native API, and index scrubbers (the A–Z rail). Sticky section headers are native.
**Fluent (Microsoft):** `DetailsList` (really a grid) vs. the lighter `List`; virtualization built in because enterprise lists are huge; heavy on selection and command-bar integration.
**Ant Design / enterprise:** `List` with `header`/`footer`/`pagination` slots, a `Meta` sub-component (avatar + title + description), built-in loading and `loadMore` patterns, and a sibling `Table` for the tabular case. Pagination-first rather than infinite-scroll-first.
**Tailwind / utility-first:** no prescribed list; you compose `<ul>`/`<li>` with utilities and (crucially) remember to add `role="list"` back when you set `list-none` (§10). Headless UI provides the `Listbox` for the select case.
**shadcn/Radix:** no single "List" primitive — you compose. `Command` (cmdk) for command/action lists, Radix's roving-focus utilities for selection, and plain semantic markup for content. Behavior/a11y from Radix, looks from Tailwind, code you own.
**Bootstrap:** `list-group` — ready-made list rows, `list-group-item-action` for clickable rows, contextual variants, badges, and flush/horizontal modifiers. Convention over ownership.

## 8b. Visual Styles (the same list, eleven skins)
Design systems are _whose_ rules; visual styles are _which look_. The same semantic list wears any of these. Style is skin — the semantics, keyboard model, focus ring, and 44px target never change. Full guidance in the 🎨 Private ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-87871/838qa-200551](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-87871/838qa-200551)) doc.

**Flat:** hairline dividers, solid row bg, crisp. The default for dense content. Reads instantly.
**Material:** 56px rows, ripple on tap, tonal selected state, explicit divider component.
**Glassmorphism:** translucent rows with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over a vivid backdrop. Watch text contrast on the primary line; dividers nearly vanish, so lean on spacing.
**Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language. Rows on a refractive translucent material with a specular rim; the selected row's highlight _refracts_ the content behind it. Beautiful for nav sidebars over a rich backdrop; guard contrast and `prefers-reduced-motion`.
**Neumorphism:** rows as soft-extruded panels on a same-color surface. Contrast-poor — fine for a short settings list, wrong for a dense inbox.
**Skeuomorphism:** the "real" list — inset grooves between rows, subtle paper texture, beveled leading icons. The iOS-6 Contacts look.
**Neo-Brutalism:** thick black dividers, hard offset shadows on selected rows, zero radius, one loud accent. High personality, great for a bold brand's nav.
**Claymorphism:** puffy rounded rows with soft inner light, generous gaps, no hard dividers. Friendly, spacious — pairs with the comfortable/spacious densities.
**Aurora / Gradient:** animated gradient on the selected/active row over a dark field. Premium; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (kill the animation, keep the state).
**Minimal / Swiss:** no dividers at all — whitespace and type hierarchy do the separating. Sharp, editorial, confident. Demands disciplined vertical rhythm.
**UJG Brand:** Goldenrod primary text and selected-accent bar over deep Eminence/Night rows, warm glow on the active item, Platinum secondary text. The house default — the dark saturated field makes selection states and any Liquid-Glass accent sing.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin. Selection semantics (`aria-selected`), the roving-focus keyboard model, the visible focus ring, and the 44px target are identical across all eleven.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML (the foundation — get the four tags right)
The correct element _is_ the semantics. Ninety percent of list accessibility is choosing `<ul>`/`<ol>`/`<dl>` honestly and not faking rows with `<div>`s.

```html
<!-- Content list: a stack of similar items -->
<ul class="list">
  <li class="list__item">
    <img class="list__lead" src="/a.jpg" alt="" />       <!-- decorative avatar -->
    <span class="list__body">
      <span class="list__primary">Ada Lovelace</span>
      <span class="list__secondary">Online · 2m ago</span>
    </span>
    <span class="list__trail" aria-hidden="true">›</span>
  </li>
  <!-- ...more items -->
```

```html
<!-- Navigation list: each item goes somewhere. The ROW is a link. -->
<ul class="list">
  <li>
    <a href="/settings/account" class="list__item" aria-current="page">
      <span class="list__primary">Account</span>
      <span class="list__trail" aria-hidden="true">›</span>
    </a>
  </li>
```

```html
<!-- Action list: each item DOES something on this screen -->
<ul class="list">
  <li><button type="button" class="list__item" data-action="archive">Archive</button></li>
```

```html
<!-- Ordered list: sequence is real, numbering is announced -->
<ol class="list">
  <li class="list__item">Preheat oven to 200°C</li>
  <li class="list__item">Combine dry ingredients</li>
```

```html
<!-- Description list: term/definition pairs (the forgotten element) -->
<dl class="spec">
  <dt>Status</dt><dd>Active</dd>
  <dt>Plan</dt><dd>Pro</dd>
</dl>
```

Key HTML rules:
**Pick the honest element.** Similar items → `<ul>`. Sequence matters → `<ol>`. Key/value → `<dl>`. Never `<div>` soup — you throw away the "list, N items" announcement and the item-to-item jump.
**One** **`<li>`** **per item, and** **`<li>`** **is a** **_direct_** **child of** **`<ul>`****/****`<ol>`****. Nothing but** **`<li>`** **(and script/template) belongs directly inside a** **`<ul>`****. Wrap links/buttons** **_inside_** **the** **`<li>`****, not the other way around.Dividers are CSS borders, not** **`<hr>`****. An** **`<hr>`** **between** **`<li>`****s is invalid and breaks the list. Style** **`border-bottom`** **on the item.Decorative leading images get** **`alt=""`** so the SR announces the name once, not "image, Ada Lovelace, Ada Lovelace."

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (a selectable, keyboard-driven listbox)
The reusable version. This is a **single-select listbox** — the interactive control case — with roving focus, `aria-selected`, and the full arrow/Home/End/typeahead keyboard model. The content-list and nav-list cases are strictly simpler (just semantic markup); the listbox is the one worth writing carefully.

```tsx
export function Listbox<T>({ label, options, value, onChange }: ListboxProps<T>) {
  const listId = useId();
  const listRef = useRef<HTMLUListElement>(null);
  // Focus is "active descendant"-free here: we manage roving tabindex on the options.
  const [activeIdx, setActiveIdx] = useState(() => {
    const i = options.findIndex((o) => o.id === value);
    return i >= 0 ? i : options.findIndex((o) => !o.disabled);
```

});

```tsx
const focusIdx = useCallback((idx: number) => {
    const el = listRef.current?.querySelectorAll<HTMLLIElement>('[role="option"]')[idx];
    el?.focus();
    setActiveIdx(idx);
```

}, \[\]);

```tsx
const step = (from: number, dir: 1 | -1) => {
    let i = from;
    for (let n = 0; n < options.length; n++) {
      i = (i + dir + options.length) % options.length;
      if (!options[i].disabled) return i;
    }
    return from;
```

};

```tsx
const onKeyDown = (e: KeyboardEvent<HTMLUListElement>) => {
    switch (e.key) {
      case "ArrowDown": e.preventDefault(); focusIdx(step(activeIdx, 1)); break;
      case "ArrowUp":   e.preventDefault(); focusIdx(step(activeIdx, -1)); break;
      case "Home":      e.preventDefault(); focusIdx(step(-1, 1)); break;
      case "End":       e.preventDefault(); focusIdx(step(0, -1)); break;
      case " ":
      case "Enter": {
        e.preventDefault();
        const o = options[activeIdx];
        if (o && !o.disabled) onChange(o.id, o.value);
        break;
      }
      default:
        // Typeahead: jump to the next option whose primary starts with the typed char
        if (e.key.length === 1 && /\S/.test(e.key)) {
          const start = (activeIdx + 1) % options.length;
          const match = options.findIndex(
            (o, i) => i >= start && !o.disabled &&
              o.primary.toLowerCase().startsWith(e.key.toLowerCase())
          );
          if (match >= 0) focusIdx(match);
        }
    }
```

};

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:
**`role="listbox"`** **+** **`role="option"`** **+** **`aria-selected`** — the SR announces "listbox," "option, selected, 3 of 8." A `<div onClick>` announces nothing.
**Roving tabindex** — the whole list is _one_ Tab stop; arrows move within it. The naive build leaves every row `tabindex="0"`, forcing a keyboard user to Tab 200 times to escape a long list.
**Disabled options are skipped by arrows** but still exist for the SR (`aria-disabled`), instead of vanishing.
**Typeahead** — type "d" to jump to "Devan." The behavior real users expect from a `<select>`, rebuilt because we left the native element behind.
For **multi-select**, switch to `aria-multiselectable="true"` on the list, toggle `aria-selected` per option on Space, and add Shift+Arrow (range) and Ctrl/Cmd+click (add to selection) — the model in §9.8.

### 9.5 Virtualization (the 50,000-row list that doesn't crash)
The non-negotiable pattern for long lists. Rendering 50k `<li>`s puts 50k nodes in the DOM, blows layout/paint budgets, and freezes the tab. **Virtualization renders only the ~20 rows in view** (plus a small overscan buffer) and fakes the scrollbar height with a spacer, recycling nodes as you scroll.

```tsx
            role="listitem"

            aria-posinset={v.index + 1}        /* ...and this row's real position */
            className="list__item"
            style={{

              transform: `translateY(${v.start}px)`,   // GPU transform, no layout thrash
```

The senior details that make it correct, not just fast:
**`aria-setsize`** **+** **`aria-posinset`** — virtualization removes off-screen rows from the DOM, which _destroys_ the SR's sense of "24 items, item 6 of 24." These two attributes hand the real totals back so assistive tech isn't lied to. Skipping them is the invisible virtualization a11y bug.
**Position with** **`transform: translateY`****, not** **`top`** — transform is compositor-only (no reflow per frame); animating `top` thrashes layout (see the Button perf section).
**Variable-height rows** need measurement (`measureElement`), not a fixed estimate, or the scrollbar drifts.
**Ctrl/Cmd+F breaks** — browser find can't locate off-screen rows. For lists where in-page find matters, provide your own search box; don't rely on native find in a virtualized list.

The three server truths every list build eventually learns:
**Cursor pagination, not offset.** `LIMIT 20 OFFSET 40` silently skips or duplicates rows when items are inserted/deleted between page loads — the "I saw the same post twice scrolling my feed" bug. A cursor keyed on a stable sort value doesn't drift.
**Fractional/**\*\*\*\***`position`** **indexing for order.** Store a float (or a fractional-index string) so moving one row is a single `UPDATE`, not renumbering all N rows on every drag. Rebalance occasionally when floats get too close.
**Authorize per row, always.** The list only showed the user _their_ items — but the reorder/delete endpoint must re-check ownership. "It wasn't in their list" is UX, never security (same lesson as the Button doc).

### 9.17 Swipe actions (mobile)
Swipe-to-reveal is native on iOS/Android (§9.15) but hand-built on the web. The pattern: track a horizontal drag on the row, translate it to reveal actions underneath, snap to open/closed on release. The non-negotiable rule: **swipe is never the** **_only_** **way to reach an action** — it's undiscoverable and inaccessible. Every swipe action must also live in a visible or "⋯" overflow control.

```js
function attachSwipe(row, { onArchive, onDelete }) {
  let startX = 0, dx = 0, dragging = false;
  const content = row.querySelector(".row__content");
  row.addEventListener("pointerdown", (e) => { startX = e.clientX; dragging = true; content.style.transition = ""; });
  row.addEventListener("pointermove", (e) => {
    if (!dragging) return;
    dx = Math.min(0, e.clientX - startX);         // left-swipe only, clamp
    content.style.transform = `translateX(${Math.max(dx, -160)}px)`;
  });
  row.addEventListener("pointerup", () => {
    dragging = false;
    content.style.transition = "transform .2s";
    content.style.transform = dx < -80 ? "translateX(-160px)" : "translateX(0)";  // snap
  });
}
// The SAME actions must exist as real buttons in an overflow menu for keyboard/SR users.
```

Guard it: don't hijack vertical scroll (only start the swipe once horizontal movement dominates), respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by snapping without the slide, and always mirror the action in an accessible control.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
Lists are where semantics are cheapest to get right and most commonly thrown away. The fork in the road: **is this content, or a control?**

**The semantic baseline (content lists):**
**Use real** **`<ul>`****/****`<ol>`****/****`<li>`** **(or** **`<dl>`****/****`<dt>`****/****`<dd>`****). They give "list, N items," item-to-item jump, and "item 6 of 24" for free.** **`<div>`** **soup gives none of it.The Safari** **`list-style: none`** **trap.** Removing bullets in Safari/VoiceOver **strips the list role**. Whenever you set `list-style: none`, add `role="list"` back on the `<ul>` (and `role="listitem"` on the `<li>` only if you also had to change _their_ display in a way that drops the role). This is the #1 list a11y bug in the wild.
**Dividers are decorative CSS**, not `<hr>` between items (invalid, and it breaks the list).
**Group headers** use real headings (`<h2>`/`<h3>`) or `aria-label` on a nested list, so SR users can navigate by heading.

**The control fork (interactive lists) — pick the right pattern, they are NOT interchangeable:**
**A list of links (navigation)** → `<ul>` of `<li>` of `<a>`. Keyboard: Tab moves between links, Enter follows. Current item: `aria-current="page"`. _Do not_ add `role="listbox"` — these are links, not options.
**A list of actions** → `<ul>` of `<li>` of `<button>`. Tab + Enter/Space. Again, not a listbox.
**A single/multi-select control** → `role="listbox"` on the container, `role="option"` + `aria-selected` on items, `aria-multiselectable` if multi. Keyboard: **roving tabindex** (one Tab stop), Arrow to move, Home/End, typeahead, Space/Enter to select, Shift+Arrow / Ctrl+A for multi. This is a _control_ — the whole list is one Tab stop.
**The mistake:** slapping `role="listbox"` on a nav list, or leaving a real selection control as plain links. The role must match the interaction, because it changes the entire keyboard contract the user expects.

**Cross-cutting:**
**Virtualization:** off-screen rows are gone from the DOM, so hand back the totals with `aria-setsize` and `aria-posinset` (§9.5) or the SR count is a lie.
**Selection/loading/reorder must be announced** via `aria-selected`, `aria-busy`, and `aria-live` regions (§9.6, §9.7, §9.9). Visual-only feedback is invisible to SR users.
**Focus management on delete:** when a row is removed, move focus to the next row (or the list container), never let it fall to `<body>`.
**Touch target ≥ 44px** even in compact density; **contrast ≥ 4.5:1** for primary text, and selection can't be signaled by color alone (add a check or accent bar).

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The list looks solved and keeps evolving:
**`content-visibility: auto`** — a CSS-native "poor man's virtualization." The browser skips rendering off-screen items automatically; near-free scroll wins on medium lists without a JS virtualizer. Pair with `contain-intrinsic-size` so the scrollbar is honest.
**CSS Scroll-Driven Animations** — sticky headers that morph, items that fade/scale as they enter the viewport, driven by `animation-timeline: view()` with zero JS scroll listeners.
**View Transitions API** — animate a list item _into_ its detail view (shared-element transition) on navigation, and animate reorders/insertions/deletions natively (`::view-transition`), replacing FLIP-animation libraries.
**Optimistic list mutations** — React's `useOptimistic` shows the inserted/reordered item instantly, reconciling with the server in the background.
**AI-native lists** — semantic reordering ("sort by what's most urgent"), inline generated summaries per item, and "smart groups" that cluster items by meaning rather than a hard key.
**Interruptible infinite scroll** — hybrid patterns that auto-load a few pages then switch to a "Load more" button so the footer stays reachable, resolving the classic infinite-scroll trap.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers (silent list mistakes)
None throw an error. They just make the app feel slow, broken, or hostile.
**`<div>`** **soup instead of** **`<ul>`****/****`<li>`**\*\*\*\***.** No "N items," no navigation, no jump — and it usually comes _with_ the Safari role bug on top. The most common and most invisible list failure.
**No empty state.** A blank rectangle on first run reads as "broken," not "nothing here yet." You lose the user at the exact moment you should be onboarding them.
**Spinner instead of skeleton.** A centered spinner that collapses layout, then jerks it back when data lands, feels slower than a skeleton that holds the shape. Perceived speed is layout stability.
**Rendering 10,000 rows.** No virtualization → janky scroll, frozen mount, blown memory. Silent until the data grows, then catastrophic.
**Infinite scroll with an unreachable footer.** Users can never get to your contact/legal links. A genuine rage-bug.
**Ragged item heights.** Inconsistent rows kill scan speed and break virtualization math. Truncate secondary text; don't let one long item balloon.
**Nested interactive collisions.** A `<button>` inside an `<a>` (invalid HTML) or a toggle whose click also navigates the row. The interactive-content case needs deliberate nesting + `stopPropagation` (§9.10).
**Sub-44px compact rows on mobile.** Looks crisp on desktop, mis-taps on every phone.
**Color-only selection.** A row that's "selected" by a faint tint alone fails for low-vision and color-blind users and is easy to miss for everyone. Add an accent bar or check.

**The through-line:** list failures are quiet. No crash, just a slower-feeling, harder-to-scan, less-accessible screen that nobody files a bug for. Audit the list before you blame the content.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns & Design Tokens
**Density as a token flip.** Drive row height, padding, and font from tokens so `--list-density` switches the whole app from comfortable to compact without touching component CSS:

```css
:root {
  --list-row-min: 48px; --list-pad-y: 10px; --list-pad-x: 16px;
  --list-primary-size: 15px; --list-secondary-size: 13px;
  --list-divider: #e5e2ea; --list-selected: #efe6f7; --focus: #DCA424;
}
[data-density="compact"] {
  --list-row-min: 36px; --list-pad-y: 4px; --list-primary-size: 13px;
}
.list__item { min-height: max(44px, var(--list-row-min)); padding: var(--list-pad-y) var(--list-pad-x); }
```

The `max(44px, …)` keeps the tap-target floor even when a designer sets a shorter visual height — the a11y rule enforced in CSS, not left to discipline.
**TypeScript-enforced anatomy.** A discriminated union that _requires_ an `aria-label` when a row has no visible primary text (icon-only rows), catching a whole a11y class at compile time — the same trick the Button doc uses for icon buttons.
**`aria-live`** **selection announcer.** A single polite region that announces "3 selected," "moved to position 2," "loaded 20 more" — wire every count-changing action through it so SR users track state without hunting.
**Fractional indexing for order** (§9.11) — store `position` as a fractional key so a drag is one `UPDATE`, not a renumber of the whole list.
**Sticky-header stacking** — when sections are nested and you want _only_ the current header pinned, `position: sticky; top: 0` on each header naturally hands off as you scroll; no JS.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A list feels free until the data grows. The "why is my list slow" chapter:
**Virtualize past ~100–200 rows.** Below that, plain rendering is fine and simpler. Above it, DOM node count is the enemy — each `<li>` is layout + paint + memory. `@tanstack/react-virtual`, `react-window`, or the CSS-native `content-visibility: auto`.
**Stable keys, not array index.** `key={item.id}`, never `key={i}` — index keys make React tear down and rebuild rows on reorder/insert/delete, killing performance and losing focus/scroll state.
**Memoize rows in long, frequently-updating lists.** `React.memo` the row component and `useCallback` its handlers so a parent re-render doesn't re-render 500 rows. Don't over-apply it — it has its own cost; reach for it when the list is large and updates often.
**Animate** **`transform`****/****`opacity`** **only.** Reorder and enter/exit animations must not animate `top`/`height`/`margin` (reflow every frame). The virtualizer already uses `translateY` for the same reason.
**One delegated listener, not one per row** (§9.10) — 10,000 rows should be one closure on the container, not 10,000.
**Tree-shake icons.** `import { Chevron } from "lucide-react"`, never `import * as Icons` — the same namespace-import trap that bloats button-heavy screens applies per row, multiplied.
**Debounce list-search filtering.** Filtering a big list on every keystroke thrashes; debounce the input (~200ms) and, for huge sets, filter on the server.

## 15\. Security
A list feels like a read-only display component, but the moment it's backed by a server, reorderable, or renders user content, it's an attack surface.

**List item content is an XSS vector.** Primary text, secondary text, and badges often come from user input or a CMS. Render them as text (framework interpolation, `textContent`), never as raw HTML. The list that renders a contact's name via `innerHTML` is one `<img onerror=…>` away from a stored-XSS breach.

**Cursor/pagination tokens are untrusted input.** A client can tamper with a base64 cursor. Validate the decoded values (types, ranges), and always re-scope the query by authorization (`WHERE user_id = …`). A manipulated cursor that pages into another tenant's data is a BOLA/IDOR vulnerability.

**Authorize every list operation server-side.** The UI shows only "their" items, but the reorder/delete/archive endpoint must re-check ownership. "It wasn't visible in their list" is UX, never security. Every PATCH, DELETE, and reorder call verifies the row belongs to the requesting user.

**Rate-limit paginated and infinite-scroll endpoints.** A script hammering `?cursor=…` in a loop is a scraping attack and a potential DoS on your database. Rate-limit per user/IP; cursor pagination makes sequential scraping marginally harder than predictable offsets, but it's not a defense on its own.

**Cap the page size.** `?limit=1000000` is a memory-exhaustion and DoS vector. Validate and clamp to a hard max (e.g. 100) server-side — the client's UI is a hint, not a contract.

**Reorder endpoints and fractional indexing.** If you persist order via a `position` float, validate that the value is within sane bounds and that the row being moved belongs to the user. An attacker who can reorder _any_ row in _any_ list has a data-integrity bug.

**Swipe-action endpoints.** Swipe-to-archive / swipe-to-delete triggers a real mutation. Those endpoints need the same auth, idempotency, and rate-limiting as any destructive action — the gesture is a UX affordance, not a security boundary.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a list is "done":
Real `<ul>`/`<ol>`/`<dl>` + `<li>` semantics — no `<div>` soup.
`role="list"` re-added wherever `list-style: none` is set (Safari fix).
Correct control pattern: links = `<a>`, actions = `<button>`, single/multi-select = `role="listbox"`/`option` + roving tabindex.
Empty, loading (skeleton), and error states all designed and rendered — not a blank box or bare spinner.
Selection signaled by more than color (check/accent bar) and exposed via `aria-selected`/`aria-current`.
Long lists virtualized, with `aria-setsize`/`aria-posinset` restored.
Reorder is pointer **and** keyboard operable, with `aria-live` announcing the move.
Infinite scroll keeps a reachable footer + a "Load more" fallback; new content announced via `aria-live`.
Swipe actions mirrored in a visible/overflow control; vertical scroll not hijacked.
Tap target ≥ 44px even in compact; primary text contrast ≥ 4.5:1.
Keyboard: arrows navigate, Enter/Space activate/select, Home/End, typeahead where it's a listbox.
Focus moves to a sane neighbor after a row is deleted.
Stable `key={id}`; rows memoized in long updating lists; one delegated handler.
Server: cursor pagination (not offset), per-row authorization, fractional order persistence.
