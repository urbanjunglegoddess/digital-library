# Lightbox

# Lightbox
A full-viewport overlay that shows an image or media item at maximum size against a dimmed backdrop, usually with gallery next/prev. It shares the Modal contract — a focus-trapped `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"` surface with Escape-to-close and focus return — but it is purpose-built for media: zoom, pan, swipe, preloading, and a caption. Here is the complete senior breakdown, from first principles to production code.
* * *

## 1\. What It Actually Is
A lightbox is a **modal media viewer**: activating a thumbnail opens a dialog that renders the same image at full resolution, centered on a near-opaque backdrop, and (usually) lets the user page through a gallery of related images without leaving the page. It is a specialization of the Modal — same overlay, same focus trap, same dismissal contract — where the payload is media and the interactions are media-specific (zoom, pan, swipe, next/prev, preloading).

The things people confuse it with, named and distinguished:
*   **Modal / Dialog (generic):** a lightbox _is_ a modal, but a modal holds arbitrary content — a form, a confirmation, a settings panel. A lightbox holds one media item and adds gallery paging, zoom, and captioning. Build it _on top of_ your dialog primitive; don't reinvent the overlay.
*   **Carousel / Slider (inline):** a carousel cycles images _in place_ in the page flow — no overlay, no focus trap, page still scrollable. A lightbox is _modal_: it takes over the viewport and traps focus. A thumbnail grid that opens a lightbox on click is the common pairing.
*   **Zoom / magnifier (inline):** hover-magnifier and pinch-zoom-in-place enlarge a region without an overlay. A lightbox's zoom happens _inside_ the modal, after it opens.
*   **Full-screen / reader view:** the Fullscreen API takes the whole screen but isn't a dialog layered over the current page; the lightbox keeps the page underneath, dimmed, and returns you to it.

**Rule of thumb:** if the media takes over the viewport in a focus-trapped overlay you dismiss to return to exactly where you were — it's a lightbox. If it cycles inline in the page with the page still scrollable — it's a carousel. If the overlay holds a form or arbitrary UI rather than media — it's a plain modal.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The lightbox is where users go to _decide_. On e-commerce it's the last look before "Add to cart"; on a portfolio it's the work itself. That makes it carry disproportionate weight:
*   **Conversion lives in the detail view.** Shoppers who open the product-image lightbox and zoom convert at materially higher rates — they're inspecting stitching, texture, scale. A lightbox that loads slowly, can't zoom, or loses their place in the gallery kills that inspection and the sale with it.
*   **It's a focus-trap accessibility surface, and an easy one to get wrong.** Because it's modal, every Modal a11y obligation applies — trap focus, `aria-modal`, Escape, return focus to the trigger thumbnail, announce "image X of N" — _plus_ image-specific ones: real `alt`, a caption association, a keyboard alternative to pinch-zoom and swipe. Ship it half-built and keyboard and screen-reader users are stranded in an overlay they can't navigate or escape.
*   **Performance is visible here.** The lightbox is the one place you legitimately load full-resolution media, so it's the easiest place to tank Largest Contentful Paint and burn a mobile data plan. Loading full-res on page load instead of on open, skipping `srcset`, or failing to preload the _next_ image are the difference between instant and janky.
*   **Trust and orientation.** A user who opens image 3 of 12, pages to 7, zooms, then closes must land back exactly where they were on the page. Body scroll that jumps, focus that vanishes, or a backdrop that won't dismiss makes the whole app feel broken even when the picture looks fine.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy
Every well-built lightbox is composed of predictable parts:
*   **Trigger (thumbnail/link):** the in-page element that opens the lightbox. It owns the return-focus contract — focus comes _back_ here on close. Ideally a real `<button>` or `<a>` wrapping the thumbnail.
*   **Overlay / backdrop:** the dimmed full-viewport layer (typically Night `#0A0A0A` at 85–92% opacity). Click-outside-the-media dismisses. It also visually isolates the media from the page.
*   **Dialog surface:** the `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"` container that holds the media and controls. This is the focus-trap boundary and the labelled region ("Image gallery, 3 of 12").
*   **Media stage:** the centered area that holds the current image/video, aspect-ratio preserved, max ~90vw × ~90vh. Also the pan/zoom surface and the swipe target.
*   **Image element:** the `<img>` with real `alt`, `srcset`/`sizes` for responsive full-res, `decoding="async"`, and a loading placeholder.
*   **Caption:** optional text below or over the media, programmatically associated with the image via `aria-describedby` (or `<figure>`/`<figcaption>`).
*   **Counter / live status:** "3 of 12" shown visually and announced through an `aria-live` region as the user pages ("Image 4 of 12, \[alt\]").
*   **Prev / Next controls:** buttons on the left/right (≥44px), mirrored by ArrowLeft/ArrowRight keys and swipe. Disabled or wrapped at the ends per your wrap policy.
*   **Close control:** the "×" button (top-right, ≥44px, `aria-label="Close"`), mirrored by Escape and backdrop click.
*   **Zoom controls:** zoom-in / zoom-out / reset buttons that provide the keyboard/pointer alternative to pinch. When zoomed, the stage becomes pannable.
*   **Loading indicator:** spinner/skeleton shown while the full-res image decodes; hidden on `load`.
*   **Thumbnail strip (optional):** a row of thumbnails for direct navigation in large galleries, with the active one marked (`aria-current`).
* * *

## 4\. Sizes / Scale / Density
The lightbox scales the _media stage_ and its _controls_ to the viewport, not a fixed pixel box. Controls, however, obey hard touch-target minimums regardless of screen size.

| Tier | Viewport | Media max | Control target | Gutter | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact | ≤600px (phone) | 100vw × 78vh | 48px | `--space-2` (8px) | Full-bleed media, controls overlaid, swipe-primary |
| Comfortable | 601–1024px (tablet) | 92vw × 88vh | 44px | `--space-3` (12px) | Media centered, arrows in side gutters |
| Spacious | ≥1025px (desktop) | 90vw × 90vh, cap 1600px | 44px | `--space-4` (16px) | Media + optional thumbnail strip + caption panel |

Design tokens that drive it:

```css
:root {
  --lb-media-max-w: 90vw;
  --lb-media-max-h: 90vh;
  --lb-media-cap: 1600px;      /* don't upscale past source on huge screens */
  --lb-control-size: 44px;     /* min touch target */
  --lb-gutter: clamp(8px, 2vw, 16px);
  --lb-backdrop: rgb(10 10 10 / 0.9);   /* Night @ 90% */
  --lb-radius: 12px;
}
```

Rules that matter:
*   **Controls stay ≥44px even when the visual glyph is smaller** — expand the hit area with padding, never shrink the button on mobile where it matters most.
*   **Never upscale past the source.** Cap the media at the intrinsic image size (or `--lb-media-cap`) so a 900px photo doesn't blur to fill a 4K screen.
*   **Reserve the stage box before the image decodes** using the known aspect ratio, so paging doesn't reflow the controls (`aspect-ratio` on the stage).
*   **On phones, controls overlay the media** (there's no room for gutters); on desktop, arrows live in the side gutters clear of the image.
* * *

## 5\. States
A production lightbox has far more than open/closed. Missing states are the tell of an amateur build.
1. **Closed (idle):** not rendered (or `hidden`), backdrop gone, body scroll normal, focus on the page. The default.
2. **Opening:** backdrop fades in, media scales/fades from the thumbnail (respecting reduced-motion), focus moving into the dialog. Transient.
3. **Open (viewing):** dialog visible, focus trapped, body scroll locked, current image shown, counter announced.
4. **Loading:** the requested full-res image hasn't decoded yet — spinner/skeleton over the stage, controls still operable, `aria-busy="true"` on the stage.
5. **Loaded:** image decoded and shown, spinner removed, `alt` and caption present.
6. **Error:** image failed to load — a visible fallback ("Couldn't load image") with a retry affordance, never a broken-image icon with no recovery.
7. **Paging (next/prev):** transitioning between gallery items; outgoing image leaves, incoming loads (ideally already preloaded), counter updates and announces.
8. **At-boundary:** first or last item — prev/next either disabled (no-wrap) or wrapping to the other end (wrap), and the state is announced/reflected on the control.
9. **Zoomed:** scale > 1; the stage is now pannable, swipe-to-page is suspended (drag pans instead), and a "reset zoom" affordance appears.
10. **Panning:** actively dragging a zoomed image; cursor `grabbing`, momentum optional.
11. **Closing:** reverse of opening; focus returns to the trigger thumbnail _after_ the animation, body scroll restored.
12. **Control states:** each button carries its own hover / `:focus-visible` / active / disabled states (prev/next disabled at boundaries in no-wrap mode).
* * *

## 6\. Types / Variants
Every functional variant named and described. A screen usually implements one or two, not all.
*   **Single-image lightbox:** one thumbnail → one enlarged image. No paging controls; Escape/backdrop/close only. The simplest correct form.
*   **Gallery lightbox (prev/next):** a set of images with arrows + arrow keys + swipe and a counter. The workhorse for product galleries and portfolios. Wrap or no-wrap is a deliberate choice (see §12).
*   **Gallery with thumbnail strip:** adds a scrollable filmstrip for direct jumps in large sets; the active thumb is `aria-current`. Good above ~8 images.
*   **Zoom & pan lightbox:** adds scroll/pinch/button zoom (typically 1×–4×) and drag-to-pan when zoomed, with a keyboard zoom alternative. Essential for e-commerce detail and maps/diagrams.
*   **Video / iframe lightbox:** the payload is a `<video>` or an embedded player (YouTube/Vimeo `<iframe>`) instead of an image. Never autoplay with sound; provide native controls; pause and tear down the player on close so audio doesn't keep playing.
*   **Mixed-media gallery:** a single gallery whose items are a mix of images and videos, each item declaring its own type so the stage renders the right element.
*   **Deep-linkable lightbox:** the open item is reflected in the URL (hash or query, e.g. `#img=7`) so a specific image is shareable and survives refresh/back-button.
*   **Full-screen lightbox:** combines the modal with the Fullscreen API for a true edge-to-edge presentation (common on photography sites); still a dialog underneath.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use (and When Not To)
Use a lightbox when the user needs to **inspect media larger than its in-page size, on demand, without losing their place**:
*   Product image galleries — the shopper wants to zoom into fabric, finish, scale before buying.
*   Photography / portfolio / art sites — the work is the point and deserves the full viewport.
*   Any thumbnail grid where the thumbnails are deliberately small to fit many, with the real image one click away.
*   Image-heavy documentation, screenshots, diagrams, maps that reward zoom.
*   Press/gallery pages where users page through a set of related shots.

Don't use one (use the named alternative) when:
*   **The image is already shown at useful size inline** — a lightbox just adds a click for no gain. Leave it inline.
*   **The content isn't media** — a form, settings, a confirmation. Use a generic **Modal/Dialog**; the lightbox's zoom/paging chrome is dead weight.
*   **You want images to cycle in the page flow** without a takeover — use a **Carousel/Slider** inline; reserve the lightbox for an on-demand enlarge.
*   **You only need a small magnification of one region** (e.g. product hover-zoom) — use an inline **magnifier/zoom**, not a full overlay.
*   **The set is a browsable feed** the user scrolls indefinitely — that's a **feed/grid**, not a trap-focus modal per item (though tapping one item _into_ a lightbox is fine).

Placement heuristics: the trigger thumbnail should look tappable (cursor, hover cue) and be a real button/link; on mobile keep the media full-bleed with overlaid controls in thumb reach; keep Close reachable at the top corner and never hide it behind the image.

## 8\. Across Design Systems
Same component, different philosophies and, notably, different levels of "batteries included" — most systems ship a Dialog/Modal and expect you to compose the lightbox on top.
*   **Material Design (Google):** No first-class "lightbox," but the pattern is an expanded/full-screen **Dialog** with the image as content, plus Material's image-list and the "hero"/container-transform motion where a thumbnail expands into the full view. Elevation + scrim backdrop; motion is a signature. You add paging and zoom.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** The system Photos/Quick Look viewer is the reference: full-screen, swipe between items, pinch-to-zoom, share sheet, swipe-down to dismiss. On the web, approximate with a dialog + gesture handling. Restraint and gesture fluency are the ethos; Liquid Glass chrome in iOS 26.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** Uses **Dialog**/overlay surfaces with acrylic/mica materials; image viewing leans on the OS Photos experience. Compact, neutral chrome, subtle depth. Compose the media viewer from the Dialog primitive.
*   **Ant Design:** Ships a real **`Image`** **component with a built-in preview** — click to open a full-screen lightbox with zoom, rotate, flip, and (via `Image.PreviewGroup`) gallery paging out of the box. The most "batteries-included" of the mainstream systems for this exact pattern.
*   **Tailwind CSS:** No component; you compose it (or reach for Headless UI's `Dialog` for the modal behavior + your own media stage). Tailwind gives the styling system; you own the behavior. Common pairing: Headless UI Dialog + a small zoom/swipe lib.
*   **shadcn/ui + Radix:** No dedicated lightbox, but `Dialog` (Radix) gives the correct modal foundation — focus trap, `aria-modal`, Escape, scroll lock, return focus — and you layer the image stage, counter, and paging. This is the cleanest modern base (see §9.7).
*   **Bootstrap 5:** No official lightbox since dropping the old plugin; teams use the **Modal** component with an image inside, or a third-party lib (e.g. GLightbox, PhotoSwipe). Modal handles backdrop/focus/scroll-lock; you add gallery/zoom.
*   **Dedicated libraries (the real-world default):** **PhotoSwipe**, **GLightbox**, **Fancybox**, **yet-another-react-lightbox**, **Flowbite Gallery** — most production lightboxes use one of these because zoom/pan/swipe/preload physics are genuinely hard to get right.

### 8b. What's Trending in 2026
*   **Liquid Glass chrome:** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language bleeds into media viewers — translucent, refractive control bars and pill buttons floating over the media rather than opaque toolbars, with the dimmed photo behind bending light through the chrome.
*   **View Transitions API for the open:** the thumbnail-to-full-image morph (long the domain of Material's "container transform" and FLIP hacks) is now a few lines of native `view-transition-name` + `document.startViewTransition`, degrading gracefully and honoring reduced-motion.
*   **Shared-element / hero galleries** carried over from mobile: tap a grid tile and it _is_ the lightbox image, animating in place, then paging within the set.
*   **AVIF/JPEG-XL + responsive** **`srcset`** **by default:** the lightbox is the one place full-res matters, so modern formats and per-DPR sourcing are becoming table stakes for LCP and data cost.
*   **Gesture-rich, spatial dismissal:** swipe-down-to-close with the image tracking the finger (iOS Photos style) is spreading to the web via pointer events, always with a button/keyboard equivalent.

### 9.1 HTML (semantic foundation with ARIA)

```html
<!-- Trigger: a real button wrapping the thumbnail. Focus returns here on close. -->
<button type="button" class="lb-thumb" data-lightbox="gallery" data-index="0"
        aria-haspopup="dialog">
  <img src="/img/chair-320.jpg"
       srcset="/img/chair-320.jpg 320w, /img/chair-640.jpg 640w"
       sizes="160px" alt="Walnut lounge chair, front view" width="160" height="160">
</button>

<!-- The lightbox dialog. Hidden until opened. -->
<div class="lb" id="lb" role="dialog" aria-modal="true"
     aria-labelledby="lb-title" aria-describedby="lb-caption" hidden>
  <div class="lb__backdrop" data-close></div>

  <div class="lb__surface">
    <h2 id="lb-title" class="sr-only">Image gallery</h2>

    <!-- Polite live region announces "Image X of N" on page -->
    <p class="sr-only" aria-live="polite" id="lb-status"></p>

    <figure class="lb__stage" aria-busy="false">
      <img id="lb-img" class="lb__img" alt="" decoding="async">
      <figcaption id="lb-caption" class="lb__caption"></figcaption>
    </figure>

    <p class="lb__counter" aria-hidden="true"><span id="lb-cur">1</span> / <span id="lb-total">1</span></p>

    <button type="button" class="lb__btn lb__prev" data-prev aria-label="Previous image">‹</button>
    <button type="button" class="lb__btn lb__next" data-next aria-label="Next image">›</button>

    <div class="lb__zoom" role="group" aria-label="Zoom controls">
      <button type="button" class="lb__btn" data-zoom-out aria-label="Zoom out">−</button>
      <button type="button" class="lb__btn" data-zoom-reset aria-label="Reset zoom">Reset</button>
      <button type="button" class="lb__btn" data-zoom-in aria-label="Zoom in">+</button>
    </div>

    <button type="button" class="lb__btn lb__close" data-close aria-label="Close">×</button>
  </div>
</div>
```

Key HTML rules:
*   The dialog is `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"` and **labelled** (`aria-labelledby`) and **described** (`aria-describedby` → the caption). `aria-modal` tells AT the rest of the page is inert.
*   The `<img>` has a **real** **`alt`** set per-image at runtime; the caption is a `<figcaption>` inside the `<figure>`, associated via `aria-describedby`.
*   The **counter is** **`aria-hidden`** (decorative); the _announcement_ comes from the polite `#lb-status` live region so it isn't double-read.
*   Every control is a real `<button>` with an `aria-label` (icon-only).

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (full reusable component)

```tsx
import {
  useCallback, useEffect, useId, useRef, useState, type KeyboardEvent,
} from "react";
import { createPortal } from "react-dom";

export interface LightboxItem {
  src: string;
  srcSet?: string;
  sizes?: string;
  alt: string;
  caption?: string;
  type?: "image" | "video";
  poster?: string;
}

interface LightboxProps {
  items: LightboxItem[];
  index: number | null;              // null = closed
  onIndexChange: (i: number) => void;
  onClose: () => void;
  wrap?: boolean;                    // wrap at gallery ends
  returnFocusRef?: React.RefObject<HTMLElement>;
}

export function Lightbox({
  items, index, onIndexChange, onClose, wrap = false, returnFocusRef,
}: LightboxProps) {
  const open = index !== null;
  const surfaceRef = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
  const statusId = useId();
  const titleId = useId();
  const captionId = useId();
  const [loading, setLoading] = useState(true);

  const item = open ? items[index] : undefined;
  const atStart = index === 0;
  const atEnd = index === items.length - 1;

  const go = useCallback(
    (delta: number) => {
      if (index === null) return;
      let next = index + delta;
      if (next < 0) next = wrap ? items.length - 1 : 0;
      if (next > items.length - 1) next = wrap ? 0 : items.length - 1;
      if (next !== index) { setLoading(true); onIndexChange(next); }
    },
    [index, items.length, wrap, onIndexChange],
  );

  // Body scroll lock — captured on open, RESTORED on close (the classic modal bug).
  useEffect(() => {
    if (!open) return;
    const { overflow, paddingRight } = document.body.style;
    const scrollbar = window.innerWidth - document.documentElement.clientWidth;
    document.body.style.overflow = "hidden";
    if (scrollbar > 0) document.body.style.paddingRight = `${scrollbar}px`;
    return () => {
      document.body.style.overflow = overflow;      // always restore, even on unmount
      document.body.style.paddingRight = paddingRight;
    };
  }, [open]);

  // Focus management: move focus in on open, return to trigger on close.
  useEffect(() => {
    if (!open) return;
    const prevActive = (returnFocusRef?.current ??
      (document.activeElement as HTMLElement | null));
    surfaceRef.current?.focus();
    return () => prevActive?.focus?.();
  }, [open, returnFocusRef]);

  // Preload adjacent images so paging is instant.
  useEffect(() => {
    if (index === null) return;
    [index - 1, index + 1].forEach((i) => {
      const it = items[i];
      if (it && it.type !== "video") {
        const img = new Image();
        if (it.srcSet) img.srcset = it.srcSet;
        img.src = it.src;
      }
    });
  }, [index, items]);

  const onKeyDown = (e: KeyboardEvent<HTMLDivElement>) => {
    switch (e.key) {
      case "Escape": e.preventDefault(); onClose(); break;
      case "ArrowRight": e.preventDefault(); go(1); break;
      case "ArrowLeft": e.preventDefault(); go(-1); break;
      case "Tab": trapFocus(e, surfaceRef.current); break;
    }
  };

  if (!open || !item) return null;

  return createPortal(
    <div
      className="lb" role="dialog" aria-modal="true"
      aria-labelledby={titleId} aria-describedby={item.caption ? captionId : undefined}
      onKeyDown={onKeyDown}
    >
      <div className="lb__backdrop" onClick={onClose} />
      <div className="lb__surface" ref={surfaceRef} tabIndex={-1}>
        <h2 id={titleId} className="sr-only">Image gallery</h2>
        <p className="sr-only" aria-live="polite" id={statusId}>
          {`Image ${index + 1} of ${items.length}. ${item.alt}`}
        </p>

        <figure className="lb__stage" aria-busy={loading}>
          {item.type === "video" ? (
            <video className="lb__img" src={item.src} poster={item.poster}
                   controls playsInline onLoadedData={() => setLoading(false)} />
          ) : (
            <img className="lb__img" src={item.src} srcSet={item.srcSet}
                 sizes={item.sizes} alt={item.alt} decoding="async"
                 onLoad={() => setLoading(false)}
                 onError={() => setLoading(false)} />
          )}
          {item.caption && (
            <figcaption id={captionId} className="lb__caption">{item.caption}</figcaption>
          )}
        </figure>

        {items.length > 1 && (
          <>
            <p className="lb__counter" aria-hidden="true">{index + 1} / {items.length}</p>
            <button type="button" className="lb__btn lb__prev" aria-label="Previous image"
                    disabled={!wrap && atStart} onClick={() => go(-1)}>‹</button>
            <button type="button" className="lb__btn lb__next" aria-label="Next image"
                    disabled={!wrap && atEnd} onClick={() => go(1)}>›</button>
          </>
        )}
        <button type="button" className="lb__btn lb__close" aria-label="Close"
                onClick={onClose}>×</button>
      </div>
    </div>,
    document.body,
  );
}

// Focus trap: keep Tab cycling inside the surface.
function trapFocus(e: KeyboardEvent, container: HTMLElement | null) {
  if (!container) return;
  const focusable = container.querySelectorAll<HTMLElement>(
    'a[href],button:not([disabled]),input,[tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"])',
  );
  if (focusable.length === 0) return;
  const first = focusable[0];
  const last = focusable[focusable.length - 1];
  const active = document.activeElement;
  if (e.shiftKey && active === first) { e.preventDefault(); last.focus(); }
  else if (!e.shiftKey && active === last) { e.preventDefault(); first.focus(); }
}
```

Why this is the senior version: scroll lock captures the prior inline styles and **restores them in the effect cleanup** (fixes the unmount-scroll-lock bug), focus returns to the trigger on close via cleanup, adjacent images are **preloaded** so paging is instant, the live region announces "Image X of N" without double-reading the visual counter, and `wrap` is an explicit prop that also drives the `disabled` state of the arrows.

export function createLightbox(items, { wrap = false } = {}) {
  const root = document.getElementById("lb");
  const img = root.querySelector("#lb-img");
  const cap = root.querySelector("#lb-caption");
  const status = root.querySelector("#lb-status");
  const stage = root.querySelector(".lb__stage");
  const cur = root.querySelector("#lb-cur");
  const total = root.querySelector("#lb-total");
  const prevBtn = root.querySelector("[data-prev]");
  const nextBtn = root.querySelector("[data-next]");
  let index = 0, trigger = null, scale = 1;

  total.textContent = items.length;

  function preload(i) {
    const it = items[i];
    if (it) { const p = new Image(); if (it.srcSet) p.srcset = it.srcSet; p.src = it.src; }
  }

  function render() {
    const it = items[index];
    stage.setAttribute("aria-busy", "true");
    img.onload = () => stage.setAttribute("aria-busy", "false");
    img.onerror = () => { stage.setAttribute("aria-busy", "false"); cap.textContent = "Couldn't load image."; };
    img.alt = it.alt;
    if (it.srcSet) img.srcset = it.srcSet;
    img.src = it.src;
    cap.textContent = it.caption ?? "";
    cur.textContent = index + 1;
    status.textContent = `Image ${index + 1} of ${items.length}. ${it.alt}`;
    prevBtn.disabled = !wrap && index === 0;
    nextBtn.disabled = !wrap && index === items.length - 1;
    preload(index + 1); preload(index - 1);
  }

  function go(delta) {
    let next = index + delta;
    if (next < 0) next = wrap ? items.length - 1 : 0;
    if (next > items.length - 1) next = wrap ? 0 : items.length - 1;
    if (next !== index) { index = next; resetZoom(); render(); }
  }

  function resetZoom() { scale = 1; img.style.setProperty("--lb-scale", "1"); img.dataset.zoomed = "false"; }
  function zoom(delta) {
    scale = Math.min(4, Math.max(1, scale + delta));
    img.style.setProperty("--lb-scale", String(scale));
    img.dataset.zoomed = scale > 1 ? "true" : "false";
  }

  let lastFocused = null;
  function lockScroll(lock) {
    if (lock) {
      root.dataset.pr = document.body.style.paddingRight;
      root.dataset.of = document.body.style.overflow;
      const sb = window.innerWidth - document.documentElement.clientWidth;
      document.body.style.overflow = "hidden";
      if (sb > 0) document.body.style.paddingRight = sb + "px";
    } else {
      document.body.style.overflow = root.dataset.of || "";
      document.body.style.paddingRight = root.dataset.pr || "";   // restore, always
    }
  }

  function open(i, triggerEl) {
    index = i; trigger = triggerEl; lastFocused = triggerEl || document.activeElement;
    root.hidden = false; lockScroll(true); resetZoom(); render();
    root.querySelector(".lb__surface").focus();
    document.addEventListener("keydown", onKey);
  }
  function close() {
    root.hidden = true; lockScroll(false);
    document.removeEventListener("keydown", onKey);
    (lastFocused || trigger)?.focus();   // return focus to the thumbnail
  }

  function onKey(e) {
    if (e.key === "Escape") { e.preventDefault(); close(); }
    else if (e.key === "ArrowRight") { e.preventDefault(); go(1); }
    else if (e.key === "ArrowLeft") { e.preventDefault(); go(-1); }
    else if (e.key === "+" || e.key === "=") zoom(0.5);
    else if (e.key === "-") zoom(-0.5);
    else if (e.key === "Tab") trap(e);
  }
  function trap(e) {
    const f = root.querySelectorAll('button:not([disabled]),[tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"])');
    if (!f.length) return;
    const first = f[0], last = f[f.length - 1];
    if (e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === first) { e.preventDefault(); last.focus(); }
    else if (!e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === last) { e.preventDefault(); first.focus(); }
  }

  root.addEventListener("click", (e) => {
    if (e.target.closest("[data-close]")) close();
    else if (e.target.closest("[data-next]")) go(1);
    else if (e.target.closest("[data-prev]")) go(-1);
    else if (e.target.closest("[data-zoom-in]")) zoom(0.5);
    else if (e.target.closest("[data-zoom-out]")) zoom(-0.5);
    else if (e.target.closest("[data-zoom-reset]")) resetZoom();
  });

  // Touch swipe with a keyboard/button equivalent already provided above.
  let x0 = null;
  stage.addEventListener("pointerdown", (e) => { if (scale === 1) x0 = e.clientX; });
  stage.addEventListener("pointerup", (e) => {
    if (x0 === null) return;
    const dx = e.clientX - x0; x0 = null;
    if (Math.abs(dx) > 50) go(dx < 0 ? 1 : -1);
  });

  return { open, close };
}

    else if (e.key === "ArrowLeft") this.go(-1);
    else if (e.key === "Tab") this.trap(e);
  };

  connectedCallback() { this.attachShadow({ mode: "open" }); this.hidden = true; }

  set gallery(items: typeof this.items) { this.items = items; }

  open(index = 0, trigger?: HTMLElement) {
    this.index = index;
    this.lastFocused = trigger ?? (document.activeElement as HTMLElement);
    this.savedOverflow = document.body.style.overflow;
    document.body.style.overflow = "hidden";
    this.hidden = false;
    this.render();
    document.addEventListener("keydown", this.onKey);
    this.shadowRoot!.querySelector<HTMLElement>(".surface")!.focus();
  }
  close() {
    this.hidden = true;
    document.body.style.overflow = this.savedOverflow;   // restore
    document.removeEventListener("keydown", this.onKey);
    this.lastFocused?.focus();                            // return focus to trigger
  }
  go(d: number) {
    const n = Math.min(this.items.length - 1, Math.max(0, this.index + d));
    if (n !== this.index) { this.index = n; this.render(); }
  }
  private trap(e: KeyboardEvent) {
    const f = this.shadowRoot!.querySelectorAll<HTMLElement>("button:not([disabled]),[tabindex='-1']");
    if (!f.length) return;
    const first = f[0], last = f[f.length - 1];
    const active = this.shadowRoot!.activeElement;
    if (e.shiftKey && active === first) { e.preventDefault(); last.focus(); }
    else if (!e.shiftKey && active === last) { e.preventDefault(); first.focus(); }
  }
  private render() {
    const it = this.items[this.index];
    this.shadowRoot!.innerHTML = `
      <style>
        :host { position: fixed; inset: 0; z-index: 1000; display: grid; place-items: center; }
        .backdrop { position: absolute; inset: 0; background: rgb(10 10 10 / .9); }
        .surface { position: relative; max-width: 90vw; max-height: 90vh; }
        img { max-width: 90vw; max-height: 90vh; object-fit: contain; }
        button { position: absolute; min-width: 44px; min-height: 44px; border-radius: 999px;
                 border: 0; background: rgb(0 0 0 / .5); color: #fff; font-size: 24px; }
        button:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid #DCA424; }
        .prev { left: 8px; top: 50%; } .next { right: 8px; top: 50%; } .close { top: 8px; right: 8px; }
        figcaption { color: #E8E6E1; text-align: center; }
        .sr { position: absolute; width: 1px; height: 1px; overflow: hidden; clip: rect(0 0 0 0); }
      </style>
      <div class="backdrop"></div>
      <div class="surface" tabindex="-1" role="dialog" aria-modal="true" aria-label="Image gallery">
        <p class="sr" aria-live="polite">Image ${this.index + 1} of ${this.items.length}. ${it.alt}</p>
        <figure>
          <img src="${it.src}" srcset="${it.srcSet ?? ""}" alt="${it.alt.replace(/"/g, """)}" decoding="async">
          ${it.caption ? `<figcaption>${it.caption}</figcaption>` : ""}
        </figure>
        <button class="prev" aria-label="Previous image" ${this.index === 0 ? "disabled" : ""}>‹</button>
        <button class="next" aria-label="Next image" ${this.index === this.items.length - 1 ? "disabled" : ""}>›</button>
        <button class="close" aria-label="Close">×</button>
      </div>`;
    const sr = this.shadowRoot!;
    sr.querySelector(".backdrop")!.addEventListener("click", () => this.close());
    sr.querySelector(".close")!.addEventListener("click", () => this.close());
    sr.querySelector(".prev")!.addEventListener("click", () => this.go(-1));
    sr.querySelector(".next")!.addEventListener("click", () => this.go(1));
  }
}

## 10\. Accessibility
STANDALONE. The lightbox inherits every Modal obligation and adds media-specific ones.

**Roles & ARIA**
*   The overlay is `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"`, labelled (`aria-labelledby` → a visually-hidden "Image gallery" heading) and, when a caption exists, described (`aria-describedby` → the `<figcaption>`).
*   The current `<img>` carries a **real, per-image** **`alt`**. Decorative gallery chrome (counter glyphs) is `aria-hidden`.
*   Position changes are announced through a **polite** **`aria-live`** **region** ("Image 3 of 12, \[alt\]") — never rely on the visual counter alone, and never make it `assertive` (it would interrupt).
*   Every control is a real `<button>` with an `aria-label` (Close, Previous image, Next image, Zoom in/out/reset).

**Keyboard map**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| `Enter` / `Space` (on trigger) | Open the lightbox at that image |
| `Escape` | Close, return focus to the trigger thumbnail |
| `ArrowRight` | Next image (wrap or clamp per policy) |
| `ArrowLeft` | Previous image |
| `Tab` / `Shift+Tab` | Move focus within the dialog only (trapped) |
| `+` / `=` | Zoom in (keyboard alternative to pinch) |
| `-` | Zoom out |
| `0` / `Home` (optional) | Reset zoom / first image |

**Focus management**
*   On open, move focus into the dialog (the surface or the Close button). On close, **return focus to the trigger thumbnail** that opened it.
*   Trap Tab within the dialog while open; the rest of the page is inert (`aria-modal` + optional `inert` on the page root).
*   When paging, keep focus on the persistent control the user pressed (Next stays Next) so a screen-reader user isn't dumped back to the top.

**Contrast & target size**
*   Controls sit on a dim/photo backdrop — give them their own semi-opaque chip so the glyph clears **≥3:1** against whatever's behind (a bright photo can wash out a white "×"). Caption text ≥4.5:1.
*   All controls **≥44×44px** hit area, even when the glyph is small; on mobile they overlay the media but must not sit under the notch/home indicator.

**Reduced motion**
*   Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: no zoom-from-thumbnail scale, no crossfade paging, no spinner spin (swap for a static indicator). The open/close becomes an instant show/hide. Never remove the _functionality_, only the animation.

**Common failures specific to the lightbox**
*   Empty or filename `alt` ("IMG\_2043.jpg") — useless to screen readers.
*   No keyboard alternative to swipe/pinch, stranding keyboard and switch users at one image or one zoom level.
*   Focus not trapped, so Tab escapes to the page behind the overlay.
*   Focus lost on close (dumped to `<body>`) instead of returning to the trigger.
*   Counter shown but never announced; or announced `assertive`, interrupting the image's own alt.
*   Body scroll not restored on close (see §12) — the page is stuck.
*   Video autoplaying with sound, or a player that keeps playing audio after close.
* * *

## 11\. Innovative / Emerging Ideas
*   **View Transitions API open/close.** Give the thumbnail and the full image a shared `view-transition-name` and wrap the open in `document.startViewTransition()` — the browser morphs the thumbnail into the full image natively, no FLIP math, and it no-ops gracefully where unsupported and under reduced-motion. (2026-era platform API now shipping across Chromium and Safari.)
*   **Liquid-Glass floating chrome.** Controls as translucent, refractive pills (Apple iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language) floating over the media rather than an opaque toolbar — the dimmed photo bends light through the control bar. Approximate on the web with layered `backdrop-filter` + highlight rims.
*   **AVIF / JPEG-XL with** **`<source>`** **+ DPR-aware** **`srcset`****.** The lightbox is the one place full-res matters, so serve the newest formats and per-device-pixel-ratio sources; fall back through `<picture>` for older engines. Cuts LCP and mobile data dramatically.
*   **Fetch Priority + speculative preload.** Mark the opening image `fetchpriority="high"` and use the Speculation Rules / prefetch to warm the _next_ gallery image before the user asks, so paging is frame-perfect.
*   **`inert`** **for the background.** Instead of hand-rolled focus traps, toggle the `inert` attribute on the page root while the lightbox is open — the platform removes everything behind it from focus and AT, a cleaner guarantee than a JS Tab-loop.
*   **Zoom via CSS** **`image-rendering`** **+ high-res swap.** On zoom-in, swap to a higher-resolution source (or a tiled deep-zoom source like IIIF/OpenSeadragon) so pixels stay crisp instead of upscaling a blur.
* * *

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers
Silent failures that cost sales and trust — none throw an error.
*   **Body scroll not restored on close.** The classic Modal bug: `overflow: hidden` is set on open but the code that removes it never runs (early return, unmount before cleanup, error mid-close). The user closes the lightbox and the whole page is frozen. _Fix:_ set the lock in one place and release it in the effect cleanup / `finally`, restoring the _previous_ value, not blindly clearing it.
*   **Loading full-res on page load.** Shipping the 2MB hero into the DOM before anyone opens the lightbox tanks LCP and burns mobile data for images most users never enlarge. _Fix:_ thumbnails `loading="lazy"`; load full-res only on open; preload just the _adjacent_ two.
*   **No loading state, so paging feels broken.** Click Next, the old image lingers or the stage goes blank while the new one decodes, and the user thinks it hung. _Fix:_ show a spinner/skeleton with `aria-busy`, keep the stage box reserved by aspect ratio, and preload neighbors so it's usually instant.
*   **Missing or junk** **`alt`****.** Empty alt (or the filename) makes the gallery invisible to screen readers and hurts image SEO. _Fix:_ require alt as data at the source; it's not optional.
*   **No keyboard/button equivalent to swipe & pinch.** Touch-only paging and pinch-only zoom strand keyboard, switch, and desktop users. _Fix:_ Arrow keys page, +/− and buttons zoom, always alongside the gestures.
*   **Video autoplay with sound / audio that outlives close.** Auto-playing a loud clip ambushes the user; a player that keeps talking after the overlay closes is worse. _Fix:_ never autoplay with sound, honor `muted`/user gesture rules, and pause + tear down the player on close.
*   **Trapping the user with no visible exit.** A lightbox with the Close button hidden behind a bright image, no Escape, and no backdrop-dismiss is a roach motel. _Fix:_ three independent exits — Close button (contrast-chipped), Escape, backdrop click — always present.
*   **Losing the user's place.** Open image 7, close, and the page has scrolled to the top or jumped because of the scrollbar-width shift. _Fix:_ compensate scrollbar width with `padding-right` on lock, restore scroll position, return focus to the trigger.
*   **Wrap ambiguity at the ends.** Silently wrapping from last to first (or dead-ending with no cue) confuses users about how many images exist. _Fix:_ pick a policy and signal it — disable arrows at the ends (no-wrap) or announce the wrap.
* * *

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
*   **TypeScript-enforced media discriminated union.** Make illegal media states unrepresentable so an image item can't carry a `poster` and a video item can't skip `alt`:

```typescript
type ImageItem = {
  kind: "image";
  src: string; srcSet?: string; sizes?: string;
  alt: string;                         // required for images
  caption?: string;
};
type VideoItem = {
  kind: "video";
  src: string; poster: string;         // required for videos
  alt: string;                         // used as accessible name
  captionsSrc?: string;                // WebVTT track
  autoplay?: false;                    // literal false: never allow autoplay-with-sound
};
export type MediaItem = ImageItem | VideoItem;

function renderStage(item: MediaItem) {
  switch (item.kind) {
    case "image": return `<img src="${item.src}" alt="${item.alt}">`;
    case "video": return `<video src="${item.src}" poster="${item.poster}" controls></video>`;
    // exhaustiveness: adding a 3rd kind forces this switch to be updated
    default: { const _never: never = item; return _never; }
  }
}
```

*   **Design-token tiers for the chrome.** Drive every surface off tokens so the lightbox reskins per brand without touching behavior: primitive (`--night: #0A0A0A`) → semantic (`--overlay-scrim: color-mix(in srgb, var(--night) 90%, transparent)`) → component (`--lb-backdrop: var(--overlay-scrim)`, `--lb-control-bg`, `--lb-control-ring: var(--goldenrod)`). The dialog only references component tokens; a rebrand swaps primitives.
*   **Focus trap via native** **`inert`** **+** **`:focus-visible`** **sentinels.** Prefer toggling `inert` on the page root over a JS Tab loop; keep two visually-hidden focus sentinels at the dialog's start and end that bounce focus back in if the platform trap ever leaks.
*   **Deep-zoom tiling (IIIF / OpenSeadragon) behind the same shell.** For maps, art, and gigapixel product shots, back the stage with a tiled pyramid source so zoom pulls sharper tiles instead of upscaling one JPEG — same lightbox contract, swap the stage renderer.
*   **Preload budget that scales with connection.** Read `navigator.connection.saveData` / `effectiveType`; on `4g` preload two neighbors each side, on `2g`/save-data preload none. The paging feels instant on fast networks without abusing metered ones.
* * *

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
*   **Lazy everything until open.** Thumbnails use `loading="lazy" decoding="async"`; the full-res source is only requested when the lightbox opens. Never put the full-res `<img>` in the DOM at page load — it competes for LCP and bandwidth with content users can actually see.
*   **Responsive sources via** **`srcset`****/****`<picture>`** **+ AVIF.** Serve DPR- and viewport-appropriate sizes so a phone doesn't download a 2048px file for a 390px screen. Modern formats cut bytes 30–50% versus JPEG; the lightbox is exactly where that saving lands.
*   **Preload only the adjacent two, and mark priority.** Warm `index±1` with `new Image()` (or `<link rel="prefetch">`) and set the opening image `fetchpriority="high"`. Don't preload the whole gallery — that's the same page-load mistake, deferred.
*   **Animate compositor-only properties.** Open/zoom/pan must animate `transform` and `opacity` only, never `width`/`height`/`top`/`left`, so there's no per-frame reflow. Put `will-change: transform` on the zoom/pan image _only while interacting_, then remove it (a permanent `will-change` wastes GPU memory).
*   **Tree-shake the lightbox lib and its icons.** Named imports from PhotoSwipe/yet-another-react-lightbox and named icon imports (`import { X } from "lucide-react"`, never `import *`) keep a single close glyph from dragging in a whole icon set. Load the lightbox component itself via dynamic `import()` so it isn't in the initial bundle for users who never open it.
*   **Virtualize giant galleries.** For a 500-image set, don't mount 500 thumbnails or a 500-node filmstrip — virtualize the grid/strip and mount only the current media item (plus preloaded neighbors) in the dialog. `contain: layout paint` on the stage isolates its work from the page.

## 15\. Security
STANDALONE. A lightbox is mostly a display surface, but it has a real attack surface the moment its content or sources are user-supplied or access-controlled.
*   **XSS through captions and alt text.** Captions and `alt` are frequently user-generated (marketplace listings, CMS, comments). Rendering them with `innerHTML`/`v-html`/`dangerouslySetInnerHTML` is a stored-XSS hole. Insert them as **text** (`textContent`, JSX `{}`, Jinja autoescape) and, if rich captions are truly required, sanitize with a vetted allowlist (DOMPurify) — never trust the string.
*   **Malicious** **`src`** **/ URL injection.** If image URLs come from user input, an attacker can supply a `javascript:` or `data:text/html` URL, or point `src` at an internal host to probe your network (SSRF via an image proxy). Validate that sources are `https:` (or same-origin relative), resolve them server-side, and never let the client dictate an arbitrary upstream for a server-side fetch.
*   **Authorization for private media.** A lightbox that shows gated galleries must not rely on "the thumbnail wasn't rendered" as the control. The **endpoint** that serves each full-res image re-checks that _this_ user may view _that_ image (see §9.13), and private media is delivered via **short-lived signed URLs** so a leaked link expires. Hiding a thumbnail is UX; the server decides access.
*   **Server-side validation of the requested item.** The client sends an image id/index to fetch; validate it server-side (exists, belongs to the gallery, user is permitted) and return 403/404 rather than trusting the number. Rate-limit the gallery-JSON endpoint so it can't be scraped image-by-image.
*   **Untrusted** **`<iframe>`****/video embeds.** For the video/iframe variant, sandbox embedded players (`sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin"` only as needed), set `referrerpolicy`, and pin `allow` to the minimum (no autoplay-with-sound, no camera/mic). A third-party embed is third-party code running over your users.
*   **Clickjacking of one-click actions.** If the lightbox contains actionable controls (buy, delete, download-original for paid assets), those actions are still gated and CSRF-protected server-side; the overlay being modal is not a security boundary.

If your lightbox only ever displays first-party, static, non-gated marketing images with hardcoded alt text, its security surface is genuinely small — but the caption/alt-escaping rule still applies the day a CMS or a user starts supplying that text.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist
Before a lightbox is "done":
- [ ] It's a real modal: `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"`, labelled, with the page behind inert.
- [ ] Three independent exits present: Close button (contrast-chipped ≥3:1), Escape, backdrop click.
- [ ] Focus moves into the dialog on open and **returns to the trigger thumbnail** on close.
- [ ] Tab is trapped inside the dialog; nothing behind it is reachable.
- [ ] Every image has a real, meaningful `alt`; captions are programmatically associated.
- [ ] Gallery position announced via polite `aria-live` ("Image X of N"); counter shown visually too.
- [ ] Keyboard equivalents for every gesture: Arrows page, +/− and buttons zoom, no touch-only paths.
- [ ] Wrap policy chosen and signalled (arrows disabled at ends, or wrap announced).
- [ ] Loading state with `aria-busy`; adjacent images preloaded; full-res never loaded before open.
- [ ] Body scroll locked on open and **restored** on close (scrollbar-width compensated, position kept).
- [ ] All controls ≥44×44px; caption contrast ≥4.5:1.
- [ ] `prefers-reduced-motion` kills open/zoom/paging animation without removing function.
- [ ] Video never autoplays with sound and is paused/torn down on close.
- [ ] Responsive `srcset`/AVIF sources; lightbox code and icons tree-shaken / dynamically imported.
- [ ] Captions/alt rendered as text (no `innerHTML`); private media authorized + signed server-side.

## 17\. Visual Styles
The same focus-trapped media viewer, wearing eleven skins. Only the chrome changes — never the behavior.
*   **Flat:** solid Night backdrop at 90%, crisp white controls with no shadow, a thin caption bar. Fast, legible, zero ornament — reads instantly on any photo.
*   **Material:** the surface arrives with a container-transform from the thumbnail; controls are circular icon buttons with elevation shadows and a ripple on tap; the scrim is Material's standard 32% black over the media area.
*   **Glassmorphism:** the control bar and caption panel are translucent frosted glass (`backdrop-filter: blur`) floating over the dimmed photo — watch that the glyph still clears 3:1 where a bright image bleeds through.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026):** control pills are refractive, specular-rimmed glass that bend the dimmed photo behind them and shift a sheen as the image pans — Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe look, gorgeous over saturated hero shots.
*   **Neumorphism:** controls are soft same-color extrusions on a charcoal control tray — the close "×" a gently raised puck, the arrows inset grooves. Pretty but contrast-poor; reserve for the chrome, never the caption text.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** the stage sits in a beveled photo-frame mat with an inner shadow, controls look like physical camera buttons with gradient bevels — the "viewing a print" metaphor made literal.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** hard black backdrop, thick-bordered square control buttons with a hard offset shadow, the counter in mono type slammed in a corner — loud, editorial, zero radius.
*   **Claymorphism:** big-radius puffy control buttons with top inner-light and bottom shadow, a soft rounded caption bubble — friendly and tactile, good for playful or kids' galleries.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** the backdrop is a slow animated multi-hue gradient over near-black instead of flat dim, controls glow faintly — premium and moody; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and keep the photo the star.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-invisible chrome — a hairline close "×", tiny "3 / 12" in a corner set in a grid, generous negative space, the image and its caption doing all the work.
*   **UJG Brand:** Night backdrop, Goldenrod focus rings and active arrows, Eminence-tinted translucent control chips with a warm glow, caption in Platinum Urbanist — the house default, confident and warm over deep saturated media.

**The rule that never changes:** style is skin, behavior is the skeleton. Across all eleven skins the semantics (`role="dialog" aria-modal`), the focus trap, the return-focus-to-trigger, the keyboard map, the "Image X of N" announcement, the ≥44px targets, and the scroll-lock-and-restore stay identical. For the full 31 styles, see the Design Styles page; the doc covers the 11 core minimum, the playground covers all 31.
