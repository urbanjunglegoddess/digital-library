## 1\. What a Side Menu Actually Is
A side menu is a **persistent vertical navigation rail**, anchored to the left (occasionally right) edge of an application shell, that lists the primary destinations of the app and stays on screen while you work inside a destination. Dashboards, admin consoles, docs sites, IDEs, email clients — the sidebar is the app's table of contents, always visible, always oriented.

The word "sidebar" gets stretched over three genuinely different things, and confusing them is the #1 architecture mistake:

*   **Side menu / sidebar nav (this doc):** _persistent_. It's part of the layout, sits _beside_ the content (the content shrinks to make room), and doesn't need dismissing. It's the app shell's left column. Think Gmail, Notion, Vercel, the Stripe dashboard, VS Code's activity bar + sidebar.
*   **Drawer (its own doc):** _transient_. It slides _over_ the content from an edge, dims the page behind a scrim, traps focus, and is dismissed with Escape or an outside click. On mobile, the side menu literally _transforms into_ a drawer — same links, different container and behavior. They are siblings, not synonyms.
*   **Navigation (top nav / menubar):** the _horizontal_ primary nav across the top, or a `role="menubar"` app menu. Different axis, different affordances, often _paired with_ a sidebar rather than replacing it.

The distinction that trips people: **a side menu is a set of links inside a** **`<nav>`** **landmark — it is not a** **`role="menu"`****\*\*\*\*.** `role="menu"`/`menuitem` is the desktop-application menu pattern (File → Edit → View), driven by arrow keys with a very specific interaction contract. A sidebar of page links is a _list of links you Tab through_, wrapped in `<nav aria-label="Sidebar">`. Slapping `role="menu"` on your sidebar is a classic amateur tell — it breaks the expected keyboard model and lies to screen readers about what the thing is.

Rule of thumb: **if it persists beside your content and holds the app's primary destinations, it's a side menu; if it slides over and must be dismissed, it's a drawer; if it's a horizontal bar or a File-menu, it's navigation/menubar.**
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The sidebar is the single most-looked-at chrome in an application. Users glance at it dozens of times a session to answer one question: _"where am I, and where can I go?"_ That gives it outsized weight:

*   **Orientation is trust.** A sidebar that clearly highlights the current section makes the app feel navigable and safe. One that doesn't — no active state, ambiguous nesting — makes users feel lost, and lost users churn. This is the "where am I" half of every app's perceived quality.
*   **It's the information architecture, made visible.** Your sidebar _is_ your IA. If the grouping is wrong, no amount of styling saves it. Getting the sections, order, and nesting right is a product decision that lives in this component.
*   **It's an accessibility make-or-break.** Screen-reader users navigate by landmarks; a labeled `<nav>` is how they jump straight to your navigation. Keyboard users Tab through it constantly. Collapse it to icon-only without accessible names and you've locked those users out of your whole app. The sidebar is where accessibility is won or quietly lost.
*   **Density vs. focus is a real tradeoff with money attached.** A collapsible rail lets power users reclaim horizontal space for the work (spreadsheets, canvases, code) while keeping novices oriented with labels. Ship the collapse wrong and you frustrate both.

## 3\. Anatomy of a Side Menu
Every well-built sidebar is composed of predictable parts:

*   **The** **`<nav>`** **landmark:** `<nav aria-label="Sidebar">` (or "Main"). The outer region that assistive tech treats as a navigation landmark. The label is what disambiguates it from other navs on the page.
*   **Brand / header slot:** logo, app switcher, or workspace picker at the top. Often doubles as the "home" link.
*   **Nav sections / groups:** labeled clusters of related links (e.g. "Workspace," "Admin"). A group header may be a plain label or a collapsible disclosure trigger.
*   **Nav items (links):** the individual destinations. Each is an `<a href>` with an **icon + label**, sometimes a badge/count, sometimes a trailing disclosure chevron for nested children.
*   **Nested item lists:** child links revealed under a parent via a disclosure. Level 2, occasionally level 3 — deeper than that and your IA is broken.
*   **Active indicator:** the visual + semantic marker of the current route (a filled background, a left accent bar, `aria-current="page"`).
*   **Collapse / expand toggle:** the control that switches the rail between labeled (expanded) and icon-only (collapsed). Carries `aria-expanded` and controls the rail.
*   **Tooltips (collapsed only):** when the rail is icon-only, each item needs a tooltip (and an accessible name) so the icon isn't a mystery.
*   **Resize handle (optional):** the draggable divider on the sidebar's inner edge (a split-pane separator — see the **Divider** doc) that lets users set the width.
*   **Pinned / favorites region:** user-curated shortcuts, usually pinned to the top.
*   **Footer slot:** user avatar/account menu, settings, help, collapse toggle — the sticky bottom cluster.
*   **Scroll region:** the middle of the sidebar scrolls independently when the nav list is long; header and footer stay pinned.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes, Density & Widths
A sidebar has two dimensions that matter: its **width** (which changes between collapsed/expanded and is often user-resizable) and its **item density** (row height, icon size, indentation per level).

| Token | Expanded width | Collapsed width | Item height | Icon | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact | 200px | 56px | 32px | 16px | Dense admin/data tools |
| Default | 256px | 64px | 40px | 20px | Most apps (16rem is the de-facto standard) |
| Comfortable | 288px | 72px | 44px | 24px | Marketing-heavy, docs, consumer |
| Wide | 320px+ | 72px | 48px | 24px | Deep hierarchies, long labels |

Rules that matter:
*   **Collapsed width is driven by the touch target, not the icon.** The icon may be 20px but the row must stay ≥44px tall and the collapsed rail wide enough to keep a ≥44px hit area. Icon-only never means tiny-target.
*   **256px (16rem) is the industry default** expanded width — it's what Tailwind's `w-64`, most dashboards, and shadcn's sidebar land on. Start there unless you have a reason.
*   **Indentation per nesting level is a token** (`--sidebar-indent: 16px`), not a magic number, so level 2 and level 3 stay visually consistent.
*   **Clamp resizable widths.** Let users drag, but `min` and `max` the width (e.g. 200–400px) so nobody drags it to 12px or eats the whole screen.
*   **Height should be driven by a spacing token** so the whole rail scales as one system.
* * *

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A sidebar is a _state machine_, and its states multiply across two layers: the **rail** has states, and each **item** has states. Missing states are the #1 tell of an amateur build.

**Rail-level states:**
1. **Expanded** — labels + icons visible. The default on desktop.
2. **Collapsed** — icon-only rail. Labels hidden; tooltips + accessible names carry meaning. `aria-expanded="false"` on the toggle.
3. **Hover-expand (fly-out)** — a collapsed rail that temporarily expands on hover/focus, or pops a fly-out submenu. Optional, powerful, easy to get wrong for keyboard users.
4. **Off-canvas (mobile)** — the rail has become a drawer: hidden by default, slid over content on toggle, with a scrim and focus trap.
5. **Resizing** — the user is dragging the handle; width is changing live, handle shows a col-resize cursor.

**Item-level states:**
1. **Default (rest)** — the baseline link.
2. **Hover** — background tint shift. (Doesn't exist on touch; never rely on it alone.)
3. **Focus** — keyboard-reachable, visible `:focus-visible` ring. Non-negotiable.
4. **Active / current** — _this is the route you're on_. Distinct background/accent **and** `aria-current="page"`. The most important state in the whole component.
5. **Active-parent** — a collapsed parent whose child is the current route: show a subtle "contains current" indicator so the user isn't stranded.
6. **Expanded / collapsed (per section)** — a disclosure group that's open vs. closed, carrying `aria-expanded` on the trigger.
7. **Disabled** — a destination not currently available (e.g. gated by plan/permission). Reduced opacity, `aria-disabled`, ideally a reason on hover.
8. **Loading / skeleton** — the sidebar's items streaming in (common in RSC apps): show skeleton rows, keep widths stable.
9. **Badged** — an item carrying a count/notification dot (e.g. "Inbox 3"). The number must be in the accessible name, not color alone.

The pair people miss: **active vs. active-parent when collapsed.** When the rail is icon-only and you're three levels deep, the _only_ way the user knows where they are is the top-level icon's active-parent indicator. Skip it and collapsed mode becomes a guessing game.
* * *

## 6\. Types & Variants
*   **Persistent rail (standard):** always-visible, pushes content. The default desktop pattern.
*   **Collapsible rail (icon ↔ label):** toggles between full and icon-only, staying in the layout the whole time. The workhorse.
*   **Mini + fly-out:** permanently collapsed to icons; hovering/focusing an item flies out a labeled submenu panel. Reclaims maximum space (VS Code activity bar, Slack).
*   **Off-canvas drawer:** hidden until toggled, slides over content. This is what the persistent rail _degrades into_ on narrow screens (see §9.4). Its own sibling doc.
*   **Dual sidebar (rail + panel):** a thin icon rail _plus_ a wider contextual panel next to it (VS Code, Discord servers + channels, Slack workspaces + channels). Two nested sidebars.
*   **Resizable / split-pane sidebar:** user drags a handle to set the width; state persists. Pairs with the **Divider** pattern.
*   **Multi-level / nested sidebar:** sections expand to reveal child routes via disclosures. Depth 2 is comfortable; depth 3 is the practical ceiling.
*   **Right-hand sidebar / inspector:** same mechanics, opposite edge — properties panels, details, contextual metadata. (Distinct from primary nav; often not a `<nav>` at all but a `<complementary>`/`<aside>`.)
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a persistent side menu when your app has **5–15+ primary destinations that users move between repeatedly** and the layout is wide enough to spare ~256px. It's the right call for dashboards, admin panels, docs, IDEs, and any "workspace" app.

Reach for something else when:
*   **You have ≤5 destinations and a content site, not an app.** A horizontal top nav (see **Navigation**) is lighter and more familiar. Don't put a rail on a landing page.
*   **The screen is narrow (mobile/tablet portrait).** A persistent rail eats too much width — transform it into a drawer (bottom tab bar is often even better for the top 3–5 destinations).
*   **The nav is genuinely flat and shallow.** A rail with three items and no nesting is over-engineering.
*   **The destinations are contextual, not global.** Per-page tabs or a segmented control belong in the content area, not the app-wide rail.

Placement heuristics:
*   **Left rail for LTR languages** (it's where the eye starts); mirror to the right for RTL.
*   **Most-used destinations near the top;** account/settings/help in the sticky footer.
*   **Keep the toggle and account menu reachable without scrolling** — pin them, don't bury them in a long scroll region.
*   **Preserve collapsed/expanded and width state** across reloads (localStorage or a cookie for SSR) so returning users don't have to re-set it every visit.

## 8\. Sidebars Across Design Systems
Same component, very different philosophies. Knowing these makes you fluent in any codebase.

*   **Material Design (Google):** the "navigation drawer" and "navigation rail." Standard drawer = 256dp, rail = 80dp icon-only. Material 3 formalizes the rail↔drawer↔bottom-bar responsive progression and the active "pill" indicator behind the selected item.
*   **Apple HIG (macOS/iPadOS):** the source-list sidebar — translucent (now _Liquid Glass_ in Tahoe/iOS 26), a real `NavigationSplitView` primitive with automatic collapse, and a system-standard toggle. Sidebars are first-class, with vibrancy and a selection tint.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** the "nav" / hamburger-toggled rail, acrylic material, compact by default, heavy in Office and Azure. Explicit expanded/compact/minimal reveal modes.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** `Layout.Sider` with `collapsible`, `Menu` with `inline` mode, nested `SubMenu`, and a built-in trigger. Dense, deep, built for admin consoles — the archetypal enterprise sidebar.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** no prescribed look; you compose the rail from utilities and usually wrap it in a `Sidebar` component. Freedom + consistency risk.
*   **shadcn/ui (2024+ standard):** ships a full `sidebar` block — `SidebarProvider`, `Sidebar`, `SidebarMenu`, `SidebarMenuButton`, collapsible groups, mobile drawer, rail resize, cookie-persisted state, and keyboard shortcut, all accessible out of the box. The current reference implementation on the web.
*   **Bootstrap:** no dedicated sidebar component — you build it from `nav flex-column`, offcanvas for mobile, and utilities. Honest answer: needs custom CSS, no turnkey class.

### What's trending in 2026

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML (the semantic foundation everyone should master first)
The whole component is _just_ a labeled nav landmark wrapping nested lists of links. Master this and every framework version below is a re-skin.

```plain
<!-- The app shell: sidebar beside content -->
<div class="app-shell">
  <nav class="sidebar" aria-label="Sidebar" id="sidebar">
    <div class="sidebar__header">
      <a href="/" class="sidebar__brand">UJG</a>
      <button type="button" class="sidebar__toggle"
              aria-expanded="true" aria-controls="sidebar"
              aria-label="Collapse sidebar">
        <svg aria-hidden="true"><!-- chevron --></svg>
      </button>
    </div>

    <!-- Scrollable middle -->
    <div class="sidebar__scroll">
      <!-- A flat group -->
      <ul class="sidebar__list">
        <li>
          <a href="/dashboard" class="sidebar__link" aria-current="page">
            <svg aria-hidden="true"><!-- icon --></svg>
            <span class="sidebar__label">Dashboard</span>
          </a>
        </li>
        <li>
          <a href="/inbox" class="sidebar__link">
            <svg aria-hidden="true"><!-- icon --></svg>
            <span class="sidebar__label">Inbox</span>
            <span class="sidebar__badge" aria-hidden="true">3</span>
            <span class="sr-only">, 3 unread</span>
          </a>
        </li>
      </ul>

      <!-- A nested, collapsible section (disclosure pattern) -->
      <div class="sidebar__group">
        <button type="button" class="sidebar__group-trigger"
                aria-expanded="false" aria-controls="grp-admin">
          <svg aria-hidden="true"><!-- icon --></svg>
          <span class="sidebar__label">Admin</span>
          <svg class="chevron" aria-hidden="true"><!-- chevron --></svg>
        </button>
        <ul id="grp-admin" class="sidebar__sublist" hidden>
          <li><a href="/admin/users" class="sidebar__link">Users</a></li>
          <li><a href="/admin/billing" class="sidebar__link">Billing</a></li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="sidebar__footer">
      <a href="/settings" class="sidebar__link">Settings</a>
    </div>
  </nav>

  <main class="app-content"><!-- routed page --></main>
</div>
```

Key HTML rules:
*   **`<nav aria-label="Sidebar">`****\*\*\*\*, always.** The label is what lets a screen-reader user distinguish this nav from the header nav and the footer nav in the landmarks list. Unlabeled navs all read as "navigation" and become a guessing game.
*   **Links are** **`<a href>`****\*\*\*\*, in a** **`<ul>`****/****`<li>`****\*\*\*\*.** Screen readers announce "list, 8 items" — free structure and count. Don't reach for `role="menu"`; this is a list of links, not an application menu.
*   **The collapse toggle is a real** **`<button>`** with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` pointing at the rail. Not a `<div onClick>`.
*   **A collapsible** **_section_** **uses the disclosure pattern:** a `<button aria-expanded aria-controls>` toggling a `hidden` `<ul>`. The chevron is decorative (`aria-hidden`).
*   **Badge numbers go in an accessible name,** not just a colored pill. `aria-hidden` the visual "3", add an `sr-only` ", 3 unread".

### 9.2 CSS (states + the collapse mechanic done right)

```css
.app-shell { display: grid; grid-template-columns: var(--sidebar-w, 256px) 1fr; }

.sidebar {
  --sidebar-w: 256px;
  display: flex;
  flex-direction: column;

  top: 0;

  overflow: hidden;
}

/* The middle scrolls; header + footer stay pinned */
.sidebar__scroll { flex: 1; overflow-y: auto; overscroll-behavior: contain; }

.sidebar__link {
  display: flex;
  align-items: center;

  min-height: 44px;               /* touch target, even when visually 40 */
  padding: 0 12px;

}

.sidebar__link:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid #DCA424;     /* Goldenrod */
  outline-offset: -2px;
}

/* Active route: color is NOT the only signal — there's a left accent bar too */
.sidebar__link[aria-current="page"] {
  background: rgba(95,44,130,.35);            /* Eminence tint */
  box-shadow: inset 3px 0 0 0 #DCA424;        /* accent bar */
  font-weight: 600;
}

.sidebar__link[aria-disabled="true"] { opacity: .5; pointer-events: none; }

/* --- Collapsed (icon-only) state --- */
.sidebar[data-collapsed="true"] { --sidebar-w: 64px; }
.sidebar[data-collapsed="true"] .sidebar__label,
.sidebar[data-collapsed="true"] .sidebar__badge,
.sidebar[data-collapsed="true"] .chevron { display: none; }
.sidebar[data-collapsed="true"] .sidebar__link { justify-content: center; padding: 0; }

/* Disclosure chevron rotates when open */
.sidebar__group-trigger[aria-expanded="true"] .chevron { transform: rotate(90deg); }

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .sidebar, .chevron { transition: none; }
}
```

The load-bearing details: the **left accent bar via** **`box-shadow: inset`** means the active state survives forced-colors mode and never relies on background color alone. `min-height: 44px` keeps the target honest. `overscroll-behavior: contain` on the scroll region stops the sidebar's scroll from bleeding into the page (scroll chaining). And the collapse is a _width token swap_ plus hiding labels — the DOM never changes, so focus and reading order are preserved.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (a proper reusable, collapsible, nested Sidebar)
The longest block, because this is where the real component lives. A `SidebarProvider` owns collapsed state (persisted), `SidebarLink` handles active + collapsed + tooltip, and `SidebarSection` is the disclosure.

```typescript
import {
  createContext, useContext, useState, useCallback, useEffect,
  type ReactNode,
} from "react";

/* ---- context: one source of truth for collapsed state ---- */
type SidebarCtx = { collapsed: boolean; toggle: () => void };
const SidebarContext = createContext<SidebarCtx | null>(null);
const useSidebar = () => {
  const ctx = useContext(SidebarContext);
  if (!ctx) throw new Error("useSidebar must be used inside <SidebarProvider>");
  return ctx;
};

export function SidebarProvider({ children }: { children: ReactNode }) {
  // hydrate from storage so the choice survives reloads
  const [collapsed, setCollapsed] = useState(false);
  useEffect(() => {
    setCollapsed(localStorage.getItem("sidebar:collapsed") === "1");
  }, []);
  const toggle = useCallback(() => {
    setCollapsed((c) => {
      const next = !c;
      localStorage.setItem("sidebar:collapsed", next ? "1" : "0");
      return next;
    });
  }, []);
  return (
    <SidebarContext.Provider value={{ collapsed, toggle }}>
      {children}
    </SidebarContext.Provider>
  );
}

/* ---- the rail ---- */
export function Sidebar({ children }: { children: ReactNode }) {
  const { collapsed, toggle } = useSidebar();
  return (
    <nav
      className="sidebar"
      id="sidebar"
      aria-label="Sidebar"
      data-collapsed={collapsed}
      style={{ width: collapsed ? 64 : 256 }}
    >
      <div className="sidebar__header">
        <a href="/" className="sidebar__brand">UJG</a>
        <button
          type="button"
          className="sidebar__toggle"
          aria-expanded={!collapsed}
          aria-controls="sidebar"
          aria-label={collapsed ? "Expand sidebar" : "Collapse sidebar"}
          onClick={toggle}
        >
          <ChevronIcon direction={collapsed ? "right" : "left"} />
        </button>
      </div>
      <div className="sidebar__scroll">{children}</div>
    </nav>
  );
}

/* ---- a single nav link: active + collapsed + tooltip ---- */
type LinkProps = {
  href: string;
  icon: ReactNode;
  label: string;
  badge?: number;
  active?: boolean;            // supplied by the router in §9.5
  disabled?: boolean;
};
export function SidebarLink({ href, icon, label, badge, active, disabled }: LinkProps) {
  const { collapsed } = useSidebar();
  return (
    <li>
      <a
        href={href}
        className="sidebar__link"
        aria-current={active ? "page" : undefined}
        aria-disabled={disabled || undefined}
        // when collapsed the visible label is gone, so the icon needs a name…
        aria-label={collapsed ? label : undefined}
        // …and a tooltip for sighted mouse users
        title={collapsed ? label : undefined}
      >
        <span aria-hidden="true">{icon}</span>
        {!collapsed && <span className="sidebar__label">{label}</span>}
        {!collapsed && badge != null && (
          <>
            <span className="sidebar__badge" aria-hidden="true">{badge}</span>
            <span className="sr-only">, {badge} new</span>
          </>
        )}
      </a>
    </li>
  );
}

/* ---- a collapsible section (disclosure) ---- */
export function SidebarSection({
  label, icon, defaultOpen = false, containsActive = false, children,
}: {
  label: string; icon: ReactNode; defaultOpen?: boolean;
  containsActive?: boolean; children: ReactNode;
}) {
  const { collapsed } = useSidebar();
  const [open, setOpen] = useState(defaultOpen || containsActive);
  const id = `sec-${label.replace(/\s+/g, "-").toLowerCase()}`;

  // when the rail is collapsed, a section trigger becomes an icon that
  // signals "contains current route" instead of expanding inline
  return (
    <div className="sidebar__group" data-contains-active={containsActive || undefined}>
      <button
        type="button"
        className="sidebar__group-trigger"
        aria-expanded={collapsed ? undefined : open}
        aria-controls={collapsed ? undefined : id}
        aria-label={collapsed ? label : undefined}
        title={collapsed ? label : undefined}
        onClick={() => !collapsed && setOpen((o) => !o)}
      >
        <span aria-hidden="true">{icon}</span>
        {!collapsed && <span className="sidebar__label">{label}</span>}
        {!collapsed && <ChevronIcon className="chevron" direction="right" />}
      </button>
      {!collapsed && (
        <ul id={id} className="sidebar__sublist" hidden={!open}>
          {children}
        </ul>
      )}
    </div>
  );
}

function ChevronIcon({ direction, className }: { direction: "left" | "right"; className?: string }) {
  return <svg className={className} aria-hidden="true" data-dir={direction}>{/* path */}</svg>;
}
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:
*   **State lives in a provider, hydrated from storage** — collapse is a whole-app concern, and it survives reloads without a flash of the wrong width (see §9.4 for the SSR-cookie upgrade).
*   **`aria-current="page"`** **is driven by the router,** not hardcoded, so it's always truthful (§9.5).
*   **Collapsed mode moves the label into** **`aria-label`** **+** **`title`** so the icon keeps an accessible name _and_ a visual tooltip — the single most-skipped detail in collapsible rails.
*   **The disclosure is a real** **`<button aria-expanded aria-controls>`** **toggling a** **`hidden`** **list** — no `role="menu"`, no div-with-onclick. When collapsed, the disclosure semantics are dropped (there's nothing to expand inline) and replaced with a "contains active" data hook.
*   **Everything degrades:** remove the JS and you still have a labeled nav full of working links.

### 9.4 The Responsive Transform (persistent rail → off-canvas drawer)
The defining behavior of a real sidebar: on desktop it's a persistent column; below a breakpoint it becomes a **drawer** — hidden, slid over content, with a scrim and focus trap. Same links, different container. This is the hand-off to the **Drawer Navigation** doc; here's the mechanic.

```css
/* Desktop: persistent, in the layout grid */
@media (min-width: 1024px) {
  .app-shell { display: grid; grid-template-columns: var(--sidebar-w) 1fr; }
  .sidebar { position: sticky; transform: none; }
  .sidebar__scrim, .sidebar__mobile-toggle { display: none; }
}

/* Mobile: off-canvas drawer */
@media (max-width: 1023px) {
  .app-shell { display: block; }
  .sidebar {
    position: fixed; inset-block: 0; inset-inline-start: 0;
    z-index: 50; width: 288px;
    transform: translateX(-100%);          /* hidden off-canvas */
    transition: transform .25s ease;
  }
  .sidebar[data-open="true"] { transform: translateX(0); }
  .sidebar__scrim {
    position: fixed; inset: 0; z-index: 40;
    background: rgba(10,10,10,.5);
  }
}
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { .sidebar { transition: none; } }
```

```typescript
// The mobile drawer adds three things a persistent rail doesn't need:
// 1) a scrim that closes on click, 2) Escape-to-close, 3) a focus trap.
function useMobileDrawer(open: boolean, onClose: () => void) {
  useEffect(() => {
    if (!open) return;
    const onKey = (e: KeyboardEvent) => e.key === "Escape" && onClose();
    document.addEventListener("keydown", onKey);
    document.body.style.overflow = "hidden";      // lock background scroll
    return () => {
      document.removeEventListener("keydown", onKey);
      document.body.style.overflow = "";
    };
  }, [open, onClose]);
}
// Full focus-trap + return-focus-to-trigger lives in the Drawer doc.
```

The senior distinction: **the same component renders both**, switched by a media query and a small amount of JS that _only activates_ the drawer affordances (scrim, Escape, focus trap, scroll lock) below the breakpoint. On desktop, none of that runs — a persistent sidebar must **not** trap focus or lock scroll; doing so is a common bug when teams reuse a Drawer component for their desktop rail. Match the container to the context.

### 9.5 Next.js (App Router: active links, server layout, client interactivity)
Next forces the useful split: the sidebar _shell_ can be a Server Component in the root layout (rendered once, no JS), but active-link highlighting and the collapse toggle need the client.

```typescript
// app/(app)/layout.tsx  — Server Component: the shell renders on the server
import { Sidebar } from "@/components/sidebar";
import { cookies } from "next/headers";

export default async function AppLayout({ children }: { children: React.ReactNode }) {
  // read collapse state from a cookie so SSR renders the correct width — no flash
  const collapsed = (await cookies()).get("sidebar")?.value === "collapsed";
  return (
    <div className="app-shell">
      <Sidebar defaultCollapsed={collapsed} />
      <main className="app-content">{children}</main>
    </div>
  );
}
```

```typescript
// components/sidebar-link.tsx  — Client Component: needs the current pathname
"use client";
import Link from "next/link";
import { usePathname } from "next/navigation";

export function NavLink({ href, children }: { href: string; children: React.ReactNode }) {
  const pathname = usePathname();
  // exact match for index routes, prefix match for section roots
  const active = href === "/" ? pathname === "/" : pathname.startsWith(href);
  return (
    <Link
      href={href}
      className="sidebar__link"
      aria-current={active ? "page" : undefined}
    >
      {children}
    </Link>
  );
}
```

Two things juniors get wrong here:
*   **Active detection.** `pathname === href` misses child routes (`/settings/billing` won't light up "Settings"); `pathname.startsWith(href)` lights up _everything_ under `/`. The fix is the exact-for-index, prefix-for-sections rule above — and for sibling routes that share a prefix, match on segment boundaries (`pathname === href || pathname.startsWith(href + "/")`).
*   **Reading collapse from a cookie, not localStorage,** for the initial render. localStorage isn't available during SSR, so a localStorage-only sidebar flashes the wrong width on every hard load. Cookie → server render → correct width immediately, then client takes over.

### 9.6 Vanilla JavaScript (collapse, disclosure, persistence — no framework)
The building block everything abstracts. Worth knowing cold.

```javascript
const sidebar = document.getElementById("sidebar");
const toggle = sidebar.querySelector(".sidebar__toggle");

// restore persisted state
if (localStorage.getItem("sidebar:collapsed") === "1") {
  sidebar.dataset.collapsed = "true";
  toggle.setAttribute("aria-expanded", "false");
}

toggle.addEventListener("click", () => {
  const collapsed = sidebar.dataset.collapsed === "true";
  sidebar.dataset.collapsed = String(!collapsed);
  toggle.setAttribute("aria-expanded", String(collapsed));   // inverse
  localStorage.setItem("sidebar:collapsed", collapsed ? "0" : "1");
});

// one delegated handler for every disclosure group (works for added items too)
sidebar.addEventListener("click", (e) => {
  const trigger = e.target.closest(".sidebar__group-trigger");
  if (!trigger) return;
  const list = document.getElementById(trigger.getAttribute("aria-controls"));
  const open = trigger.getAttribute("aria-expanded") === "true";
  trigger.setAttribute("aria-expanded", String(!open));
  list.hidden = open;
});
```

Note the **event delegation** — one listener on the rail handles every group, and keeps working for dynamically-added sections. And `aria-expanded` is toggled in lockstep with the visual state; the ARIA is never allowed to drift from what's on screen.

### 9.9 Resizable Sidebar (split-pane / drag handle)
Power-user feature: a draggable handle on the sidebar's inner edge. This is a split-pane separator — see the **Divider** doc for the full separator semantics; here's the sidebar-specific wiring, including the _keyboard_ resize most builds forget.

```typescript
"use client";
import { useCallback, useRef, useState } from "react";

const MIN = 200, MAX = 400;

export function ResizableSidebar({ children }: { children: React.ReactNode }) {
  const [width, setWidth] = useState(256);
  const dragging = useRef(false);

  const onPointerDown = (e: React.PointerEvent) => {
    dragging.current = true;
    (e.target as HTMLElement).setPointerCapture(e.pointerId);
  };
  const onPointerMove = useCallback((e: React.PointerEvent) => {
    if (!dragging.current) return;
    setWidth(Math.min(MAX, Math.max(MIN, e.clientX)));   // clamp
  }, []);
  const onPointerUp = (e: React.PointerEvent) => {
    dragging.current = false;
    (e.target as HTMLElement).releasePointerCapture(e.pointerId);
    document.cookie = `sidebar-w=${width}; path=/; max-age=31536000`;
  };
  // keyboard: the handle IS a separator, arrow keys nudge it
  const onKeyDown = (e: React.KeyboardEvent) => {
    if (e.key === "ArrowLeft")  setWidth((w) => Math.max(MIN, w - 16));
    if (e.key === "ArrowRight") setWidth((w) => Math.min(MAX, w + 16));
  };

  return (
    <div style={{ display: "grid", gridTemplateColumns: `${width}px 1fr` }}>
      <nav className="sidebar" aria-label="Sidebar" style={{ width }}>{children}</nav>
      <div
        role="separator"
        aria-orientation="vertical"
        aria-label="Resize sidebar"
        aria-valuenow={width} aria-valuemin={MIN} aria-valuemax={MAX}
        tabIndex={0}
        className="sidebar__resize-handle"
        onPointerDown={onPointerDown}
        onPointerMove={onPointerMove}
        onPointerUp={onPointerUp}
        onKeyDown={onKeyDown}
      />
    </div>
  );
}
```

The details that separate a real resize from a broken one: **`role="separator"`** **+** **`aria-valuenow/min/max`** so screen readers announce it as an adjustable divider; **`tabIndex={0}`** **\+ arrow-key handling** so it's operable without a mouse (drag-only handles are an accessibility failure); **clamping** to a sane min/max; **pointer capture** so the drag keeps working when the cursor outruns the handle; and **persisting width to a cookie** so SSR can restore it without a flash.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
The sidebar is a landmark-and-links pattern; get the semantics right and most of a11y is free.

*   **Landmark + label:** `<nav aria-label="Sidebar">` (or `aria-labelledby` a visible heading). The label is mandatory when there's more than one nav on the page — which there almost always is (header, sidebar, footer).
*   **Links, not menuitems:** a sidebar of page destinations is a **list of links**, navigated by Tab. Do **not** use `role="menu"`/`menuitem` (that's the File-menu keyboard model with arrow-key navigation and different expectations). Wrap items in `<ul>`/`<li>` so count and structure are announced.
*   **Current route:** the active item gets **`aria-current="page"`**. This is the single most important a11y attribute in the component — it's how a screen-reader user knows where they are. Never signal "current" with color alone; pair it with the accent bar / weight (see §9.2) so it survives color-blindness and forced-colors mode.
*   **Collapsed = still named:** when the rail is icon-only, every item **must** keep an accessible name via `aria-label` (and a `title` tooltip for sighted users). An icon with no name is a dead end for screen readers. This is the #1 collapsed-sidebar a11y failure.
*   **Disclosure sections:** the section trigger is a `<button>` with **`aria-expanded`** and **`aria-controls`** pointing at the sublist. State and target are both announced.
*   **Collapse toggle:** a `<button>` with `aria-expanded` reflecting whether the rail is open, `aria-controls` the rail, and a name that changes ("Expand sidebar" / "Collapse sidebar").
*   **Keyboard model:** Tab moves through the links in DOM order; Enter follows a link; Enter/Space toggles a disclosure or the collapse button. A resize handle is a focusable `role="separator"` nudged with arrow keys. Optionally add roving-`tabindex` + arrow-key movement _within_ the rail for power users, but Tab-through is the baseline and must always work.
*   **Focus management on the mobile drawer:** when it opens, move focus into it and **trap** focus until it closes; on close, **return focus to the trigger**. (Full pattern in the **Drawer Navigation** doc.) A _persistent_ desktop rail does the opposite — it must **not** trap focus.
*   **Contrast:** text vs. sidebar background ≥ 4.5:1; the active indicator's non-text signal (accent bar) ≥ 3:1. Dark sidebars (the common case) need their muted labels checked — "subtle gray on dark green" is a frequent AA failure.
*   **Target size:** every item ≥ 44px tall, and the collapsed rail wide enough to keep a 44px hit area. Small icons, big targets.
*   **Reduced motion:** the collapse-width transition and drawer-slide respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The sidebar looks solved but keeps evolving:
*   **Command palette as co-navigation.** ⌘K to jump anywhere by typing, with the sidebar as the browsable, discoverable counterpart. The rail teaches the IA; the palette is the shortcut.
*   **Liquid Glass source lists.** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe sidebar is _the_ showcase surface for the refractive material — the rail bends and tints the content scrolling behind it.

*   **Contextual dual rails.** A thin global icon rail plus a route-aware secondary panel (VS Code, Linear, Slack) that swaps contents based on the active top-level section.
*   **Density & theme controls in-rail.** User-chosen compact/comfortable row height and light/dark, remembered per user.

## 12\. UX & Conversion Killers (mistakes that bleed trust silently)
None of these throw an error. They just quietly make the app feel broken or leave users lost.
*   **No active-route indicator.** The cardinal sin. Users can't tell where they are, so every page feels disorienting. `aria-current` + a visible marker, always.
*   **Collapsed icons with no tooltips or names.** A rail of mystery-meat glyphs. Sighted users guess; screen-reader users are locked out. Every collapsed item needs a tooltip _and_ an accessible name.
*   **Active state signaled by color alone.** Fails color-blind users and forced-colors mode. Pair color with an accent bar, weight, or icon fill.
*   **Reusing the Drawer component for the desktop rail.** Drags a focus trap and scroll-lock onto a persistent sidebar, so keyboard users get stuck in it and the page can't scroll. Persistent rail ≠ drawer.
*   **`role="menu"`** **on a list of page links.** Lies to assistive tech and imposes an arrow-key model users don't expect. It's a list of links.
*   **Over-deep nesting.** Level 4+ disclosures mean your IA is broken; users can't hold the tree in their head. Cap at 2, occasionally 3.
*   **Losing state on reload.** The user collapses the rail or resizes it, reloads, and it's back to default. Persist collapse + width (cookie for SSR).
*   **No independent scroll region.** A long nav that scrolls the whole page (or hides the account/settings footer below the fold) instead of scrolling just the middle. Pin header + footer, scroll the middle.
*   **Flash of wrong width on load.** localStorage-only collapse state renders expanded then snaps to collapsed. Read from a cookie at SSR.
*   **A rail on a marketing site with 4 links.** Over-engineering; a top nav is lighter and more expected.

**The through-line:** these fail _quietly_. No crash, just a slightly-lost user and an app that feels less trustworthy than it is. Audit the sidebar before you blame "the onboarding."
* * *

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
Techniques that lean on the platform and the type system instead of custom JavaScript.

**Config-driven nav (one source of truth).** Don't hand-write `<li>`s. Drive the whole rail from a typed config so ordering, permissions, and active-matching live in one place:

```typescript
type NavItem = {
  href: string; label: string; icon: IconName;
  children?: NavItem[];
  requires?: Permission;          // gate by role; filtered before render
};
const NAV: NavItem[] = [
  { href: "/dashboard", label: "Dashboard", icon: "grid" },
  { href: "/admin", label: "Admin", icon: "shield", requires: "admin",
    children: [{ href: "/admin/users", label: "Users", icon: "users" }] },
];
// render = NAV.filter(canSee).map(renderItem) — auth, order, and nesting in one tree.
```

**Design tokens for the whole rail.** Three-tier tokens (primitive → semantic → component) so a rebrand or dark-mode is a token swap, not a sidebar rewrite:

```css
:root {
  --purple-800: oklch(42% 0.145 305);            /* primitive */
  --gold-500:   oklch(78% 0.135 82);
  --sidebar-bg:        var(--dark-green, #042D1D); /* component */
  --sidebar-active-bg: color-mix(in oklch, var(--purple-800) 35%, transparent);
  --sidebar-accent:    var(--gold-500);
  --sidebar-w:         16rem;
  --sidebar-w-collapsed: 4rem;
  --sidebar-indent:    1rem;
}
```

**TypeScript-enforced accessible names.** A discriminated union that _requires_ an `aria-label` on any icon-only item, catching a whole class of collapsed-rail a11y bugs at compile time:

```typescript
type LabeledItem = { icon: ReactNode; label: string };                    // has visible text
type IconOnlyItem = { icon: ReactNode; iconOnly: true; "aria-label": string }; // name required
type SidebarItem = LabeledItem | IconOnlyItem;
// <SidebarLink iconOnly icon={<Gear/>} />  ← won't compile without aria-label
```

**Scroll-spy active state for docs sidebars.** For a table-of-contents rail on a single long page, drive `aria-current` off an `IntersectionObserver` watching the headings, so the active item tracks the scroll position, not the URL.

**`<details>`****/****`<summary>`** **as a zero-JS disclosure.** For a purely static nested section, native `<details><summary>` gives you the expand/collapse with `aria-expanded` semantics and keyboard support for free — no state, no JS. Upgrade to the controlled version only when you need to sync open-state across sections.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A sidebar renders on every route and lives for the whole session, so its costs compound.

*   **The icon-import trap.** The #1 sidebar perf mistake: `import * as Icons from "lucide-react"` to grab twelve nav glyphs pulls the _entire_ library. Use named imports (`import { Home, Inbox } from "lucide-react"`) so the bundler tree-shakes the rest — hundreds of KB saved.
*   **Don't re-render the rail on every navigation.** In Next.js App Router, put the sidebar in the _layout_ so it persists across route changes and doesn't unmount/remount. Only the active-link highlighting (a small client component) needs to react to the pathname — memoize the rest.
*   **Animate** **`transform`****/****`width`** **carefully.** The collapse transition on `width` triggers layout; it's acceptable for a one-off user action but never animate width on scroll or hover-spam. For the drawer slide, animate `transform: translateX` (compositor-only, no reflow).
*   **CSS containment** on the rail so its internal scroll and repaint don't invalidate the page layout:

```css
.sidebar { contain: layout style; }
```

*   **Virtualize enormous nav lists.** A file-tree or hundreds-of-items sidebar (IDEs, big org charts) should virtualize the scroll region so only visible rows are in the DOM.
*   **Event delegation over per-item listeners.** One delegated handler on the rail (§9.6) instead of N listeners on N links — real memory and setup-time savings on large nav trees.
*   **Persist state cheaply.** A single cookie/localStorage write on toggle, not a write on every render.

## 15\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a sidebar is "done":
- [ ] Wrapped in `<nav aria-label="Sidebar">` (labeled; distinct from other navs).
- [ ] Items are `<a href>` inside `<ul>`/`<li>` — **not** `role="menu"`.
- [ ] Current route carries `aria-current="page"` **and** a non-color indicator.
- [ ] Active-parent shown when the rail is collapsed and a child is current.
- [ ] Collapsed icon-only items keep an accessible name (`aria-label`) + tooltip (`title`).
- [ ] Nested sections use `<button aria-expanded aria-controls>` disclosures; depth ≤ 3.
- [ ] Collapse toggle is a `<button>` with `aria-expanded` + name that changes.
- [ ] Persistent on desktop; transforms into an off-canvas drawer (scrim, Escape, focus trap, scroll lock) on mobile — and does **not** trap focus when persistent.
- [ ] Collapse + width state persist across reloads (cookie for SSR; no width flash).
- [ ] Independent scroll region in the middle; header + footer pinned.
- [ ] Resize handle (if any) is a focusable `role="separator"` with arrow-key support and clamped min/max.
- [ ] Every item ≥ 44px target; text ≥ 4.5:1 contrast; reduced-motion respected.
- [ ] Icons named-imported (tree-shakeable); rail lives in the layout, not re-mounted per route.

## 16\. Security & Authorization (the rail is not a permission boundary)
A sidebar frequently gates access — hiding "Admin" from non-admins. The trap: **hiding a nav item is UX, never security.**

*   **Filter the nav by permission for UX, re-check on the server for real.** Removing "Admin" from the rail is a convenience so users don't see dead ends. The `/admin` route and its API must _independently_ re-check authorization on every request — the user can type the URL, deep-link, or hit the endpoint with `curl` regardless of what the rail showed.
*   **Never ship secret destinations in the client bundle.** If a nav config with admin-only hrefs is shipped to every client, you've leaked the existence and shape of privileged routes. Filter server-side before the config reaches the browser, or accept that route _existence_ is public and lock down the routes themselves.
*   **Deep links must resolve to auth, not a blank rail.** A user landing on `/admin/billing` without permission should hit a real 403/redirect from the route, not just find the item missing from their sidebar.
*   **Badge/count data is still data.** An "Inbox 3" count fetched for the rail goes through the same authorization as any API read — don't leak counts across tenants because the number lives in chrome.

The one-liner: **the sidebar reflects permissions; it doesn't enforce them.** Enforcement is always at the route and the endpoint.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same rail, eleven skins)
Style is skin; the `<nav>` landmark, keyboard model, `aria-current`, focus ring, and 44px targets never change. See the [Design Styles gallery](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) for the live set.

*   **Flat:** solid dark fill, crisp edges, a simple filled pill or left accent bar for active, no shadow. Reads instantly, scales fastest. The safe default for dense tools.
*   **Material:** the nav-rail/drawer with an elevation shadow on the raised drawer, a **pill-shaped active indicator** behind the selected item, and a ripple on tap. Uppercase group labels.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent rail with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over a vivid page behind it. Looks premium; guard label contrast hard — muted text on a busy blurred backdrop fails AA fast.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's refractive source list — the rail _bends_ the content scrolling behind it, with a specular rim and a sheen that shifts with motion. Native `.glassEffect()` in SwiftUI; a frosted, performance-aware approximation on the web. The sidebar is this style's showcase surface.
*   **Neumorphism:** the rail and items share one surface color, separated only by soft dual light/dark shadows; active item looks pressed-in. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent use only, never for a primary app rail.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** a "real panel" look — inset bevel, subtle leather/metal texture, gradient item fills, a physical selected groove. Heavy; reserve for themed products.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black borders, a hard offset shadow on the rail, zero radius, a clashing solid block for the active item. Maximum personality, deliberately raw.
*   **Claymorphism:** big radius, puffy items with inner top-light + bottom-shadow, soft glow on the active pill. Friendly, playful consumer apps.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** an animated multi-hue gradient on a dark rail, active item glowing brighter. Premium and moody; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (kill the animation) and watch label contrast over the shifting field.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-zero decoration — type, generous space, and a hairline rule do the work; active state is weight + a thin accent, nothing more. Great for docs and editorial tools.
*   **UJG Brand:** the Afro-Futurist house rail — Platinum labels on a deep **Dark Green / Eminence** surface, **Goldenrod** left-accent bar + tint for the active route, confident 8px radius, a warm glow on hover, and a Liquid-Glass sheen on the active item over the saturated backdrop. Icon-only collapsed rail keeps Goldenrod tooltips. Contrast and reduced-motion stay non-negotiable.
* * *

**Bottom line:** a side menu looks like a list of links and is actually a small state machine wearing a landmark. Master the four things juniors skip — a labeled `<nav>` of real links (not `role="menu"`), a truthful `aria-current` active state that isn't color-only, collapsed icons that keep their accessible names, and a clean transform into a focus-trapping drawer on mobile without dragging that behavior onto the desktop rail — and you've built the spine of an app that feels navigable, trustworthy, and fast. Everything else is skin.
