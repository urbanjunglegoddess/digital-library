# Progress bar

# The Progress Bar: A Senior Engineer's Complete Breakdown
The component that tells a user "work is happening, and here's how much is left." Small surface, enormous effect on perceived speed and trust. Here's everything from first principles to production code.

**Try it live:** the companion playground will let you tune determinate vs. indeterminate, size, radius, label mode, and every visual style, and output live code for each target with one-click copy. It's built in a later pass, so treat this doc as the source of truth until it lands.

**Audit a progress bar:** a companion audit will paste-check any progress markup for the silent failures — missing `role`/`aria-valuenow`, `aria-live` spam, a fake determinate bar with no real signal behind it, indeterminate animation that ignores `prefers-reduced-motion` — and export a client-ready report.

## 1\. What a Progress Bar Actually Is
A progress bar is a **visual meter of task completion over time**. It answers two questions at once: _is something happening?_ and _how far along is it?_ That second question is the whole game, and it splits the component into two fundamentally different things people constantly conflate:

*   **Determinate:** you know the percentage. Uploading a 40MB file, importing 1,200 rows, a 5-step wizard. The fill maps to real, measurable progress (`value` / `max`). It has an `aria-valuenow`.
*   **Indeterminate:** you know work is happening but _not how much is left_. The animated barber-pole / sliding sheen. Waiting on a server that won't tell you a percentage. `role="progressbar"` with **no** `aria-valuenow` — because lying about a number you don't have is worse than admitting you don't have it.

Then there are two _siblings_ people file under "progress" but shouldn't:

*   **Spinner** (own doc): brief, indeterminate, **space-constrained**. A spinner is what you reach for when the wait is short (< ~1s felt) and you have no room for a bar. It says "working" and nothing more. A progress bar is what you reach for when the wait is long enough that a human needs reassurance _and_ a sense of how much longer.
*   **Skeleton** (own doc): a shaped placeholder of the content that's loading — gray blocks in the layout of the eventual card/list. It communicates "this specific content is arriving" and reduces perceived latency by showing structure. It is not a meter; it has no percentage.

The senior distinction to burn in: **determinate when you have a number, indeterminate when you have a duration but no number, spinner when it's short and cramped, skeleton when you're waiting on content that has a known shape.** Reaching for the wrong one is the #1 tell — a spinner on a 90-second export is cruelty; a determinate bar wired to a fake timer is a lie (see §12).

A note on the word "bar." The _bar_ is the dominant form, but the same semantics wear a **ring** (circular/SVG), a **segmented stepper**, or a thin **top-of-page loading line**. Same `role="progressbar"`, different skin. §9.4 builds the ring.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
Progress is a **perception and trust instrument**, and the stakes are higher than the pixel count suggests.

*   **Perceived speed beats real speed.** A large slice of UX research (going back to Nielsen's response-time thresholds and every "progress bar psychology" study since) says a task _with_ a visible progress indicator feels faster and is abandoned less than the identical task with a blank screen — even when the version with the bar is objectively slower. The bar is buying you patience.
*   **Abandonment lives here.** Uploads, checkouts, imports, exports, onboarding wizards: every one has a drop-off curve, and a missing or frozen progress indicator is a cliff on that curve. "Is this broken or just slow?" is the thought that closes the tab. A moving bar answers it before it's asked.
*   **Trust is fragile and cumulative.** A bar that jumps to 90% and sits there, or races to 100% and _then_ makes you wait, teaches users that your progress bars lie. Once learned, they ignore all of them — including the honest ones. Progress is a promise; break it and you lose the instrument.
*   **Accessibility is non-optional.** A sighted user sees the fill move. A screen-reader user gets _nothing_ unless you wired `role`, `aria-valuenow`, and the right announcements. For long operations, a silent progress bar is an inaccessible operation.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy of a Progress Bar
Every well-built bar decomposes into predictable parts:

*   **Track (rail):** the full-length container, the "100%" background. Muted, low contrast, defines the total.
*   **Indicator (fill / value):** the portion representing completed work. Brand color, animates from old value to new.
*   **Buffer (optional):** a second, lighter fill _ahead_ of the main one — how much is loaded/ready but not yet played/committed. Classic in video (buffered vs. played). See §9.6.
*   **Label / value text:** the human-readable state. "75%", "Step 3 of 5", "12 of 40 files", "Uploading…". This is what feeds `aria-valuetext`.
*   **Track endpoints & radius:** rounded vs. square caps. Cosmetic, but a rounded cap on a 2%-filled bar can make "2%" render as a fat pill that reads like more — a subtle honesty bug.
*   **Announcement region:** for determinate long-running work, a paired `aria-live` region (or the bar's own `aria-valuetext`) that speaks milestones. Invisible, essential.

For a **ring** the parts remap: track = the background circle stroke, indicator = the foreground arc (`stroke-dasharray`), label = centered text. For a **stepper** the parts are discrete segments, each a node with a done/current/upcoming state.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes & Scale
Thickness communicates weight and context. A typical scale for the linear bar:

| Size | Height (track) | Radius | Label | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| XS (hairline) | 2–3px | 0–2px | none | Top-of-page route/loading line, inline in a row |
| SM | 4px | full | optional, beside | Cards, list items, compact dashboards |
| MD | 8px | full | "75%" beside or above | Default: forms, uploads, settings |
| LG | 12–16px | full | inline % or count | Prominent single-task (import, export) |
| Ring | 40–64px ⌀, 4–6px stroke | — | centered % | Dashboards, mobile, "one big task" |

Rules that matter:

*   **Height is a token, not a magic number,** so the whole system scales. `--progress-h-md: 8px`.
*   **Don't let radius lie.** A `border-radius` larger than the current fill width makes tiny values look bigger than they are. Clamp radius or the fill's min-width so 1% doesn't render as 6%.
*   **The label is data, not decoration.** If you show "%", show the _real_ number you'd put in `aria-valuenow`. A visual "84%" next to an `aria-valuenow="60"` is a bug an audit will catch.
*   **Full-width bars** on mobile read as more confident and leave room for the label above rather than cramped beside.
* * *

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A production progress component has far more than "empty" and "full." Missing states are the amateur tell.

1. **Indeterminate (unknown):** work started, no percentage yet. Animated. `role="progressbar"`, no `aria-valuenow`.
2. **Determinate — in progress:** `0 < value < max`. Fill animates smoothly between updates.
3. **Zero / start:** `value = 0`. Track visible, no fill. Still announce "0%" or "Starting…" so it's not mistaken for broken.
4. **Complete / success:** `value = max`. Often a brief hold + color shift to green + a checkmark, then the bar dismisses or morphs into the result.
5. **Error / failed:** the operation died at 43%. The bar should **stop, turn destructive (red), and say why** — never silently freeze at 43% (indistinguishable from "still working"). Offer retry.
6. **Paused / stalled:** upload paused, or no bytes moved for N seconds. Distinct visual (dimmed, "Paused" / "Stalled — retrying…"). A frozen determinate bar with no "stalled" state is a trust killer.
7. **Buffering (media):** played position + buffered-ahead position, both moving independently.
8. **Reduced-motion:** the indeterminate animation must degrade to something non-animating or gently pulsing for `prefers-reduced-motion` users (see §9.2, §5-note).

**Determinate vs. indeterminate is itself a state transition.** Real flows start indeterminate ("connecting…") and _become_ determinate once the server sends a total ("0 of 1,200 rows"). Handle the switch: swap the animation off, add `aria-valuenow`, keep the same node so focus/AT context isn't lost.
* * *

## 6\. Types & Variants
By form and function, not just color:

*   **Linear determinate:** the default meter. `<progress value max>` or a custom div bar.
*   **Linear indeterminate:** the barber-pole / sliding sheen. No value.
*   **Circular / ring determinate:** SVG arc, centered %. Great for dashboards and "one hero task."
*   **Circular indeterminate:** the rotating arc — this is essentially a **spinner** (sibling). If it shows no percentage and just spins, call it what it is.
*   **Segmented / stepped:** discrete chunks ("3 of 5 steps done"). Overlaps heavily with a **Stepper** component — if each segment is a labeled, sometimes-clickable stage in a wizard, that's a stepper (cross-link, own doc). If they're just visual chunks of one continuous task, it's a segmented progress bar.
*   **Top-of-page loading line:** the thin bar (YouTube/GitHub style) at the viewport top for route/navigation loads. Usually indeterminate or a "trickle to 90%, snap to 100% on done" pattern — an _honest_ version of the fake-progress trick (§12).
*   **Buffering (dual-value):** media players — played + buffered.
*   **Skeleton & spinner:** siblings, not variants. Named here only so you stop filing them under "progress bar."
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a progress bar when the wait is **long enough to need reassurance** and you can convey either a percentage or at least "still working." Decision tree:

*   **< ~1s, tiny space** → spinner (or nothing; instant is best).
*   **Content with known shape loading** → skeleton.
*   **You have a real percentage** → determinate bar or ring.
*   **You have a duration but no number** → indeterminate bar.
*   **Multi-stage flow with named steps** → stepper (segmented progress is the lightweight cousin).

Don't use one when:

*   The action is genuinely instant — a progress bar for a 50ms operation is theater and adds perceived latency.
*   You'd have to **fake the number** to show a determinate bar. If you don't know the percentage, be honest: go indeterminate. A fabricated determinate bar is the fastest way to teach users your progress means nothing (§12).
*   It's really _navigation between items_ or _selecting a value_ — a bar that the user drags to set a position is a [**Slider**](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200531) (interactive, `role="slider"`), not a progress bar (read-only, `role="progressbar"`). This is the single most-confused pairing: **progress reports, slider controls.** Same shape, opposite intent, different role, different keyboard model.

Placement heuristics:

*   **Put the bar where the work is** — attached to the file row that's uploading, inside the button that's morphing, at the top of the page for global navigation.
*   **Keep the label near the bar** and readable; don't make users guess whether "80%" refers to this bar or the one above it.
*   **Pair long operations with a Toast** on completion (sibling [Toast](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200531) doc) so users who navigated away still learn it finished.

## 8\. Progress Across Design Systems
Same meter, different philosophies. Fluency here makes you fast in any codebase.

*   **Material Design (Google):** `LinearProgressIndicator` and `CircularProgressIndicator`, each determinate or indeterminate. Material 3 refreshed them with a track gap, rounded caps, and (in "Expressive") a wavy/animated track. Strong opinions on the indeterminate motion timing.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** `ProgressView` — one API that renders linear or circular, determinate (bound to a `value`/`total`) or indeterminate (spinner) depending on how you initialize it. Restrained, thin, tinted.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** `ProgressBar` and `ProgressRing`, with an explicit "indeterminate" boolean and a paused/error state baked in for file-transfer UIs.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** the richest `Progress` API — `line`, `circle`, `dashboard` (gauge) types, `status` prop (`success`/`exception`/`active`), `steps` for segmented, `strokeColor` gradients, and `format` for custom center text. Built for data-dense admin dashboards.
*   **Bootstrap:** `.progress` wrapper + `.progress-bar` child; `striped` and `animated` utility classes; you set width via inline style or CSS var and mirror it into `aria-valuenow`.
*   **Tailwind:** no component — you compose a track div + fill div, or style native `<progress>`. Usually wrapped into one `Progress` component to enforce consistency.
*   **shadcn/Radix:** `@radix-ui/react-progress` gives you accessible `Root`/`Indicator` primitives (role, value clamping, data-state) and you skin with Tailwind. The 2024+ web default for a custom-styled but correct bar.

## 9\. The Code
The biggest section. Real, production-shaped snippets per target, with commentary.

### 9.1 HTML — the native `<progress>` element (start here)
The platform gives you a real element. Most engineers skip it and hand-roll divs; know it first, because it's the most accessible baseline that exists.

```plain
<!-- Determinate: value + max. Browser exposes role/value to AT for free. -->
<label for="upload">Uploading photos</label>
<progress id="upload" value="0.75" max="1">75%</progress>
<!-- the text child ("75%") is fallback for ancient browsers, NOT the a11y value -->

<!-- Count-based determinate -->
<progress value="12" max="40" aria-label="Importing rows"></progress>

<!-- Indeterminate: OMIT the value attribute entirely -->
<progress aria-label="Loading" max="1"></progress>
<!-- no value => browser renders the animated indeterminate state,
     and exposes it as an indeterminate progressbar (no valuenow) -->
```

What the native element buys you: correct `role="progressbar"`, `aria-valuenow`/`valuemin`/`valuemax` derived from `value`/`max`, keyboard-irrelevant (it's not focusable — it's a status, not a control), and free indeterminate rendering when you omit `value`. What it costs you: **styling is genuinely painful** (§9.2), and you can't easily put a buffer track, custom label positioning, or a gradient sheen inside it. That trade — free a11y vs. styling ceiling — is why teams graduate to a custom div bar with the ARIA added back by hand (§9.3).

### 9.2 CSS — styling the native element (the pain) + the reduced-motion rule
Native `<progress>` has no standard interior. You style it through **vendor pseudo-elements**, and they don't agree with each other.

```css
/* 1. Kill the default appearance so pseudo-elements take over */
progress {
  appearance: none;
  -webkit-appearance: none;
  -moz-appearance: none;
  width: 100%;
  height: 8px;
  border: none;
  border-radius: 999px;
  overflow: hidden;
  background: #2a1f33;                 /* the TRACK color (Firefox reads this) */
}

/* 2. WebKit/Blink (Chrome, Safari, Edge): TWO pseudo-elements */
progress::-webkit-progress-bar   { background: #2a1f33; border-radius: 999px; }  /* track */
progress::-webkit-progress-value {                                              /* fill  */
  background: #DCA424;                 /* Goldenrod */
  border-radius: 999px;
  transition: width .2s ease;          /* smooth movement between value updates */
}

/* 3. Firefox: ONE pseudo-element for the fill */
progress::-moz-progress-bar { background: #DCA424; border-radius: 999px; }

/* 4. Indeterminate (no value attr) — style the animated state.
      WebKit exposes the moving bit via ::-webkit-progress-value only when
      determinate, so indeterminate skinning is limited — many teams switch
      to a custom bar (9.3) specifically to control this. */
```

The barber-pole / sliding indeterminate animation on a **custom** bar, done right:

```css
.progress--indeterminate .progress__fill {
  width: 40%;
  border-radius: inherit;
  animation: indeterminate 1.4s ease-in-out infinite;
}
@keyframes indeterminate {
  0%   { transform: translateX(-100%); }
  100% { transform: translateX(350%); }   /* slides across and off */
}

/* NON-NEGOTIABLE: reduced-motion users get no barber-pole.
   Replace the slide with a gentle opacity pulse (or a static "Working…" state). */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .progress--indeterminate .progress__fill {
    animation: none;
    width: 100%;
    opacity: .6;
    animation: pulse 2s ease-in-out infinite;   /* pulse only changes opacity */
  }
  @keyframes pulse { 50% { opacity: .3; } }
}
```

Two rules the pros never break: **animate** **`transform`****/****`opacity`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*, never** **`width`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*, for the indeterminate loop** (width animation reflows every frame — see §14), and **the determinate fill transitions** **`width`** **only between discrete value updates** (a few per second), not continuously, so the reflow cost is trivial and the movement reads as smooth.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript — a proper reusable ProgressBar (determinate + indeterminate)
The custom div bar most teams actually ship. It re-adds the ARIA the native element gave for free, and handles both modes in one component.

```typescript
import { forwardRef, HTMLAttributes } from "react";

type ProgressProps = HTMLAttributes<HTMLDivElement> & {
  /** 0–100 for determinate. Omit / null => indeterminate. */
  value?: number | null;
  max?: number;
  /** Human label for AT + optional visible text: "Step 3 of 5", "75% uploaded" */
  label?: string;
  size?: "sm" | "md" | "lg";
  showValue?: boolean;
};

export const ProgressBar = forwardRef<HTMLDivElement, ProgressProps>(
  ({ value = null, max = 100, label, size = "md", showValue = false, className = "", ...rest }, ref) => {
    const indeterminate = value == null;
    // Clamp so a bad value can't overflow the track or produce a wrong aria number
    const pct = indeterminate ? undefined : Math.max(0, Math.min(100, (value! / max) * 100));

    return (
      <div className={`progress-wrap progress--${size} ${className}`}>
        {(label || showValue) && (
          <div className="progress__meta">
            {label && <span>{label}</span>}
            {showValue && !indeterminate && <span>{Math.round(pct!)}%</span>}
          </div>
        )}
        <div
          ref={ref}
          className={`progress ${indeterminate ? "progress--indeterminate" : ""}`}
          role="progressbar"
          aria-valuemin={indeterminate ? undefined : 0}
          aria-valuemax={indeterminate ? undefined : 100}
          aria-valuenow={indeterminate ? undefined : Math.round(pct!)}
          // Human-readable override of the raw number, for screen readers:
          aria-valuetext={indeterminate ? undefined : label ?? `${Math.round(pct!)}%`}
          aria-label={label || "Progress"}
          {...rest}
        >
          <div
            className="progress__fill"
            style={indeterminate ? undefined : { inlineSize: `${pct}%` }}
          />
        </div>
      </div>
    );
  }
);
ProgressBar.displayName = "ProgressBar";
```

Why this is the senior version:

*   **`value`** **nullable is the mode switch.** `null` => indeterminate, and the ARIA number attributes _disappear_ (not set to 0 — an indeterminate bar must have no `aria-valuenow`). This is the single most-missed detail.
*   **Clamped** **`pct`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*.** A `value` of 140 or −5 from a flaky backend can't render an overflowing or negative bar, and `aria-valuenow` stays truthful.
*   **`aria-valuetext`** **for humans.** Screen readers otherwise announce a bare "75". `aria-valuetext="Step 3 of 5"` or `"75% uploaded"` is what a person actually needs. Use it whenever the raw percentage isn't self-explanatory.
*   **`inlineSize`** **not** **`width`** so it's RTL-correct via logical properties.

Real usage with async work and **milestone announcements** (see §10 for why not every %):

```typescript
function ImportButton() {
  const [pct, setPct] = useState<number | null>(null);
  const lastAnnounced = useRef(0);
  const [announce, setAnnounce] = useState("");

  async function run() {
    setPct(null);                          // indeterminate: "connecting…"
    const total = await api.startImport(); // now we have a number
    setPct(0);
    for (let done = 0; done <= total; done++) {
      await api.importRow(done);
      const p = Math.round((done / total) * 100);
      setPct(p);
      // Announce only at 25/50/75/100 — not every row (that would spam AT)
      if (p >= lastAnnounced.current + 25) {
        lastAnnounced.current = p;
        setAnnounce(`${p} percent, ${done} of ${total} rows imported`);
      }
    }
  }

  return (
    <>
      <button onClick={run}>Import</button>
      <ProgressBar value={pct} label={pct == null ? "Connecting…" : `${pct}% imported`} showValue />
      <p className="sr-only" aria-live="polite">{announce}</p>
    </>
  );
}
```

### 9.4 SVG Circular / Ring Progress (with the a11y that's usually missing)
The ring is `stroke-dasharray` math on two stacked circles. The trick everyone gets: a circle's stroke can be "drawn" partway by setting `stroke-dashoffset` against its circumference.

```typescript
function RingProgress({ value, size = 64, stroke = 6 }: { value: number; size?: number; stroke?: number }) {
  const pct = Math.max(0, Math.min(100, value));
  const r = (size - stroke) / 2;                 // radius inside the stroke
  const circ = 2 * Math.PI * r;                  // full circumference
  const offset = circ - (pct / 100) * circ;      // how much of the ring stays "empty"

  return (
    <div
      role="progressbar"
      aria-valuemin={0}
      aria-valuemax={100}
      aria-valuenow={Math.round(pct)}
      aria-valuetext={`${Math.round(pct)}% complete`}
      style={{ inlineSize: size, blockSize: size }}
    >
      <svg width={size} height={size} viewBox={`0 0 ${size} ${size}`}>
        {/* track */}
        <circle cx={size / 2} cy={size / 2} r={r} fill="none" stroke="#2a1f33" strokeWidth={stroke} />
        {/* value arc — rotate -90deg so it starts at 12 o'clock */}
        <circle
          cx={size / 2} cy={size / 2} r={r} fill="none"
          stroke="#DCA424" strokeWidth={stroke} strokeLinecap="round"
          strokeDasharray={circ} strokeDashoffset={offset}
          transform={`rotate(-90 ${size / 2} ${size / 2})`}
          style={{ transition: "stroke-dashoffset .3s ease" }}
        />
        {/* SVG text is decorative here; the a11y value lives on the wrapper's ARIA */}
        <text x="50%" y="50%" textAnchor="middle" dominantBaseline="central"
              aria-hidden="true" fontSize={size * 0.24} fill="#E8E6E1">
          {Math.round(pct)}%
        </text>
      </svg>
    </div>
  );
}
```

The a11y point people miss on rings: **the SVG itself is not a progressbar.** Put `role="progressbar"` and the `aria-value*` on the **wrapping element**, mark the visible `<text>` `aria-hidden` (otherwise a screen reader may read "75%" twice — once from the text, once from `aria-valuenow`), and never rely on the arc alone to convey state to AT. Indeterminate ring = rotate the whole arc with a CSS spin (`@keyframes` on `transform: rotate`), drop the `aria-valuenow`, and honor `prefers-reduced-motion` by pausing the spin.

### 9.5 The ARIA pattern (the canonical shape, framework-free)
Distilled so you can drop it into anything:

```plain
<!-- DETERMINATE -->
<!-- aria-valuetext ("Step 3 of 5") is the human label that wins over the raw number -->
<div role="progressbar"
     aria-valuenow="60" aria-valuemin="0" aria-valuemax="100"
     aria-valuetext="Step 3 of 5"
     aria-labelledby="importLabel">
  <div class="fill" style="inline-size:60%"></div>
</div>
<span id="importLabel">Importing contacts</span>

<!-- INDETERMINATE: role stays, the value attributes DISAPPEAR -->
<div role="progressbar" aria-label="Loading results">
  <div class="fill indeterminate"></div>
</div>
```

The four rules that make it correct: (1) `role="progressbar"` on the meter itself; (2) `aria-valuemin`/`max`/`now` present **only** when you know the number; (3) an accessible **name** via `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`; (4) `aria-valuetext` whenever a bare number would be confusing ("60" vs. "Step 3 of 5", "60% — 3 minutes left"). A progressbar is **not focusable and takes no keyboard** — it reports, it doesn't control. (That's the line vs. a [Slider](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200531), which is focusable and arrow-key driven.)

### 9.6 Buffering / dual-value progress (video)
Media players stack two fills on one track: **played** (where you are) and **buffered** (how much is downloaded ahead). The native `<video>` exposes both via its `buffered` TimeRanges and `currentTime`.

```javascript
const video = document.querySelector("video");
const played = document.querySelector(".bar__played");
const buffered = document.querySelector(".bar__buffered");

video.addEventListener("timeupdate", () => {
  played.style.inlineSize = `${(video.currentTime / video.duration) * 100}%`;
});
video.addEventListener("progress", () => {
  if (video.buffered.length) {
    const end = video.buffered.end(video.buffered.length - 1);   // furthest buffered point
    buffered.style.inlineSize = `${(end / video.duration) * 100}%`;
  }
});
```

Layer `.bar__buffered` (lighter) under `.bar__played` (brand color) in the same track. The played bar is also draggable — at which point that portion is a **slider** (`role="slider"`, seekable), while the buffered layer stays a **progressbar** (read-only). One widget, two roles, because it does two jobs.

### 9.7 Tailwind CSS
No native component — compose a track + fill, mirror the width into ARIA.

```plain
<div role="progressbar" aria-valuenow="75" aria-valuemin="0" aria-valuemax="100"
     aria-label="Upload progress"
     class="h-2 w-full overflow-hidden rounded-full bg-purple-950">
  <div class="h-full rounded-full bg-amber-400 transition-[width] duration-300"
       style="inline-size: 75%"></div>
</div>

<!-- Indeterminate: animate a partial fill across the track -->
<div role="progressbar" aria-label="Loading"
     class="h-2 w-full overflow-hidden rounded-full bg-purple-950">
  <div class="h-full w-2/5 rounded-full bg-amber-400
              motion-safe:animate-[indeterminate_1.4s_ease-in-out_infinite]
              motion-reduce:w-full motion-reduce:animate-pulse"></div>
</div>
```

Note `motion-safe:` / `motion-reduce:` — Tailwind's built-in reduced-motion variants do the §9.2 rule for you. The `_[indeterminate_…]` arbitrary keyframe still needs defining in your config.

### 9.8 shadcn/ui (Radix primitive)
`@radix-ui/react-progress` handles role, value clamping, and `data-state`/`data-value` for you; you skin the indicator.

```typescript
import * as React from "react";
import * as ProgressPrimitive from "@radix-ui/react-progress";
import { cn } from "@/lib/utils";

const Progress = React.forwardRef<
  React.ElementRef<typeof ProgressPrimitive.Root>,
  React.ComponentPropsWithoutRef<typeof ProgressPrimitive.Root>
>(({ className, value, ...props }, ref) => (
  <ProgressPrimitive.Root
    ref={ref}
    className={cn("relative h-2 w-full overflow-hidden rounded-full bg-primary/20", className)}
    value={value}
    {...props}
  >
    <ProgressPrimitive.Indicator
      className="h-full w-full flex-1 bg-primary transition-all"
      style={{ transform: `translateX(-${100 - (value || 0)}%)` }}
    />
  </ProgressPrimitive.Root>
));
Progress.displayName = ProgressPrimitive.Root.displayName;
```

Radix's default trick: the indicator is full-width and slid left via `translateX(-(100−value)%)` — a `transform`, so it's compositor-cheap and reflow-free (§14). Pass no `value` for indeterminate (`data-state="indeterminate"`), then style off that attribute.

### 9.10 Node.js — streaming real progress to the bar
A determinate bar is only honest if the server actually reports progress. For uploads, the _browser_ measures it (`XMLHttpRequest`/`fetch` upload stream). For server-side work (import, export, transcode), you stream progress events — SSE is the clean fit.

```typescript
// Client: real upload progress from the browser (fetch can't do upload progress yet in all browsers; XHR can)
const xhr = new XMLHttpRequest();
xhr.upload.addEventListener("progress", (e) => {
  if (e.lengthComputable) setPct(Math.round((e.loaded / e.total) * 100));  // REAL bytes, not a timer
});
xhr.open("POST", "/api/upload");
xhr.send(formData);
```

```typescript
// Server: stream job progress with Server-Sent Events (Express)
app.get("/api/import/:id/progress", requireAuth, (req, res) => {
  const job = jobs.get(req.params.id);
  // Authorize THIS caller for THIS specific job, or /import/:id/progress is a textbook IDOR (§15)
  if (!job || job.ownerId !== req.user.id) return res.status(404).end();
  res.writeHead(200, {
    "Content-Type": "text/event-stream",
    "Cache-Control": "no-cache",
    Connection: "keep-alive",
  });
  const send = (p: number) => res.write(`data: ${JSON.stringify({ pct: p })}\n\n`);
  job.on("progress", send);
  job.on("done", () => { res.write(`event: done\ndata: {}\n\n`); res.end(); });
  req.on("close", () => job.off("progress", send));   // client navigated away — clean up
});
```

The rule under this: **the number on the bar must trace back to a real measurement** — bytes transferred, rows processed, a server heartbeat. The moment it traces back to `setInterval` guessing, you've built a liar (§12). If the server genuinely can't report a number, that's your signal to go indeterminate, not to invent one.

### 9.11 Python (backend progress + a rendered bar)
**Backend** (FastAPI) streaming progress for a long job:

```python
from fastapi import FastAPI, Depends, HTTPException
from fastapi.responses import StreamingResponse
import asyncio, json

app = FastAPI()

@app.get("/api/export/{job_id}/progress")
async def export_progress(job_id: str, user=Depends(current_user)):
    job = JOBS.get(job_id)
    # Authorize THIS caller for THIS specific job, or /export/{job_id}/progress is a textbook IDOR (§15)
    if job is None or job.owner_id != user.id:
        raise HTTPException(status_code=404)
    async def event_stream():
        while not job.done:
            await asyncio.sleep(0.5)
            # yield the REAL processed-row count, not a fabricated tick
            yield f"data: {json.dumps({'pct': job.percent, 'rows': job.rows_done})}\n\n"
        yield "event: done\ndata: {}\n\n"
    return StreamingResponse(event_stream(), media_type="text/event-stream")
```

**Rendering** a bar server-side (Django/Jinja), value baked from real state:

```plain
<div role="progressbar" aria-valuenow="{{ pct }}" aria-valuemin="0" aria-valuemax="100"
     aria-valuetext="{{ done }} of {{ total }} files" aria-label="Export">
  <div class="fill" style="inline-size: {{ pct }}%"></div>
</div>
```

Python's job is identical to Node's: measure real progress and stream it. The bar still lives in HTML/CSS; the backend's only role is to feed it an honest number.

### 9.14 Web Component (framework-agnostic)

### 9.16 Testing (unit + a11y)
A progress bar is state exposed to assistive tech, so you test the _exposed value_, not the pixel width.

```typescript
import { render, screen } from "@testing-library/react";
import { axe } from "jest-axe";
import { describe, it, expect } from "vitest";
import { ProgressBar } from "./ProgressBar";

describe("ProgressBar", () => {
  it("exposes the value to assistive tech", () => {
    render(<ProgressBar value={60} label="Importing" />);
    const bar = screen.getByRole("progressbar", { name: "Importing" });
    expect(bar).toHaveAttribute("aria-valuenow", "60");
    expect(bar).toHaveAttribute("aria-valuemin", "0");
    expect(bar).toHaveAttribute("aria-valuemax", "100");
  });

  it("uses aria-valuetext for human labels", () => {
    render(<ProgressBar value={60} max={100} label="Step 3 of 5" />);
    expect(screen.getByRole("progressbar")).toHaveAttribute("aria-valuetext", "Step 3 of 5");
  });

  it("has NO valuenow when indeterminate", () => {
    render(<ProgressBar value={null} label="Loading" />);
    const bar = screen.getByRole("progressbar");
    expect(bar).not.toHaveAttribute("aria-valuenow");   // the critical indeterminate rule
  });

  it("clamps out-of-range values", () => {
    render(<ProgressBar value={140} label="X" />);
    expect(screen.getByRole("progressbar")).toHaveAttribute("aria-valuenow", "100");
  });

  it("has no axe violations", async () => {
    const { container } = render(<ProgressBar value={40} label="Export" />);
    expect(await axe(container)).toHaveNoViolations();
  });
});
```

What to assert (and skip): **do** test the role, the accessible name, `aria-valuenow`/min/max, `aria-valuetext`, the indeterminate "no valuenow" rule, and clamping. **Don't** test the exact fill width in px, the transition timing, or hex colors — implementation details that break tests without catching real bugs. **Bonus:** an E2E (Playwright) test that watches the bar actually move during a real upload catches the "wired to nothing" failure that unit tests can't.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
*   **Role:** `role="progressbar"` on the meter (native `<progress>` gives it free). It is a **status/live** role, **not focusable, no keyboard** — it reports, it doesn't control. If a user is meant to drag it, it's a Slider, not a progress bar.
*   **Values:** `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax`, `aria-valuenow` **only when determinate**. Indeterminate = `role="progressbar"` with **no** `aria-valuenow` (this is how AT knows the duration is unknown). Setting `aria-valuenow="0"` on an indeterminate bar is wrong — it says "0% and stuck."
*   **`aria-valuetext`** **for humans.** Screen readers announce the bare number by default ("60"). When the number alone is unclear, override it: `aria-valuetext="Step 3 of 5"`, `"75% uploaded"`, `"60% — about 2 minutes left"`. This is one of the most under-used, highest-impact ARIA attributes.
*   **Name it.** Every bar needs an accessible name via `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` ("Upload progress"), or the user hears "progress bar, 60" with no idea what's progressing.
*   **Announce milestones, don't spam.** A progressbar's value changes are _not_ automatically announced by every screen reader, and even where they are, announcing every 1% is torture. Pair long operations with a **polite** **`aria-live`** **region** and speak at meaningful milestones — 25/50/75/100, or on state change (started, stalled, done, failed) — never every tick. On completion, announce "Import complete, 1,200 rows" and consider a [Toast](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200531) for users who looked away.
*   **Contrast & not-color-alone.** Fill vs. track and label text must meet WCAG AA (text ≥ 4.5:1). Don't signal error/success by color change alone — add an icon or text ("Failed", "Done").
*   **Reduced motion.** The indeterminate animation must degrade under `prefers-reduced-motion` (§9.2). A spinning/sliding element with no off-switch can trigger vestibular symptoms.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The bar looks solved but keeps moving (2026-aware):

*   **Streaming / agentic progress.** AI features replaced the spinner with _streaming_ progress: token-by-token text, "thinking… searching… drafting…" stage labels, and step-progress for multi-tool agent runs. The bar became a narrated timeline.
*   **Morphing button → bar → check.** The button that becomes its own progress bar and then a checkmark (from Button §11's "morphing buttons"). Reduces perceived latency and eliminates a layout jump.
*   **Expressive Material 3 wavy tracks.** Google's Material Expressive ships an animated wavy indeterminate/determinate track — motion as personality, still behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Skeleton + progress hybrids.** Content skeletons that fill in top-to-bottom as real data streams, blurring the line between skeleton (sibling) and progress.
*   **Scroll & reading progress.** The thin bar tracking how far down a long article you are — a progressbar bound to scroll position, increasingly standard on blogs and docs.
*   **View Transitions.** Native browser morphing between a loading state and the loaded content, triggered as the bar completes — no heavy JS.
*   **Realistic time estimates.** Backends emitting genuine ETA ("about 2 minutes left") from real throughput, surfaced via `aria-valuetext`, instead of a bare percentage.

## 12\. UX & Trust Killers (the honesty section)
None of these throw an error. They quietly erode trust or bleed conversion, which makes them dangerous.

*   **The fake progress bar.** A determinate bar driven by `setInterval` guessing, unconnected to any real work. It'll hit 95% while the server's still chewing, then either freeze (user assumes crash) or snap to 100% before the data's actually there (user clicks ahead into a broken state). **Fabricated determinate progress is the fastest way to teach users your progress means nothing** — and once learned, they distrust every bar you ship, including honest ones. If you don't have a number, go **indeterminate**. That's not a lesser option; it's the truthful one.
*   **The legit exception — honest easing.** There _is_ a defensible cheat: the "trickle to ~90%, then snap to 100% the instant the real event fires" pattern (GitHub/YouTube top bar). It's honest because it never claims to be done until it _is_ done — it only smooths the unknown middle. The line: **you may ease perception of an unknown duration; you may never claim completion before completion.**
*   **Freezing with no "stalled" state.** A determinate bar stuck at 43% is indistinguishable from a dead one. If bytes stop moving, say so ("Stalled — retrying…") and offer a retry. Silence reads as "broken."
*   **The backward / resetting bar.** A bar that jumps 60% → 40% because you recomputed the total mid-flight destroys credibility instantly. Lock the total before you start, or only ever move forward.
*   **Spinner for a long wait.** A bare spinner on a 60-second export gives no sense of progress or end — anxiety with no information. Past a few seconds, upgrade to a bar (even indeterminate is better: it at least animates "alive").
*   **No completion signal.** The bar hits 100% and… nothing. Users don't know if it worked. Confirm explicitly (checkmark, "Done", a Toast) and then get out of the way.
*   **Missing the AT path entirely.** A visually perfect bar with no role/value/announcement is _invisible_ to screen-reader users — the operation has no progress indicator at all for them. Silent, common, and an instant audit fail.

**The through-line:** progress is a promise about the future. Every one of these breaks the promise quietly — no crash, just a user who trusts your interface a little less each time.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
*   **Design tokens, three tiers.** Same model as Button: primitive (`--gold-500`), semantic (`--color-progress-fill`), component (`--progress-fill`, `--progress-track`, `--progress-h-md`). The bar references only component tokens; rebrand by swapping primitives, dark-mode by swapping semantics — no bar CSS rewrite.
*   **TS-enforced honesty.** Model the mode as a discriminated union so an indeterminate bar _cannot_ be given a value, and a determinate one _must_ have min/max, at compile time:

```typescript
type Progress =
  | { mode: "determinate"; value: number; max: number; label?: string }
  | { mode: "indeterminate"; label: string };   // label required so it's never a nameless spinner
```

*   **Smoothed value updates.** Real byte streams are jumpy. Interpolate (lerp) the displayed value toward the true value over ~200ms so the bar glides instead of jerking — while `aria-valuenow` reflects the true value, not the animation frame.
*   **ETA from throughput.** Track bytes/rows per second over a rolling window; surface "~2 min left" in `aria-valuetext`. Smooth the estimate or it'll flicker wildly at the start.
*   **Segmented/stepper overlap.** For a multi-stage flow, a segmented progress bar is the lightweight view of a **Stepper**. If the segments are named, navigable stages, build the Stepper (own doc, cross-linked) and let it expose overall progress; if they're just chunks of one task, a segmented bar is enough. Don't build both for the same flow.
*   **Optimistic completion.** Pair with optimistic UI (Button §11): show the result the instant the bar completes, roll back if the server later rejects — needs careful error handling but feels instant.
* * *

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
*   **Animate** **`transform`****/****`opacity`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*, never** **`width`****\*\*\*\*\*\*\*\*, for the indeterminate loop.** A `width`/`inline-size` animation running 60fps reflows the layout every frame; `transform: translateX` runs on the compositor, no reflow. The Radix `translateX(-(100−value)%)` trick (§9.8) applies this even to the determinate fill.
*   **Throttle value updates.** A fast upload can fire progress events dozens of times a second. Updating React state (and re-rendering) that often is wasted work — throttle to ~10–15fps for the visual; the eye can't resolve faster, and `aria-valuenow` doesn't need every byte.
*   **The determinate fill transitions between discrete updates only** — a CSS `transition` on a value that changes a few times a second is cheap and reads smooth. Don't run a continuous animation on it.
*   **CSS containment.** `contain: layout style` on the bar isolates its internal recalcs from the page.
*   **Kill the loop when hidden/done.** An indeterminate animation running on an off-screen or completed bar burns cycles for nothing. Stop the animation (and any SSE/polling behind it) on unmount or completion — the §9.10 `req.on("close")` cleanup is the server half of the same discipline.
*   **No library needed.** A progress bar is ~30 lines of CSS/JS; reaching for an npm package for a plain bar is bundle cost with no payoff. Rings and steppers may justify a small helper, but the linear bar rarely does.
* * *

## 15\. Security
A progress bar is a read-only display of a value, so its own attack surface is small — but the value and the machinery feeding it are not automatically safe. Treat the number as untrusted input: clamp and validate it to `0`–`max` on the way in (§9.3 already does this) so a hostile or buggy backend can't push the fill negative, past 100%, or into a wrong `aria-valuenow`. More important, a progress value is **not** an authorization or completion signal — a client-side "100%" only means the animation finished, never that the server did; gate the next action (enabling a download, marking an order paid, advancing a wizard) on a server-confirmed state, not on the bar reaching the end. If the bar is fed by a polled or streamed progress endpoint (§9.10, §9.11), that endpoint must authorize the caller to view _that specific job_ — an incrementing `/import/:id/progress` is a textbook IDOR, and one user watching another's export progress leaks the job's existence, size, and timing even when the payload itself is hidden. Finally, any human-readable label you render — filename, step name, "12 of 40 files" — is user-controlled content, so bind it as text (`textContent`, JSX children, framework interpolation), never `innerHTML`, or a filename like `<img onerror=...>` becomes stored XSS in the one part of this component that shows arbitrary strings.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a progress bar is "done":
- [ ] Correct mode: determinate only when you have a **real** number; otherwise indeterminate.
- [ ] `role="progressbar"` present (or native `<progress>`); accessible name via label.
- [ ] Determinate has `aria-valuemin`/`max`/`now`; indeterminate has **none** of them.
- [ ] `aria-valuetext` set whenever the bare number needs human context ("Step 3 of 5").
- [ ] Value clamped 0–max; the bar can never render backward or overflow.
- [ ] Milestones announced via polite `aria-live` (not every %); completion + failure announced.
- [ ] Error/stalled state is distinct and honest — never a silent freeze.
- [ ] Indeterminate animation respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- [ ] Animation uses `transform`/`opacity`, not `width`; value updates throttled.
- [ ] The number traces to a real measurement (bytes/rows/heartbeat), not a fake timer.
- [ ] Contrast ≥ AA; state never signaled by color alone.
- [ ] Completion is explicitly confirmed (checkmark/Toast), then the bar gets out of the way.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same bar, eleven skins)
Style is skin; the semantics, role, value model, and reduced-motion handling never change. The same `role="progressbar"` wears any of these.

*   **Flat:** solid fill on a muted track, crisp caps, no shadow. Reads instantly; the safe default.
*   **Material:** thin track with a small gap, rounded caps, opinionated indeterminate timing; ripple-era motion.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent track with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over a vivid backdrop, frosted fill. Guard contrast — a glass track on a busy image can make the fill unreadable.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe material — the fill is a refractive pill with a specular rim and a moving sheen that reacts to the value. Web = honest approximation with layered highlights; SwiftUI emits native `.glassEffect()`. Guard for contrast _and_ `prefers-reduced-motion` (the sheen is motion).
*   **Neumorphism:** track is an inset "pressed" channel with dual light/dark shadows, fill is a soft extruded ridge. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent use only, and check the fill/track ratio.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** glossy gradient fill with an inner top highlight and a beveled track — the old "loading bar" look, tactile and literal.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black border, zero radius, hard offset shadow, clashing fill color. High personality; the fill often has a visible hatch or stripe.
*   **Claymorphism:** big-radius puffy track and fill, soft inner top-light + bottom-shadow, gentle glow. Friendly and toy-like.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue gradient fill on a dark track. Premium; the moving gradient must honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** a hairline track, no radius, the label and grid doing the work — near-zero decoration.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod (`#DCA424`) fill on a deep Eminence/Night track (`#2a1f33` over `#0A0A0A`), full radius, a warm glow at the leading edge, and a Liquid-Glass sheen reserved for hero moments over the dark saturated field. The house default.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin. Role, value semantics, the "indeterminate has no valuenow" law, the announcement discipline, and reduced-motion handling stay identical across all eleven. Full guidance in the [Design Styles](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) doc.
* * *

**Bottom line:** a progress bar is a small component carrying a large promise — _work is happening, and here's how much is left._ Master the determinate/indeterminate split (and the sibling line to spinners and skeletons), wire the ARIA so the value reaches assistive tech, announce milestones without spamming, honor reduced-motion, and — above all — keep it honest: never show a number you didn't measure, and never claim done before done. Get those right and your interface feels faster and more trustworthy than it actually is, which is the entire point.
