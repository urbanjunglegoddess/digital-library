## 1\. What a Video Player Actually Is
A video player is a control surface wrapped around a decoded media stream. The browser already ships one — `<video>` is a full player with a decode pipeline, a timeline, a buffer, and a default control bar. Everything else you build is a _skin and a remote control_ on top of that engine. People conflate four different things:
*   **The** **`<video>`** **element:** the native media engine. Handles decode, buffering, the media clock, and (with `controls`) a built-in UI. This is the thing.
*   **A custom player:** your own HTML controls (play, scrub, mute, fullscreen) driving the same `<video>` via the [HTMLMediaElement](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/HTMLMediaElement) JS API. The `<video>` is still doing all the real work; you've hidden its chrome (`controls` off) and wired your own buttons to `.play()`, `.currentTime`, `.muted`.
*   **An embed / iframe player:** YouTube, Vimeo, Wistia. A whole third-party app in an `<iframe>` — their player, their brand, their ~1MB of JavaScript, their tracking. You give up control and pay a weight tax for hosting/CDN/transcoding you didn't have to build.
*   **A streaming player:** the same `<video>` fed not one file but a _manifest_ (HLS `.m3u8` / DASH `.mpd`) that points at hundreds of small segments at multiple bitrates, switched on the fly. This is how Netflix, YouTube, and every serious video product actually ship. More in §9.5.

The single most common mistake: **shipping a 200MB** **`.mp4`** **in a** **`<video src>`** **and calling it done.** That's not a video product, it's a download with a play button. The whole file must arrive (or at least buffer heavily) before smooth playback, it can't adapt to a weak connection, and it can't skip ahead without re-fetching. Real video is _adaptive_ and _segmented_. Rule of thumb: **a short muted loop or a <30s clip can be a progressive** **`.mp4`****\*\*\*\*; anything longer or premium should stream.**
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
Video is the most expensive, most fragile, and most legally-exposed element on the page. It carries disproportionate weight in three directions at once.
*   **Bandwidth is real money and real bounce.** Video is, by a wide margin, the biggest byte-cost on most pages. A mis-configured `preload="auto"` on five below-the-fold videos can pull tens of megabytes before the user scrolls to any of them — on mobile data, that's their money and your bounce rate. Weight here dwarfs every button and image on the page combined.
*   **Accessibility is not optional — it's the law.** Video without captions locks out deaf and hard-of-hearing users, and captions help the far larger group watching muted in public. WCAG 2.x requires **captions for prerecorded video (1.2.2, Level A)** and **audio description or a media alternative (1.2.3/1.2.5)**. This is the single most-litigated media accessibility failure on the web. Captions are the spine of this entire doc — if you take one thing away, it's that a `<video>` without a `<track kind="captions">` is an unfinished, non-compliant build.
*   **It sets the trust and quality tone instantly.** A hero video that autoplays with a jarring blast of sound, janks the layout as it loads, or shows a broken-file icon reads as "amateur" in under a second. Perceived production value is mostly: does it load smoothly, does it respect my settings, and can I make it stop.

## 3\. Anatomy of a Video Player
Every well-built player, native or custom, is composed of predictable parts:
*   **Media surface:** the `<video>` element itself — the pixels. Has intrinsic dimensions, an aspect ratio, and an object-fit behavior.
*   **Poster:** the still image shown before playback (`poster` attribute). Doubles as the thing that prevents layout shift and gives the facade pattern its click target.
*   **Sources:** one or more `<source>` children offering the same content in different codecs/containers (`.webm`, `.mp4`), letting the browser pick what it can decode.
*   **Text tracks:** `<track>` children for captions, subtitles, descriptions, and chapters (WebVTT files). The accessibility spine.
*   **Control bar:** play/pause, timeline/scrubber with a buffered-range indicator, current-time/duration, volume + mute, captions toggle, playback-rate, picture-in-picture, and fullscreen.
*   **Timeline / scrubber:** a slider bound to `currentTime`, showing played, buffered, and total ranges. The hardest control to build accessibly.
*   **Buffering/loading indicator:** spinner shown while the network can't keep up with the clock (`waiting` event).
*   **Big center play button:** the primary affordance over the poster, and the click target for lazy/facade loading.
*   **Live region + transcript:** an off-screen `aria-live` for state changes and, ideally, a full text transcript beneath the player.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes, Aspect Ratios & Density
Video isn't sized in a "sm/md/lg" scale — it's sized by **aspect ratio and container width**. Locking the ratio is what prevents the single worst video layout bug: cumulative layout shift as the metadata loads and the box snaps to real dimensions.

| Ratio | Name | Where it's used |
| ---| ---| --- |
| 16:9 | Widescreen | The default. YouTube, most web video, landscape hero. |
| 9:16 | Vertical | Shorts/Reels/TikTok, mobile-first story content. |
| 1:1 | Square | Social feeds, product loops. |
| 4:5 | Portrait feed | Instagram feed video, mobile-optimized. |
| 21:9 | Cinemascope | Cinematic hero banners, ultrawide. |
| 4:3 | Legacy | Archival footage, some screencasts. |

Rules that matter:
*   **Always reserve the box with** **`aspect-ratio`** **(CSS) so the layout never shifts** when metadata loads. This is the video equivalent of putting `width`/`height` on an `<img>` — see the Image reference for the same CLS story.
*   **Never fix a pixel height.** Set `width: 100%` and let `aspect-ratio` compute the height, so the player is responsive by default.
*   **`object-fit: cover`** **vs** **`contain`****\*\*\*\*:** `cover` fills the box and crops (right for background/hero); `contain` letterboxes to show the whole frame (right for content you must not crop). Default `<video>` behaves like `contain`.
*   **Control-bar density scales with player size.** A 320px-wide inline player can't show playback-rate, PiP, _and_ chapters — collapse secondary controls into an overflow menu below ~480px.
* * *

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A production player has far more than play/pause. Missing states are the #1 tell that someone wired `onClick` to `.play()` and shipped it.
1. **Empty / poster (idle):** before load, showing the `poster`. The only bytes fetched should be the poster image (with `preload="none"` or `"metadata"`).
2. **Loading metadata:** fetching duration/dimensions (`loadedmetadata` fires). The box should already be reserved by `aspect-ratio` so nothing shifts.
3. **Ready / can play:** enough is buffered to start (`canplay` / `canplaythrough`).
4. **Playing:** the clock is running (`play`, `playing` events).
5. **Paused:** stopped, position retained.
6. **Buffering / stalled (waiting):** the clock outran the buffer (`waiting`, `stalled`). Show a spinner — this is the state that makes a player feel broken if you skip it.
7. **Seeking:** user dragged the scrubber; frames are being fetched for the new position (`seeking` → `seeked`).
8. **Ended:** playback reached the end (`ended`). Show a replay affordance, an end-card, or advance a playlist.
9. **Muted vs unmuted:** distinct and critical — autoplay is _only_ allowed while muted (§9.1).
10. **Fullscreen / PiP:** presentation modes with their own UI implications.
11. **Error:** the source failed to load or decode (`error` event + `video.error.code`). Show a real message and a retry, never a silent black box.
12. **Captions on/off:** a first-class, persistent, user-visible state.

Bonus for streaming: **quality/bitrate switching** (auto vs. a pinned resolution) and **live-edge vs. behind-live** for live streams.
* * *

## 6\. Types & Variants
Same `<video>` engine, very different products. Pick by _intent_:
*   **Content video (the default):** a clip the user chose to watch. Full controls, captions, transcript. Never autoplays with sound.
*   **Custom-skinned player:** controls hidden, your own UI. Choose this when brand, layout, or a captions/transcript experience the native chrome can't give you justifies re-implementing accessibility from scratch (a real cost — see §10).
*   **Background / hero video:** muted, looped, `playsinline`, no controls, decorative. Must have a reduced-motion off-switch and must not ship on mobile data. It's wallpaper, not content — so it needs no captions but _does_ need a pause control for vestibular safety.
*   **Autoplay preview loop:** short muted loop (product demos, feed video) that starts on scroll-into-view via IntersectionObserver. Muted, `playsinline`, cheap.
*   **Live stream:** HLS/DASH low-latency, a live-edge indicator, no seeking past the edge, DVR window optional.
*   **Embedded third-party (YouTube/Vimeo):** an iframe app. Fastest to ship, heaviest to load — always wrap in a **facade** (§9.6).
*   **Short-form vertical (Shorts/Reels):** 9:16, tap-to-mute, swipe-to-next, autoplay-on-view. A feed pattern more than a player pattern — cross-reference the Carousel doc for the swipe/snap mechanics.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use Video (and When Not To)
Use video when motion, timing, or demonstration genuinely carry the message — a product in use, a face building trust, a process shown step by step. Don't reach for it when:
*   **Text would be faster to consume.** A 90-second "how to reset your password" video that's a 4-step numbered list in disguise wastes the user's time and your bandwidth. Video is a poor random-access medium.
*   **It's decorative motion you could achieve in CSS/Lottie.** A looping gradient or a subtle animation shouldn't be a 4MB `.mp4`; it should be CSS, SVG, or a tiny Lottie/animated image — cheaper, sharper, and reduced-motion-friendly by construction.
*   **The content changes constantly.** Video is expensive to produce and re-produce; don't put your pricing table in a video.
*   **You can't caption it.** If you don't have the resources to caption and transcribe, you don't have the resources to publish accessible video. Fix that before shipping, not after the complaint.

Placement heuristics:
*   **Above the fold ≠ autoplay-with-sound.** A hero can autoplay _muted_; content video waits for a click.
*   **One hero video per view.** Competing autoplay loops fight for the decoder and the eye.
*   **Give the pause control real estate.** For anything that moves on its own, the stop/pause affordance must be visible without hunting.

## 8\. Players Across Design Systems
Same element, different philosophies and defaults. Knowing these makes you fluent anywhere.
*   **Material Design (Google):** Media controls follow the elevation/ripple language; Android's ExoPlayer/Media3 is the reference implementation with a highly themeable control layer. Bold, touch-first, big targets.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** `AVPlayerViewController` gives a system player with AirPlay, PiP, and native captions honoring the user's OS caption style settings. Restrained chrome, translucent controls, strong system integration. Safari _requires_ `playsinline` or video goes fullscreen on iPhone.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** Subtle, compact controls with acrylic materials; media is comparatively rare in Fluent surfaces and leans on the OS player.
*   **Ant / enterprise:** Video is uncommon in data-heavy admin UIs; when present it's a thin wrapper around native controls, no frills.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** No prescribed player. You style `<video>` and, for a custom UI, hand-build the control bar. Freedom plus the full accessibility burden.
*   **shadcn/Radix (2024+):** No official video component, but the Radix Slider primitive is the correct accessible base for a scrubber, and the shadcn ecosystem now has community media players built on `media-chrome` / Vidstack. Headless behavior, styled by you.
*   **Dedicated player libraries:** **Video.js** (the long-time workhorse, plugin ecosystem, HLS via `@videojs/http-streaming`), **Vidstack** and **media-chrome** (modern web-component players, framework-agnostic, accessibility-forward), **Plyr** (lightweight, clean default skin), **Shaka Player** (Google's DASH/HLS + DRM heavyweight), **hls.js**/**dash.js** (the streaming engines the others wrap). Reach for one of these before hand-rolling a full player — they've already fought the accessibility and streaming battles.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML — the native `<video>` done right
The foundation. Ninety percent of "we need a video player" is a correctly-configured `<video>` tag, and most bugs are attributes people got wrong.

```plain
<!-- Content video: the correct baseline -->
<video
  controls
  preload="metadata"
  poster="/media/demo-poster.jpg"
  width="1280" height="720"
  playsinline>
  <!-- Offer modern codec first; browser picks the first it can decode -->
  <source src="/media/demo.webm" type="video/webm" />
  <source src="/media/demo.mp4"  type="video/mp4" />

  <!-- The accessibility spine: captions are non-negotiable -->
  <track kind="captions" src="/media/demo.en.vtt" srclang="en" label="English" default />
  <track kind="subtitles" src="/media/demo.es.vtt" srclang="es" label="Español" />
  <!-- Audio description track (describes on-screen action for blind users) -->
  <track kind="descriptions" src="/media/demo.en.desc.vtt" srclang="en" label="Descriptions" />
  <!-- Chapters power a segmented scrubber -->
  <track kind="chapters" src="/media/demo.chapters.vtt" srclang="en" label="Chapters" />

  <!-- Fallback for the rare no-video-support case -->
  <p>Your browser can't play this video.
     <a href="/media/demo.mp4">Download it</a> or
     <a href="/media/demo-transcript">read the transcript</a>.</p>
</video>
```

Attribute rules that trip people up:
*   **`preload`** decides how much loads before play. `"none"` (fetch nothing until pressed — best for below-the-fold and facades), `"metadata"` (just duration/dimensions — the sane default), `"auto"` (browser may pull the whole thing — only for a video you're confident will be watched). **Defaulting to** **`auto`** **on multiple videos is the classic bandwidth blowout.**
*   **`poster`** shows a still before load. Always set it: it kills the ugly black box, gives users something to decide against, and doubles as the facade click target. Size it to the video's ratio to avoid CLS.
*   **`playsinline`** is mandatory for inline mobile playback. Without it, iOS Safari forces the video fullscreen on play — death for autoplay loops and background video.
*   **`muted`** **+** **`autoplay`** **are a package deal.** See §9.1b. `autoplay` alone is silently blocked by every modern browser if there's sound.
*   **`<source>`** **order = preference order.** List the codec you _want_ (smaller, e.g. AV1/VP9 in WebM) first; the browser walks down to the first it can play. `.mp4` (H.264) is the universal safety net.
*   **`controls`** gives you the native player free — use it unless you have a real reason to build your own. It's fully accessible out of the box; your hand-rolled version probably won't be on day one.
*   **`crossorigin="anonymous"`** is required if your `<track>` VTT files live on a different origin (CORS), or captions silently fail to load.

### 9.1b The autoplay rules (the thing that "randomly doesn't work")
Autoplay isn't a bug when it fails — it's the browser enforcing policy. Internalize this and half your "video won't play" tickets vanish.
*   **Autoplay with sound is blocked** unless the user has "engaged" with the domain (Chrome's Media Engagement Index) or you have explicit permission. You cannot rely on it.
*   **Muted autoplay is allowed** on essentially every browser. So the recipe for a background/hero loop is always **`muted autoplay loop playsinline`** — all four, together.
*   **`.play()`** **returns a Promise that can reject.** If you call it programmatically and autoplay is disallowed, it throws. Always `.catch()` it and fall back to showing a play button:

```js
video.muted = true;                    // give yourself the best autoplay odds
video.play().catch(() => {
  // Autoplay was blocked — reveal the manual play affordance instead
  playButton.hidden = false;
});
```

*   **Respect** **`prefers-reduced-motion`****\*\*\*\*.** Autoplaying motion can trigger vestibular disorders. For decorative/background video, _don't autoplay at all_ when the user asked for reduced motion — show the poster and a play button (§9.7).

### 9.2 CSS — responsive, no layout shift, states honored

```css
.player {
  position: relative;
  width: 100%;
  aspect-ratio: 16 / 9;        /* reserve the box: zero CLS when metadata loads */
  background: #0A0A0A;          /* poster gap shows Night, not white flash */
  border-radius: 12px;
  overflow: clip;
}
.player > video {
  width: 100%;
  height: 100%;
  display: block;
  object-fit: cover;           /* 'contain' if you must never crop the frame */
}

/* Custom control bar sits over the video */
.player__controls {
  position: absolute;
  inset-inline: 0;
  inset-block-end: 0;
  display: flex;
  align-items: center;
  gap: 8px;
  padding: 8px 12px;
  background: linear-gradient(transparent, rgba(10,10,10,.72));
  opacity: 0;
  transition: opacity .2s;
}
.player:hover .player__controls,
.player:focus-within .player__controls,   /* keyboard users must see controls too */
.player[data-paused="true"] .player__controls { opacity: 1; }

/* Every control keeps a visible keyboard ring and a 44px target */
.player button {
  min-width: 44px; min-height: 44px;
  display: grid; place-items: center;
  background: none; border: 0; color: #E8E6E1; cursor: pointer;
}
.player button:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid #DCA424;   /* Goldenrod */
  outline-offset: 2px;
}

/* Buffering spinner only while waiting */
.player__spinner { display: none; }
.player[data-buffering="true"] .player__spinner { display: block; }

/* Reduced motion: no spin, no auto-anything */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .player__spinner { animation: none; }
  .player__controls { transition: none; }
}
```

The load-bearing line is `aspect-ratio: 16 / 9` on a reserved box: it's what stops the page from jumping when the video's real dimensions arrive — the same CLS defense the Image doc puts on `<img width height>`.

### 9.3 Vanilla JS — driving the HTMLMediaElement API
Every player, in every framework, is ultimately these few API calls. Know them cold.

```js
const video = document.querySelector("video");
const playBtn = document.querySelector("#play");
const scrubber = document.querySelector("#scrubber");   // <input type="range">
const player = document.querySelector(".player");

// Play / pause (note: play() is async and can reject — §9.1b)
playBtn.addEventListener("click", () => {
  if (video.paused) video.play().catch(() => {/* blocked */});
  else video.pause();
});
video.addEventListener("play",  () => player.dataset.paused = "false");
video.addEventListener("pause", () => player.dataset.paused = "true");

// Timeline: reflect currentTime -> scrubber, and scrubber -> currentTime
video.addEventListener("timeupdate", () => {
  scrubber.value = String((video.currentTime / video.duration) * 100 || 0);
  scrubber.setAttribute("aria-valuetext", fmt(video.currentTime) + " of " + fmt(video.duration));
});
scrubber.addEventListener("input", () => {
  video.currentTime = (Number(scrubber.value) / 100) * video.duration;
});

// Buffering state -> spinner
video.addEventListener("waiting", () => player.dataset.buffering = "true");
video.addEventListener("playing", () => player.dataset.buffering = "false");

// Error: never a silent black box
video.addEventListener("error", () => {
  const codes = { 1: "aborted", 2: "network", 3: "decode", 4: "src not supported" };
  showError("Video failed: " + (codes[video.error?.code] ?? "unknown"));
});

// Ended
video.addEventListener("ended", () => player.dataset.ended = "true");

function fmt(s) {                       // 75 -> "1:15"
  s = Math.floor(s || 0);
  return `${Math.floor(s / 60)}:${String(s % 60).padStart(2, "0")}`;
}
```

The events that matter and when they fire: `loadedmetadata` (duration/size known), `canplay` (enough to start), `play`/`pause`/`playing`, `timeupdate` (~4x/sec, drive the scrubber here), `waiting`/`stalled` (buffering — show spinner), `seeking`/`seeked`, `volumechange`, `ended`, and `error`. Toggling captions is done through the JS text-track API, not the DOM: `video.textTracks[i].mode = "showing" | "hidden" | "disabled"`.

### 9.4 React + TypeScript — an accessible custom player
The longest block, because a custom player is where accessibility is won or lost. This is a real, reusable component with a full keyboard map and a captions toggle.

```tsx
import { useRef, useState, useEffect, useCallback } from "react";

interface Source { src: string; type: string }
interface CaptionTrack { src: string; srclang: string; label: string; default?: boolean }

interface VideoPlayerProps {
  sources: Source[];
  poster?: string;
  captions?: CaptionTrack[];
  /** Full transcript is rendered below the player and cross-linked from the a11y region */
  transcriptHref?: string;
  ratio?: string;                 // "16 / 9"
}

export function VideoPlayer({
  sources, poster, captions = [], transcriptHref, ratio = "16 / 9",
}: VideoPlayerProps) {
  const ref = useRef<HTMLVideoElement>(null);
  const [playing, setPlaying]   = useState(false);
  const [muted, setMuted]       = useState(false);
  const [buffering, setBuffering] = useState(false);
  const [progress, setProgress] = useState(0);        // 0..1
  const [duration, setDuration] = useState(0);
  const [captionsOn, setCaptionsOn] = useState(captions.some(c => c.default));
  const [status, setStatus]     = useState("");       // aria-live text

  const togglePlay = useCallback(() => {
    const v = ref.current; if (!v) return;
    if (v.paused) v.play().catch(() => setStatus("Playback was blocked. Press play."));
    else v.pause();
  }, []);

  const seekBy = useCallback((delta: number) => {
    const v = ref.current; if (!v) return;
    v.currentTime = Math.min(Math.max(0, v.currentTime + delta), v.duration || 0);
    setStatus(`Seek to ${Math.floor(v.currentTime)} seconds`);
  }, []);

  const toggleCaptions = useCallback(() => {
    const v = ref.current; if (!v) return;
    const track = v.textTracks[0];
    if (!track) return;
    const on = track.mode !== "showing";
    track.mode = on ? "showing" : "hidden";
    setCaptionsOn(on);
    setStatus(on ? "Captions on" : "Captions off");
  }, []);

  const toggleFullscreen = useCallback(() => {
    const el = ref.current?.parentElement;
    if (!document.fullscreenElement) el?.requestFullscreen?.();
    else document.exitFullscreen?.();
  }, []);

  // The keyboard map — the heart of an accessible custom player
  const onKeyDown = useCallback((e: React.KeyboardEvent) => {
    switch (e.key) {
      case " ": case "k": e.preventDefault(); togglePlay(); break;
      case "ArrowRight": e.preventDefault(); seekBy(5);  break;
      case "ArrowLeft":  e.preventDefault(); seekBy(-5); break;
      case "j": seekBy(-10); break;
      case "l": seekBy(10);  break;
      case "m": {
        const v = ref.current; if (v) { v.muted = !v.muted; setMuted(v.muted); }
        break;
      }
      case "f": toggleFullscreen(); break;
      case "c": toggleCaptions(); break;
    }
  }, [togglePlay, seekBy, toggleFullscreen, toggleCaptions]);

  useEffect(() => {
    const v = ref.current; if (!v) return;
    const onTime = () => { setProgress(v.currentTime / (v.duration || 1)); };
    const onMeta = () => setDuration(v.duration);
    const onPlay = () => setPlaying(true);
    const onPause = () => setPlaying(false);
    const onWaiting = () => setBuffering(true);
    const onPlaying = () => setBuffering(false);
    const onEnded = () => setStatus("Video ended");
    v.addEventListener("play",  onPlay);
    v.addEventListener("pause", onPause);
    v.addEventListener("timeupdate", onTime);
    v.addEventListener("loadedmetadata", onMeta);
    v.addEventListener("waiting", onWaiting);
    v.addEventListener("playing", onPlaying);
    v.addEventListener("ended",   onEnded);
    // Named handlers so unmount actually detaches them (anonymous ones leak and
    // double-attach under StrictMode's mount/remount).
    return () => {
      v.removeEventListener("play",  onPlay);
      v.removeEventListener("pause", onPause);
      v.removeEventListener("timeupdate", onTime);
      v.removeEventListener("loadedmetadata", onMeta);
      v.removeEventListener("waiting", onWaiting);
      v.removeEventListener("playing", onPlaying);
      v.removeEventListener("ended",   onEnded);
    };
  }, []);

  return (
    <figure className="player" style={{ aspectRatio: ratio }} onKeyDown={onKeyDown}>
      <video
        ref={ref}
        poster={poster}
        playsInline
        preload="metadata"
        onClick={togglePlay}
      >
        {sources.map(s => <source key={s.src} src={s.src} type={s.type} />)}
        {captions.map(c => (
          <track key={c.srclang} kind="captions" src={c.src}
                 srcLang={c.srclang} label={c.label} default={c.default} />
        ))}
      </video>

      {buffering && <div className="player__spinner" role="status" aria-label="Buffering" />}

      <div className="player__controls" role="group" aria-label="Video controls">
        <button type="button" onClick={togglePlay}
                aria-label={playing ? "Pause" : "Play"} aria-pressed={playing}>
          {playing ? "⏸" : "▶"}
        </button>

        {/* Scrubber: a real slider with full ARIA */}
        <input
          type="range" min={0} max={100} value={Math.round(progress * 100)}
          onChange={e => {
            const v = ref.current; if (v) v.currentTime = (+e.target.value / 100) * v.duration;
          }}
          aria-label="Seek"
          aria-valuetext={`${Math.floor(progress * duration)} of ${Math.floor(duration)} seconds`}
        />

        <button type="button" aria-label={muted ? "Unmute" : "Mute"} aria-pressed={muted}
                onClick={() => { const v = ref.current; if (v) { v.muted = !v.muted; setMuted(v.muted); } }}>
          {muted ? "🔇" : "🔊"}
        </button>

        {captions.length > 0 && (
          <button type="button" onClick={toggleCaptions}
                  aria-label="Captions" aria-pressed={captionsOn}>CC</button>
        )}

        <button type="button" onClick={toggleFullscreen} aria-label="Fullscreen">⛶</button>
      </div>

      {/* Screen-reader announcements for state the visuals convey to sighted users */}
      <div aria-live="polite" className="sr-only">{status}</div>

      {transcriptHref && (
        <figcaption>
          <a href={transcriptHref}>Read the full transcript</a>
        </figcaption>
      )}
    </figure>
  );
}
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:
*   **A keyboard map, not just a clickable** **`<div>`****\*\*\*\*.** Space/K play, arrows seek ±5s, J/L ±10s, M mute, F fullscreen, C captions — the conventions users already know from YouTube. Every native `<button>` also stays Enter/Space-activatable for free.
*   **`aria-pressed`** **on toggles** (play, mute, captions) so screen readers announce state, not just the icon glyph.
*   **The scrubber is a real** **`<input type="range">`** with `aria-valuetext` in human terms ("42 of 180 seconds"), not raw percentages. A `<div>` scrubber is an accessibility dead end.
*   **An** **`aria-live`** **region** narrates state changes (buffering, captions on/off, ended) that sighted users read from the visuals — this is the piece almost every custom player omits.
*   **A transcript link** is part of the component's contract, not an afterthought.
*   **`onClick`** **on the** **`<video>`** **itself** so click-anywhere-to-toggle works, matching user expectation.

### 9.5 Streaming — HLS.js and why you don't ship a 200MB mp4
A single progressive `.mp4` has three fatal flaws at scale: the whole thing must download to guarantee smooth play, it can't adapt when the network weakens (the user just buffers), and seeking re-fetches. **Adaptive bitrate streaming (ABR)** fixes all three. The video is transcoded once into a _ladder_ of renditions (e.g. 240p/480p/720p/1080p), each chopped into ~2–6s segments, described by a **manifest** — HLS's `.m3u8` or DASH's `.mpd`. The player downloads only the next few segments and, every segment, re-picks the bitrate that matches measured bandwidth. Weak connection → it drops to 480p and keeps playing instead of freezing.

Safari plays HLS natively (`<video src="stream.m3u8">` just works). Everyone else needs a JS engine — **hls.js** for HLS, **dash.js** for DASH — that uses Media Source Extensions to feed segments into the `<video>`:

```ts
import Hls from "hls.js";

function attachHls(video: HTMLVideoElement, src: string) {
  // Safari: native HLS, no library needed
  if (video.canPlayType("application/vnd.apple.mpegurl")) {
    video.src = src;
    return;
  }
  // Everyone else: hls.js feeds segments via Media Source Extensions
  if (Hls.isSupported()) {
    const hls = new Hls({ maxBufferLength: 30 }); // don't over-buffer and waste data
    hls.loadSource(src);
    hls.attachMedia(video);
    hls.on(Hls.Events.ERROR, (_e, data) => {
      if (data.fatal) {
        // network vs media error need different recovery
        if (data.type === "networkError") hls.startLoad();
        else if (data.type === "mediaError") hls.recoverMediaError();
        else hls.destroy();
      }
    });
    return hls; // remember to hls.destroy() on unmount
  }
}
```

When to actually stream: **anything over ~30 seconds, anything premium, anything with a global/mobile audience, and every live stream.** For a 5-second muted product loop, a small optimized `.mp4`/`.webm` is fine and simpler. The transcoding itself is a build/upload step — services like Mux, Cloudflare Stream, AWS MediaConvert, or `ffmpeg` generate the ladder and manifest for you; you rarely hand-roll it. **DRM** (Widevine/PlayReady/FairPlay via Encrypted Media Extensions) enters only when you're protecting licensed content — Shaka Player is the usual choice there.

### 9.6 The YouTube facade — the 1MB-embed fix
Dropping a YouTube `<iframe>` on the page loads **~1MB+ of JavaScript and dozens of requests before the user clicks play** — often the single heaviest thing on a marketing page, and it tanks your Lighthouse score. The fix is the **facade pattern**: render a lightweight poster (just an image + a play button) and only swap in the real iframe on click.

```tsx
function YouTubeFacade({ id, title }: { id: string; title: string }) {
  const [active, setActive] = useState(false);
  const poster = `https://i.ytimg.com/vi/${id}/maxresdefault.jpg`;

  if (active) {
    return (
      <iframe
        className="player"
        src={`https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/${id}?autoplay=1`}
        title={title}
        allow="accelerometer; autoplay; encrypted-media; picture-in-picture"
        allowFullScreen
      />
    );
  }
  return (
    <button
      type="button"
      className="player yt-facade"
      style={{ backgroundImage: `url(${poster})` }}
      onClick={() => setActive(true)}
      aria-label={`Play video: ${title}`}
    >
      <span className="yt-facade__play" aria-hidden="true">▶</span>
    </button>
  );
}
```

Notes: use `youtube-nocookie.com` for the privacy-friendlier domain; the button is a real `<button>` with an `aria-label` so it's keyboard-operable and announced; on click you set `autoplay=1` so the user's click carries through to playback (the click counts as the engagement gesture, so autoplay is allowed). The battle-tested drop-in is the `lite-youtube-embed` web component — same idea, hardened. This one change routinely cuts a page's initial JS by a megabyte. It's the video analog of lazy-loading an image (see the Image doc's `loading="lazy"` section).

### 9.7 Background / hero video (muted, looped, and safe)
The wallpaper pattern. Get the attribute set exactly right and honor two off-switches, or it becomes an accessibility and data-cost problem.

```tsx
function HeroVideo({ src, poster }: { src: string; poster: string }) {
  const ref = useRef<HTMLVideoElement>(null);
  const reduceMotion = useReducedMotion();          // matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)')
  const saveData = (navigator as any).connection?.saveData;

  useEffect(() => {
    const v = ref.current; if (!v) return;
    if (reduceMotion || saveData) return;            // don't autoplay: show poster only
    v.play().catch(() => {/* blocked; poster stays */});
  }, [reduceMotion, saveData]);

  return (
    <video
      ref={ref}
      poster={poster}
      muted loop playsInline
      preload={reduceMotion || saveData ? "none" : "auto"}
      aria-hidden="true"                              // decorative: hide from AT
      tabIndex={-1}
    >
      {/* Serve the loop only when we intend to play it */}
      {!(reduceMotion || saveData) && <source src={src} type="video/mp4" />}
    </video>
  );
}
```

The rules, non-negotiable:
*   **`muted loop playsinline`** together — that's what makes autoplay legal and keeps it inline on mobile.
*   **A reduced-motion off-switch:** if the user prefers reduced motion, _don't autoplay_ — show the poster. Autoplaying full-frame motion at people with vestibular disorders is a real harm, not a nitpick.
*   **Respect** **`saveData`** **/ mobile data:** don't pull a multi-megabyte loop over a metered connection. Show the poster, skip the source.
*   **`aria-hidden="true"`** because it's decoration — but still provide a visible pause control if the motion is prominent (WCAG 2.2.2: any motion over 5s needs a mechanism to pause it).
*   Keep the file **short, muted-optimized, and heavily compressed** — a hero loop should be measured in hundreds of KB, not megabytes.

### 9.9 Node.js — serving video with HTTP Range (the seek requirement)
If you serve your own `.mp4`, the endpoint **must** support HTTP `Range` requests, or seeking and even basic playback break in Safari. The browser asks for byte ranges; you answer `206 Partial Content`.

```ts
import express from "express";
import fs from "fs";
const app = express();

app.get("/media/:file", (req, res) => {
  const path = safeResolve(req.params.file);          // guard path traversal!
  const { size } = fs.statSync(path);
  const range = req.headers.range;

  if (!range) {                                        // no range: send whole file
    res.writeHead(200, { "Content-Length": size, "Content-Type": "video/mp4", "X-Content-Type-Options": "nosniff" });
    return fs.createReadStream(path).pipe(res);
  }
  // Parse "bytes=START-END"
  const [startStr, endStr] = range.replace(/bytes=/, "").split("-");
  const start = Number(startStr);
  const end = endStr ? Number(endStr) : Math.min(start + 1_000_000, size - 1);
  res.writeHead(206, {
    "Content-Range": `bytes ${start}-${end}/${size}`,
    "Accept-Ranges": "bytes",
    "Content-Length": end - start + 1,
    "Content-Type": "video/mp4",
    "X-Content-Type-Options": "nosniff",
  });
  fs.createReadStream(path, { start, end }).pipe(res);
});
```

In production you almost never serve video from your app server — you put it on a **CDN / object store** (S3+CloudFront, Cloudflare Stream, Mux) that handles Range, caching, and global edge delivery. Streaming a video through your Node process is a bandwidth and cost mistake. Security notes carry over from any file endpoint: **resolve paths safely (no** **`../`** **traversal)**, sign URLs for private content, and set correct `Content-Type` so browsers don't sniff.

### 9.10 Python (backend + a rendered player)
**Backend** (FastAPI) issuing a signed, expiring stream URL — the click-to-watch equivalent of the Button doc's guarded endpoint:

```python
from fastapi import FastAPI, Depends, HTTPException
import time, hmac, hashlib

app = FastAPI()

@app.get("/api/watch/{video_id}")
def get_stream(video_id: str, user=Depends(require_auth)):
    if not user.can("view", video_id):
        raise HTTPException(403, "Not allowed")
    expires = int(time.time()) + 300                      # 5-minute URL
    sig = hmac.new(SECRET, f"{video_id}:{expires}".encode(), hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
    return {"url": f"https://cdn.ujg.example/{video_id}/master.m3u8?exp={expires}&sig={sig}"}
```

**Rendering** the player server-side (Django/Jinja) — the element still lives in HTML:

```plain
<video controls preload="metadata" poster="{{ video.poster_url }}" playsinline>
  <source src="{{ video.hls_url }}" type="application/vnd.apple.mpegurl">
  {% for cap in video.captions %}
    <track kind="captions" src="{{ cap.url }}" srclang="{{ cap.lang }}"
           label="{{ cap.label }}" {% if cap.default %}default{% endif %}>
  {% endfor %}
</video>
```

Python's job here is what Node's was: authorize the viewer, sign the URL, render the markup. The player itself is always HTML/JS.

### 9.11 Testing the player
You test _behavior and accessibility_, not pixels. Media is famously hard to test because jsdom has no real decode pipeline — so you mock the media element's methods.

```ts
import { render, screen, fireEvent } from "@testing-library/react";
import userEvent from "@testing-library/user-event";
import { axe } from "jest-axe";
import { VideoPlayer } from "./VideoPlayer";

// jsdom doesn't implement play/pause — mock them
beforeAll(() => {
  window.HTMLMediaElement.prototype.play = vi.fn().mockResolvedValue(undefined);
  window.HTMLMediaElement.prototype.pause = vi.fn();
});

const props = {
  sources: [{ src: "/d.mp4", type: "video/mp4" }],
  captions: [{ src: "/d.en.vtt", srclang: "en", label: "English", default: true }],
};

it("exposes a play control reachable by role", () => {
  render(<VideoPlayer {...props} />);
  expect(screen.getByRole("button", { name: /play/i })).toBeInTheDocument();
});

it("toggles play state and announces it", async () => {
  render(<VideoPlayer {...props} />);
  await userEvent.click(screen.getByRole("button", { name: /play/i }));
  expect(window.HTMLMediaElement.prototype.play).toHaveBeenCalled();
});

it("Space toggles playback (keyboard)", async () => {
  render(<VideoPlayer {...props} />);
  screen.getByRole("group", { name: /video controls/i });
  fireEvent.keyDown(document.querySelector(".player")!, { key: " " });
  expect(window.HTMLMediaElement.prototype.play).toHaveBeenCalled();
});

it("renders a captions track", () => {
  const { container } = render(<VideoPlayer {...props} />);

});

});
```

What to assert (and skip):
*   **Do test:** controls are reachable `byRole`, click _and_ keyboard toggle playback, a captions `<track>` exists, the a11y region announces, `axe` passes.
*   **Don't test:** exact `currentTime` values from a fake clock, icon glyphs, or CSS. jsdom's media timeline is fiction.
*   **Real playback (Playwright).** Only an actual browser plays media — use E2E to assert the poster shows, clicking starts playback, and captions render. Point it at a tiny fixture clip, not your production stream.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
Video is the highest-stakes accessibility component on the web because it excludes people across _multiple_ senses at once. The bar, concretely:
*   **Captions (WCAG 1.2.2, Level A):** every prerecorded video with audio needs synchronized captions via `<track kind="captions">`. Captions include speaker IDs and important non-speech sound ("\[door slams\]"); subtitles are translation only and don't satisfy the requirement. This is the spine — no captions, not shippable.
*   **Audio description (WCAG 1.2.3/1.2.5):** for content where the visuals carry information the audio doesn't (a silent demo, on-screen text), provide a description track or an alternate described version so blind users aren't left out.
*   **Transcript:** a full text transcript beneath the player serves deaf-blind users, is skimmable/searchable, and is great for SEO. Not strictly required if captions + description exist, but the cheapest high-value add there is.
*   **Keyboard operable:** everything works without a mouse. Native `controls` gives this free. A custom player must implement the map in §9.4 — Space/K play, arrows seek, M mute, F fullscreen, C captions — with a logical tab order through the control bar.
*   **Visible focus:** every control shows a `:focus-visible` ring (Goldenrod, in UJG). Controls that appear on hover must also appear on `:focus-within` or keyboard users can't see what they're operating.
*   **ARIA on custom controls:** icon-only buttons need `aria-label`; toggles use `aria-pressed` (play, mute, captions); the scrubber is a real slider with `aria-valuetext` in human units; the control cluster is a labeled `role="group"`; state changes route through an `aria-live="polite"` region.
*   **No autoplay-with-sound, ever**, and no autoplay of motion under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **No seizure triggers:** nothing flashes more than 3 times per second (WCAG 2.3.1).
*   **Touch targets ≥ 44px** on every control; **contrast ≥ 4.5:1** for control icons against the video — which is why control bars sit on a scrim gradient, not bare over unpredictable frames.
*   **Respect OS caption styling** on native platforms (users set caption size/color at the system level; honor it).

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas (2026-aware)
*   **AI auto-captions and transcripts:** Whisper-class speech-to-text now generates a first-draft VTT in seconds. The 2026 move is auto-generate, then _human-review_ — machine captions are a starting point, not a compliance checkbox (auto-caption errors still fail WCAG).
*   **Auto audio description via AI:** generative description of on-screen action is emerging (vision models narrating the visuals into a `descriptions` track). Promising, still needs review for anything high-stakes.
*   **AV1 and the codec shift:** AV1 delivers ~30% smaller files than H.264 at equal quality and is now broadly decodable (often hardware-accelerated). Serving AV1 first with an H.264 fallback is the 2026 bandwidth win.
*   **The** **`MediaSession`** **API:** wire your custom player into OS media controls (lock screen, headphone buttons, car displays) with `navigator.mediaSession.setActionHandler`. Makes a web player feel native.
*   **WebCodecs + WebGL/WebGPU:** low-level frame access enables in-browser editing, real-time filters, and frame-accurate scrubbing beyond what `<video>` alone offers.
*   **Low-latency live (LL-HLS / WebRTC):** sub-second live latency is now practical for interactive streams (auctions, sports, watch-parties).
*   **View Transitions for video:** morphing a thumbnail into a full player (the "expand to play" gesture) using the native View Transitions API instead of heavy JS.
*   **Interactive / shoppable video:** clickable hotspots and chapter-linked CTAs layered over the media surface, driven by the chapters track.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers (the silent bleeders)
None of these throw an error. They quietly cost you money, trust, and legal exposure.
*   **Autoplay with sound.** The fastest way to make a user slam the tab shut. Muted-only, always.
*   **No captions.** Excludes deaf users (and the majority watching muted), fails WCAG, and invites the demand letter. The single most common serious media defect.
*   **Shipping a giant progressive** **`.mp4`****\*\*\*\*.** The user stares at a spinner, burns their data, and leaves. Stream anything substantial.
*   **`preload="auto"`** **everywhere.** Silently pulls megabytes for videos nobody scrolls to. Default to `metadata`, or `none` behind a facade.
*   **No poster / no reserved box.** Black flash, then a layout jump as dimensions load (CLS). Set `poster` and lock `aspect-ratio`.
*   **Raw YouTube iframes.** A megabyte of third-party JS before anyone clicks play, wrecking your Core Web Vitals. Facade it.
*   **No buffering state.** The video hitches and the user thinks it's broken. Show the spinner on `waiting`.
*   **A silent error state.** Source fails, user sees a black box with no explanation and no retry. Handle the `error` event with a real message.
*   **Ignoring** **`prefers-reduced-motion`** **on hero loops.** Not a nicety — a vestibular-safety and accessibility failure.
*   **A** **`<div>`** **scrubber.** Looks fine with a mouse, completely unusable by keyboard and screen reader. Use a real slider.

**The through-line:** every one of these fails _quietly_ — no crash, just a higher bounce rate, a worse Lighthouse score, or a compliance risk that surfaces as a lawyer's letter. Audit your video before you blame the funnel.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
*   **IntersectionObserver-driven play/pause.** Play muted loops only while in view; pause (and free the decoder) when scrolled away. Essential for feed video and multiple loops on one page:

```js
const io = new IntersectionObserver((entries) => {
  entries.forEach(e => e.isIntersecting ? e.target.play().catch(()=>{}) : e.target.pause());
}, { threshold: 0.5 });
document.querySelectorAll("video[data-autoplay]").forEach(v => io.observe(v));
```

*   **Chapters from a WebVTT chapters track** to render a segmented scrubber with labeled sections and jump-to-chapter — read `video.textTracks` where `kind === "chapters"` and paint the cues onto the timeline.
*   **Picture-in-Picture:** `video.requestPictureInPicture()` (feature-detect first) lets users pop the video out and keep browsing. Pair with the MediaSession API for OS controls.
*   **Thumbnail preview on scrub:** a sprite sheet (a "storyboard" VTT of thumbnail coordinates) shows a preview frame as the user drags the scrubber — the polish move on premium players.
*   **Design tokens for the player.** Skin the control bar through the same primitive→semantic→component token tiers the Button doc describes, so the player rethemes without touching player logic (`--player-accent: var(--color-focus)` etc.).
*   **TypeScript-enforced captions.** Make the caption prop non-optional on the content-video variant of your component so a teammate _cannot_ ship a captionless content player — the type system enforces the a11y rule, exactly like the Button doc's discriminated-union `aria-label` trick.
*   **Save-Data and Network Information API branching.** Serve a lower rendition, skip autoplay, or drop the hero loop entirely when `navigator.connection.saveData` or `effectiveType` signals a constrained connection.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
Video is where "the page is slow" usually actually lives.
*   **Bytes over the wire dwarf everything.** One un-facaded YouTube embed ≈ 1MB+ of JS; one `preload="auto"` HD video can be tens of MB. This is the biggest single lever on most pages — bigger than every image and script combined. Facade embeds, default `preload` to `metadata`/`none`, and stream anything long.
*   **Poster image cost.** The poster loads eagerly and is often the LCP element — optimize it like any hero image (right dimensions, modern format, see the Image doc). A 2MB poster defeats the point of a facade.
*   **Decoder pressure.** Each playing video holds a hardware decoder; several autoplay loops at once thrash it and drain battery. IntersectionObserver-gate them so only visible ones play.
*   **Streaming-engine weight.** hls.js is ~100–400KB depending on build — real, but it earns its keep for adaptive delivery. Load it dynamically (`import()`) only on pages that need it, and skip it entirely on Safari (native HLS).
*   **`content-visibility: auto`** on off-screen player containers lets the browser skip their render/layout work until scrolled near.
*   **Lazy-load the player component.** Code-split the custom player (and its streaming engine) so pages without video never pay for it.
*   **CDN, not your origin.** Serving video from your app server is a cost and latency mistake — edge-cache it. (See §9.9.)
* * *

## 15\. Security (video reaches the server too)
*   **Signed, expiring URLs for private content.** Don't hand out permanent public URLs to gated video. Sign short-lived manifest/segment URLs and re-check authorization server-side (§9.10) — the UI hiding a video is never the boundary.
*   **DRM (EME) for licensed content.** Widevine/PlayReady/FairPlay via Encrypted Media Extensions when you're contractually protecting content. Shaka Player is the common path. Understand it slows nothing for legit users but is real integration work.
*   **Path-traversal & content-type on self-served files.** Resolve file paths safely (no `../`), set the correct `Content-Type`, and disable content sniffing so an uploaded "video" can't be served as executable.
*   **User-uploaded video is hostile input.** Transcode uploads through a sandboxed pipeline (ffmpeg in an isolated worker) — never trust a client-declared codec/container, and strip metadata that can leak location/identity.
*   **CORS for cross-origin tracks.** `crossorigin` must be set correctly or captions from another origin fail; but also don't open your VTT/media buckets to `*` if the content is private.
*   **Hotlink / bandwidth abuse.** Referer checks, signed URLs, and per-IP rate limits protect against others embedding your (expensive) video on their sites.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same player, eleven skins)
Design systems are _whose_ rules; visual styles are _which look_. The same semantic player — same `<video>`, same keyboard map, same captions, same 44px targets — can wear any of these. **Style is skin; behavior is the skeleton.** Full guidance in the [Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) doc.
*   **Flat:** solid control bar, crisp icons, no shadow. Reads instantly, scales fast.
*   **Material:** elevated control surface, ripple on control press, bold accent scrubber, uppercase time labels.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent control bar with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over the video. Looks premium — but guard icon contrast against unpredictable frames with a scrim behind the blur.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language — the control bar is a refractive pill that bends the frame behind it with a specular rim and a moving sheen, not a static blur. Native `AVPlayerViewController` emits it for free; the web approximates with layered highlights. Gorgeous over video, but guard contrast _and_ `prefers-reduced-motion` (kill the sheen).
*   **Neumorphism:** soft same-color extruded controls. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent only, never the captions button (which must stay legible).
*   **Skeuomorphism:** the "real remote" look — beveled play button, brushed-metal scrubber, tactile knobs. Retro, heavy, characterful.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** hard offset shadow on the control bar, thick borders, zero radius, one clashing accent on the scrubber. Loud and editorial.
*   **Claymorphism:** puffy rounded controls, soft inner-light, friendly big play button. Great for kid/education content.

Captions styled in Platinum (`#E8E6E1`) for legibility. Lean Liquid Glass accents over the dark Eminence surface for hero moments; hold Flat/Swiss for tutorial and data content.

**The rule that never changes:** across all eleven skins, the semantics, the keyboard map, the captions track, the focus ring, and the 44px targets stay identical. You're restyling the remote, never rewiring the engine.
* * *

**Bottom line:** a video player is the heaviest, most exclusionary, and most legally-exposed component you can ship — and almost every failure is quiet. Nail the four things that matter most — **captions on every video, muted-and-reduced-motion-aware autoplay, a reserved box with a poster so nothing shifts, and adaptive streaming (plus a facade for embeds) so you don't blow the byte budget** — and you've handled the part that separates a real video product from a `.mp4` with a play button. The native `<video>` already gives you a fully accessible player for free; only build your own when you're prepared to re-earn every bit of that accessibility by hand.
