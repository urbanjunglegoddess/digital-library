## 1\. What a Field Actually Is
A **Field** is the composition unit that wraps a form control and gives it context: a **label**, an optional **help/description**, an **error message**, a **required/optional marker**, and — critically — the _programmatic wiring_ that binds all of those to the control so assistive tech reads them as one thing.

The single most important distinction in this whole doc, and the one juniors flatten: **a Field is not an input.**

*   **Field (wrapper / composition):** the layout-and-semantics envelope. It owns the label, the help text, the error slot, the required marker, the spacing, and the id wiring. It renders _around_ a control. It has no `value` of its own.
*   **Input / Select / Textarea / Checkbox (the control):** the thing that actually holds state, takes keystrokes, and submits a value. The Field wraps it; it is not the Field.

Read the sibling **Inputs** doc for the control itself — types (`email`, `password`, `number`), masking, autofill, `inputmode`, validation constraints. _This_ doc is about everything _around_ the control. Same relationship as a picture frame to the photo: the frame (Field) is reusable and consistent; the photo (Input, Select, Combobox, Date picker) swaps in and out. A well-built Field wraps _any_ control without caring which one.

Three more distinctions people get wrong:

*   **Field vs. Form.** A Form is the `<form>` — the collection, the submit boundary, the action. A Field is one row inside it. A form has many fields.
*   **Field vs. Fieldset.** A `<fieldset>` groups _multiple_ related controls under one shared `<legend>` (a set of radios, a pair of "from/to" dates, an address block). A single Field wraps _one_ control with its _own_ label. Grouped controls need a fieldset because a single `<label>` can't caption three radio buttons at once — see §9.16.
*   **Field vs. Form Group / Form Item.** Different libraries name the same wrapper differently: Bootstrap's `.mb-3` form group, Ant's `Form.Item`, MUI's `FormControl`, shadcn's `FormItem`. Same job — the composition envelope. "Field" is the vendor-neutral name.

Rule of thumb: **if it holds a value, it's a control; if it labels, describes, validates, and spaces that control, it's a Field.** Never conflate the two, and never build a form by scattering bare `<input>`s with sibling `<label>`s and hoping the browser guesses the association.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The Field is where a form becomes _usable_ or becomes a wall. It carries weight far beyond its visual footprint:

*   **Form completion is conversion.** Signup, checkout, lead capture, intake — every dollar that flows through a form flows through fields. A field with an unclear label, a buried error, or a missing help text is measured directly in abandonment. The field is the unit where users get stuck, and a stuck user leaves.
*   **It's the accessibility make-or-break.** More than any other component, forms are where screen-reader and keyboard users succeed or fail. A label that isn't _programmatically_ associated is invisible to a screen reader even when it's visible on screen. An error announced only in red text does not exist for a blind user. The Field is the single component where accessibility is least optional and most often botched.
*   **Error recovery is trust.** The gap between "we couldn't process your form" and "your card's expiry date is in the past — try again" is the gap between a rage-quit and a completed purchase. The Field owns error placement, wording, and announcement. Get it right and users self-correct; get it wrong and they call support or disappear.
*   **It sets the perceived professionalism of the whole product.** Misaligned labels, help text that appears _after_ the error, required markers that mean nothing, a form that scrolls the user _away_ from the field that failed — these read as "unfinished" even when the backend is flawless.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy of a Field
Every well-built field is composed of predictable parts. Most are optional; the wiring between them is not.

*   **Label:** the caption. A real `<label>` element, programmatically bound to the control via `for`/`id`. Answers "what do I type here?" Always visible for text entry (placeholders are _not_ labels — see §12).
*   **Required / optional marker:** the `*`, the "(required)", or the "(optional)" suffix. A _convention_, not a mechanism — the machine-readable signal is `aria-required` / `required` on the control (§10).
*   **Control slot:** the wrapper's one job is to render _a_ control here — input, select, textarea, checkbox group, combobox, whatever. The Field is control-agnostic.
*   **Description / help text:** persistent guidance ("Use 8+ characters," "We'll never share this"). Sits below the label or below the control, and is tied to the control with `aria-describedby` so it's _announced_, not just shown.
*   **Error message:** the validation failure. Replaces or joins the help text, is _also_ referenced by `aria-describedby`, flips `aria-invalid="true"` on the control, and lives in a live region so it's announced the moment it appears.
*   **The wiring (invisible but load-bearing):** `label[for] → control[id]`; `control[aria-describedby] → description id + error id`; `control[aria-invalid]`; `control[aria-required]`. This is the actual Field. Everything else is paint.

A field's DOM order, top to bottom, is almost always: **label → (description) → control → (error)**. The description-above-control placement matters — screen readers and sighted users both benefit from reading the guidance _before_ they act.
* * *

## 4\. Sizing, Spacing & Density
A Field's "size" is mostly _spacing_ — the control has its own height (that's the Inputs doc); the Field owns the vertical rhythm around it. A workable scale:

| Token | Value | Applies to |
| ---| ---| --- |
| `--field-label-gap` | 6px | Label → control |
| `--field-help-gap` | 6px | Control → description/error |
| `--field-row-gap` | 20px | Field → next field (comfortable) |
| `--field-row-gap-dense` | 12px | Dense/admin forms |
| `--field-label-size` | 14px | Label font size |
| `--field-help-size` | 13px | Description + error font size |
| `--field-inline-label-w` | 160px | Fixed label column in inline layout |

Rules that matter:

*   **Spacing is a token, not a magic number.** The gap between label and control must be visibly _smaller_ than the gap between one field and the next, or the eye can't tell which label belongs to which control (proximity = grouping, Gestalt law).
*   **Help and error text share one size** and one slot position, so swapping error for help causes zero layout shift.
*   **Reserve the error line's height** (or animate it in from a min-height slot) so the form doesn't jump every time validation fires — a jumping form is the #1 tell of an unspaced build.
*   **Label column width** in inline layouts should be fixed and wide enough for the longest label _after translation_ (§9.18), or labels wrap and destroy alignment.
* * *

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A field has more meaningful states than a button, because it composes the control's state with its own validation state. Missing states are what separate a real form from a prototype.

1. **Default (rest)** — label + control + optional help. No validation has run.
2. **Focused** — the _control_ has focus; the Field may raise the label emphasis or show a focus ring around the whole row. Focus lives on the control, never on the wrapper.
3. **Filled / dirty** — the user has entered a value. Relevant for floating-label layouts (the label stays lifted) and for "only validate touched fields" logic.
4. **Invalid / error** — validation failed. `aria-invalid="true"` on the control, error text shown and referenced, error announced. The loudest state; must never rely on color alone (§10).
5. **Valid / success** — passed validation. Often a subtle checkmark. Use sparingly — a green tick on every field is noise; reserve it for fields that were _previously_ in error or that carry real uncertainty (username availability).
6. **Disabled** — not currently editable and _not submitted_. Reduced opacity, `disabled` on the control. The label should dim with it.
7. **Read-only** — value shown, not editable, _but still submitted and still focusable/copyable_. `readonly` ≠ `disabled` — a classic mix-up. Use read-only for "you can see it, you can't change it here."
8. **Required** — must be filled. Marked visually _and_ via `aria-required`/`required`.
9. **Optional** — explicitly not required. In a mostly-required form, mark the _optional_ ones instead of starring everything (§12).
10. **Pending / validating** — async check in flight (username taken? promo code valid?). Show a spinner in the field and set `aria-busy`; announce the result via the live region.

Bonus: **partially complete group** (a fieldset where some but not all radios/checkboxes are chosen) and **indeterminate** (tri-state checkbox inside a field) — both belong to the control but the Field owns their error/help messaging.
* * *

## 6\. Layout Variants
The Field's variants are almost entirely about **layout** and **which parts are present** — the semantics never change with the layout.

*   **Stacked / label-on-top:** label above the control, full-width control. The default for most web forms and _all_ mobile. Scannable, translation-proof, forgiving of long labels. When in doubt, stack.
*   **Inline / horizontal:** label in a fixed left column, control to the right. Compact, dense, good for settings panels and desktop admin. Fragile under translation and bad on narrow screens (collapse to stacked below a breakpoint).
*   **Floating label:** label sits inside the control at rest, animates up to a caption when focused/filled. Space-saving and trendy, but has real accessibility and usability traps (empty-looking fields, tiny lifted labels, clashing with browser autofill) — use deliberately, never as the default (§12).
*   **Field with description:** adds persistent help text. The workhorse for anything non-obvious.
*   **Field with error:** the invalid variant — error text present, help text hidden or joined.
*   **Grouped field (fieldset + legend):** multiple controls, one shared caption. Radio groups, checkbox lists, segmented date/phone inputs, address blocks. This is its own pattern — see §9.16.
*   **Compound field:** several inputs that read as one logical value (card number + expiry + CVC; area code + number). One legend, sub-labels per part, one combined error.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a Field wrapper around **every** data-entry control that a user fills in. There is essentially no bare input in production that shouldn't be wrapped — the wrapper is where the label association and error slot live, and both are mandatory. Specifics:

*   **Always wrap** text inputs, selects, textareas, comboboxes, date pickers, file inputs. Each gets its own Field with its own label.
*   **Use a fieldset (grouped Field), not many single Fields,** when several controls answer _one_ question: a set of radio options, a checklist, "from/to" date range, a multi-part phone or card number. The `<legend>` is the shared question; per-control `<label>`s are the answers.
*   **Don't wrap a standalone submit/reset button in a Field** — a button isn't a data-entry control and has no label/error/required semantics. That's the Button doc.
*   **Don't invent a Field for a purely presentational readout** — if the user can't edit it and it isn't submitted, it's text, not a field. (Read-only is the edge: submitted-but-not-editable _is_ a field.)
*   **Don't stack a placeholder in place of a label** to "save space." That's not a layout variant, it's an accessibility bug (§12).

Placement heuristics:

*   **One field per row** on mobile and for anything important; only pair fields side-by-side (city/state, first/last) when they're short and obviously related.
*   **Order fields by the mental model,** not the database schema — name before email before password, not `id`, `created_at`, `email`.
*   **Keep the error adjacent to its field,** never in a summary box far from the control (a summary _plus_ inline errors is fine; a summary _instead of_ inline is a recovery killer).

## 8\. Fields Across Design Systems
Same wrapper, different names and defaults. Fluency here means reading any codebase's form layer instantly.

*   **Material Design (Google):** the `TextField` bundles the field _and_ the control — label (floating by default), "supporting text" (help), error text, and a character counter, all in one component. Filled and outlined variants. Helper text and error text share the same slot. Material's opinion: the wrapper and control are one unit.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** grouped-list forms. Labels often sit _left_ of the control in inset grouped tables; validation is quiet and often deferred to submit. Less "error text under every field," more "gentle inline correction." SwiftUI's `Form` + `Section` + `LabeledContent` express it.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** the `Field` component is explicit and well-named — `label`, `validationMessage`, `validationState`, `hint`. One of the cleaner mental models: a Field _slot_ that any control drops into. Compact, enterprise-dense.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** `Form.Item` is the wrapper — it owns `label`, `required`, `help`, `validateStatus`, `extra`, and wires into Ant's `Form` for validation. Label-column / wrapper-column grid props control inline vs. stacked. Built for dense data-entry apps.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** no prescribed Field; you compose `<label>` + control + help `<p>` + error `<p>` from utilities and _must_ wire the ids yourself. Freedom plus the risk of forgetting `aria-describedby` on every field — which is exactly why teams wrap it.
*   **shadcn/ui + react-hook-form (2024+ web standard):** the `Form` primitives — `FormField`, `FormItem`, `FormLabel`, `FormControl`, `FormDescription`, `FormMessage` — auto-generate ids and wire `htmlFor`, `aria-describedby`, and `aria-invalid` through React context so you literally cannot forget them. The gold standard for accessible composition; §9.7.
*   **Radix Form:** headless `Form.Field` / `Form.Label` / `Form.Control` / `Form.Message` with built-in `matchable` validation and correct wiring. Behavior and a11y provided; styling is yours.

The through-line: every serious system has _a wrapper_ — TextField, Field, Form.Item, FormItem. The immature codebases are the ones with bare inputs and hand-rolled sibling labels.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML (the foundation everyone should master first)
The wiring is the whole point. Master this and every framework below is just sugar over it.

```plain
<!-- The canonical text field: label + help + control + error, all wired -->
<div class="field">
  <label for="email" class="field__label">
    Email address
    <span class="field__req" aria-hidden="true">*</span>
  </label>

  <p id="email-help" class="field__help">
    We'll only use this to send your receipt.
  </p>

  <input
    type="email"
    id="email"
    name="email"
    class="field__control"
    required
    aria-required="true"
    aria-describedby="email-help email-error"
    aria-invalid="true"
  />

  <!-- Live region so the error is ANNOUNCED, not just shown -->
  <p id="email-error" class="field__error" role="alert">
    Enter a valid email, like name@example.com.
  </p>
</div>
```

Key HTML rules — these are the load-bearing lines:

*   **`label[for]`** **must equal** **`control[id]`****\*\*\*\*.** This is the programmatic association. Without it, a screen reader announces "edit text, blank" — no label at all. Wrapping the input _inside_ the `<label>` also works and needs no id, but explicit `for`/`id` is more flexible and required the moment the control isn't a direct child.
*   **`aria-describedby`** **takes a** **_space-separated list_** **of ids** — point it at _both_ the help and the error. Both get announced after the label. This is the single most-forgotten attribute in form building.
*   **`aria-invalid="true"`** on the _control_ (never the wrapper) tells AT the value is wrong. Remove it (or set `"false"`) when the field is valid.
*   **`required`** **+** **`aria-required="true"`** — `required` gives you native browser validation and the semantic; `aria-required` guarantees the announcement across older AT. Belt and suspenders.
*   **The error is** **`role="alert"`** (an assertive live region) so it interrupts and is read the instant it's inserted. Help text is _not_ a live region — it's static, referenced by `describedby`.
*   **The** **`*`** **is** **`aria-hidden`** — the machine reads "required" from `aria-required`, not from a glyph it might announce as "star" or "asterisk."

### 9.2 CSS (states done right)
The Field owns spacing and the visual expression of state; it reads its colors from tokens (§9.22).

```css
.field {
  display: flex;
  flex-direction: column;
  gap: var(--field-help-gap, 6px);
  margin-block-end: var(--field-row-gap, 20px);
}
.field__label {
  font: 500 var(--field-label-size, 14px)/1.3 system-ui, sans-serif;
  color: var(--field-label-color, #E8E6E1);
  margin-block-end: var(--field-label-gap, 6px);
}
.field__req { color: var(--field-danger, #E86100); margin-inline-start: 2px; }

.field__control {
  min-height: 44px;                 /* tap target, even if the visual is smaller */
  padding: 0 12px;
  border: 1px solid var(--field-border, #5F2C82);
  border-radius: 8px;
  background: var(--field-bg, #0A0A0A);
  color: inherit;
}
.field__control:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid var(--field-focus, #DCA424);
  outline-offset: 2px;
}

.field__help  { font-size: var(--field-help-size, 13px); color: var(--field-help-color, #9a90a8); }
.field__error { font-size: var(--field-help-size, 13px); color: var(--field-danger, #E86100); min-height: 1lh; }

/* Error state: driven by aria-invalid on the CONTROL, not a wrapper class.
   This keeps the source of truth in one place. */
.field__control[aria-invalid="true"] {
  border-color: var(--field-danger, #E86100);
}
.field__control[aria-invalid="true"]:focus-visible {
  outline-color: var(--field-danger, #E86100);
}

/* Disabled dims the whole row, including the label */
.field:has(.field__control:disabled) { opacity: .55; }
.field__control:disabled { cursor: not-allowed; }

/* Read-only is NOT disabled: still legible, still selectable */
.field__control:read-only { background: var(--field-readonly-bg, #16121c); }
```

Notice the error style keys off **`[aria-invalid="true"]`**, not a `.is-error` class. Tie the _visual_ to the same attribute the _screen reader_ reads, and the two can never drift apart — you can't accidentally paint a field red without also telling AT it's invalid. `:has()` lets the wrapper react to the control's disabled state without a JS-toggled class. Don't rely on color alone for the error border — the error _text_ and `aria-invalid` carry the meaning; the color reinforces it.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript — the compound component
This is the block that matters most. A Field should be a **compound component** — `Field`, `Field.Label`, `Field.Description`, `Field.Error`, `Field.Control` — sharing generated ids through context so _you can never forget the wiring_. This is the shadcn/Radix Form pattern, built from scratch so you understand it.

```plain
import {
  createContext, useContext, useId, forwardRef,
  type ReactNode, type LabelHTMLAttributes, type HTMLAttributes,
} from "react";

/* The context is the wiring hub: every part reads the shared ids from here. */
interface FieldContextValue {
  id: string;            // the control's id  -> label[for], name anchor
  descriptionId: string; // help text id      -> aria-describedby
  errorId: string;       // error text id     -> aria-describedby
  invalid: boolean;
  required: boolean;
}
const FieldContext = createContext<FieldContextValue | null>(null);

function useField() {
  const ctx = useContext(FieldContext);
  if (!ctx) throw new Error("Field.* must be used inside <Field>");
  return ctx;
}

interface FieldProps extends HTMLAttributes<HTMLDivElement> {
  invalid?: boolean;
  required?: boolean;
  children: ReactNode;
}

/* The wrapper: generates ONE stable id and derives the rest. */
export function Field({ invalid = false, required = false, children, ...rest }: FieldProps) {
  const id = useId();
  const value: FieldContextValue = {
    id,
    descriptionId: `${id}-description`,
    errorId: `${id}-error`,
    invalid,
    required,
  };
  return (
    <FieldContext.Provider value={value}>
      <div className="field" data-invalid={invalid || undefined} {...rest}>
        {children}
      </div>
    </FieldContext.Provider>
  );
}

/* Label: htmlFor is wired automatically. */
function FieldLabel({ children, ...rest }: LabelHTMLAttributes<HTMLLabelElement>) {
  const { id, required } = useField();
  return (
    <label htmlFor={id} className="field__label" {...rest}>
      {children}
      {required && <span className="field__req" aria-hidden="true">*</span>}
    </label>
  );
}

/* Description: owns the descriptionId that the control points at. */
function FieldDescription({ children, ...rest }: HTMLAttributes<HTMLParagraphElement>) {
  const { descriptionId } = useField();
  return <p id={descriptionId} className="field__help" {...rest}>{children}</p>;
}

/* Error: live region, only rendered when there's a message. */
function FieldError({ children, ...rest }: HTMLAttributes<HTMLParagraphElement>) {
  const { errorId, invalid } = useField();
  if (!invalid || !children) return null;
  return (
    <p id={errorId} className="field__error" role="alert" {...rest}>
      {children}
    </p>
  );
}

/* Control: the slot. Clones the child control and injects EVERY wiring attr
   so no consumer ever has to remember them. Works with input/select/textarea. */
interface FieldControlProps {
  children: (props: {
    id: string;
    "aria-describedby": string;
    "aria-invalid": boolean | undefined;
    "aria-required": boolean | undefined;
  }) => ReactNode;
}
function FieldControl({ children }: FieldControlProps) {
  const { id, descriptionId, errorId, invalid, required } = useField();
  return (
    <>
      {children({
        id,
        // point at BOTH help and error; the browser ignores ids that don't exist
        "aria-describedby": `${descriptionId} ${errorId}`,
        "aria-invalid": invalid || undefined,
        "aria-required": required || undefined,
      })}
    </>
  );
}

Field.Label = FieldLabel;
Field.Description = FieldDescription;
Field.Error = FieldError;
Field.Control = FieldControl;
```

Usage — the consumer writes declarative markup and gets bulletproof wiring for free:

```plain
<Field required invalid={!!errors.email}>
  <Field.Label>Email address</Field.Label>
  <Field.Description>We'll only use this for your receipt.</Field.Description>
  <Field.Control>
    {(a11y) => (
      <input type="email" name="email" className="field__control" {...a11y} />
    )}
  </Field.Control>
  <Field.Error>{errors.email}</Field.Error>
</Field>
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:

*   **`useId()`** **generates SSR-safe, collision-proof ids.** Never hand-write `id="email"` in a component that might render twice on a page (a login form and a footer newsletter both wanting `#email` is a real, common bug — duplicate ids break `for`/`describedby` silently).
*   **The wiring is** **_centralized_****\*\*\*\*.** `aria-describedby`, `aria-invalid`, `aria-required`, and `htmlFor` are derived once in context. A consumer physically cannot ship a field with an unlabeled control or an unreferenced error.
*   **`Field.Control`** **uses render-props** so it wraps _any_ control — input, select, textarea, a custom combobox — injecting the same a11y contract into each. The Field stays control-agnostic (the whole thesis of §1).
*   **`aria-invalid`****/****`aria-required`** **collapse to** **`undefined`** **when false** so the attributes simply aren't emitted rather than rendering `aria-invalid="false"` everywhere (cleaner DOM, same semantics).

### 9.4 The validation contract (client ↔ server)
A Field displays errors; it does not _decide_ them — validation logic is shared between client (fast feedback) and server (source of truth). The Field just needs a consistent **error shape** keyed by field name.

```plain
// One schema, reused on both sides (zod). The field names ARE the keys.
import { z } from "zod";

export const SignupSchema = z.object({
  email: z.string().email("Enter a valid email, like name@example.com."),
  password: z.string().min(8, "Use at least 8 characters."),
  age: z.coerce.number().int().min(13, "You must be 13 or older."),
});

// The universal shape the Field layer consumes: { fieldName: message }
export type FieldErrors = Partial<Record<keyof z.infer<typeof SignupSchema>, string>>;

export function toFieldErrors(err: z.ZodError): FieldErrors {
  const out: FieldErrors = {};
  for (const issue of err.issues) {
    const key = issue.path[0] as keyof FieldErrors;
    if (!out[key]) out[key] = issue.message; // first error per field wins
  }
  return out;
}
```

The Field consumes `errors[name]` — nothing more. Whether that string came from the client schema or from the server, the wrapper renders it identically into the `role="alert"` slot and flips `aria-invalid`. **The client validation is a courtesy; the server validation is the law** — never trust the browser to be the only gate (§9.24).

### 9.5 Vanilla JavaScript (wiring + live announcement, no framework)
The building block every framework abstracts. This is how you'd wire a field and announce an error by hand.

```plain
const input = document.querySelector("#email");
const errorEl = document.querySelector("#email-error");

function setError(message) {
  if (message) {
    input.setAttribute("aria-invalid", "true");
    // ensure the error id is in the describedby list (idempotent)
    const ids = new Set((input.getAttribute("aria-describedby") || "").split(/\s+/).filter(Boolean));
    ids.add("email-error");
    input.setAttribute("aria-describedby", [...ids].join(" "));
    errorEl.textContent = message;   // role="alert" -> announced on insert
  } else {
    input.removeAttribute("aria-invalid");
    errorEl.textContent = "";
  }
}

// Validate on blur (not every keystroke), re-validate on input ONCE it's been touched.
let touched = false;
input.addEventListener("blur", () => {
  touched = true;
  setError(input.validity.valid ? "" : "Enter a valid email, like name@example.com.");
});
input.addEventListener("input", () => {
  if (touched) setError(input.validity.valid ? "" : "Enter a valid email, like name@example.com.");
});
```

Two senior details baked in: **validate on blur, not on every keystroke** (don't scream "invalid email" while someone is still typing the first letter), and once a field has been touched and shown an error, **re-validate live on input** so the error clears the instant they fix it. That "eager then lazy" rhythm is what separates a forgiving form from an annoying one (§9.20).

### 9.6 Tailwind CSS (utility-first)

### 9.7 shadcn/ui + react-hook-form (the 2024+ standard)
The reason this stack won: the `Form` primitives _generate and wire every id for you_ through context. You write semantic parts; `FormControl` injects `id`, `aria-describedby`, and `aria-invalid` into whatever control it wraps.

```plain
"use client";
import { useForm } from "react-hook-form";
import { zodResolver } from "@hookform/resolvers/zod";
import {
  Form, FormControl, FormDescription, FormField,
  FormItem, FormLabel, FormMessage,
} from "@/components/ui/form";
import { Input } from "@/components/ui/input";
import { Button } from "@/components/ui/button";
import { SignupSchema } from "@/lib/schema";

export function EmailField() {
  const form = useForm({
    resolver: zodResolver(SignupSchema),
    defaultValues: { email: "" },
    mode: "onTouched", // eager-then-lazy: validate on first blur, live-correct after (§9.20)
  });

  return (
    <Form {...form}>
      <form onSubmit={form.handleSubmit((v) => console.log(v))}>
        <FormField
          control={form.control}
          name="email"
          render={({ field }) => (
            <FormItem>
              <FormLabel>Email address</FormLabel>
              <FormControl>
                {/* FormControl injects id + aria-describedby + aria-invalid automatically */}
                <Input type="email" placeholder="name@example.com" {...field} />
              </FormControl>
              <FormDescription>We'll only use this for your receipt.</FormDescription>
              <FormMessage /> {/* renders the field's error, wired to describedby */}
            </FormItem>
          )}
        />
        <Button type="submit">Create account</Button>
      </form>
    </Form>
  );
}
```

`FormItem` calls `useId()`; `FormLabel` reads it for `htmlFor`; `FormControl` spreads `id`, `aria-describedby="{descriptionId} {messageId}"`, and `aria-invalid` onto its child; `FormMessage` renders `errors[name]` and only takes up space when there's an error. **You cannot ship an unwired field with this API** — which is exactly why it's the reference implementation of everything in §9.3.

### 9.8 Next.js (App Router: server action + field errors)
Server Actions let a form validate on the server and return **field-keyed errors** that map straight onto the Field's error slot — with progressive enhancement (works before JS loads).

```plain
// app/actions.ts
"use server";
import { SignupSchema, toFieldErrors } from "@/lib/schema";

export type FormState = { errors: Record<string, string>; ok: boolean };

export async function signup(_prev: FormState, formData: FormData): Promise<FormState> {
  const parsed = SignupSchema.safeParse(Object.fromEntries(formData));
  if (!parsed.success) return { errors: toFieldErrors(parsed.error), ok: false };
  await db.users.create(parsed.data);
  return { errors: {}, ok: true };
}
```

```plain
// app/signup/EmailField.tsx
"use client";
import { useActionState } from "react";
import { signup } from "@/app/actions";

export function SignupForm() {
  const [state, action, pending] = useActionState(signup, { errors: {}, ok: false });
  const err = state.errors.email;

  return (
    <form action={action}>
      <div className="field">
        <label htmlFor="email" className="field__label">Email address</label>

```

Because the field errors come back keyed by `name`, the same wrapper renders them whether validation ran on the client or the server. The `<form action={fn}>` submits natively if JS hasn't hydrated — the field's label association and error rendering survive with zero JS.

### 9.9 Node.js (the endpoint that returns field errors)
The server is the real validator. The important contract: return a **422 with a field-keyed error map** the front end can drop straight onto fields.

```plain
import express from "express";
import { SignupSchema, toFieldErrors } from "./schema";

const router = express.Router();

router.post("/api/signup", requireAuth, async (req, res) => {
  const parsed = SignupSchema.safeParse(req.body);   // never trust the client
  if (!parsed.success) {
    // { errors: { email: "…", password: "…" } } — shaped for the Field layer
    return res.status(422).json({ errors: toFieldErrors(parsed.error) });
  }

  // Cross-field / stateful checks the client can't do (uniqueness, etc.)
  if (await db.users.existsByEmail(parsed.data.email)) {
    return res.status(422).json({ errors: { email: "That email is already registered." } });
  }

  const user = await db.users.create(parsed.data);
  return res.status(201).json({ id: user.id });
});

export default router;
```

Two things the client physically cannot validate and the Field must be ready to display: **uniqueness** ("email already registered") and any **cross-field / server-state rule**. Design the error map so those land on the right field, not in a generic banner.

### 9.10 Python (backend validation + server-rendered field)
**FastAPI + Pydantic** — validation errors come out already keyed by field; reshape them for the wrapper:

```python
from fastapi import FastAPI, Request
from fastapi.responses import JSONResponse
from fastapi.exceptions import RequestValidationError
from pydantic import BaseModel, EmailStr, field_validator

app = FastAPI()

class Signup(BaseModel):
    email: EmailStr
    password: str

    @field_validator("password")
    @classmethod
    def strong_enough(cls, v: str) -> str:
        if len(v) < 8:
            raise ValueError("Use at least 8 characters.")
        return v

@app.exception_handler(RequestValidationError)
async def field_errors(_req: Request, exc: RequestValidationError):
    # Collapse Pydantic's list into { field: message } for the Field layer
    errors = {}
    for e in exc.errors():
        field = e["loc"][-1]
        errors.setdefault(field, e["msg"])
    return JSONResponse(status_code=422, content={"errors": errors})

@app.post("/api/signup", status_code=201)
def signup(body: Signup):
    return {"ok": True}
```

### 9.11 Testing the Field
Test the _contract_, not the paint: is the control reachable **by its label**, is help/error **associated**, does the error **announce** and set `aria-invalid`. `getByLabelText` failing is the single best signal that your association is broken.

```plain
import { render, screen } from "@testing-library/react";
import userEvent from "@testing-library/user-event";
import { axe } from "jest-axe";
import { describe, it, expect } from "vitest";
import { EmailField } from "./EmailField";

describe("Email field", () => {
  it("associates the label with the control", () => {
    render(<EmailField />);
    // If for/id is wired, the input is findable BY ITS LABEL. This is the a11y proof.
    expect(screen.getByLabelText(/email address/i)).toBeInTheDocument();
  });

  it("exposes help text via aria-describedby", () => {
    render(<EmailField />);
    const input = screen.getByLabelText(/email address/i);
    expect(input).toHaveAccessibleDescription(/receipt/i);
  });

  it("marks the field invalid and announces the error", async () => {
    render(<EmailField />);
    const input = screen.getByLabelText(/email address/i);
    await userEvent.type(input, "not-an-email");
    await userEvent.tab(); // blur triggers validation
    expect(input).toHaveAttribute("aria-invalid", "true");
    // role=alert makes it queryable and proves it's in a live region
    expect(screen.getByRole("alert")).toHaveTextContent(/valid email/i);
    // and the error id is now part of the description
    expect(input).toHaveAccessibleDescription(/valid email/i);
  });

  it("clears the error once corrected", async () => {
    render(<EmailField />);
    const input = screen.getByLabelText(/email address/i);
    await userEvent.type(input, "bad");
    await userEvent.tab();
    await userEvent.clear(input);
    await userEvent.type(input, "name@example.com");
    expect(input).not.toHaveAttribute("aria-invalid", "true");
  });

  it("has no axe violations in default or error state", async () => {
    const { container } = render(<EmailField />);
    expect(await axe(container)).toHaveNoViolations();
  });
});
```

What to actually assert (and what to skip):

*   **Do test:** control is found `byLabelText` (proves `for`/`id`), `toHaveAccessibleDescription` (proves `aria-describedby` → help + error), `aria-invalid` flips on failure, the error is a `role="alert"`, and it clears on correction.
*   **Don't test:** exact class names, the `*` glyph, pixel spacing, error color. Those are paint and break tests without catching real bugs.
*   **Always run axe** on both the resting and the error state — the error state is where associations most often break.

### 9.13 Other Frameworks (Vue, Svelte, Angular)
Same wiring, different syntax. The `<label for>` / `aria-describedby` / `aria-invalid` never change.

**Vue 3 (SFC) — a reusable Field with generated ids:**

```plain

const id = useId();
const helpId = `${id}-help`;
const errorId = `${id}-error`;

  <div class="field">

    </label>

    <!-- consumer's control goes in the slot; we pass the wiring down -->
    <slot :id="id" :describedBy="describedBy" :invalid="!!error" :required="required" />

  </div>
</template>
```

### 9.15 Web Components (framework-agnostic)
A Field that works in any framework or none. The trick with a wrapper custom element: the control is _slotted_ (light DOM) so form participation and native label association keep working — don't bury the control in shadow DOM or you lose form submission.

```plain
class UIField extends HTMLElement {
  connectedCallback() {
    const id = this.getAttribute("field-id") || `f-${Math.random().toString(36).slice(2)}`;
    const label = this.getAttribute("label") ?? "";
    const help = this.getAttribute("help") ?? "";
    const error = this.getAttribute("error") ?? "";
    const control = this.querySelector("input, select, textarea");
    if (control) {
      control.id = id;
      const ids = [help && `${id}-help`, error && `${id}-error`].filter(Boolean).join(" ");
      if (ids) control.setAttribute("aria-describedby", ids);
      if (error) control.setAttribute("aria-invalid", "true");
    }
    // shadow renders label/help/error AROUND the slotted (light-DOM) control.
    // Build the structure with STATIC markup only, then set the dynamic
    // label/help/error via textContent — never interpolate attribute values
    // into innerHTML, or the error slot becomes the reflected-XSS sink §15 warns about.
    const root = this.attachShadow({ mode: "open" });
    root.innerHTML = `
      <style>/* scoped field styles */</style>
      <label part="label"></label>
      <p part="help" hidden></p>
      <slot></slot>
      <p role="alert" part="error" hidden></p>`;
    const labelEl = root.querySelector('[part="label"]');
    labelEl.setAttribute("for", id);
    labelEl.textContent = label;            // text, not HTML
    if (help) {
      const helpEl = root.querySelector('[part="help"]');
      helpEl.id = `${id}-help`;
      helpEl.textContent = help;            // text, not HTML
      helpEl.hidden = false;
    }
    if (error) {
      const errorEl = root.querySelector('[part="error"]');
      errorEl.id = `${id}-error`;
      errorEl.textContent = error;          // text, not HTML — §15
      errorEl.hidden = false;
    }
  }
}
customElements.define("ui-field", UIField);
// <ui-field label="Email" help="For your receipt" field-id="email">
//   <input type="email" name="email" />
// </ui-field>
```

### 9.16 Fieldset & Legend — grouping multiple controls
The pattern most people get wrong. A single `<label>` captions **one** control. When several controls answer **one** question — radio group, checkbox list, "from/to" dates, an address block — you need `<fieldset>` + `<legend>`. The legend is the group's caption; each control keeps its own `<label>`.

```plain
<fieldset class="field-group" aria-describedby="ship-error">
  <legend class="field-group__legend">Shipping speed</legend>
  <p id="ship-help" class="field__help">Affects your delivery date at checkout.</p>

  <label><input type="radio" name="ship" value="standard" required /> Standard (5–7 days)</label>
  <label><input type="radio" name="ship" value="express"  /> Express (2 days)</label>
  <label><input type="radio" name="ship" value="overnight" /> Overnight</label>

  <p id="ship-error" role="alert" class="field__error">Choose a shipping speed.</p>
</fieldset>
```

Why this and not three separate fields:

*   **`<legend>`** **is announced with** **_every_** **option.** A screen reader reads "Shipping speed, Standard 5 to 7 days, radio button 1 of 3." Without the fieldset, each radio is an orphan with no group context.
*   **The error and help attach to the** **_group_****\*\*\*\*,** via `aria-describedby` on the `<fieldset>` (support is uneven across AT, so also consider putting the error text adjacent and, for critical flows, referencing it from a grouping element with `role="radiogroup"`/`role="group"` + `aria-describedby`).
*   **Radios share one** **`name`****\*\*\*\*;** that's what makes them mutually exclusive and what makes `required` on one enforce the whole group.
*   **Don't nest a** **`<fieldset>`** **per option** — the fieldset wraps the _set_, not each item. And don't reach for a fieldset for a _single_ control; that's a plain Field.

For a custom (non-native) group — say styled toggle chips — use `role="radiogroup"` on the container with `aria-labelledby` pointing at the legend text and `aria-describedby` pointing at the error, and implement roving tabindex + arrow-key selection yourself (see the Button doc's §9.20 for the roving-tabindex mechanics).

### 9.17 System & Environment States
Production fields respond to the user's environment, not just your design.

**Reduced motion.** Floating labels and error slide-ins animate; kill the motion for users who opt out.

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .field__label--floating, .field__error { transition: none; animation: none; }
}
```

**Forced colors / Windows High Contrast.** Your red error border vanishes under a forced palette — lean on system keywords and keep the _text_ error (which survives) as the real signal.

```css
@media (forced-colors: active) {
  .field__control[aria-invalid="true"] { border-color: Mark; }   /* system highlight */
  .field__error { color: Mark; }
  .field__control:focus-visible { outline-color: Highlight; }
}
```

**RTL.** The `*` marker, the label, and any inline layout must mirror. Use logical properties so it's automatic.

```css
.field__req { margin-inline-start: 2px; }   /* not margin-left */
.field { margin-block-end: var(--field-row-gap); }
```

**Print.** Fields on paper should show the _label and value_ legibly (for printed forms), but hide interactive-only affordances like the focus ring and any "validating…" spinner. Ensure the label prints even for controls that render their label visually only on focus (floating labels are a print hazard — another mark against them).

### 9.18 Internationalization & Microcopy
Labels and errors are copy, and copy breaks across languages and cultures.

*   **Labels grow.** German runs ~35% longer; an inline-label column sized to English will wrap or clip. Stacked layout is the translation-safe default; if you must go inline, size the label column to the _longest translated_ label.
*   **Externalize every string** — label, help, error, and the required/optional word. `t("field.email.label")`, never a hardcoded literal, so translators never touch markup.
*   **The** **`*`** **isn't universal.** "Required"/"Optional" conventions differ; some locales expect the _optional_ fields marked. Provide a legend ("\* = required") and make sure the machine-readable `aria-required` carries the real meaning regardless of glyph.
*   **Translate whole error phrases,** not fragments. "Enter a valid {field}" doesn't grammatically compose in every language — some need gendered or case-inflected forms. Give translators complete sentences.
*   **Number, date, and address fields are locale-shaped** — the _label_ copy is the least of it; the control's format expectations (comma vs. period decimals, DD/MM vs. MM/DD, postal-code presence) belong to the Inputs doc, but the Field's _help text_ should state the expected format in the user's locale.

Microcopy rulebook for the label + help + error trio:

*   **Label:** a noun or short noun phrase — "Email address," "Card number." Not a question, not a sentence.
*   **Help:** states a _rule_ or _reassurance_ before the user acts — "8+ characters," "We'll never share this." Present tense, calm.
*   **Error:** says _what's wrong and how to fix it_ — "Enter a valid email, like [name@example.com](mailto:name@example.com)," not "Invalid input." Name the fix, show the shape, never blame ("You entered…").

### 9.19 Association Mechanics (the deep cut)
The wiring deserves its own section because it's where fields silently fail.

*   **`aria-describedby`** **is a** **_token list_****\*\*\*\*.** Multiple ids, space-separated, read in DOM order. Point it at help _and_ error simultaneously: `aria-describedby="x-help x-error"`. When the error is absent, either omit its id or leave a stable empty element — an id that resolves to _nothing_ is harmless (AT skips missing/empty targets), which is why many implementations keep both ids in the list permanently and just toggle the error element's content.
*   **`aria-labelledby`** **vs.** **`<label for>`****\*\*\*\*.** Prefer a real `<label for>` for single controls (it also gives you the _click-the-label-to-focus_ behavior for free). Use `aria-labelledby` when the caption is a `<legend>`\-like element that can't be a `<label>` (grouped/custom controls). Never use `aria-label` on a text field if a _visible_ label exists — you'd hide the visible text from the a11y name mismatch and break voice control ("click Email" must match the visible word).
*   **`aria-invalid`** **lives on the control, always.** Not the wrapper, not the label, not the error `<p>`. The control is what AT queries for validity.
*   **`aria-errormessage`** is the newer, more precise pairing: `aria-invalid="true"` + `aria-errormessage="x-error"` explicitly links the control to _its error_. Support is improving but still behind `aria-describedby`; use `describedby` as the reliable baseline and layer `errormessage` on top if your AT matrix supports it.
*   **`aria-required`** **vs.** **`required`****\*\*\*\*.** `required` is the native constraint (triggers browser validation, submission blocking). `aria-required` is the announced semantic. For a native control, `required` _usually_ implies the semantic — but add `aria-required="true"` for maximum AT coverage; for a _custom_ control (a div-based combobox), `aria-required` is your _only_ option.

### 9.20 Validation Timing & Live-Region Announcement
_When_ you validate and _how_ you announce is as important as the rules themselves. Get the timing wrong and a correct form feels hostile.

**The timing rhythm** — eager-then-lazy is the humane default:

*   **On submit:** validate _everything_, focus the _first_ invalid field, announce a summary. Never let a submit fail silently.
*   **On blur:** validate the field the user just left. This is the sweet spot for first feedback — they've finished, so it's fair to check.
*   **On input, but only after first error:** once a field has _shown_ an error, re-check on each keystroke so the error clears the instant it's fixed. Validating on input _before_ the first blur ("Invalid email" after one character) is the most-hated form behavior there is.

**The live region.** An error rendered into the DOM is _seen_; to be _heard_ it must be in a live region:

*   `role="alert"` (= `aria-live="assertive"`) for errors that appear _after_ the user acts — it interrupts and reads immediately.
*   `aria-live="polite"` for non-urgent status ("Checking availability…", "Username available") — it waits for a pause.
*   The live region element must **exist in the DOM before** you put text in it. Injecting the whole `<p role="alert">…</p>` at once _usually_ announces, but the robust pattern is a persistent empty container whose _text content_ you update — some AT misses freshly-inserted alert nodes.

```plain
<!-- Persistent regions, populated on demand -->
<p id="email-error" role="alert" class="field__error"></p>            <!-- errors: assertive -->
<p id="email-status" aria-live="polite" class="field__help"></p>      <!-- async: polite -->
```

**On submit, move focus** to the first invalid control (`firstInvalid.focus()`), don't just scroll to it. Focusing announces the field's label, help, and error in one breath and puts the cursor where the fix happens. A form that reports "3 errors" but leaves the user hunting for them is a recovery killer (§12).

### 9.21 Performance & Bundle Cost
A field feels free; a _form_ of them isn't.

*   **Don't re-render every field on every keystroke.** In React, an uncontrolled or field-isolated approach (react-hook-form subscribes per-field) means typing in one field doesn't re-render the other forty. A naive `useState` per field lifted to the form re-renders the whole form on each keystroke — fine for 3 fields, janky for 30.
*   **`useId`** **is cheap; UUID-per-render is not.** Generate the id _once_ (mount), not on every render. `crypto.randomUUID()` inside a render body creates a new id each pass and breaks the `for`/`id` link across re-renders — a subtle, real bug.
*   **Debounce async field validation** (username/email availability) so you're not firing a request per keystroke; announce via the polite region only when the check resolves.
*   **Don't animate layout on error.** Reserve the error line's height (`min-height: 1lh`) so revealing an error doesn't reflow the whole form below it. Animate `opacity`/`transform` on the error, never `height` of the row.
*   **Reuse one validation schema** (§9.4) across client and server instead of shipping two copies of the rules — smaller bundle, zero drift.

### 9.22 Theming & Design Tokens
The field becomes a system, not a one-off, through tiered tokens — so a rebrand or dark mode is a token swap, not a form rewrite.

```css
:root {
  /* primitive */
  --purple-800: oklch(42% 0.145 305);   /* Eminence */
  --gold-500:   oklch(78% 0.135 82);    /* Goldenrod */
  --orange-600: #E86100;                /* Spanish Orange */
  /* semantic */
  --color-border:  var(--purple-800);
  --color-focus:   var(--gold-500);
  --color-danger:  var(--orange-600);
  /* component (the field only ever references THESE) */
  --field-border: var(--color-border);
  --field-focus:  var(--color-focus);
  --field-danger: var(--color-danger);
  --field-label-color: #E8E6E1;
  --field-help-color:  #9a90a8;
  --field-row-gap: 20px;
  --field-label-gap: 6px;
}
@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) {
  :root { --field-label-color: #E8E6E1; --field-help-color: #b4a9c4; }
}
```

The field references only `--field-*` component tokens; those reference semantic tokens; those reference primitives. Rebrand = swap primitives. Retheme just the fields = swap `--field-*`. Nothing hardcodes a hex, and the error/focus colors stay consistent with the rest of the system automatically.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
Fields are _the_ accessibility component. If you internalize one section of this doc, make it this one.

*   **Every control has a programmatically-associated label.** `<label for>` = `control[id]`, or wrap the control in the `<label>`. A placeholder is not a label. An adjacent-but-unassociated `<div>` is not a label. If `getByLabelText` can't find it, neither can a screen reader.
*   **Help text is referenced,** not just visually near the control. `aria-describedby` → help id. Otherwise the guidance is invisible to AT users precisely when they most need it.
*   **Errors are announced** **_and_** **associated.** `role="alert"` (or a persistent polite/assertive live region) so the error is _heard_ when it appears, plus its id in `aria-describedby` so it's re-read whenever the field is refocused, plus `aria-invalid="true"` on the control so its invalid _state_ is exposed.
*   **Required state is machine-readable.** `required` and/or `aria-required="true"` — not just a visual `*`. Provide a legend explaining the `*`.
*   **Grouped controls use** **`<fieldset>`****/****`<legend>`** (or `role="group"`/`role="radiogroup"` + `aria-labelledby`) so the group question is announced with each option (§9.16).
*   **Focus goes to the first invalid field on submit** — move focus, don't just scroll. The user lands where the fix is, and the label+error are announced on arrival.
*   **Never signal error by color alone.** Red border + red text is invisible to color-blind users and vanishes in forced-colors mode. The error _text_ and `aria-invalid` carry the meaning; color reinforces.
*   **Contrast:** label, help, and error text all ≥ 4.5:1 against the background (help text at 13px muted-gray is the most common contrast failure in forms). The focus ring ≥ 3:1 against adjacent colors.
*   **Target size:** the control's interactive area ≥ 44×44px, and the _label_ should be clickable to focus the control (native `<label for>` gives this free) — a bigger, more forgiving hit target.
*   **Don't disable the submit until the form is "perfect."** A disabled submit with no explanation strands AT users with no announced reason. Let them submit, then validate and announce (§12).

**The keyboard/AT map for a field:**

| Action | Result |
| ---| --- |
| `Tab` to control | Focus control; AT announces label, then help + error (from `describedby`), then required/invalid state |
| Click the label | Focus (or toggle, for checkbox/radio) the control |
| Type an invalid value, `Tab` away | On blur: `aria-invalid="true"`, error announced via `role="alert"` |
| Correct the value | `aria-invalid` removed, error slot cleared |
| Submit an invalid form | Focus moves to first invalid field; its label + error announced |
| `Tab` into a radio/checkbox group | `<legend>` announced with the first option; arrows move within (native radios) |

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The field looks solved but keeps evolving, and 2026 is an active year for it:

*   **Native CSS** **`:user-invalid`** **/** **`:user-valid`****\*\*\*\*:** the browser now distinguishes "invalid" from "invalid _after the user has interacted_," giving you eager-then-lazy styling with zero JS — `.field__control:user-invalid { border-color: var(--field-danger); }` won't fire until the user has actually engaged and left the field. This is native support for the timing rhythm of §9.20.
*   **`field-sizing: content`****\*\*\*\*:** textareas and inputs that grow with their content natively, no JS auto-resize — the field's height becomes self-managing.
*   **Customizable native** **`<select>`** (the `appearance: base-select` work) is finally letting the platform select be styled like a custom one while keeping native a11y and mobile behavior — shrinking the reasons to hand-roll comboboxes.
*   **AI-assisted fields:** inline "fix this for me" affordances, format auto-correction (a phone field that reshapes as you type), and generative help text that adapts to what the user has entered. The wrapper is where these affordances mount — as first-class parts, not bolt-ons.
*   **Passkeys & one-time-code fields:** `autocomplete="one-time-code"` and WebAuthn are collapsing whole multi-field auth flows into a single field or no field — the most accessible field is sometimes the one you removed.
*   **Anchor-positioned error popovers:** CSS anchor positioning lets an error float precisely beside its control without JS position math, while staying in the accessibility tree.
*   **Server-driven validation with progressive enhancement:** React Server Components / Server Actions returning field-keyed errors (§9.8) mean forms that validate correctly _before JS loads_ — a resilience win the field layer inherits.

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers (mistakes that bleed money silently)
None of these throw an error. They just quietly tank form completion — which makes them expensive and easy to blame on "users being lazy."

*   **Placeholder-as-label.** Using the placeholder instead of a real label saves a line and costs you the form. The label vanishes the moment the user types, so they forget what the field was; it fails contrast (placeholder gray is intentionally faint); it's not reliably read by AT; and it defeats browser autofill. **Always a persistent, visible, associated label.** This is the #1 field mistake on the web.
*   **Error placement far from the field.** A red summary box at the top with no inline errors makes the user hunt for which field failed. Errors belong _at the field_, and on submit, focus jumps to the first one. (A summary _plus_ inline is fine.)
*   **Validating too early.** "Invalid email" after the first keystroke, before they've finished typing, reads as the form yelling at them. Validate on blur, then live-correct only after the first error (§9.20).
*   **Disabled submit with no explanation.** Graying out "Continue" until the form is perfect leaves the user staring at a dead button with no idea what's missing. Let them submit and surface the errors, or say what's missing inline.
*   **Everything marked required.** If 11 of 12 fields have a `*`, the marker means nothing. Mark the _optional_ ones instead, or drop the truly optional fields entirely — the shortest form wins.
*   **No help text where the format is non-obvious.** A "Company ID" field with no hint about format guarantees wrong entries, errors, and abandonment. Persistent help beats an error every time — prevent, don't punish.
*   **Layout shift on error.** The form jumps when an error appears because no space was reserved. Users lose their place, mis-click, and distrust the form. Reserve the error line's height.
*   **Cramped or mis-associated labels.** A label equidistant between two controls (or closer to the _wrong_ one) makes users fill the wrong field. Proximity is meaning: label hugs _its_ control.
*   **Asking for what you don't need.** Every field is friction. The highest-ROI field optimization is _deletion_ — phone number "just in case," company name on a personal signup, confirm-password when you could show the password. Cut the field and completion rises.

**The through-line:** these all fail _quietly_. No crash, no log — just a form-completion rate that's easy to blame on the market. Audit your fields before you audit your traffic.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
Techniques that lean on the platform and the type system instead of custom glue.

**Native constraint validation, styled.** Let the browser validate and use `:user-invalid` to style only after interaction — no JS validation library for simple cases:

```css
.field__control:user-invalid { border-color: var(--field-danger); }
.field__control:user-invalid ~ .field__error { display: block; }
```

```plain
<input type="email" required aria-describedby="e-help e-error" />
<p id="e-error" role="alert" class="field__error">Enter a valid email.</p>
```

**TypeScript: force a label at compile time.** A discriminated union that _requires_ either visible `label` text or an explicit `aria-label`, so an unlabeled field can't compile:

```plain
type BaseField = { name: string; description?: string; error?: string; required?: boolean };
type LabeledField    = BaseField & { label: string; "aria-label"?: never };
type AriaLabeledField = BaseField & { label?: never; "aria-label": string };
type FieldProps = LabeledField | AriaLabeledField;
// <Field name="email" />  ❌ won't compile — no label and no aria-label
```

**One schema, two runtimes.** Share the zod/valibot schema between the client field validation and the server handler (§9.4, §9.9). The Field renders `errors[name]` identically regardless of which runtime produced it — no rule drift, half the code.

**`aria-errormessage`** **for precise error binding.** Layer it over the `describedby` baseline where AT support exists:

```plain
<input aria-invalid="true" aria-describedby="pw-help" aria-errormessage="pw-error" />
<p id="pw-help">8+ characters.</p>
<p id="pw-error" role="alert">Password is too short.</p>
```

**The systems view.** Treat a Field as a _system component_, not markup you retype per form. A production Field has: a documented slot API (label / description / control / error / required), guaranteed id wiring, a live-region error contract, a shared validation-error shape, layout variants that never alter semantics, and tests that assert the _association_, not the paint. That's what separates a form that scales across a product from twelve hand-wired inputs that each fail a11y differently.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
Covered in depth in §9.21 — the headlines for a ship-review:

*   **Field-level subscriptions** (react-hook-form / uncontrolled) so typing in one field doesn't re-render the whole form. Matters past ~10 fields.
*   **Generate ids once,** never per render; `useId()` over `crypto.randomUUID()` in a render body.
*   **Debounce async validation;** announce via a polite live region on resolve, not per keystroke.
*   **Reserve error-line height** to prevent reflow; animate only `opacity`/`transform`.
*   **One shared schema** across client and server — smaller bundle, zero drift.

## 15\. Security

A field's client-side validation is a UX courtesy, never a security control: `required`, `pattern`, `min`/`max`, `type`, and your zod schema all run in a browser the attacker fully owns, so every constraint that _matters_ must be re-validated and sanitized server-side — the endpoint (§9.9) is callable directly with `curl`, and "the field wouldn't let me submit" must never mean "the value can't reach the database." The Field's **error text is a classic reflected-XSS sink** the moment it echoes a server response or the user's own input ("'`<script>`' is not a valid email") — escape error content and render it as _text_, never assign it through `innerHTML`. Label, help, and error associations (`for`, `aria-describedby`) are correctness rather than security, but the Field is where the discipline for sensitive data starts: set an appropriate `autocomplete` (`new-password`, `one-time-code`, `off`), and keep a sensitive value out of the DOM and out of logs rather than leaving it serialized where it doesn't belong. Field-level server errors must stay clean of system internals — no stack traces, SQL fragments, or ORM messages echoed back into the error slot — because whatever the server returns is exactly what the Field renders next to the input. Keep auth-related field errors generic too ("Email or password is incorrect"), since a precise "no account with that email" turns the field into an account-enumeration oracle. And remember the boundary: the field is the payload, but the form it lives in is the CSRF surface, so state-changing submissions still need an anti-CSRF token and `SameSite` cookies.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a field is "done":

- [ ] Every control has a **visible, persistent, programmatically-associated** `<label>` (`for`/`id` or wrapping) — no placeholder-as-label.
- [ ] Help text is referenced via `aria-describedby`.
- [ ] Error is in a **live region** (`role="alert"`), referenced by `aria-describedby`, and sets `aria-invalid="true"` on the control.
- [ ] Required state is machine-readable (`required` / `aria-required`), not just a `*`; a legend explains the marker.
- [ ] Grouped controls use `<fieldset>`/`<legend>` (or `role="group"`/`radiogroup` + `aria-labelledby`).
- [ ] Validation timing is **eager-then-lazy** (blur first, live-correct after first error); submit validates all and **focuses the first invalid field**.
- [ ] Error meaning survives without color (text + `aria-invalid`); passes forced-colors mode.
- [ ] Label, help, and error text all ≥ 4.5:1 contrast; focus ring ≥ 3:1.
- [ ] Control target ≥ 44px; clicking the label focuses the control.
- [ ] No layout shift when the error appears (reserved height).
- [ ] Ids are generated once (SSR-safe), never duplicated across the page.
- [ ] Server re-validates every rule and returns field-keyed errors; error text is rendered as text, not HTML.
- [ ] Only truly-needed fields exist; optional fields marked (or cut).

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same field, eleven aesthetics)
Design systems are _whose_ rules; visual styles are _which look_. The same semantic field — same label association, same `aria-describedby`, same `aria-invalid`, same live-region error, same 44px control target — can wear any of these skins. **Style is skin; behavior is the skeleton.** None of the accessibility contract changes across the eleven.

*   **Flat:** solid fields, 1px borders, crisp corners, error state = border + text color swap. Reads instantly, scales fastest, the safe default for data-entry.
*   **Material:** filled or outlined field, floating label that animates up on focus, supporting/error text in the reserved slot below, a colored underline that thickens on focus. Label and control feel like one unit.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent field surface with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over a vivid background. Watch label and help-text contrast _hard_ — faint text on frosted glass is the fastest way to fail WCAG. Rare for forms; guard contrast.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language. The field surface is a refractive translucent material with a specular rim that reacts to focus and motion — the focus ring reads as light catching an edge rather than a flat outline. Gorgeous on deep saturated backdrops; on the web it's an honest `backdrop-filter` + layered-highlight approximation, and SwiftUI emits native `.glassEffect()`. Guard contrast _and_ `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Neumorphism:** the field appears _inset_ — a soft pressed-in well via dual light/dark inner shadows on a same-color surface. Pretty, but notoriously contrast-poor and the border between "field" and "background" nearly disappears; accent use only, never a whole form.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** inset field with an inner top-shadow (the "recessed slot" look), beveled label plates, textured background. The tactile "real paper form" aesthetic.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black borders, hard offset shadow on focus, zero radius, a loud error state (solid red fill, not a subtle border). High personality; the error state is _unmissable_, which is actually a11y-friendly if contrast holds.
*   **Claymorphism:** big radius, puffy soft field with inner top-light and bottom-shadow, gentle glow on focus. Friendly, rounded, consumer/onboarding energy.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue gradient border or focus glow on a dark field. Premium and eye-catching; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and keep the label/help text on a solid backing so contrast never rides the gradient.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-invisible fields — a single bottom rule or no border at all, label doing the work, generous whitespace, typography-led. Elegant; make sure the _focus_ and _error_ states are still obvious when the resting state is so quiet.
*   **UJG Brand:** Afro-Futurist house style. Goldenrod focus ring and required marker on deep Eminence/Night field surfaces, confident radius, Platinum labels, muted-violet help text, Spanish-Orange errors with a warm glow. Deep saturated ground makes a Liquid-Glass focus accent sing; keep Flat/Swiss for dense/data forms.

**Rule that never changes:** the field's semantics, label association, `aria-describedby` wiring, error live region, required signaling, focus ring, and 44px target stay identical across all eleven skins. Full guidance lives in the [Design Styles reference](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551).
* * *

**Bottom line:** a Field is not an input — it's the wrapper that makes an input _usable and accessible_: the label that's actually associated, the help that's actually announced, the error that's actually heard, and the required state that's actually machine-readable. The control holds the value; the Field holds the meaning. Master the `for`/`id`/`aria-describedby`/`aria-invalid` wiring, the fieldset/legend group pattern, the eager-then-lazy validation rhythm, and the live-region error contract, and you've mastered the component where accessibility and conversion are won or lost — the composition primitive every real form is built from.
