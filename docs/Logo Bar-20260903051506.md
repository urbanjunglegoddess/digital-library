# Logo Bar

# Logo Bar

A horizontal "trusted by" / "as seen in" strip of client, partner, or press logos, used as social proof. This is the full breakdown: what it actually is, why the accessibility contract is trickier than it looks, the marquee anti-pattern, logo normalization, and production code across 17 targets.
* * *

## 1\. What It Actually Is

A Logo Bar is a compact, usually single-row band of third-party brand marks — clients, partners, integrations, press outlets, or certifications — placed near a hero or CTA to borrow those brands' credibility. It is a _display_ component whose whole job is recognition: the viewer scans it, sees a name they trust, and transfers a sliver of that trust to you. Logos are typically rendered at a uniform optical height, often desaturated (grayscale) at rest, and either sit statically or auto-scroll.

The word "logo bar" gets stretched over several different things people confuse:

*   **Logo Bar (social proof strip):** third-party marks that say "these brands vouch for us." Display-only, or lightly interactive if each logo links to a case study. This page.
*   **Navigation logo / brand header:** your _own_ single wordmark in the site header. It's one mark, it links home, it lives in `<header>`, and it is _navigation_, not proof. Never build your own header logo as a "logo bar."
*   **Footer logo cloud:** partner/payment/certification marks in the `<footer>` (Visa, Stripe, "SOC 2"). Same mechanics as a logo bar but a different intent (reassurance at the point of decision, not headline credibility).
*   **Logo grid / client gallery:** a multi-row _grid_ of many logos on an "Our Clients" page. It's a grid, not a bar — wrapping and density rules differ, and it's rarely desaturated or scrolling.
*   **Sponsor/partner wall:** an event or from-the-community wall, often ordered by tier (Platinum/Gold). Visually a logo bar, but the ordering carries meaning, so it can't be shuffled or marquee'd freely.

**Rule of thumb:** if the marks belong to _other_ organizations and exist to transfer _their_ credibility to _you_, it's a Logo Bar. If it's _your_ mark and it navigates, it's a header logo. If it's many marks across multiple rows on a dedicated page, it's a logo grid. One row of borrowed trust = Logo Bar.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters

The Logo Bar is one of the highest-leverage, lowest-code trust elements on a landing page. The stakes are real:

*   **Conversion.** Social proof is one of the most reliable conversion levers there is. A row of recognizable logos placed just below the hero or immediately above a pricing CTA answers the visitor's silent "who else trusts these people?" before they have to ask. The lift is real _only if the logos are recognizable to that specific audience_ — a Fortune 500 logo means nothing to a buyer who's never heard of the sub-industry vendor next to it.
*   **Accessibility.** This is the part teams get wrong. A logo is an image of text (the company name) rendered in a custom typeface. To a screen-reader user, an `<img>` with no `alt` is either silence or a read-aloud of the filename ("acme-logo-final-v2.svg"). Get the alt-text contract wrong and the entire proof value is invisible to assistive tech — and you've shipped a WCAG 1.1.1 failure on your most-visited page.
*   **Trust and perceived quality.** A logo bar with mismatched sizes, one logo twice as tall as its neighbor, JPEG artifacts, or a jittery marquee reads as _amateur_ and actively _lowers_ trust — the opposite of its purpose. Optical normalization and crisp SVGs are not polish; they're the difference between "credible" and "cobbled together."
*   **Legal exposure.** Displaying another company's logo is displaying their trademark. Doing it to imply endorsement or a relationship you don't have is a trademark and potentially false-advertising problem. This component carries a permissions obligation most front-end devs never think about (see §15).
*   **Performance.** A logo bar is 5–20 image requests high on the page, often above the fold. Done wrong (unoptimized PNGs, no lazy-loading below the fold, a marquee that repaints the whole strip every frame) it hurts LCP and CLS on the exact page where speed converts.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy

Even a "simple" logo strip has named parts, and the interactive (marquee/linked) variants add more:

*   **Region / container:** the band itself. Full-bleed or content-width, with vertical padding and a background that the monochrome logos must contrast against. When labeled, it becomes a landmark `role="region"` / `aria-labelledby`.
*   **Eyebrow heading:** the "Trusted by," "As seen in," "Powering teams at" line. Small, muted, centered above or inline-left. **Must be programmatically associated** with the region (via `aria-labelledby`) so AT users know _what_ this list of names is.
*   **Track / list:** the flex or grid row that holds the logos. In a marquee, this is the moving element (the transform target); in a static bar it's a wrapping flex container. Semantically a **list** (`<ul>`/`role="list"`) when each logo is a distinct entity.
*   **Logo item:** the wrapper for one mark (`<li>`). Holds sizing constraints and, in linked variants, the anchor.
*   **Logo mark:** the actual `<img>`, inline `<svg>`, or `next/image`. Normalized to a **consistent optical height**, not width (see §4). Carries the alt-text (or `aria-hidden` in the decorative-set pattern).
*   **Link wrapper (optional):** an `<a>` around the mark when logos are clickable (to a case study or the partner site). Turns a display component into an interactive one, with all the focus/keyboard obligations that follow.
*   **Divider (optional):** a thin vertical rule or dot between logos. Purely decorative — `aria-hidden`.
*   **Edge fade / mask (marquee only):** a `mask-image` gradient at the left/right edges so logos fade in/out instead of hard-cutting at the container border.
*   **Duplicated set (marquee only):** the second, cloned copy of the track that makes the infinite scroll seamless. **This clone must be** **`aria-hidden`** **and its images given empty alt** — otherwise every company name is announced twice.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes / Scale / Density

The governing rule of a logo bar is **normalize on height (optical), never on a fixed box.** Logos have wildly different aspect ratios — a square emblem, a long wordmark, a tall stacked lockup — so constraining them all to the same _width_ makes the square one huge and the wordmark tiny. Constrain **max-height**, let width be `auto`, and then hand-tune per-logo scale for _optical_ balance (a visually "light" wordmark may need to be a touch taller than a dense emblem to feel equal).

| Tier | Container padding (block) | Logo max-height | Gap | Token | Use case |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact | 16px | 20px | 24px | `--logobar-h-sm` | Footer trust row, dense B2B pages, mobile |
| Default | 32px | 28px | 40px | `--logobar-h-md` | Standard "trusted by" strip below hero |
| Prominent | 48px | 40px | 56px | `--logobar-h-lg` | Hero-adjacent headline proof, enterprise landing |
| Marquee | 24px | 32px | 48px | `--logobar-h-mq` | Auto-scrolling infinite strip |

Rules that matter:

*   **Height, not width.** `img { max-height: var(--logo-h); width: auto; }`. Never set both a fixed height and width on logos of different aspect ratios — you'll squash them. `object-fit: contain` is the safety net if a box is forced.
*   **Per-logo optical scale.** Ship an override hook (`--logo-scale`, or a `data-scale` attribute) because raw pixel-height equality is _not_ visual equality. A thin monoline wordmark next to a bold filled emblem at the same height looks smaller; nudge it up 10–15%.
*   **Gap is a token, not a magic number.** Gaps scale with tier so the rhythm holds when the bar shrinks on mobile.
*   **Touch/target size only applies to linked logos.** If a logo is a link, its hit area (the `<a>`, padded) must be **≥44×44px** even if the visible mark is 20px tall. Expand with padding on the anchor, not by enlarging the image.
*   **Responsive: wrap or scroll, decide deliberately.** Below ~640px a 6-logo row either wraps to 2–3 rows (`flex-wrap`) or becomes a horizontally scrollable/marquee strip. Wrapping keeps everything perceivable at once (better for a11y); scrolling saves vertical space. Don't let it silently overflow and clip.

## 5\. States

A logo bar is mostly display, but it has more states than people expect once you account for color treatment, the marquee, and linked logos. Each is real and must be designed, not left to default:

*   **Default (rest).** The baseline. Most commonly **desaturated** — grayscale and/or reduced opacity (e.g. `filter: grayscale(1); opacity: .65`) so the strip reads as a calm, unified band rather than a clown-car of competing brand colors.
*   **Hover (per logo).** On pointer, an individual logo animates to full color / full opacity (`filter: grayscale(0); opacity: 1`, ~200–300ms). This is a _delight_ affordance, **not** an information channel — the logo must be fully perceivable in its rest state too (see §10). Doesn't exist on touch; never the only way to see a logo clearly.
*   **Focus (linked logos).** When logos are links, keyboard focus must show a visible ring on the `<a>`, and typically also lift the logo to full color so sighted keyboard users get the same reveal mouse users get.
*   **Active/pressed (linked logos).** The moment of click on a linked logo — a slight scale or opacity dip to confirm the press.
*   **Marquee: playing.** The track translates continuously; the cloned set keeps it seamless.
*   **Marquee: paused.** On `:hover` or `:focus-within` the animation pauses so users can actually read a logo, and so a keyboard user tabbing into a link isn't chasing a moving target.
*   **Reduced-motion (marquee).** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, the marquee **does not animate at all** — it renders as a static (wrapped or scrollable) bar. This is a state, not a nice-to-have.
*   **Loading / lazy.** Below-the-fold logos are `loading="lazy"`; while decoding, reserve their space (aspect-ratio box or fixed-height item) so nothing shifts (CLS).
*   **Error / broken image.** A logo file 404s. Ship a fallback: `onerror` swaps to the company name as _text_ (which is arguably better for a11y anyway), never a broken-image icon in your trust strip.
*   **Empty / too-few.** Fewer than ~4 logos: the bar looks sparse and _weakens_ trust. The "state" here is a design decision — hide the bar or switch to a single-testimonial pattern rather than show three lonely marks.

## 6\. Types / Variants

*   **Static centered row.** The default. A single flex row, logos centered, wraps on small screens. Most accessible, most performant, hardest to get wrong.
*   **Infinite marquee (auto-scroll).** The moving "logo strip." Duplicates the set and translates continuously. High visual energy, useful when you have many logos, but the **most error-prone** variant (see the anti-pattern note in §12): duplication confuses AT, motion excludes users, and it can hurt performance. Ship it only with pause-on-hover/focus, `aria-hidden` on the clone, and a reduced-motion static fallback.
*   **Horizontal scroll (manual).** A `scroll-snap` overflow strip the user swipes/drags. Honest alternative to the marquee on mobile — no motion, everything reachable, native scrollbar semantics.
*   **Wrapping grid-bar.** For 8–20 logos, a centered `flex-wrap` / auto-fit grid that forms 2–3 tidy rows. This is the boundary with the "logo grid"; keep it a bar if it's still a compact proof block, not a full "Our Clients" page.
*   **Categorized bar.** Grouped under sub-labels ("Clients," "Partners," "Press"). Each group is its own labeled sub-list. Good when the _type_ of proof matters.
*   **Linked / clickable bar.** Each logo is an `<a>` to a case study or the partner's site. Turns display into interactive — now you owe focus rings, 44px targets, and descriptive link text.
*   **Monochrome-tinted bar.** Instead of grayscale-of-original, all logos are recolored to a single brand tint (e.g. all Platinum on Eminence) via SVG `fill`/`currentColor` or CSS masks. Coherent and on-brand, but only works with single-color SVG sources and needs a contrast check.
*   **Ticker / press strip.** Marquee variant specifically for press/media logos, often paired with "As featured in."

## 7\. When to Use (and When Not To)

**Use a Logo Bar when:**

*   You have **4+ logos your specific audience recognizes** and permission to show them.
*   You want fast, scannable social proof just below a hero, immediately above a pricing/CTA block, or on an About/Home page.
*   Press coverage exists and "As featured in \[outlets\]" is genuinely impressive to your buyer.
*   You want to reinforce integrations/partners near a decision point ("Works with Stripe, Slack, Notion").

**Don't use one — use something else — when:**

*   You have **fewer than ~4 recognizable logos.** Use a single strong **testimonial** or a **case-study card** instead; three unknown logos read as weak, not credible.
*   The logos aren't recognizable to _this_ audience. A named-quote testimonial or a metric ("2,000+ teams") beats an unknown-logo wall.
*   You'd be implying an endorsement or relationship you don't have. That's not a design choice, it's a legal one — don't (see §15).
*   It's _your_ brand mark for the header — that's a **navigation logo** in `<header>`, not a logo bar.
*   You have 20+ logos to show off deliberately — that's a **logo grid** on a dedicated clients page, with its own density and pagination rules.
*   The marks carry ranked meaning (sponsor tiers) — use a **tiered sponsor wall**, and never marquee it (order would scramble).

**Placement heuristics:** highest-impact spot is directly under the hero headline (first proof the eye hits) or immediately above the primary CTA (last reassurance before the click). Keep it visually _quieter_ than the hero and CTA — desaturated, small — so it supports rather than competes. Give it breathing room; a cramped logo bar reads as a banner ad.

## 8\. Across Design Systems

No major design system ships a first-class "Logo Bar" primitive — it's a composition, which is exactly why teams reinvent it badly. Here's how each system's parts assemble it:

*   **Material Design (Google):** No logo-bar component; you compose it from an `Image` list inside a surface. Material's guidance pushes you toward consistent baseline grids and 8dp spacing tokens — apply those to the gap. Material would resist the grayscale-only treatment on accessibility grounds and steer you to keep logos legible at rest.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** No component; you'd build it with a horizontal `ScrollView` of `Image`s. HIG emphasizes SF-quality vector assets and restraint — Apple-flavored logo bars tend to be static, monochrome, and calm, never a bouncing marquee. HIG also stresses that decorative imagery gets no accessibility label while meaningful imagery must.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** Composed from `Image` + `Stack`. Fluent's density and neutral-surface philosophy fits the muted, understated logo strip well; you'd lean on its motion tokens (and honor reduced motion) if you animated it.
*   **Ant Design:** No logo bar, but its `Space` component is the natural track (consistent gap), and `Image` handles fallback (`fallback` prop for broken logos out of the box). Ant's enterprise bent means you'd often see this as a static partner row, not a marquee.
*   **Tailwind CSS:** No component — you compose utilities: `flex flex-wrap items-center justify-center gap-x-10 gap-y-6`, `grayscale hover:grayscale-0`, `opacity-70 hover:opacity-100`, plus a `motion-reduce:` variant to kill the marquee. Tailwind's `motion-reduce` and `motion-safe` variants make the reduced-motion contract a one-word prefix.
*   **shadcn/ui + Radix:** Nothing prebuilt, but the idiom is a small composed component with `cva` variants (`static` | `marquee` | `grid`). Radix contributes no logo primitive, but its `VisuallyHidden` is exactly what you use for the decorative-set's hidden list and the marquee clone's screen-reader handling.
*   **Bootstrap 5:** Composed from the grid/flex utilities: `d-flex flex-wrap justify-content-center align-items-center gap-4`, `img-fluid` on the marks. No logo-bar component, no built-in grayscale — you add the filter yourself. Bootstrap's `visually-hidden` class covers the labeled-region list.

**Common thread:** every system makes you _assemble_ this from an image list + a flex/grid track + spacing tokens. That's why the accessibility and legal contract has no guardrails — nobody's component enforces alt-text discipline or a reduced-motion marquee fallback for you.

### 8b. Visual Styles / Trends

In 2026 the logo bar is quietly getting more dynamic. **Liquid Glass** (Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe language) shows up as logo strips sitting on a translucent, refractive band that subtly shifts as you scroll — the monochrome marks float on frosted material. The counter-trend is **Swiss/minimal**: a single hairline-bordered band, evenly spaced monochrome logos, zero motion, which reads as the most premium and enterprise-credible. **Motion-forward but restrained** marquees (spring easing, edge-fade masks) are replacing the old constant-speed CSS scroll, always gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`. And **monochrome-tint bars** (all logos recolored to one brand hue via CSS mask / `currentColor`) are trending as a way to make a chaotic set of brand colors feel like part of _your_ system rather than a sponsor billboard. The through-line: quieter, more unified, more respectful of motion preferences.

## 9\. The Code

Real, runnable, production-shaped implementations. The recurring hard parts — height-normalization, grayscale-with-legible-rest-state, the labeled region + heading association, and the marquee's `aria-hidden` clone + reduced-motion fallback — appear in every target.

### 9.1 HTML (semantic foundation with ARIA)

```plain
<!-- Static bar: a labeled region + a real list. The heading is programmatically
     associated via aria-labelledby, so AT announces "Trusted by, list, 5 items." -->
<section class="logobar" aria-labelledby="logobar-title">
  <h2 id="logobar-title" class="logobar__eyebrow">Trusted by teams at</h2>
  <ul class="logobar__track" role="list">
    <li class="logobar__item">
      <!-- Each logo is an image OF the company name → alt = the company name -->
      <img class="logobar__logo" src="/logos/acme.svg" alt="Acme Corporation"
           width="120" height="28" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
    </li>
    <li class="logobar__item">
      <img class="logobar__logo" src="/logos/globex.svg" alt="Globex"
           width="110" height="28" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
    </li>
    <li class="logobar__item">
      <!-- Linked logo: the <a> is the 44px target; alt still names the company -->
      <a class="logobar__link" href="/customers/initech">
        <img class="logobar__logo" src="/logos/initech.svg" alt="Initech"
             width="130" height="28" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
      </a>
    </li>
  </ul>
</section>

<!-- Decorative-as-a-SET alternative: when the strip is a single unit of proof and
     naming each mark adds noise, label the region and provide ONE visually-hidden
     list; the images themselves are aria-hidden with empty alt. -->
<section class="logobar" role="region" aria-label="Trusted by 5 leading brands">
  <ul class="logobar__track" role="list">
    <li class="logobar__item"><img src="/logos/acme.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true"></li>
    <li class="logobar__item"><img src="/logos/globex.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true"></li>
  </ul>
  <p class="visually-hidden">Includes Acme Corporation, Globex, Initech, Umbrella, and Soylent.</p>
</section>
```

Key rules: an image _of a company name_ has that company name as its `alt` — never empty, never the filename. Only use empty `alt` + a hidden list when you deliberately treat the strip as one decorative unit. The eyebrow heading is associated with `aria-labelledby`, not just visually stacked.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (reusable component)

```plain
import { useId } from "react";

export interface Logo {
  name: string;              // company name → alt text, never optional
  src: string;
  href?: string;             // present = linked logo
  scale?: number;            // per-logo optical tuning
  width?: number;
  height?: number;
}

type LogoBarProps = {
  logos: Logo[];
  /** Heading shown + programmatically associated. If omitted, region falls back to aria-label. */
  title?: string;
  variant?: "static" | "marquee";
  className?: string;
};

export function LogoBar({ logos, title, variant = "static", className = "" }: LogoBarProps) {
  const headingId = useId();
  if (logos.length === 0) return null;

  // Marquee needs a duplicated, aria-hidden set for seamless scroll.
  const sets = variant === "marquee" ? [false, true] : [false];

  return (
    <section
      className={`logobar logobar--${variant} ${className}`}
      aria-labelledby={title ? headingId : undefined}
      aria-label={title ? undefined : `Trusted by ${logos.length} brands`}
    >
      {title && <h2 id={headingId} className="logobar__eyebrow">{title}</h2>}
      {sets.map((isClone) => (
        <ul
          key={isClone ? "clone" : "primary"}
          className="logobar__track"
          role="list"
          aria-hidden={isClone || undefined}
          data-clone={isClone || undefined}
        >
          {logos.map((logo) => (
            <li className="logobar__item" key={logo.name}>
              <LogoMark logo={logo} decorative={isClone} />
            </li>
          ))}
        </ul>
      ))}
    </section>
  );
}

function LogoMark({ logo, decorative }: { logo: Logo; decorative: boolean }) {
  const img = (
    <img
      className="logobar__logo"
      src={logo.src}
      alt={decorative ? "" : logo.name}
      aria-hidden={decorative || undefined}
      width={logo.width}
      height={logo.height}
      loading="lazy"
      decoding="async"
      style={logo.scale ? ({ "--logo-scale": logo.scale } as React.CSSProperties) : undefined}
      onError={(e) => {
        // Broken logo → replace with the company name as text, not a broken icon.
        const el = e.currentTarget;
        const text = document.createElement("span");
        text.className = "logobar__fallback";
        text.textContent = logo.name;
        el.replaceWith(text);
      }}
    />
  );
  if (logo.href && !decorative) {
    return (
      <a className="logobar__link" href={logo.href} aria-label={`${logo.name} — read case study`}>
        {img}
      </a>
    );
  }
  return img;
}
```

### 9.4 Vanilla JavaScript (marquee control, lazy, reduced-motion, fallback)

```plain
class LogoBar {
  constructor(root) {
    this.root = root;
    this.track = root.querySelector(".logobar__track");
    this.isMarquee = root.classList.contains("logobar--marquee");
    this.mql = window.matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)");
    this.init();
  }

  init() {
    this.wireFallbacks();
    if (this.isMarquee) {
      this.setupMarquee();
      this.mql.addEventListener("change", () => this.setupMarquee());
    }
  }

  // Broken logo images degrade to the company name as text.
  wireFallbacks() {
    this.root.querySelectorAll(".logobar__logo").forEach((img) => {
      img.addEventListener("error", () => {
        const span = document.createElement("span");
        span.className = "logobar__fallback";
        span.textContent = img.getAttribute("alt") || img.dataset.name || "";
        img.replaceWith(span);
      });
    });
  }

  setupMarquee() {
    // Remove any prior clone so toggling reduced-motion is clean.
    this.root.querySelectorAll('.logobar__track[data-clone]').forEach((n) => n.remove());
    if (this.mql.matches) return; // reduced motion → CSS renders a static wrapped bar

    const clone = this.track.cloneNode(true);
    clone.setAttribute("aria-hidden", "true");
    clone.setAttribute("data-clone", "");
    clone.querySelectorAll("img").forEach((img) => { img.alt = ""; img.setAttribute("aria-hidden", "true"); });
    clone.querySelectorAll("a").forEach((a) => { a.tabIndex = -1; }); // clone links not tabbable
    this.track.after(clone);
  }
}

document.querySelectorAll(".logobar").forEach((el) => new LogoBar(el));
```

## 10\. Accessibility

The logo bar's accessibility contract is subtler than a button's because the content is _images of text_ and the marquee variant actively creates AT hazards. Standalone, non-negotiable.

**The two correct patterns (pick one, don't mix):**

1. **Named list (default).** Each logo is a meaningful image → `<img>` with `alt` = the **company name** (not "logo," not the filename). Wrap in a `<ul role="list">` so the count is announced. Associate the eyebrow heading with the region via `aria-labelledby`.
2. **Decorative set.** If you deliberately treat the whole strip as one unit of proof and naming each mark is noise, give the region an `aria-label` ("Trusted by 5 leading brands"), set every image `alt=""` + `aria-hidden="true"`, and provide **one** `visually-hidden` list of the names so the information still exists for AT.

Never leave `alt` missing — that's a raw WCAG **1.1.1 (Non-text Content)** failure, and screen readers fall back to reading the file path aloud.

**ARIA roles & attributes:**

*   Region: `role="region"` + `aria-labelledby` (heading) or `aria-label`. This makes it a navigable landmark.
*   Track: `role="list"`, items `role="listitem"` (native `<ul>`/`<li>` give these for free).
*   Marquee clone: `aria-hidden="true"` on the entire duplicated track, `alt=""` on its images, and `tabindex="-1"` on any cloned links.
*   Eyebrow: a real heading (`<h2>`), associated — not a styled `<div>`.

**Keyboard map (interactive/linked variant only):**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| `Tab` | Move focus to the next logo link (static logos are not focusable and are skipped) |
| `Shift + Tab` | Move focus to the previous logo link |
| `Enter` | Activate the focused logo link (navigate to case study / partner) |
| `Esc` | (Marquee) no-op, but focus-within keeps it paused while you tab through |

Static (non-linked) logos are **not** in the tab order and must not be given `tabindex="0"` — there's nothing to activate; that just adds empty tab stops.

**Focus management:** linked logos show a visible focus ring on the `<a>` (never `outline:none` without a replacement), and focusing a logo inside a marquee must **pause** the scroll (`:focus-within`) so the target isn't moving under the user.

**Contrast (the treatment trap):** desaturated logos still must meet **non-text contrast (WCAG 1.4.11, ≥3:1)** against the band background — a light-gray monochrome logo at `opacity:.6` on a white band can silently drop below the threshold and become unreadable. Keep an opacity floor and test the monochrome rendering, not the full-color original. And color-on-hover must **not** be the only way to perceive a logo: it must be legible at rest for touch users, keyboard users, and anyone who never hovers.

**Target size:** linked logos need a **≥44×44px** activation area (WCAG 2.5.8), achieved with padding on the `<a>`, even when the visible mark is 20–28px tall.

**Reduced motion:** under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, the marquee **must not animate** — render a static wrapped or scrollable bar and drop the duplicated set. Also disable the grayscale→color transition.

**Common failures specific to this component:**

*   Missing/filename `alt` — the #1 issue, kills the whole proof for AT.
*   Marquee reads every company **twice** because the clone wasn't `aria-hidden`.
*   Color-only-on-hover logos invisible/illegible at rest on touch and keyboard.
*   Desaturated logos failing non-text contrast against the band.
*   Static logos wrapped in `<a href="#">` or `tabindex="0"` creating dead tab stops.
*   Marquee that can't be paused — a moving link is nearly impossible to click, and the motion itself excludes vestibular-sensitive users.
* * *

## 11\. Innovative / Emerging Ideas

*   **Scroll-driven, motion-safe marquee (2026 CSS).** Replace the JS/`@keyframes` scroll with the CSS **Scroll-Driven Animations API** (`animation-timeline: scroll()`), so the strip advances with the page scroll and naturally stops when the user stops — no runaway motion, and it's trivially gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **`content-visibility: auto`** **+ container queries.** Skip rendering/painting a below-the-fold logo bar until it's near the viewport, and use container queries so the _bar itself_ decides wrap-vs-scroll based on its own width, not the global breakpoint — the logo bar becomes genuinely self-contained.
*   **Auto-normalizing logos via SVG viewBox math.** A build step that reads each SVG's `viewBox`, computes its optical weight, and emits a per-logo `--logo-scale` so height-equal logos also _look_ equal — killing the hand-tuning step.
*   **Personalized / dynamic proof.** Server-render the logo set to match the visitor's inferred industry (from referrer, campaign, or account) so a healthcare buyer sees healthcare logos — the same slot, higher relevance, measured lift. (Requires honest permission for every logo in every set.)
*   **AI-generated alt discipline.** A CI check that flags any logo `<img>` whose `alt` equals the filename or is empty-without-decorative-intent, so the #1 a11y failure can't ship.
*   **Liquid-glass proof band.** The 2026 Apple aesthetic: monochrome marks floating on a translucent refractive band that subtly parallaxes on scroll — energy without a literal marquee.
* * *

## 12\. Conversion / UX Killers

Every one of these silently _lowers_ trust — the opposite of the component's job — and none throw an error.

*   **Too few / unknown logos.** Three logos nobody recognizes reads as "we scraped these," and _weakens_ credibility. Fix: 4+ recognizable marks, or drop the bar for a named testimonial. Relevance to _this_ buyer beats fame.
*   **The marquee as an anti-pattern.** An auto-scrolling strip that never stops is often worse than a static row: it's hard to actually read a moving logo, it excludes motion-sensitive users, it doubles AT announcements when the clone isn't hidden, and it repaints constantly. Fix: prefer static; if you must marquee, pause on hover/focus, `aria-hidden` the clone, and fall back to static under reduced-motion. Don't animate proof for animation's sake.
*   **Mismatched logo sizing.** Normalizing on a fixed _box_ (or eyeballing) makes one logo tower over another, and the whole strip reads amateur — which _lowers_ trust. Fix: normalize on optical height with per-logo scale tuning.
*   **Grayscale that's actually illegible.** Desaturate + low opacity until a light logo disappears into the background, and you've hidden your proof. Fix: keep an opacity floor, verify non-text contrast on the monochrome render, ensure legibility at rest (not just on hover).
*   **Blurry raster logos.** Shipping 1x PNGs that go soft on retina, or JPEG-artifacted marks, signals low quality on your highest-intent page. Fix: SVG wherever possible; 2x PNG fallback with `srcset`.
*   **Dead-linking logos to nothing (or to the partner, losing the visitor).** Making every logo a link to the partner's homepage sends your hard-won traffic _away_. Fix: link only to _your_ case study for that client, or keep them static.
*   **Logo bar competing with the hero/CTA.** Full-color, large, animated logos pull attention from the actual conversion action. Fix: keep it quiet — desaturated, small, generous spacing — so it supports, not competes.
*   **Layout shift on load.** Un-dimensioned logos pop in and shove the CTA down (CLS), and users mis-click. Fix: set `width`/`height` or an aspect-ratio box; lazy-load below the fold but reserve space.
* * *

## 13\. Advanced Patterns

*   **TypeScript-enforced alt discipline (discriminated union).** Make it impossible to add a logo without a name, and impossible to mark one decorative without opting the whole set in:

```plain
type NamedLogo = { decorative?: false; name: string; src: string; href?: string };
type DecorativeLogo = { decorative: true; name: string; src: string }; // name still required for the hidden list
export type Logo = NamedLogo | DecorativeLogo;

// The region must be labeled one of two mutually-exclusive ways — never both, never neither:
type LabeledByHeading = { title: string; ariaLabel?: never };
type LabeledByAria = { title?: never; ariaLabel: string };
export type LogoBarProps = { logos: Logo[] } & (LabeledByHeading | LabeledByAria);
```

Now `<LogoBar logos={...} />` with neither `title` nor `ariaLabel` fails to compile — the unlabeled-region a11y bug can't ship.

*   **Three-tier design tokens for the treatment.** Keep the desaturation, opacity floor, height, and gap as component tokens referencing semantics, so a rebrand or a per-client skin is a token swap, not a CSS rewrite:

```css
:root {
  /* primitive */    --platinum: #E8E6E1; --eminence: #5F2C82; --gold: #DCA424;
  /* semantic */     --surface-proof: #0A0A0A; --focus: var(--gold);
  /* component */     --logobar-bg: var(--surface-proof);
                      --logobar-logo-h: 28px;
                      --logobar-logo-opacity: .72;   /* the contrast floor lives here */
                      --logobar-gap: 40px;
                      --logobar-desaturate: 1;       /* set to 0 for a full-color skin */
}
.logobar { background: var(--logobar-bg); }
.logobar__logo { height: var(--logobar-logo-h); opacity: var(--logobar-logo-opacity);
                 filter: grayscale(var(--logobar-desaturate)); }
```

*   **CSS-mask monochrome tinting for single-color SVGs.** Recolor any logo to a brand hue without editing the asset, so a chaotic set of brand colors becomes one coherent tint:

```css
.logobar--tinted .logobar__logo {
  background: var(--eminence);
  -webkit-mask: var(--logo-src) center / contain no-repeat;
          mask: var(--logo-src) center / contain no-repeat;
}
/* <img style="--logo-src:url(/logos/acme.svg)"> — the img's own pixels are masked out, the fill shows through */
```

*   **Seamless marquee math.** Duplicate the set exactly once and translate `-50%` (not `-100%`): because the track is two identical halves, at `-50%` the second half sits exactly where the first started, so the loop has no visible jump regardless of logo count.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost

*   **SVG over raster, inline vs. request tradeoff.** SVG logos are tiny, crisp at any DPI, and tintable. But inlining 15 SVGs bloats HTML and can't be cached separately; `<img src>` to a cached SVG file is usually better above ~2KB. For raster fallbacks, ship `srcset` (1x/2x) and modern formats (`avif`/`webp`) — never a single heavy PNG per logo.
*   **Lazy-load below the fold, eager above.** A logo bar under the hero is often above the fold → do **not** lazy-load those (lazy delays LCP-adjacent content); mark them `loading="eager"`/`fetchpriority` as needed. A logo bar deep in the page → `loading="lazy"` so it doesn't compete for bandwidth on first paint. Reserve dimensions either way to protect CLS.
*   **Compositor-only marquee.** Animate **only** `transform: translateX()` — never `margin-left`, `left`, or `width`, which force layout/reflow every frame across the whole strip. Add `will-change: transform` sparingly (only while animating) and `contain: layout paint` on the region so the moving track can't trigger outside reflow.
*   **`content-visibility: auto`****.** For a logo bar far down the page, `content-visibility: auto` with a `contain-intrinsic-size` skips rendering/painting until it scrolls near — measurable savings when the bar has many marks.
*   **Tree-shaking / import discipline.** If logos come from an icon/logo package, use **named imports** (`import { AcmeLogo } from "@/logos"`), never `import * as Logos` — the namespace import defeats tree-shaking and can pull hundreds of KB of marks you don't render. Better still, keep logos as static assets, not JS modules, so they never enter the bundle at all.
*   **Don't animate what's paused/off-screen.** Pause the marquee when the tab is hidden (`document.visibilityState`) and when it's scrolled out of view (IntersectionObserver) so an unseen strip isn't burning a repaint every frame on battery.

## 15\. Security

The logo bar's _technical_ attack surface is small — it's mostly static images — but it is not zero, and its **legal** surface is unusually large for a display component.

**Technical:**

*   **Injection via logo data.** If the logo list (names, `src`, `href`) comes from a CMS, DB, or user-editable config, every field is untrusted. A `name` rendered into markup must be escaped (XSS if a "company name" is `<img onerror=...>`). An `href` must be validated to `https:`/relative — reject `javascript:` URLs, which would turn a "logo link" into script execution. The Jinja/React examples escape by default; the danger is any `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` / `v-html` / `[innerHTML]` shortcut for logo markup.
*   **Untrusted SVG is active content.** SVG files can contain `<script>` and event handlers. If logos are **uploaded** (partner-submitted, user-generated), never inline them or serve them from your origin without sanitization (e.g. DOMPurify with an SVG profile, or serve from a separate sandboxed domain with `Content-Security-Policy`). A trusted, hand-curated SVG in your repo is fine; an uploaded one is an XSS vector.
*   **SSRF / hotlinking via remote** **`src`****.** If a CMS lets editors point `src` at arbitrary URLs and your server proxies/optimizes them (e.g. `next/image` with a remote loader), restrict `remotePatterns` to known hosts so the optimizer can't be aimed at internal services.
*   **Endpoint (when logos are fetched).** The `/api/logos` endpoint in §9.13 returns public data, so authz is minimal, but still validate/whitelist which logos are returned and rate-limit it like any public endpoint.

**Legal (the real risk):** displaying another company's logo displays their **trademark**. Using it to imply an endorsement, partnership, or customer relationship you don't actually have is trademark infringement and can be false advertising. Before a logo goes in the bar: confirm you have **permission** (a signed customer, a logo-usage agreement, or an explicit brand-guidelines allowance), use the **approved** version of the mark, and don't distort/recolor it in ways the owner's brand guidelines forbid (the grayscale/tint treatment itself can violate some brand guidelines — check). "As seen in" press logos generally have more latitude (nominative fair use for factual coverage), but "Trusted by" customer logos need real permission. When a client relationship ends, pull their logo.

This is the section teams skip because "it's just images" — but permission and trademark care are the component's defining obligation.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist

Ship-ready gate. A logo bar is not done until:

- [ ] **Semantics:** a labeled `role="region"` (heading via `aria-labelledby`, or `aria-label`); logos in a real `role="list"`.
- [ ] **Alt-text contract:** every meaningful logo's `alt` = the company name; OR the decorative-set pattern (`alt=""` + one visually-hidden name list) — never missing, never the filename.
- [ ] **Marquee (if used):** duplicated set is `aria-hidden` with empty alt and non-tabbable links; pauses on hover/focus; renders static under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- [ ] **Reduced motion + transitions:** no marquee animation and no forced grayscale→color transition when reduce is set.
- [ ] **Perception without hover:** logos legible at rest; color-on-hover is delight only, never the sole channel.
- [ ] **Contrast:** monochrome/desaturated logos meet non-text contrast (≥3:1) against the band; opacity floor enforced.
- [ ] **Target size:** linked logos have ≥44×44px activation area via anchor padding; static logos have **no** tab stop.
- [ ] **Sizing:** normalized on optical height (`width:auto`, `object-fit:contain`), per-logo scale hook available; no squashed marks.
- [ ] **Assets & performance:** SVG or `srcset` raster; dimensions reserved (no CLS); lazy below the fold, eager above; transform-only marquee.
- [ ] **Resilience:** broken-image fallback degrades to the company name as text.
- [ ] **Empty/too-few handling:** component hides itself (or swaps pattern) below ~4 logos.
- [ ] **Legal:** written permission for every customer logo; approved mark version; press "as seen in" is factual; removed when a relationship ends.

## 17\. Visual Styles

The same semantic logo bar — labeled region, real list, normalized heights, alt-text, reduced-motion contract — wears any of these skins. Descriptions are specific to _this_ component.

*   **Flat:** logos on a solid, single-color band, no shadow; desaturated to one gray, even gap, hard edges. The most common and most robust logo-bar skin — reads instantly, scales fast.
*   **Material:** the band is a tonal surface with a subtle elevation shadow separating it from the section above; logos sit on an 8dp grid, and a linked logo gets a faint ripple/state-layer on press. Motion honors Material's reduced-motion tokens.
*   **Glassmorphism:** the band is translucent with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over a vivid hero image, monochrome logos floating on the frosted panel. Watch non-text contrast hard here — the blur can drop logos below 3:1.
*   **Liquid Glass (2026, Apple):** the proof band is a refractive translucent material with a specular rim; logos float on it and the surface subtly shifts/parallaxes as you scroll, light bending behind the marks. Deep Eminence backdrop makes the refraction sing; reduced-motion kills the shift.
*   **Neumorphism:** each logo sits in a softly extruded or inset "pill" on a same-color surface, dual light/dark shadows giving a pressed-in trust plaque. Pretty but contrast-poor — reserve for a light, low-stakes footer strip, and keep logos legible.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** the bar as a physical "sponsor plaque" — a beveled metal or wood rail with each logo etched/embossed into it, inner highlights and gradient fill. Heavy, tactile, rare — event or awards contexts.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black band border, hard offset shadow, zero radius, logos left full-color and un-normalized on a stark background, maybe a chunky "TRUSTED BY" in a mono face. High personality, deliberately anti-polished.
*   **Claymorphism:** logos on big-radius, puffy pastel tiles with inner top-light and soft outer glow, the strip reading as a friendly row of rounded chips. Playful consumer/education brands.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** an animated multi-hue gradient band on dark, monochrome-white logos floating over the moving color. Premium and expressive; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (freeze the gradient) and check white-on-gradient contrast at every frame.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** a single hairline top-and-bottom rule, evenly spaced monochrome logos, generous whitespace, zero motion, a tiny uppercase eyebrow. Reads as the most premium and enterprise-credible skin.
*   **UJG Brand:** Platinum-tinted monochrome logos on a deep Eminence/Night band, a Goldenrod focus ring on linked marks, Methanerse uppercase eyebrow ("Trusted by"), gap and height on the house token scale, warm and confident. The default.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin; the behavior is the skeleton. The labeled region, the real list, the alt-text contract, the reduced-motion marquee fallback, the focus ring, non-text contrast, and the 44px target for linked logos are **identical across all eleven skins**.
