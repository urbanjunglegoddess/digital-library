# Avatar Group

# The Avatar Group: A Senior Engineer's Complete Breakdown
A pile of overlapping faces that says "these people" at a glance — and the accessibility trap that turns it into screen-reader noise. Here's everything from first principles to production code.

**Try it live:** the companion **playground** for this component isn't built yet — it lands in a later pass. When it ships it'll let you tune `max`, size, overlap, ring width, and the overflow behavior, and output live code for every target below. For now this doc is the source of truth.

**Audit angle:** the companion **a11y audit** for Avatar Group (coming later) will paste in your group markup and check the one thing everyone gets wrong — whether the group announces as _one meaningful thing_ ("3 collaborators: Ada, Grace, and 5 more") or as eight anonymous "image" nodes in a row.

This component is a _composition_, not a primitive. It stacks the [Avatar](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217111) — go there for the image/initials/icon fallback chain, `object-fit`, ring tokens, and single-avatar sizing. This doc does **not** re-derive that; it covers what happens when you put _many_ avatars in a row and one of them becomes a "+N" button. Also cross-links the [Tag/Chip (Badge)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217151) — the overflow counter is a badge-shaped affordance, and the two share styling DNA.
* * *

## 1\. What an Avatar Group Actually Is
An avatar group is a set of avatars laid out with deliberate overlap to represent a _collection of people_ (or entities) as a single visual unit: the collaborators on a doc, the attendees on an event, the assignees on a task, the members of a team. It compresses "who's involved" into a few square inches.

Three things people lump together and shouldn't:

*   **Avatar (one entity):** a single person's face/initials/icon. The atom. Covered in its [own doc](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217111).
*   **Avatar Group (a set, summarized):** _N_ people shown as _M_ overlapping avatars plus a "+`N−M`" overflow indicator. The point is the _summary_ — you are deliberately **not** showing everyone.
*   **Avatar List / Member list (a set, enumerated):** every member shown, usually vertically, each with name and role, often with actions (remove, change permission). This is a `<ul>`, not a stack. If the user needs to _act on individuals_, you want a list, not a group.

The single most common mistake: using an avatar _group_ where the job actually needs an avatar _list_. A group answers "roughly who and how many." A list answers "manage these specific people." Rule of thumb: **if the overlap is decorative-summary, it's a group; if each row needs its own affordances, it's a list; the "+N" chip is the bridge between them** — it opens the list.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The avatar group looks like pure decoration. It isn't — it carries social proof, wayfinding, and a nasty accessibility liability all at once.

*   **Social proof and presence.** "6 people are on this" is one of the strongest trust signals in collaborative software. Figma, Google Docs, Linear, GitHub — the live face pile _is_ the "this is real, people are here" cue. Empty where a group should be reads as a dead product.
*   **It's a compression decision with a cost.** Every avatar you hide behind "+N" is information you chose to drop. Get the ordering wrong (show randoms, hide the owner) and you've buried the one face that mattered.
*   **It's the single worst offender for screen-reader noise in the whole component library.** Eight avatars naively marked up = eight "image" or eight "Ada Lovelace, image" announcements in a row, with the "+5" landing as a meaningless "5". A blind user hears a wall of nothing. Done right, the _entire group_ announces as one phrase. This is the crux of the component and §10 is non-negotiable.
*   **Perceived performance.** These render in headers, sidebars, table rows, and lists — often dozens per screen. A group that reflows, pops in, or ships full-size images for 24px circles makes the whole app feel janky.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy of an Avatar Group
Predictable parts, each with a job:

*   **Container:** the wrapper that establishes the row and the stacking context. Semantically a **list** (`role="list"` / `<ul>`) _or_ a single labelled image, depending on strategy (see §10). Not a naked `<div>` full of `<img>`.
*   **Avatar slots:** the individual [Avatars](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217111), pulled leftward with negative margin so each overlaps its neighbor.
*   **Ring / border:** the ring around each avatar — usually 2px in the _surface_ color — that separates one overlapping face from the next. Without it, overlapping circles smear into an unreadable blob. This is the load-bearing detail juniors skip.
*   **Overflow indicator ("+N"):** the trailing chip showing how many are hidden. Two flavors: a static count (dumb) or a **real button** that opens a popover/list of the rest (correct for anything interactive).
*   **Stacking order (z-index):** who sits on top of whom. The first avatar usually sits highest, so the group reads left-to-right like a fan of cards.
*   **Tooltip / hover-reveal:** name (and often role/status) surfaced on hover/focus of each avatar. See [Tooltip](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216511).
*   **Presence dot (optional):** the online/away status badge on a corner of each avatar — a [Badge](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217711) inherited from the Avatar.
*   **Add-member affordance (optional):** a trailing dashed "+" button to invite someone. Different from the overflow "+N" — one _reveals_, one _adds_. Never make them look identical.
* * *

## 4\. Sizes & Density
Two dimensions to tune: the avatar size and the _overlap_ (how much each is pulled over its neighbor). Overlap is usually expressed as a fraction of the avatar diameter — 25–35% is the readable sweet spot; more than ~40% and faces disappear.

| Size | Diameter | Ring | Overlap (margin-left) | Typical "+N" font | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| XS | 20px | 1.5px | −6px (30%) | 10px | Dense table rows, inline "assignees" |
| SM | 24px | 2px | −8px (33%) | 11px | Cards, list items, comment threads |
| MD | 32px | 2px | −10px (31%) | 12px | Default — headers, task detail |
| LG | 40px | 2.5px | −12px (30%) | 14px | Team pages, event attendees |
| XL | 56px | 3px | −16px (29%) | 18px | Profile/hero "featured people" |

Rules that matter:

*   **Overlap is a token, not a magic number.** Drive it off the diameter (`--overlap: calc(var(--size) * -0.3)`) so every size stays proportional.
*   **The ring must match the surface the group sits on**, not a fixed white. On a dark Eminence header the ring is Eminence; on a card it's the card color. A fixed-white ring on a dark surface draws ugly halos.
*   **`max`** **(visible count) is responsive.** Show 5 on desktop, 3 on mobile — the "+N" absorbs the difference. Don't fix the count to the widest screen.
*   **The "+N" chip is the same diameter as the avatars.** It's a circle in the stack, not a pill hanging off the end (unless you _intend_ a pill — that's a legitimate variant, just be deliberate).
* * *

## 5\. States
A single Avatar has fallback states (image → initials → icon → skeleton — see its [doc](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217111)). The _group_ adds its own layer of states on top:

1. **Default (rest)** — the stack at rest, first avatar on top, "+N" trailing.
2. **Loading** — data not back yet. Show _skeleton_ circles in the stack (see [Skeleton](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217191)), not a spinner, and reserve the row height so nothing reflows when real faces arrive.
3. **Empty (zero members)** — the state everyone forgets. Show an "Invite people" affordance or a muted "No one assigned," _never_ a bare collapsed row that looks broken.
4. **Single member** — degrade gracefully: one avatar, no overflow, no awkward "+0".
5. **Exactly at** **`max`** — no overflow chip at all; `+0` must never render.
6. **Over** **`max`** **(overflow)** — the "+N" chip appears; `N = total − shown`. Off-by-one here (showing "+N" that includes a visible face) is the classic bug.
7. **Hover (per avatar)** — the hovered avatar lifts to the top of the stack (z-index bump) and/or reveals its name tooltip. The lift is what makes a dense stack legible.
8. **Focus (per avatar / chip)** — keyboard focus ring on the individual interactive element; the focused avatar also lifts so it's not clipped behind a neighbor.
9. **Overflow open** — the "+N" button is pressed (`aria-expanded="true"`) and its popover/list of hidden members is showing.
10. **Interactive vs. static** — a group whose avatars link to profiles has hover/focus/active per avatar; a purely informational group has none and must **not** fake affordances (no pointer cursor, no hover lift on something you can't click).

The through-line: a group is a _container of stateful things_ **and** a stateful thing itself. Both layers need designing.
* * *

## 6\. Types & Variants
*   **Summary group (default):** _M_ faces + "+N". The everyday case.
*   **Full group (no overflow):** small fixed teams where everyone fits; `max` effectively infinite.
*   **Interactive group:** each avatar is a link/button to a profile; "+N" opens the rest. The heavyweight version.
*   **Static / informational group:** pure display, no clicks. Lighter, but still needs a real group label.
*   **Presence group:** each avatar carries an online/away status dot — common in chat and live-collab headers.
*   **Stacked with add-CTA:** trailing dashed "+" to invite. The add button is a _separate_ affordance from overflow.
*   **Pill-overflow variant:** the "+N" renders as a wider pill ("+5 more") instead of a circle — reads more as a link, good when you want to invite the click.
*   **Vertical / fanned:** stacked top-to-bottom or arced (rare, mostly marketing). Same semantics, different transform.
*   **Grid fallback:** past a threshold, abandon the stack and render a wrapped grid or a full [List](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216771). A stack of 40 is nonsense; know when to switch components.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use an avatar group when the user needs a **fast, summarized sense of** **_who_** **and** **_how many_** — and does _not_ need to act on each person individually in place.

Reach for it when:

*   Showing collaborators/attendees/assignees at a glance in a header, card, or row.
*   Presence matters ("who's here right now").
*   Space is tight and a full list would dominate.

Don't use one when:

*   **The user must manage individuals** (remove, change role, message). That's a [List](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216771) or member table. Don't cram management into a face pile.
*   **Exact identity of everyone matters** and there are many — a stack that hides most of them under "+N" is the wrong tool.
*   **There's only ever one person** — just render an [Avatar](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217111) with a name.
*   **The count is the real message** (e.g. "1,204 attendees") — a number with one avatar, or a plain count, beats a meaningless 5-face stack.

Placement heuristics:

*   Order by _relevance_, not alphabet: owner/host first, then most-active or currently-present, then the rest into overflow (see §12).
*   Keep the "+N" chip the natural click target for "see everyone" — it's where users reach when the stack isn't enough.
*   In table rows, cap `max` low (2–3) and lean on overflow; density beats completeness there.

## 8\. Avatar Groups Across Design Systems
Same face pile, different opinions.

*   **Material Design (Google / MUI):** `AvatarGroup` with a `max` prop; surplus collapse into a `+N` "surplus avatar." Rings default to the background paper color. `spacing` prop controls overlap. Total can be overridden so "+N" reflects a server count larger than what's loaded.
*   **Apple HIG:** no single "avatar group" primitive; you compose it. Presence and "and N others" phrasing show up in Messages/FaceTime. SF Symbols + `.overlappingContentInsets`\-style layout done by hand. The HIG instinct: restraint, real names on tap, no clutter.
*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** `AvatarGroup` is first-class (Teams is built on it) with `layout` = `stack` | `pie` | `spread`, an `overflowIndicator`, and a strong built-in story for **overflow menus** and presence badges. The most mature enterprise take.
*   **Ant Design:** `Avatar.Group` with `maxCount`, `maxPopoverTrigger` (hover/click), and `maxStyle` for the "+N" node — overflow opening a popover list is baked in, which is exactly the right default.
*   **Chakra / Radix-land:** Chakra ships `AvatarGroup` with `max`; Radix keeps `Avatar` headless and leaves grouping to you (you own the list semantics and the popover), which is the honest, composable path.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** no component — you compose it from `-space-x-*` negative-margin utilities, `ring-2 ring-*`, and `z-*`. Freedom plus the responsibility to get the semantics right yourself.
*   **shadcn/ui:** an `Avatar` (Radix under the hood); the _group_ is a small composition you write — negative margins + a "+N" that triggers a `Popover`. Shown in §9.7.
* * *

## 8b. Visual Styles (the same stack, eleven skins)
The stacking behavior, the ring, and the "+N" logic are identical across every look. Only the surface treatment changes. Cross every skin in the coming playground; full guidance lives in the [🎨 Design Styles (visual languages)](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551) doc.

*   **Flat:** solid ring in the surface color, crisp circles, no shadow. The default and fastest.
*   **Material:** each avatar gets a subtle elevation shadow; the "+N" surplus avatar sits on a tonal fill. Overlap reads as stacked cards.
*   **Glassmorphism:** the "+N" chip is a frosted translucent circle over a vivid backdrop. Guard contrast — a `+5` that can't be read is decoration, not information.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe material — the rings and the "+N" chip become refractive translucent discs with a specular rim and a sheen that shifts with content behind them. On a deep Eminence header the refraction sings. SwiftUI emits it natively via `.glassEffect()`; the web is an honest approximation.
*   **Neumorphism:** avatars sit in soft same-color wells with dual light/dark shadow. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent use only, never for a legibility-critical count.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** beveled rings, inner highlight, a faux-photo-frame edge. The "physical stack of Polaroids" look.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black rings, hard offset shadow, zero-radius square avatars, a loud "+N" in a boxed chip. Maximum personality.
*   **Claymorphism:** puffy avatars with big radius, inner top-light and bottom-shadow, soft glow. Friendly, rounded, toy-like.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** the "+N" chip and rings carry an animated multi-hue gradient on dark. Premium — honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** thin hairline rings, tight grid, the count set in a precise numeral. Typography does the work.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod rings on deep Eminence/Night surfaces, the "+N" chip in Goldenrod-on-Eminence with a warm glow, confident radius. Afro-Futurist house default.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is skin. The list semantics, the group label, keyboard order, focus ring, and 44px targets on interactive avatars stay identical across all eleven.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML (semantics first)
The wrong instinct is a `<div>` full of `<img>`. The right foundation is a labelled list — or, for a static summary, a single labelled image. Here's the list version:

```plain
<!-- Interactive group: each avatar links to a profile, "+N" opens the rest -->
<ul class="avatar-group" role="list"
    aria-label="Collaborators: Ada Lovelace, Grace Hopper, Alan Turing, and 5 more">
  <li class="avatar-group__item">
    <a href="/u/ada" class="avatar" aria-label="Ada Lovelace">
      <img src="/ada.jpg" alt="" width="32" height="32">
    </a>
  </li>
  <li class="avatar-group__item">
    <a href="/u/grace" class="avatar" aria-label="Grace Hopper">
      <img src="/grace.jpg" alt="" width="32" height="32">
    </a>
  </li>
  <li class="avatar-group__item">
    <a href="/u/alan" class="avatar" aria-label="Alan Turing">
      <span aria-hidden="true">AT</span>   <!-- initials fallback -->
    </a>
  </li>
  <li class="avatar-group__item">
    <!-- overflow is a REAL button, not a styled div -->
    <button type="button" class="avatar avatar--overflow"
            aria-haspopup="dialog" aria-expanded="false"
            aria-label="Show 5 more collaborators">
      +5
    </button>
  </li>
</ul>
```

Key HTML rules:

*   **Inner** **`<img>`** **gets** **`alt=""`** because the _name_ is already on the parent's `aria-label`. Doubling it makes the screen reader say the name twice. This is the single subtlest thing in the whole component.
*   **The group carries a real** **`aria-label`** that names the first few and summarizes the rest — that's what turns eight nodes into one sentence (§10).
*   **The "+N" is a** **`<button>`** with `aria-haspopup` + `aria-expanded`, because it _does something_. A `<div>+5</div>` is an accessibility bug and a dead end for keyboard users.
*   **Static, non-interactive summary?** Skip the list entirely and use one node: `<span role="img" aria-label="3 collaborators: Ada, Grace, and 5 more">` wrapping `aria-hidden` avatars. Fewer stops, one clean announcement.

### 9.2 CSS (the stack done right)
The whole visual trick is negative margin + a ring + z-index order + a hover/focus lift.

```css
.avatar-group {
  display: flex;
  list-style: none;
  margin: 0;
  padding: 0;
  /* the item padding-based hit area needs room; see a11y */
}

.avatar-group__item {
  margin-inline-start: var(--overlap, -10px); /* logical prop = RTL-safe */
}
.avatar-group__item:first-child {
  margin-inline-start: 0;
}

.avatar {
  --size: 32px;
  inline-size: var(--size);
  block-size: var(--size);
  display: inline-flex;
  align-items: center;
  justify-content: center;
  border-radius: 50%;
  overflow: hidden;
  background: var(--avatar-bg, #5F2C82);
  color: #fff;
  font: 600 12px/1 system-ui, sans-serif;
  /* the RING that separates overlapping faces — matches the surface */
  box-shadow: 0 0 0 2px var(--surface, #E8E6E1);
  position: relative;
  z-index: 1;
  transition: transform .12s ease, z-index 0s;
}
.avatar img { inline-size: 100%; block-size: 100%; object-fit: cover; }

/* Stack order: earlier avatars sit on top, reading L→R like a fan.
   Set inline (style="--i:0..") or with :nth-child in a fixed-size group. */
.avatar-group__item:nth-child(1) .avatar { z-index: 5; }
.avatar-group__item:nth-child(2) .avatar { z-index: 4; }
.avatar-group__item:nth-child(3) .avatar { z-index: 3; }
.avatar-group__item:nth-child(4) .avatar { z-index: 2; }

/* Hover/focus LIFT so a buried face becomes legible and un-clipped */
.avatar:hover,
.avatar:focus-visible {
  transform: translateY(-2px);
  z-index: 10;            /* jump above every neighbor */
}
.avatar:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid #DCA424;   /* Goldenrod focus ring */
  outline-offset: 2px;
}

/* The "+N" overflow chip: a circle in the stack, muted fill */
.avatar--overflow {
  background: var(--overflow-bg, #042D1D);
  color: var(--overflow-fg, #E8E6E1);
  cursor: pointer;
}

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .avatar { transition: none; }
  .avatar:hover, .avatar:focus-visible { transform: none; }
}
```

Why these choices:

*   **`box-shadow`** **ring, not** **`border`****\*\*\*\*.** A border eats into the box and shifts layout on hover; an inset-free `box-shadow` spread ring sits _outside_ the circle and never nudges the geometry.
*   **`margin-inline-start`****\*\*\*\*, not** **`margin-left`****\*\*\*\*.** In RTL the stack flips automatically — the first avatar stays "first" in reading order.
*   **Explicit** **`z-index`** **order** is what makes the fan read left-to-right. Reverse it and the stack fans the other way; either is fine, just be deliberate.
*   **Animate** **`transform`** **only** on the lift — no reflow, compositor-only, cheap at any count.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (the reusable component)
The senior version: `max` + overflow that opens a real popover, correct group labelling derived from the data, and a sensible degrade for empty/loading.

```typescript
import { forwardRef, useState, useId, type HTMLAttributes } from "react";
import { Avatar } from "./Avatar";          // the sibling primitive
import { OverflowPopover } from "./OverflowPopover";

export interface Person {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  src?: string;
  href?: string;
  status?: "online" | "away" | "offline";
}

type Size = "xs" | "sm" | "md" | "lg" | "xl";

interface AvatarGroupProps extends HTMLAttributes<HTMLUListElement> {
  people: Person[];
  max?: number;          // how many faces before overflow
  size?: Size;
  total?: number;        // server-side total when people[] is a partial page
  label?: string;        // override the auto group label
}

/** Build the one sentence a screen reader should hear. */
function groupLabel(people: Person[], shown: number, total: number, noun = "collaborators") {
  const names = people.slice(0, shown).map((p) => p.name);
  const hidden = total - shown;
  const list =
    hidden > 0
      ? names.join(", ")                                   // overflow: plain comma list; "and N more" is appended below
      : names.length <= 1
      ? names.join("")
      : names.slice(0, -1).join(", ") + ", and " + names.at(-1);
  const more = hidden > 0 ? `, and ${hidden} more` : "";
  return `${total} ${noun}: ${list}${more}`;
}

export const AvatarGroup = forwardRef<HTMLUListElement, AvatarGroupProps>(
  ({ people, max = 5, size = "md", total, label, className = "", ...rest }, ref) => {
    const [open, setOpen] = useState(false);
    const popoverId = useId();

    const count = total ?? people.length;
    const shown = Math.min(people.length, max);
    const overflow = count - shown;               // never renders "+0"
    const visible = people.slice(0, shown);
    const hidden = people.slice(shown);

    // Empty state: don't render a broken-looking bare row.
    if (count === 0) {
      return (
        <div className="avatar-group avatar-group--empty">No one assigned</div>
      );
    }

    return (
      <ul
        ref={ref}
        role="list"
        aria-label={label ?? groupLabel(people, shown, count)}
        className={`avatar-group avatar-group--${size} ${className}`}
        {...rest}
      >
        {visible.map((p, i) => (
          <li className="avatar-group__item" style={{ zIndex: shown - i }} key={p.id}>
            <Avatar person={p} size={size} interactive={!!p.href} />
          </li>
        ))}

        {overflow > 0 && (
          <li className="avatar-group__item" style={{ zIndex: 0 }}>
            <button
              type="button"
              className="avatar avatar--overflow"
              aria-haspopup="dialog"
              aria-expanded={open}
              aria-controls={open ? popoverId : undefined}
              aria-label={`Show ${overflow} more ${label ? "" : "collaborators"}`.trim()}
              onClick={() => setOpen((v) => !v)}
            >
              +{overflow}
            </button>
            {open && (
              <OverflowPopover
                id={popoverId}
                people={hidden}
                onClose={() => setOpen(false)}
              />
            )}
          </li>
        )}
      </ul>
    );
  }
);
AvatarGroup.displayName = "AvatarGroup";
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:

*   **`total`** **decouples display from data.** You show 5 faces but the label says "24 collaborators" because the server sent a count — you don't have to load 24 avatars to say 24.
*   **`groupLabel()`** **is the whole ballgame.** The auto-generated `aria-label` is what makes the group announce as one sentence instead of eight nodes. Everything else is polish; this is correctness.
*   **`overflow = count − shown`** computed once, guards `+0`, and stays correct when `total > people.length`.
*   **Empty and single-member both degrade** without special-casing at the call site.
*   **`aria-expanded`** **/** **`aria-controls`** wire the "+N" button to its popover so assistive tech knows it's a disclosure, not a mystery.

The popover itself is just a focus-trapped [Popover](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216551) wrapping a `<ul>` of the hidden people — a real [List](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216771), each row a link to the profile. Don't reinvent it here; compose the existing primitives.

### 9.5 shadcn/ui (Radix Avatar + Popover)
shadcn ships `Avatar`; the _group_ is a small composition — negative margins around Radix `Avatar`s, and a `Popover` triggered by the "+N".


### 9.8 Web Component (framework-agnostic)
A grouping element that stacks whatever `<img>`/avatar children you slot in and appends a computed "+N".

```typescript
class AvatarGroup extends HTMLElement {
  static observedAttributes = ["max", "label"];
  connectedCallback() { this.render(); }
  attributeChangedCallback() { this.render(); }
  render() {
    const max = Number(this.getAttribute("max") ?? 5);
    const kids = [...this.children];
    const overflow = Math.max(0, kids.length - max);
    kids.forEach((el, i) => {
      (el as HTMLElement).style.display = i < max ? "" : "none";
      (el as HTMLElement).style.zIndex = String(kids.length - i);
      (el as HTMLElement).style.marginInlineStart = i === 0 ? "0" : "-10px";
    });
    // append/update a real overflow button — omitted for brevity, but it's a <button>
    this.setAttribute("role", "list");
    if (this.getAttribute("label")) this.setAttribute("aria-label", this.getAttribute("label")!);
  }
}
customElements.define("avatar-group", AvatarGroup);
```

## 10\. Accessibility (the whole reason this doc exists)
A single Avatar's a11y is simple (alt text or an `aria-label`). The _group_ is where it goes wrong, and it goes wrong the same way every time: **N avatars announced as N separate meaningless nodes.** A sighted user sees "a few people." A screen-reader user hears "image, image, image, image, image, image, 5" — noise.

Two correct strategies. Pick one; never leave the naive default.

**Strategy A — one labelled image (static summary).** If the group is _not_ interactive, collapse it into a single node:

```plain
<span role="img" aria-label="3 collaborators: Ada, Grace, and 5 more">
  <img src="/ada.jpg" alt="" aria-hidden="true">
  <img src="/grace.jpg" alt="" aria-hidden="true">
  <span aria-hidden="true">+5</span>
</span>
```

One stop, one meaningful sentence. The children are `aria-hidden`; the _label_ carries all the meaning.

**Strategy B — a labelled list (interactive).** If avatars link to profiles and "+N" opens a menu, use a `role="list"` with a summarizing `aria-label`, each avatar labelled _once_, and the overflow as a real disclosure button:

*   Container: `role="list"` + `aria-label="6 collaborators: Ada Lovelace, Grace Hopper, Alan Turing, and 3 more"`. Reading the label alone tells the whole story; drilling in gives each person.
*   Each interactive avatar: labelled _once_ — either the inner `<img alt="Name">` **or** an `aria-label` on the link, **never both** (double-announce). The recommended pattern is `alt=""` on the img and `aria-label` on the link.
*   The "+N": a `<button>` with `aria-haspopup="dialog"` (or `menu`), `aria-expanded`, and a descriptive `aria-label="Show 3 more collaborators"` — never a bare "+3" that a screen reader reads as the number five.

**Keyboard map (interactive group):**

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| `Tab` | Move to the next interactive avatar / the "+N" button |
| `Shift+Tab` | Move backward |
| `Enter` / `Space` | Activate the focused avatar (go to profile) or open the "+N" popover |
| `Escape` | Close the open overflow popover, return focus to the "+N" button |
| Arrow keys | Within the open overflow list, move between members |

*   **Focus management on the popover:** opening "+N" moves focus into the list; closing returns focus to the "+N" button. Never drop focus to `<body>`.
*   **The focus/hover lift is functional, not decorative:** a focused avatar buried under a neighbor must lift (z-index bump) so its focus ring isn't clipped. A clipped focus ring is a WCAG focus-visibility failure.
*   **Consider a roving tabindex** for very large interactive stacks so the group is one Tab stop and arrows move within it — same pattern as a toolbar. For the common 3–6 avatar case, plain Tab order is fine.
*   **Tooltips are supplementary, never the only label.** Hover-to-reveal-name is great for sighted mouse users; it does nothing for keyboard or screen-reader users, so the name must _also_ be in the accessible name. See [Tooltip](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216511).
*   **Contrast:** the "+N" text vs. its chip fill ≥ 4.5:1; the separating ring must be visible against _both_ the avatar and the surface (a light ring on a light surface disappears — the faces smear).
*   **Target size:** interactive avatars and the "+N" need a ≥44×44px hit area even when the visual circle is 24–32px. Expand the hit area with padding on the `<li>`/link; the overlap must not steal clickable area from the neighbor underneath (the on-top avatar wins the pointer — make sure it's the one the user _sees_ on top).
*   **Presence dots** must not be color-only ("green = online"). Pair with text in the accessible name ("Ada Lovelace, online") or an icon shape.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
*   **Live presence with motion.** Real-time collab (Figma/Liveblocks-style) animates avatars in and out as people join/leave, often with a cursor color that matches the avatar ring. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — fade, don't fly.
*   **Liquid Glass overflow chips.** The 2026 Apple material on the "+N" disc: a refractive translucent counter that picks up the color of whatever it floats over. Gorgeous on a dark Eminence header; an honest `backdrop-filter` approximation on the web.
*   **AI-summarized groups.** Instead of "+5," a smart chip that summarizes the hidden set by relevance ("+5 from Design") — the overflow label becomes semantic, not just a count.
*   **Density-adaptive** **`max`****\*\*\*\*.** Container-query-driven groups that recompute how many faces fit as the container resizes, moving the boundary between shown and "+N" live, with no JS resize listener.
*   **Status-aware ordering.** Currently-online members auto-float to the front of the stack; offline members sink into overflow. The group becomes a live presence indicator, not a static roster.
*   **Hover-card previews.** Hovering an avatar opens a rich [Hover Card](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217451) (name, role, recent activity, message button) instead of a plain tooltip — the group becomes a directory.
* * *

## 12\. Conversion & Trust Killers
None of these throw an error. They quietly erode trust or bury the wrong information.

*   **The "+0" bug.** Off-by-one math renders "+0" when the count exactly equals `max`. It screams "nobody tested this." Guard it: overflow only renders when `hidden > 0`.
*   **Double-announced names.** `alt="Ada"` on the img _and_ `aria-label="Ada"` on the link = "Ada Lovelace, Ada Lovelace." Subtle, common, and it makes screen-reader users distrust the whole UI. Label once.
*   **Wrong ordering.** Showing five random members and hiding the owner/host under "+N" buries the one face that carried the meaning. Order by relevance: owner → present → active → the rest.
*   **The dead "+N".** A "+5" that isn't clickable is a promise the UI doesn't keep — users _will_ try to click it. Either make it a real button that opens the list, or (rarely) make it visibly non-interactive.
*   **Rings that don't separate.** No ring, or a ring the same color as the surface, and the overlapping faces smear into an unreadable blob. The ring is the whole reason the stack is legible.
*   **Reflow on load.** No reserved height, so the row is 0px until avatars arrive, then jumps — shoving the header content around. Reserve the row; skeleton the circles.
*   **Faking presence.** Showing a face pile for a feature nobody's actually using ("2 viewing" when it's just you in two tabs) burns trust the moment users notice. Presence must be real.
*   **Overflow that hides the count that matters.** For "1,204 attendees," a 5-face stack + "+1199" is worse than the number itself. When the _count_ is the message, lead with the number.

**The through-line:** these fail quietly. No crash, just a UI that feels slightly untrustworthy or slightly broken to the people who notice most — including every screen-reader user.

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
### Design tokens for the stack
Drive overlap, ring, and sizes off tokens so a rebrand or a density change is one edit, not a hunt:

```css
:root {
  --avatar-size-md: 32px;
  --avatar-ring: 2px;
  --avatar-overlap-ratio: -0.30;                 /* 30% overlap */
  --avatar-overlap: calc(var(--avatar-size-md) * var(--avatar-overlap-ratio));
  --surface: #E8E6E1;                            /* the ring matches THIS */
  --overflow-bg: #042D1D;
  --overflow-fg: #E8E6E1;
  --focus: #DCA424;
}
.avatar-group__item + .avatar-group__item { margin-inline-start: var(--avatar-overlap); }
.avatar { box-shadow: 0 0 0 var(--avatar-ring) var(--surface); }
```

Swap `--surface` per context (card vs. dark header) and the rings re-tune themselves. Vary `--avatar-overlap-ratio` for a denser table variant without touching markup.

### TypeScript: make the label un-forgettable
The one bug that matters is a missing/meaningless group label. Force it at compile time — the component _requires_ either a `label` or the `people` data it derives one from, and there's no path that renders without an accessible name:

```typescript
type LabeledGroup =
  | { people: Person[]; label?: string }          // derive from people
  | { people?: never; label: string };            // or supply one explicitly
// A group with neither won't compile — you can't ship an unlabeled stack.
```

### Container queries for adaptive `max`
Let the container decide how many faces fit, no resize listener:

```css
.avatar-group { container-type: inline-size; }
@container (max-width: 200px) { .avatar-group__item:nth-child(n+4) { display: none; } }
/* pair with JS/SSR that recomputes the "+N" count to match what's hidden */
```

The CSS hides overflow faces responsively; keep the "+N" count authoritative in your component so the label never lies about how many are hidden.

### Presence-driven z-order
Sort online members to the front and lift them visually so the stack reads as live presence — the ordering _is_ the information.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A face pile feels free. Multiplied across a table it isn't.

*   **Right-size the images.** A 32px avatar does not need a 512px source. Serve a thumbnail (`srcset`/`sizes` or a CDN transform); shipping full-res images for tiny circles is the #1 avatar-group weight mistake — megabytes for a few square inches.
*   **`loading="lazy"`** **and** **`decoding="async"`** on off-screen avatars (long lists of rows each with a group). Don't block the main thread decoding faces nobody's scrolled to.
*   **Cap what you load.** With `total` you show `max` faces and one number — you never fetch the other 19 avatars. Loading 24 images to display 5 is wasted bandwidth.
*   **Animate** **`transform`** **only.** The hover/focus lift and presence in/out use `transform`/`opacity` (compositor-only). Animating `margin` or `width` reflows the whole row every frame.
*   **Delegate the overflow handler.** In a table with 100 rows, don't bind 100 "+N" click listeners — one delegated listener on the table body reads which chip was hit. Fewer closures, works for virtualized rows.
*   **Virtualize the overflow list**, not the stack. The stack is always ≤ `max` nodes; if "+N" opens a list of 400 people, virtualize _that_ list.
* * *

## 15\. Security
An avatar group looks inert, but every piece of it is user-supplied data rendered into a shared surface, so treat it with the same suspicion as any other untrusted content. The image `src` on each avatar is attacker-influenced: an `<img>` can't execute JS, but it can be a tracking pixel or — if you proxy or fetch it server-side for thumbnailing — an SSRF vector, so allowlist hosts, run remote images through a resizing proxy you control, and set `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` so a broken or malicious `src` never leaks a viewer's referrer to an attacker's host (and fall back to initials, never to some hotlinked unknown). Names and initials are user content too: render them as text nodes only — never `innerHTML` — or a display name like `<img src=x onerror=…>` becomes stored XSS the moment it lands in the group label, a tooltip, or the overflow list. The subtlest risk is disclosure: the "+N" count and the tooltip/overflow member list can leak the identity — or the mere existence — of people a viewer isn't authorized to see, so compute both the visible faces and the hidden count from a server-side, authorization-filtered roster; never ship the full member list to the client and slice it with `max` in the browser. Presence data ("online now") is the same problem in miniature — only surface a status the viewer is allowed to know. None of this is exotic, but a display component is exactly where teams forget it, because it "just shows faces."

## 15\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before an avatar group is "done":

- [ ] Group announces as **one meaningful phrase**, not N separate avatar nodes.
- [ ] Names labelled **once** — no `alt` + `aria-label` double-announce.
- [ ] "+N" is a real `<button>` with `aria-haspopup` + `aria-expanded`, and it opens the rest.
- [ ] `+0` can never render; overflow math is `total − shown`, guarded.
- [ ] `total` supported so display count and true count can differ.
- [ ] Ordering is by relevance (owner/present/active first), not arbitrary.
- [ ] Ring separates overlapping faces against _this_ surface; not a fixed white.
- [ ] Empty, loading (skeleton), and single-member states all degrade cleanly.
- [ ] Interactive avatars/chip have ≥44px hit area and a visible `:focus-visible` ring that isn't clipped by a neighbor (focused avatar lifts).
- [ ] Tooltip name is supplementary — the accessible name doesn't depend on hover.
- [ ] Images right-sized (thumbnail, not full-res), lazy-loaded off-screen.
- [ ] `prefers-reduced-motion` honored on lift and presence animation.
- [ ] Popover focus is trapped on open and returned to "+N" on close.

**Bottom line:** an avatar group is a deceptively simple pile of circles with two hard parts hiding inside it — the _math_ (correct overflow, correct ordering, no "+0", a `total` that can outrun what's loaded) and the _a11y_ (one meaningful group label instead of a wall of anonymous "image" nodes).
