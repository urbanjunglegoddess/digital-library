# Message Bar

# The Message Bar: A Senior Engineer's Complete Breakdown
The full-width strip that tells the whole app what's going on. Here's everything from first principles to production code — including the one decision (announce now, or don't) that quietly makes or breaks it for screen-reader users.

The panel will let you tune severity, single vs. multiline, actions, dismissibility, and placement, and output live code for each target — HTML, CSS, Tailwind, React+TS, Next.js, shadcn/ui, Vue, Svelte, Angular, Bootstrap, Web Components, Python, SwiftUI, Jetpack Compose, and Flutter — with one-click copy.

**Audit a bar:** the companion **Message Bar a11y audit** (also a later pass) will let you paste a bar's markup and run live checks — is the live-region role right for the severity? does dismiss return focus somewhere sane? does it push content or float over it? — then export a client-ready report.

Sibling docs in the feedback family: the [Button](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200231) (the gold-standard reference), [Cards](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200471), the [Component Asset Template](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200531), and the [Design Styles gallery](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551). Cross-link its two closest cousins as you read: **Alert** (scoped, inline) and **Toast** (transient, floating) — most Message Bar bugs come from confusing it with those two.

## 1\. What a Message Bar Actually Is
A Message Bar is a **full-width, in-flow (or pinned) strip that carries a persistent contextual message about a region or the whole app**, usually with a severity, sometimes with actions, sometimes dismissible. "You're offline." "Trial ends in 3 days — upgrade." "Changes saved." "We're doing scheduled maintenance at 9pm." It spans its container edge to edge and it _stays_ until the condition changes or the user dismisses it.

That definition hides three cousins people constantly blur together. Getting the distinction right is the whole game:

*   **Message Bar (this component):** full-width, spans a region or the app, **persistent**, part of the layout flow (or pinned to the top of it). Communicates a _state_ — a condition that is true right now. Lives until the state changes.
*   **Alert (inline):** scoped to a _specific section_ — a form field's error summary, a settings panel's warning. It's about _that thing_, not the app. Narrower, sits next to what it describes. See the sibling **Alert** doc.
*   **Toast / Snackbar:** **transient**, floats above the UI in a corner, auto-dismisses after a few seconds. Communicates the _result of an action_ that just happened ("Copied to clipboard"). It does not span the layout and it does not persist. See the sibling **Toast** doc.

And a fourth term that muddies everything: **Banner**. In most systems "banner" is used loosely — sometimes it means a marketing/promo strip, sometimes it's a synonym for exactly this component. Fluent calls it `MessageBar`; GitHub Primer calls it `Banner`; Material calls its close relative a `Banner` too. **Treat "banner" as a naming variant of Message Bar, not a different thing** — but know that ARIA has a reserved `role="banner"` for the page header landmark, which is _not_ this. Never put `role="banner"` on a message strip; that word collision has burned many engineers.

The single most common mistake: **using a Toast for something persistent, or a Message Bar for something transient.** "You're offline" as a toast that vanishes in 4 seconds is a bug — the condition is still true after 4 seconds. "Copied!" as a full-width bar that sticks around forever is noise. Rule of thumb: **if the condition persists, it's a bar; if it's the fading echo of one action, it's a toast; if it's about one section, it's an alert.**
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters
The Message Bar is the app's **honesty channel**. It's how the software tells the truth about its own state — and users' trust is built or destroyed on exactly that.

*   **Trust lives here.** An app that silently fails — save didn't work, you're offline, your session expired — feels broken and untrustworthy even when everything else is polished. A Message Bar is often the _only_ signal that the system noticed a problem. Its absence reads as the app not caring.
*   **Revenue lives here too.** The upsell variant — "Trial ends in 3 days," "You're over your plan limit," "Reactivate to keep your data" — is one of the highest-leverage strips of pixels in a SaaS product. It's contextual, persistent, and impossible to ignore without being a modal. Getting its tone and timing right is measured in upgrade conversion and churn.
*   **It's an accessibility landmine.** Because a Message Bar frequently appears _after_ page load (you went offline, a save failed, a limit was hit), it has to be **announced** to screen-reader users who can't see it pop in. Choose the wrong live-region role and you either interrupt someone mid-sentence (`role="alert"` on a low-priority info bar) or you say _nothing at all_ (a critical error rendered into a `<div>` with no live region). §10 is where this component earns its keep.
*   **It's a layout-shift trap.** A bar that inserts itself at the top of the app and shoves everything down half a second after load is a Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) event — it hurts Core Web Vitals _and_ causes mis-clicks when the button someone was aiming for jumps. The decision between "push content down" and "overlay content" is a real engineering call, not a cosmetic one (§12, §14).

## 3\. Anatomy of a Message Bar
Every well-built bar is composed of predictable parts. Not all are always present, but the slots are stable:

*   **Container:** the full-width strip. Has a background tinted by severity, optional border (usually a thicker _leading_ border — the colored left edge is a genre convention), and internal padding. Spans its region edge to edge.
*   **Severity icon (optional but recommended):** a leading glyph that signals the kind of message at a glance — info (ⓘ), success (✓), warning (⚠), error (✕/⊘). Decorative when the text already carries the meaning (`aria-hidden`), meaningful when it's the only signal (needs a label). **Never rely on color alone** — the icon and text carry the severity for colorblind users.
*   **Message content:** the text. Often a short single line; sometimes a title + description; sometimes rich content with an inline link. Verb-and-outcome clarity, same as button copy.
*   **Action(s) (optional):** zero, one, or two buttons/links — "Retry," "Upgrade," "View details," "Undo." More than two and you've built a toolbar; collapse into an overflow menu or rethink.
*   **Dismiss / close (optional):** an icon button (usually an ✕ at the trailing edge) that removes the bar. Only present when the bar is _dismissible_ — critical error bars often are not.
*   **Expand / collapse affordance (optional):** for multiline bars that truncate, a "Show more" / chevron that reveals the full text.
*   **The live region wrapper:** invisible but load-bearing. The container (or an ancestor) carries `role="status"` / `role="alert"` / `aria-live` so assistive tech announces the message. This is anatomy even though it has no pixels.
*   **Placement context:** where it sits — top-of-app system bar, top-of-section, or sticky/pinned. Placement isn't decoration; it changes the semantics of _what scope_ the message applies to.

## 4\. Sizes, Density & Scale
A Message Bar scales less by "size" (like a button) and more by **density and line count**. Real values you can ship:

| Property | Compact | Default | Comfortable |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Min height (single line) | 32px | 40px | 48px |
| Vertical padding | 6px | 10px | 14px |
| Horizontal padding (inline) | 12px | 16px | 24px |
| Icon size | 16px | 20px | 24px |
| Gap icon→text | 8px | 10px | 12px |
| Font size | 13px | 14px | 15px |
| Leading border width | 3px | 4px | 6px |
| Action button height | 28px | 32px | 36px |

Rules that matter:

*   **The bar grows vertically, never horizontally.** It's always 100% of its container's inline size. When text wraps, height increases; width is fixed by definition. This is the opposite of a toast (which sizes to content) and it's _why_ a bar is a layout element, not a floating one.
*   **Keep the dismiss/actions vertically centered** for single-line, and **top-aligned** for multiline so a tall wrapped message doesn't float its ✕ in the middle of a paragraph.
*   **A system-level bar (top of app) is usually the compact/default density** — it's persistent chrome, so it should be quiet. A one-off section warning can be roomier.
*   Height should be driven by a spacing token and `min-height`, not a fixed `height`, so multiline content isn't clipped.
*   **Tap targets on the dismiss and actions stay ≥44px** even when the bar itself is 32px tall — expand the hit area with padding, don't shrink the button to fit the bar.

## 5\. States (the part juniors forget)
A Message Bar has fewer _interaction_ states than a button but more _lifecycle_ states, and the lifecycle is where the bugs live.

1. **Hidden / not-rendered** — the condition is false; the bar isn't in the DOM (preferred) or is `hidden`. Which one you choose affects layout shift and announcement — see §12.
2. **Entering (animating in)** — the moment it appears. Slide/fade in. Must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. The announcement fires here.
3. **Rest (visible)** — the baseline. Displaying its message, holding its space in the layout.
4. **Hover (on actions/dismiss)** — pointer feedback on the interactive bits only. The bar body itself is not hoverable (it's not a button).
5. **Focus (on actions/dismiss)** — keyboard reachable; visible `:focus-visible` ring on each interactive child.
6. **Action pending** — an action button is mid-request ("Retrying…"). Same loading discipline as any button: disable, keep width, block double-fire.
7. **Expanded / collapsed** — for truncating multiline bars, whether the full text is shown.
8. **Dismissing (animating out)** — the exit. Collapse height + fade so surrounding content settles smoothly rather than snapping up.
9. **Dismissed** — gone. If it should _stay_ gone (user closed the "cookie" bar), that state must be persisted (localStorage / server), or it'll nag on every reload.
10. **Severity states** — info / success / warning / error / upsell. These aren't interaction states but they change the entire visual + the live-region role (§6, §10).
11. **Stacked / queued** — when multiple bars are active at once, each is in a position in a priority-ordered stack (§13).

The two states juniors skip: **dismissing (they snap the layout instead of animating height)** and **persisted-dismissed (the bar comes back every reload because nothing remembered the close).** Both are the #1 tells of a rushed build.
* * *

## 6\. Severity Variants
Severity is the Message Bar's primary type axis. Five carry their weight; each maps to a color, an icon, a default tone, and — critically — a live-region politeness (§10).

*   **Info (neutral):** blue/tinted-neutral. "Scheduled maintenance tonight." "New feature available." Non-urgent context. `role="status"` (polite) — announced when the user finishes their current thought, never interrupting.
*   **Success (positive):** green. "Changes saved." "Payment received." Confirms a persistent good state. `role="status"` (polite).
*   **Warning (caution):** amber/goldenrod. "Your trial ends in 3 days." "This action can't be undone." Something needs attention soon but nothing is broken yet. `role="status"` usually; `role="alert"` only if genuinely time-sensitive.
*   **Error (negative/blocking):** red. "You're offline." "Save failed — retry." Something is wrong right now. `role="alert"` (assertive) — interrupts, because the user needs to know immediately.
*   **Upsell / promotional:** brand-colored (for UJG, Goldenrod on Eminence). "Upgrade to unlock." "Reactivate your plan." This is the commercial variant — it's persuasive, not a system state. **Politeness = polite (****`role="status"`****) at most**, and often no live region at all, because a promo interrupting a screen-reader user mid-task is hostile. Treat it as ordinary content that happens to look like a bar.

Two functional (non-severity) variants worth naming:

*   **System bar vs. contextual bar:** a _system_ bar is app-global chrome (offline, maintenance, impersonation-mode). A _contextual_ bar is scoped to the page or a section. Same component, different placement + scope semantics.
*   **Dismissible vs. persistent:** error/system bars are frequently _non_\-dismissible (you can't "close" being offline — it closes itself when you reconnect). Info/upsell bars are usually dismissible. **Never make a bar dismissible if closing it would hide a still-true critical condition.**

**The color-alone trap:** severity must be signaled by icon + text, not just background hue. ~8% of men have some color vision deficiency; a red bar and a green bar can look identical to them. The icon and the words carry the meaning; color reinforces it.
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Use a Message Bar when a **condition is true right now, applies to a region or the whole app, and should persist until it changes.** Reach for something else when:

*   **The message is about the result of one action and can fade** → **Toast.** "Copied," "Sent," "Item added to cart." Don't pin a bar for these.
*   **The message is scoped to one form field or one section** → **Alert** (inline). A password-strength warning belongs next to the password field, not across the top of the app.
*   **The message must block the user until they respond** → **Modal / Dialog.** A bar is _non-blocking_ by design; if the user genuinely cannot proceed, a bar is too easy to ignore.
*   **It's a passive, always-on label** (like a "Beta" tag) → that's a **Badge/Chip**, not a message.
*   **You have five things to announce at once** → you have a prioritization problem, not five bars. Stack at most 1–2; queue the rest (§13).

Placement heuristics:

*   **App-global state goes top-of-app**, above or just below the primary nav, spanning the full viewport width. Offline, maintenance, "you're impersonating a user."
*   **Page/section state goes top-of-section**, spanning that region's width, so its scope is visually obvious. A warning about _this_ settings panel sits inside the panel.
*   **Sticky/pinned** when the message must stay visible while the user scrolls a long page (a persistent "unsaved changes" bar, a checkout deadline). Pin sparingly — a sticky bar permanently eats vertical space.
*   **One bar owns one scope.** Don't stack an app-global bar and a page bar so they look like one strip; separate them visually or the user can't tell what each applies to.

## 8\. Message Bars Across Design Systems
Same component, different names and philosophies. Fluency here means you can drop into any codebase.

*   **Fluent (Microsoft):** the canonical `MessageBar`. Five `intent` values (info/success/warning/error, plus a neutral), a leading icon, an `actions` slot, and a `dismissible` prop. Fluent v9 splits it into `MessageBar`, `MessageBarBody`, `MessageBarActions` — composable parts. This is the closest thing to a "reference" Message Bar and the mental model most of this doc follows.
*   **Material Design (Google):** Material calls the persistent, full-width version a **Banner** — "a prominent message with related optional actions," explicitly for the top of a screen, one at a time, dismissed by user action. Material's transient cousin is the **Snackbar** (our Toast). Material is emphatic that a Banner is _persistent_ and a Snackbar is _transient_ — the exact distinction in §1.
*   **Apple HIG (iOS/macOS):** no single "message bar" primitive; Apple leans on **notification banners** (transient, top of screen) and, in-app, on contextual text. The persistent-state pattern is often a custom view. iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe's **Liquid Glass** makes system status surfaces translucent and refractive — see §17.
*   **Ant Design / enterprise:** `Alert` component with a `banner` prop that turns it into a full-width, top-of-page strip — Ant literally toggles between the inline Alert and the Message Bar with one boolean. Supports `type` (success/info/warning/error), `closable`, `action`, and `showIcon`. Great illustration that Alert and Message Bar are one component wearing two placements.
*   **GitHub Primer:** `Banner` — full-width, five variants (critical/warning/success/upsell/info), leading icon, primary + secondary actions, optional dismiss. Primer's `upsell` variant is the explicit commercial one, matching our §6.
*   **Atlassian / others:** `SectionMessage` — scoped to a section, exactly the "top-of-section" placement. Same DNA.
*   **Tailwind / utility-first:** no prescribed component; you compose it from utilities and (crucially) wire the `role`/`aria-live` yourself. Freedom plus the risk that the accessibility gets skipped — because utilities style the box but don't add the live region.
*   **shadcn/Radix:** shadcn ships an `Alert` (static, styled, `role="alert"` by default — watch that, §10) that you extend into a Message Bar. Radix has no dedicated bar primitive; you compose behavior yourself. Full ownership, full responsibility.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML (the foundation everyone should master first)

```plain
<!-- Info bar: polite announcement, persistent, dismissible -->
<div class="msgbar msgbar--info" role="status" aria-live="polite">
  <svg class="msgbar__icon" aria-hidden="true"><!-- info glyph --></svg>
  <p class="msgbar__body">Scheduled maintenance tonight from 9–10pm ET.</p>
  <button type="button" class="msgbar__dismiss" aria-label="Dismiss message">
    <svg aria-hidden="true"><!-- × --></svg>
  </button>
</div>

<!-- Error bar: assertive, interrupts, NOT dismissible, has a retry action -->
<div class="msgbar msgbar--error" role="alert">
  <svg class="msgbar__icon" aria-hidden="true"><!-- error glyph --></svg>
  <p class="msgbar__body">You're offline. Changes will sync when you reconnect.</p>
  <button type="button" class="msgbar__action">Retry now</button>
</div>

<!-- Upsell bar: it's content, not a system state — no live region -->
<div class="msgbar msgbar--upsell">
  <svg class="msgbar__icon" aria-hidden="true"></svg>
  <p class="msgbar__body">
    Your trial ends in 3 days. <a href="/upgrade">Upgrade to keep your projects</a>.
  </p>
  <button type="button" class="msgbar__dismiss" aria-label="Dismiss">×</button>
</div>
```

Key HTML rules:

*   **The live-region role is chosen by severity, not by habit.** `role="status"` (polite) for info/success/warning; `role="alert"` (assertive) for error. Upsell/promo gets _no_ live region — it's ordinary content, and interrupting a screen-reader user to sell them something is hostile. (Full decision table in §10.)
*   **The live region must exist in the DOM** **_before_** **the message is inserted** for the announcement to fire reliably. Rendering the whole bar (region + text together) in one shot can be missed by some AT. §10 covers the timing fix.
*   **The dismiss button is a real** **`<button>`** **with an** **`aria-label`** — it's icon-only. Never a `<div onClick>`.
*   **Don't put** **`role="alert"`** **on a bar that's present at initial page load.** `alert` is for things that appear _dynamically_. A bar that's in the initial HTML with `role="alert"` may or may not announce depending on the AT, and if it does it interrupts the page-load reading. Static-at-load bars should use `role="status"` or no role.

### 9.2 CSS (states done right)

```css
.msgbar {
  display: flex;
  align-items: flex-start;      /* top-align for multiline */
  gap: var(--msgbar-gap, 10px);
  width: 100%;                  /* full-width is the definition */
  min-height: 40px;            /* min, not fixed, so wrapping isn't clipped */
  padding: 10px 16px;
  border-inline-start: 4px solid transparent;  /* the colored leading edge */
  font: 400 14px/1.4 system-ui, sans-serif;
  box-sizing: border-box;
}
.msgbar__body { margin: 0; flex: 1 1 auto; }
.msgbar__icon { flex: 0 0 auto; width: 20px; height: 20px; margin-top: 1px; }

/* Severity skins — color reinforces, icon + text carry the meaning */
.msgbar--info    { background: #EEF4FF; color: #1E3A8A; border-inline-start-color: #2563EB; }
.msgbar--success { background: #ECFDF3; color: #05603A; border-inline-start-color: #12B76A; }
.msgbar--warning { background: #FFFAEB; color: #7A4F01; border-inline-start-color: #DCA424; }
.msgbar--error   { background: #FEF3F2; color: #912018; border-inline-start-color: #F04438; }
.msgbar--upsell  { background: #042D1D; color: #E8E6E1; border-inline-start-color: #DCA424; }

/* Interactive children get the focus ring — the bar body never does */
.msgbar__action,
.msgbar__dismiss { min-height: 44px; min-width: 44px; }  /* target size */
.msgbar__dismiss {
  flex: 0 0 auto; margin: -6px -6px -6px 0;  /* expand hit area without growing the bar */
  display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center;
  background: none; border: 0; cursor: pointer; border-radius: 6px;
}
.msgbar__action:focus-visible,
.msgbar__dismiss:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid var(--msgbar-focus, #DCA424);
  outline-offset: 2px;
}

/* Enter/exit animation — height + opacity, guarded for reduced motion */
.msgbar { transition: opacity .2s ease, transform .2s ease; }
.msgbar[data-entering] { opacity: 0; transform: translateY(-4px); }
.msgbar[data-leaving]  { opacity: 0; transform: translateY(-4px); }
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .msgbar { transition: none; }
}
```

The `border-inline-start` (not `border-left`) is deliberate: in RTL layouts the colored edge flips to the correct side automatically. The negative margin on the dismiss button is the trick that keeps a ≥44px tap target without forcing the bar taller than 40px.

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (a proper reusable component)
The heart of the doc. A Message Bar that picks its own live-region role from severity, supports actions + dismiss, animates out, and never lets the caller create an inaccessible one.

```typescript
import { forwardRef, useId, type ReactNode, type HTMLAttributes } from "react";

type Severity = "info" | "success" | "warning" | "error" | "upsell";

interface MessageBarAction {
  label: string;
  onClick: () => void;
  loading?: boolean;
}

interface MessageBarProps extends Omit<HTMLAttributes<HTMLDivElement>, "role"> {
  severity?: Severity;
  /** primary + optional secondary; more than two is a design smell */
  actions?: [MessageBarAction] | [MessageBarAction, MessageBarAction];
  onDismiss?: () => void;          // presence of this = dismissible
  icon?: ReactNode;
  children: ReactNode;
}

// Severity → live-region politeness. This is the whole accessibility contract.
const ROLE: Record<Severity, "status" | "alert" | undefined> = {
  info: "status",
  success: "status",
  warning: "status",
  error: "alert",     // assertive: interrupts, because it's wrong right now
  upsell: undefined,  // promo is content, never an announcement
};

export const MessageBar = forwardRef<HTMLDivElement, MessageBarProps>(
  ({ severity = "info", actions, onDismiss, icon, children, className = "", ...rest }, ref) => {
    const labelId = useId();
    const role = ROLE[severity];
    return (
      <div
        ref={ref}
        role={role}
        // status = polite, alert = assertive; omit aria-live when role handles it
        aria-live={role === "status" ? "polite" : role === "alert" ? "assertive" : undefined}
        className={`msgbar msgbar--${severity} ${className}`}
        {...rest}
      >
        {icon && <span className="msgbar__icon" aria-hidden="true">{icon}</span>}
        <p id={labelId} className="msgbar__body">{children}</p>

        {actions?.map((a) => (
          <button
            key={a.label}
            type="button"
            className="msgbar__action"
            disabled={a.loading}
            aria-busy={a.loading || undefined}
            onClick={a.onClick}
          >
            {a.label}
          </button>
        ))}

        {onDismiss && (
          <button
            type="button"
            className="msgbar__dismiss"
            aria-label="Dismiss message"
            onClick={onDismiss}
          >
            <span aria-hidden="true">×</span>
          </button>
        )}
      </div>
    );
  }
);
MessageBar.displayName = "MessageBar";
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:

*   **Severity drives the ARIA role** through the `ROLE` map — the caller can't accidentally ship an info bar that interrupts, or an error bar that stays silent. The accessibility decision is encoded once, in the component, not left to whoever uses it.
*   **`upsell`** **gets no role at all** — promos announcing themselves to screen-reader users is a real, hostile bug this design prevents by construction.
*   **`onDismiss`** **presence** **_is_** **the dismissible flag** — no separate `dismissible` boolean to get out of sync with the handler.
*   **`actions`** **is typed as a 1- or 2-tuple**, so the type system refuses a three-action bar at compile time — the design rule becomes a type error.
*   **Extends native** **`HTMLAttributes`** (minus `role`, which we own) so `id`, `data-*`, `className` pass through for free.

The real-world usage — a bar that survives dismissal across reloads:

```typescript
function TrialBar() {
  const [dismissed, setDismissed] = useState(
    () => localStorage.getItem("trialBarDismissed") === "1"
  );
  if (dismissed) return null;   // unmount = it leaves the layout entirely

  return (
    <MessageBar
      severity="warning"
      onDismiss={() => {
        localStorage.setItem("trialBarDismissed", "1");   // remember the close
        setDismissed(true);
      }}
      actions={[{ label: "Upgrade", onClick: () => router.push("/upgrade") }]}
    >
      Your trial ends in 3 days.
    </MessageBar>
  );
}
```

The persisted-dismiss is the detail that separates a real bar from a demo: close it once, it stays closed. For a promo you'd key the storage flag to the campaign so a _new_ offer can show again.

### 9.4 Animating in and out (the height-collapse exit)
The exit animation is where most bars snap ugly. You can't transition `height: auto`, so the production pattern is a wrapper that animates `grid-template-rows` from `1fr` to `0fr` — the modern, JS-free way to collapse height smoothly:

```css
.msgbar-slot {
  display: grid;
  grid-template-rows: 1fr;
  transition: grid-template-rows .2s ease;
}
.msgbar-slot[data-leaving] { grid-template-rows: 0fr; }
.msgbar-slot > * { overflow: hidden; min-height: 0; }
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .msgbar-slot { transition: none; }
}
```

Set `data-leaving` on the slot, wait for `transitionend`, then unmount. Surrounding content glides up instead of jumping — which also avoids a layout-shift jolt (§14). With a framework, a presence library (Framer Motion's `AnimatePresence`, Svelte transitions, Vue `<Transition>`) does this bookkeeping for you, but always guard reduced-motion.

### 9.5 Vanilla JavaScript (no framework)
Announcing correctly without React comes down to **inserting text into a live region that already exists**, not creating the region and text together:

```javascript
// The live region is in the DOM at page load, empty. Announcements go INTO it.
// <div id="app-status" role="status" aria-live="polite" class="sr-region"></div>

function showBar({ severity = "info", message, dismissible = true }) {
  const isError = severity === "error";
  const isUpsell = severity === "upsell";
  const bar = document.createElement("div");
  bar.className = `msgbar msgbar--${severity}`;
  // Error interrupts; everything else is polite. Upsell gets NEITHER — a promo
  // is content, not a status, and announcing it to a screen reader is hostile.
  if (!isUpsell) {
    bar.setAttribute("role", isError ? "alert" : "status");
    bar.setAttribute("aria-live", isError ? "assertive" : "polite");
  }

  const text = document.createElement("p");
  text.className = "msgbar__body";
  text.textContent = message;
  bar.append(text);

  if (dismissible) {
    const x = document.createElement("button");
    x.type = "button";
    x.className = "msgbar__dismiss";
    x.setAttribute("aria-label", "Dismiss message");
    x.textContent = "×";
    x.addEventListener("click", () => removeBar(bar));
    bar.append(x);
  }

  document.querySelector("#bar-region").prepend(bar);
  return bar;
}

function removeBar(bar) {
  bar.dataset.leaving = "true";
  bar.addEventListener("transitionend", () => bar.remove(), { once: true });
  // Fallback if reduced-motion killed the transition (no transitionend fires):
  setTimeout(() => bar.isConnected && bar.remove(), 250);
}
```

The `transitionend` + `setTimeout` fallback is the detail people miss: if `prefers-reduced-motion` disabled the transition, `transitionend` never fires and the bar would never actually unmount. Belt and suspenders.

### 9.7 shadcn/ui (extend the Alert into a bar)
shadcn ships `Alert`, not `MessageBar` — but its Alert is the seed. **The gotcha:** shadcn's Alert doesn't set a role by default in all versions, and where it does it may hardcode `role="alert"`. For a Message Bar you drive the role off the variant, exactly like §9.3:

```typescript
import * as React from "react";
import { cva, type VariantProps } from "class-variance-authority";
import { cn } from "@/lib/utils";

const barVariants = cva(
  "flex items-start gap-2.5 w-full min-h-10 px-4 py-2.5 border-s-4 text-sm leading-relaxed",
  {
    variants: {
      severity: {
        info:    "bg-blue-50 text-blue-900 border-blue-600",
        success: "bg-green-50 text-green-900 border-green-600",
        warning: "bg-amber-50 text-amber-900 border-amber-500",
        error:   "bg-red-50 text-red-900 border-red-500",
        upsell:  "bg-[#042D1D] text-[#E8E6E1] border-[#DCA424]",
      },
    },
    defaultVariants: { severity: "info" },
  }
);

const ROLE = { info: "status", success: "status", warning: "status",
               error: "alert", upsell: undefined } as const;

export interface MessageBarProps
  extends React.HTMLAttributes<HTMLDivElement>,
    VariantProps<typeof barVariants> {}

export const MessageBar = React.forwardRef<HTMLDivElement, MessageBarProps>(
  ({ className, severity = "info", ...props }, ref) => {
    const role = ROLE[severity ?? "info"];
    return (
      <div
        ref={ref}
        role={role}
        aria-live={role === "status" ? "polite" : role === "alert" ? "assertive" : undefined}
        className={cn(barVariants({ severity }), className)}
        {...props}
      />
    );
  }
);
MessageBar.displayName = "MessageBar";
```

Usage: `<MessageBar severity="error">Save failed.</MessageBar>`. The lesson to carry into any codebase: **if you're extending an existing Alert component into a bar, audit its default role — a hardcoded** **`role="alert"`** **on your info and upsell bars is an interruption bug.**

### 9.8 Next.js (App Router: server-detected state, client dismiss)
The interesting split: whether a bar should show is often a **server** fact (are we in maintenance? is this user over their limit?), but dismissing it is a **client** interaction. Render the decision on the server, hydrate the dismissal on the client:

```typescript
// app/components/SystemBar.tsx  — Server Component, no "use client"
import { getSystemStatus } from "@/lib/status";
import { DismissibleBar } from "./DismissibleBar";

export async function SystemBar() {
  const status = await getSystemStatus();          // runs on the server
  if (!status.maintenance) return null;             // nothing renders, no bar in flow
  return (
    <DismissibleBar severity="warning" storageKey={`maint-${status.windowId}`}>
      Scheduled maintenance {status.window}. Some features may be unavailable.
    </DismissibleBar>
  );
}
```

```typescript
// app/components/DismissibleBar.tsx
"use client";                                        // needs state + localStorage
import { useState } from "react";
import { MessageBar } from "@/components/MessageBar";

export function DismissibleBar({ storageKey, children, ...rest }: {
  storageKey: string; severity: "info" | "warning" | "error"; children: React.ReactNode;
}) {
  const [open, setOpen] = useState(() =>
    typeof window === "undefined" ? true : localStorage.getItem(storageKey) !== "1"
  );
  if (!open) return null;
  return (
    <MessageBar {...rest} onDismiss={() => { localStorage.setItem(storageKey, "1"); setOpen(false); }}>
      {children}
    </MessageBar>
  );
}
```

Keying the storage flag to `status.windowId` means a _new_ maintenance window shows a fresh bar even if the user dismissed the last one. Rendering the server component to `null` (rather than a hidden div) keeps the bar out of the layout entirely until it's genuinely needed — no phantom space, no hydration flash.

### 9.9 Node.js (the endpoint behind an action bar)
When a bar's action reaches the server ("Retry sync," "Dismiss for everyone," "Extend trial"), the endpoint is defended like any other state-changing call. A "Retry" that hits a mutating endpoint needs the same idempotency discipline as a Pay button:

```typescript
import express from "express";
import { z } from "zod";

const router = express.Router();
const Retry = z.object({ jobId: z.string().uuid() });
const seen = new Map<string, unknown>();   // real apps: Redis

router.post("/api/sync/retry", requireAuth, rateLimit, async (req, res) => {
  const parsed = Retry.safeParse(req.body);
  if (!parsed.success) return res.status(422).json(parsed.error.issues);
  if (!req.user.can("retry", parsed.data.jobId)) return res.sendStatus(403);

  // Idempotency is checked AFTER authz, and the cache key is scoped to the
  // actor — otherwise a replayed Idempotency-Key would hand one user's cached
  // result to another before the per-resource authorization check ever ran.
  const idem = req.header("Idempotency-Key");
  const key = idem ? `${req.user.id}:${idem}` : undefined;
  if (key && seen.has(key)) return res.status(200).json(seen.get(key));

  const result = await sync.retry(parsed.data.jobId, req.user.id);
  if (key) seen.set(key, result);
  return res.status(200).json(result);
});

export default router;
```

The bar's UI showing a "Retry" button is UX; the server independently re-checks auth, validates input, and dedupes double-clicks. A user rage-clicking "Retry" on an error bar is exactly the double-fire idempotency protects against.

### 9.10 Python (backend decision + rendered bar)
**Backend** (FastAPI) computing whether the bar should show — the show/hide decision belongs on the server for anything security- or billing-relevant:

### 9.15 Web Components (framework-agnostic)
A bar that works in any framework or none, with severity-driven role baked in:

```typescript
class UiMessageBar extends HTMLElement {
  static observedAttributes = ["severity", "dismissible"];
  connectedCallback() { this.render(); }
  attributeChangedCallback() { this.render(); }
  render() {
    const sev = this.getAttribute("severity") ?? "info";
    const role = sev === "error" ? "alert" : sev === "upsell" ? "" : "status";
    const live = sev === "error" ? "assertive" : sev === "upsell" ? "" : "polite";
    this.setAttribute("class", `msgbar msgbar--${sev}`);
    if (role) { this.setAttribute("role", role); this.setAttribute("aria-live", live); }
    else { this.removeAttribute("role"); this.removeAttribute("aria-live"); }
    // Light-DOM slot so page CSS + <slot> text both work; no shadow root here
    // so the live region is exposed directly to assistive tech.
  }
}
customElements.define("ui-message-bar", UiMessageBar);
// <ui-message-bar severity="error">You're offline</ui-message-bar>
```

Note the deliberate choice _not_ to use a shadow root: live regions inside shadow DOM have historically had spotty AT support. Keeping the bar in the light DOM guarantees the announcement lands.

### 9.16 Stacking, Priority & the Bar Manager
The moment two conditions are true at once (you're offline _and_ your trial is ending), you need a manager, not two independent bars fighting for the top of the screen. The pattern: a small store that holds active bars, orders them by priority, caps how many render, and queues the rest.

```typescript
type Severity = "info" | "success" | "warning" | "error" | "upsell";
const PRIORITY: Record<Severity, number> = { error: 0, warning: 1, upsell: 2, info: 3, success: 4 };

interface Bar { id: string; severity: Severity; message: string; }

class BarManager {
  private bars: Bar[] = [];
  private max = 2;                    // never show more than 2 at once
  private listeners = new Set<() => void>();

  add(bar: Bar) {
    if (this.bars.some((b) => b.id === bar.id)) return;   // dedupe by id
    this.bars.push(bar);
    this.bars.sort((a, b) => PRIORITY[a.severity] - PRIORITY[b.severity]);
    this.emit();
  }
  remove(id: string) { this.bars = this.bars.filter((b) => b.id !== id); this.emit(); }
  visible() { return this.bars.slice(0, this.max); }      // the rest are queued
  queuedCount() { return Math.max(0, this.bars.length - this.max); }

  subscribe(fn: () => void) { this.listeners.add(fn); return () => this.listeners.delete(fn); }
  private emit() { this.listeners.forEach((fn) => fn()); }
}
```

Priority rules that hold up in production:

*   **Errors always win the top slot.** An offline error outranks a trial-ending warning outranks an upsell. Sort by severity, not by arrival time.
*   **Cap at 1–2 visible.** A wall of five bars is a modal in disguise and pushes all the actual content below the fold. Show the top 1–2, and if you must, render a "+2 more notices" affordance.
*   **Dedupe by a stable id**, not by message text — so a re-fired "offline" event doesn't stack three identical bars.
*   **Only one live-region announcement at a time.** If two bars appear in the same tick, don't fire two assertive announcements — they'll clobber each other. Announce the highest-priority one; let the rest be read on navigation.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
This is the section that separates a real Message Bar from a colored div. The bar's entire reason to exist — telling the user something changed — fails silently for screen-reader users if the live region is wrong.

**The** **`role="status"`** **vs** **`role="alert"`** **decision — the single most important call:**

| Situation | Role | aria-live | Behavior |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Info / success / warning, appears dynamically | `status` | `polite` | Announced at the next pause — never interrupts |
| Error / critical, appears dynamically | `alert` | `assertive` | Interrupts immediately — the user needs to know now |
| Upsell / promotional | none | none | It's content, not a status — never announce a promo |
| Present in the initial HTML at page load | none (or `status`) | — | Don't use `alert` for load-time content; it's for changes |

*   **`role="alert"`** **implies** **`aria-live="assertive"`** **and** **`aria-atomic="true"`** — you rarely need to set them all; the role carries it. Same for `role="status"` → polite.
*   **The live region must be present in the DOM** **_before_** **the message text is injected.** If you create the region and its text in the same render, some screen readers miss the change. The robust pattern (§9.5): render an empty live-region container at load, then inject the message into it. In React, mounting the bar into an already-present region, or updating text within a stable region, achieves the same.
*   **Timing:** if you swap a live region's text too fast (clear then re-set in the same frame), the announcement can be dropped. Set it once; if you must replace, clear it, then set the new text on the next tick.
*   **Don't over-announce.** A bar that re-renders on every keystroke and re-fires its announcement is torture on a screen reader. Announce on appearance and on meaningful change only.
*   **Keyboard:** the bar body is not focusable (it's not interactive). Its **actions and dismiss button are** — reachable by Tab, activated by Enter/Space, with a visible `:focus-visible` ring. A bar that appears should _not_ steal focus (that's a modal's job); the announcement is enough.
*   **Focus on dismiss:** when the user closes a bar, focus must not vanish into the void (which dumps keyboard users back at the top of the page). Move focus to a sensible neighbor — the next bar, the element after the bar, or back to whatever triggered it.
*   **Icon labeling:** if the severity icon is the _only_ severity signal, it needs a label ("Error:"); if the text already says "Error," the icon is decorative → `aria-hidden="true"`. Prefer the latter — say the severity in words.
*   **Contrast:** text vs. the tinted background ≥ 4.5:1 (WCAG AA). Tinted pastel backgrounds are exactly where contrast quietly fails — verify every severity, both themes. Don't rely on color alone to convey severity (§6).
*   **Target size:** dismiss and action controls ≥ 44px hit area even on a 32px-tall compact bar.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The bar looks solved but keeps evolving (2026-aware):

*   **AI-native status bars:** streaming, self-updating bars — "Summarizing your inbox… 40%," an agent's progress reported in a persistent strip instead of a spinner. The live-region politeness matters even more here: stream to a `status` region, throttled, or you'll spam the screen reader.
*   **Anchored / contextual system bars from the OS:** iOS 26 Live Activities and the Dynamic Island are effectively OS-level message bars; the web equivalent is the emerging **Notifications +** **`showBadge`** and app-badging APIs feeding a single in-app bar.
*   **Connection-aware bars via the Network Information API +** **`navigator.onLine`****\*\*\*\*:** the offline bar that's genuinely reactive — degrade gracefully on slow connections ("You're on a slow network, some features are limited"), not just binary online/offline.
*   **Popover-API / top-layer bars:** the native `popover` and CSS `position: fixed` + top-layer let a pinned bar sit above content without a z-index war — useful for a sticky "unsaved changes" bar.
*   **View Transitions API:** native cross-fade/slide as a bar enters or leaves, no JS animation library, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected by the browser.
*   **Server-driven bar config:** the message, severity, and even the CTA delivered from a config service (LaunchDarkly / a CMS) so product can ship a maintenance or upsell bar without a deploy. The component reads a payload; the copy lives in the config.
*   **Undo bars (the Gmail pattern):** a persistent-ish bar with an "Undo" action and a countdown — the bridge between a toast and a bar. Persistent enough to act on, time-boxed enough not to nag.

## 12\. Conversion & UX Killers (the silent bleeders)
None of these throw an error. They just quietly erode trust, conversion, and accessibility.

*   **Toast where a bar belongs (and vice versa).** "You're offline" as a 4-second toast vanishes while still true; "Copied!" as a permanent bar is clutter. Match persistence to the condition's lifetime.
*   **The layout-shift jolt.** A bar that inserts at the top 500ms after load shoves the whole page down — hurting CLS _and_ causing mis-clicks as the button someone aimed for jumps. Either reserve the space, or animate the height open so nothing snaps (§9.4, §14).
*   **Silent errors.** An error rendered into a plain `<div>` with no live region is invisible to screen-reader users and easy to miss for everyone. The most expensive bar bug: the app failed and only sighted, attentive users found out.
*   **The interrupting info bar.** `role="alert"` on a low-priority "New feature!" bar interrupts screen-reader users mid-sentence. Assertive is a scarce resource — spend it only on errors.
*   **The nagging bar.** A dismissible bar that reappears on every reload because the close wasn't persisted. Users learn to ignore it — including the day it says something important.
*   **The un-closable promo.** An upsell bar with no dismiss, permanently eating space at the top of the app, reads as hostile and trains users to tune out that whole region (banner blindness). Make promos dismissible.
*   **Competing bars.** Three stacked bars = a modal nobody agreed to, pushing real content below the fold. Cap and prioritize (§9.16).
*   **Vague action labels.** "OK" / "Click here" on a bar action wastes the one CTA. "Retry sync," "Upgrade plan," "Reconnect" — name the outcome.
*   **Color-only severity.** Red vs. green with no icon or word is invisible to colorblind users. Icon + text always.

**The through-line:** every one fails _quietly_. No crash, just a lower conversion number, a frustrated screen-reader user, or a trust tax you can't see in the logs. Audit your bars before you audit your funnel.
* * *

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
The senior-move versions of the patterns above.

**Priority-ordered stack with a queue (recap + rule).** A `BarManager` (§9.16) that sorts by severity, caps visible count, dedupes by id, and — critically — coordinates announcements so two bars appearing at once don't fire two assertive live-region updates that clobber each other. Announce the top-priority bar; let the rest read on navigation.

**Design tokens, three tiers.** Same discipline as the button: primitive → semantic → component, so a rebrand touches one line.

```css
:root {
  /* primitive */
  --red-50: #FEF3F2;  --red-700: #912018;  --gold-500: oklch(78% 0.135 82);
  /* semantic */
  --color-danger-surface: var(--red-50);
  --color-danger-text:    var(--red-700);
  --color-focus:          var(--gold-500);
  /* component */
  --msgbar-error-bg:   var(--color-danger-surface);
  --msgbar-error-text: var(--color-danger-text);
  --msgbar-focus:      var(--color-focus);
}
.msgbar--error { background: var(--msgbar-error-bg); color: var(--msgbar-error-text); }
```

Dark mode is a token swap, not new bar CSS: lift the surface lightness and ease the tint's chroma so the pastel doesn't glow. Use OKLCH so each severity's surface/text pair stays perceptually even across themes.

**TypeScript-enforced accessibility.** Make the compiler refuse a bar that would be inaccessible. A discriminated union that _requires_ a live-region decision, and forbids a dismissible critical error where closing would hide a still-true state:

```typescript
type BarBase = { message: string; icon?: React.ReactNode };

type InfoBar    = BarBase & { severity: "info" | "success" | "warning"; dismissible?: boolean };
type ErrorBar   = BarBase & { severity: "error"; dismissible?: false };   // can't dismiss a live error
type UpsellBar  = BarBase & { severity: "upsell"; dismissible: true };     // promos MUST be dismissible

type BarConfig = InfoBar | ErrorBar | UpsellBar;
```

Now `{ severity: "upsell" }` without `dismissible: true` won't compile, and `{ severity: "error", dismissible: true }` is a type error — the design rules become compile-time guarantees instead of code-review comments.

**Persisted dismissal, keyed to identity.** Store the dismissal against the _thing_ (`maint-${windowId}`, `promo-${campaignId}`), never a generic `barDismissed` flag — so a new maintenance window or a new campaign shows a fresh bar while the old one stays closed.

**`aria-live`** **region that outlives the bar.** Keep a single, always-present visually-hidden live region at the app root; route _all_ bar announcements through it rather than relying on each bar's own role. This sidesteps the "region created with its content" timing bug entirely and gives you one throttle point for the whole app.

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A bar feels free. At scale, three costs show up.

*   **Layout shift (the real cost).** The bar's signature performance sin is CLS. A bar inserted at the top after hydration reflows everything below it. Fixes: (1) render the decision on the server so the bar is in the initial HTML when it applies (§9.8); (2) if it must appear client-side, animate `grid-template-rows` from `0fr` (§9.4) so content glides rather than snaps — visually smoother _and_ it doesn't count as an unexpected shift if it's user-initiated; (3) never reserve a fixed empty gap "just in case" — that's permanent wasted space for the 99% of loads with no bar.
*   **Animate only compositor-friendly properties.** Transition `opacity` and `transform`, not `height`/`margin`/`top`. The `grid-template-rows` collapse is the one deliberate exception, and it's cheap because the bar is small and it's a one-shot on dismiss, not a per-frame loop.
*   **Icon-import trap.** Same as any component: `import { WifiOff } from "lucide-react"`, never `import * as Icons`. One glyph shouldn't drag in the whole library.
*   **Don't re-announce on every render.** A bar wired to a frequently-updating store can re-fire its live region on each render — a performance _and_ accessibility problem. Memoize; announce on appearance and meaningful change only.
*   **Bar manager memory.** A global manager with a `Set` of listeners is fine; just make sure components unsubscribe on unmount so you don't leak closures as pages come and go.
* * *

## 15\. Security (when the bar reaches the server)
Most bars are display-only, but the moment one carries an action or its content comes from data, the usual rules apply.

*   **Server-side content is never trusted markup.** A bar whose message comes from a config service or user data must be rendered as **text**, not `innerHTML` — an "upgrade" message pulled from a CMS is an XSS vector if injected as HTML. Escape it; if rich content is required, sanitize against an allowlist.
*   **Action endpoints are defended independently.** A "Retry," "Extend trial," or "Dismiss for all users" action re-checks auth, authorization, and validation server-side and is idempotent (§9.9). The bar showing the button is UX, not permission.
*   **Don't leak internals in error bars.** "Save failed: `ECONNREFUSED postgres://…`" is an information disclosure. Show the user a human message ("Couldn't save — retry"); log the stack trace server-side.
*   **The show/hide decision for billing/entitlement bars belongs on the server.** "You're over your plan limit" gating a feature must be enforced by the API, not by whether the client rendered a bar. Hiding or faking the bar client-side must never grant access.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a Message Bar is "done":

- [ ] Right component chosen: persistent state → bar; transient result → toast; section-scoped → alert; blocking → modal.
- [ ] Live-region role driven by severity: `status`/polite for info-success-warning, `alert`/assertive for error, none for upsell.
- [ ] `role="alert"` used only for _dynamically appearing_ critical messages, never for load-time content.
- [ ] Live region exists in the DOM before text is injected; announcement fires on appearance, not on every render.
- [ ] Severity signaled by icon + text, not color alone; contrast ≥ 4.5:1 in both themes.
- [ ] Dismiss is a labeled `<button>`; on close, focus moves somewhere sane (not into the void).
- [ ] Dismissal is persisted, keyed to the message's identity, so it doesn't nag on reload.
- [ ] Critical/system bars are non-dismissible if closing would hide a still-true condition.
- [ ] Full-width by construction; grows vertically on wrap; ≥44px tap targets on actions/dismiss.
- [ ] No layout-shift jolt: server-render when possible, or animate height open; never reserve empty space.
- [ ] Multiple bars managed by priority (errors first), capped at 1–2 visible, deduped by id, one announcement at a time.
- [ ] Animations respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; RTL uses logical properties (`border-inline-start`).
- [ ] Action endpoints re-check auth + validate + idempotent; server-sourced content escaped.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same bar, eleven aesthetics)
Design systems are _whose_ rules; visual styles are _which look_. The same semantic Message Bar — same role, same keyboard behavior, same 44px targets — can wear any of these skins. **Style is skin; behavior is the skeleton.** See them all in the [Design Styles gallery](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551).

*   **Flat:** solid severity-tinted fill, crisp edges, a colored leading border, no shadow. Reads instantly; the default for dense apps.
*   **Material:** the `Banner` look — subtle elevation where the bar meets content, uppercase text-button actions, a leading severity icon in a filled circle.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent bar with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over the content behind it. Looks premium pinned over a hero; **guard contrast** — text over a blurred photo fails AA fast. Rare for status bars where legibility is the whole point.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe material — a translucent strip that _refracts_ the content scrolling behind it, with a specular rim and a sheen that shifts with motion. Gorgeous for a pinned system bar over a rich background. Web = honest approximation with layered `backdrop-filter` + highlights; SwiftUI emits native `.glassEffect()`. **Guard both contrast and** **`prefers-reduced-motion`** — the moving sheen must hold still for users who asked for less motion, and the text must stay ≥4.5:1 as the backdrop changes.
*   **Neumorphism:** the bar as a soft extruded strip, dual light/dark shadows on a same-color surface. Pretty, but contrast-poor — a bad fit for _severity_ signaling, where the whole point is that error looks different from info. Accent use only.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** a "physical" notification strip — inner highlight, subtle gradient, a beveled edge like an old toolbar alert. Warm and tactile; heavy for a status bar.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** thick black border, hard offset shadow, zero radius, unapologetic severity color. High personality — great for a campaign or a dev-tool warning bar, loud for a calm SaaS.
*   **Claymorphism:** a puffy, big-radius bar with soft inner light and a gentle glow. Friendly — suits an onboarding or success bar; the rounded form fights the "edge-to-edge" instinct, so use inset with margin rather than true full-bleed.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue gradient sweep, usually for the upsell/promo variant on a dark surface. Premium and attention-grabbing — **honor** **`prefers-reduced-motion`** and never use it for error (a moving gradient undercuts urgency).
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-zero decoration — a hairline top/bottom rule, a single accent dot or thin leading border for severity, typography doing the work. The most legible, least noisy option; excellent for data-dense apps.
*   **UJG Brand:** the Afro-Futurist house style — for the upsell bar, Goldenrod (`#DCA424`) text and CTA on a deep Eminence (`#5F2C82`) / Night (`#0A0A0A`) or Dark Green (`#042D1D`) field, a confident 4px Goldenrod leading edge, and a warm glow. For _system_ severities, keep the standard info/success/warning/error tints so meaning stays legible — reserve the branded Eminence/Goldenrod treatment for the commercial and neutral-info bars, where brand expression doesn't compete with a safety signal.

**The rule that never changes:** across all eleven skins, the bar stays full-width, its live-region role still tracks its severity, its dismiss and actions still hit 44px and show a focus ring, and its contrast still clears AA. Guard glass / liquid-glass / neumorphism for contrast; guard aurora / liquid-glass for reduced motion. The look is negotiable; the skeleton is not.
* * *

**Bottom line:** a Message Bar is a deceptively simple strip whose entire value is telling the truth about the app's state — and its hardest 20% is invisible. Get the `status`\-vs-`alert` decision right, persist the dismissal, cap and prioritize the stack, and don't shove the layout when it appears, and you've built the component that makes an app feel trustworthy instead of broken. Get the live region wrong and it's a colored div that lies to half your users. That gap — between a bar that announces correctly and one that only _looks_ right — is the whole difference between a hobby build and production software.
