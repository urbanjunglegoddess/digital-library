## 1\. What a Header Actually Is
A header is the **top region of a page or app** — the banner that runs across the top and holds the things a user needs everywhere: who they are (brand/logo), where they can go (primary navigation), what they can find (search), and what they can do (account, cart, primary CTA). In HTML it maps to the `<header>` element, and when it's the top-level header for the whole page it exposes the ARIA `banner` landmark.

Three words get lumped together and they are _not_ the same thing:

**Header (the region):** the top container. It's a _landmark_ and a _layout slot_. It usually contains navigation, but it is not itself navigation. Think of it as the shelf, not the books.
**Navigation (****`<nav>`****):** the set of links to elsewhere. A header commonly _contains_ a `<nav>`, but nav can also live in a sidebar, a footer, or a drawer with no header at all. See the sibling Navigation ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216971](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216971)) doc.
**Toolbar (****`role="toolbar"`****):** a grouped set of _controls_ (buttons, toggles) that act on the current view — formatting bars, editor toolbars. A toolbar acts; a header locates and identifies. A header may embed a toolbar, but calling your whole header a toolbar is a semantics bug.

The single most common structural mistake: treating the header as _just a nav bar_. A real header is a **composition slot** — logo + nav + search + actions — and each of those has its own semantics. The second most common mistake: shipping two `banner` landmarks on one page (a `<header>` inside `<main>` is fine and is _not_ a banner; a second top-level `<header>` is a bug). Rule of thumb: **one page, one top-level** **`<header>`****, one** **`banner`****. Everything visually "at the top" is arranged** **_inside_** **it, not stacked as siblings.**

> Naming note: "navbar," "top bar," "app bar," "masthead," and "top app bar" (Material) all refer to this same region. This doc uses **Header** for the landmark region and reserves **Navigation** for the `<nav>` it contains.

## 2\. Why It Matters
The header is the most-seen component you own. It renders on essentially every page, above the fold, first in the DOM. That gives it disproportionate weight:

**Orientation and trust.** The header answers "where am I, whose site is this, and how do I get out of here?" in under a second. A missing or shifting header reads as _broken_ or _untrustworthy_ before a user has read a single word of content. The logo in the top-left that returns you home is one of the most deeply learned conventions on the web — break it and you tax every visitor.
**Conversion routing.** The header's primary CTA ("Start free trial," "Book a call," "Get pricing") and its search box are where high-intent users self-select. On e-commerce, the header search bar drives a wildly disproportionate share of revenue — searchers convert at multiples of browsers. The header is prime real estate; what you put there is a business decision, not a design one.
**Accessibility frontier.** The `banner` landmark, the skip-link that targets it, and the keyboard path through nav/search/account are the first things a screen-reader or keyboard user hits. Sticky headers in particular create a specific, testable WCAG failure (2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured) that most teams ship blind. Get the header right and you've handled the entry point for every assistive-tech user.
**Performance and layout stability.** The header is above the fold, so it's in the critical rendering path and it's a prime **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)** offender: a header that loads at one height then jumps when the logo image arrives, or a sticky bar that's added after hydration, shoves the whole page. First impressions of _speed_ are largely header impressions.

## 3\. Anatomy of a Header
A production header is a horizontal composition of predictable slots. Not every header has all of them, but every header is assembled from this vocabulary:

**Container / bar:** the `<header>` itself. Full-bleed background, an inner max-width wrapper that aligns to the page grid, fixed or `min-height` (not fixed height — let it grow), horizontal padding that respects safe areas on mobile.
**Brand / logo:** top-left in LTR. Wrapped in a link to `/` (home). Needs a real accessible name — an `<img alt="Urban Jungle Goddess home">` or an SVG with a `<title>`, not a background image. This is the anchor of the whole component.
**Primary navigation slot:** the `<nav aria-label="Primary">` with the main links. On desktop it's a horizontal list; on mobile it collapses into the drawer behind the hamburger. May contain **mega-menu** triggers (see §9 and §13).
**Search:** either an always-visible input or an icon that expands into one. Search is a `<form role="search">` with a labeled input, not a naked box.
**Utility / actions cluster:** top-right. Secondary links (Docs, Support), theme toggle, language switcher, cart, notifications.
**Primary CTA:** the one high-emphasis button — "Sign up," "Book now." One per header, same discipline as the Button doc: one primary per view.
**Account / user menu:** avatar or "Sign in" that opens a menu (`aria-haspopup="menu"`, `aria-expanded`). Post-auth this becomes the identity anchor.
**Hamburger / menu toggle:** the mobile-only button (`aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`) that opens the Drawer Navigation ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217031](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-217031)). Icon-only, so it _must_ carry an `aria-label`.
**Skip link:** the visually-hidden-until-focused "Skip to content" link that is usually the very first focusable thing on the page, sitting just before or inside the header. Non-negotiable for keyboard users because the header is the wall they'd otherwise Tab through on every page.
**Progress / context row (optional):** a sub-bar for breadcrumbs, a reading-progress indicator, or an announcement banner stacked above or below the main bar.

## 4\. Sizes, Density & Scale
Header height sets the vertical rhythm of the whole page and eats above-the-fold space, so it's a real tradeoff: taller = more brand presence and easier tap targets; shorter = more content visible. Real-world values:

| Density | Height | Logo mark | H-padding | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Compact / condensed | 48–56px | 24–28px | 12–16px | Dense web apps, admin dashboards, the scrolled state of a marketing header |
| Standard | 64px | 28–32px | 24px | Default for most sites and apps (Material top app bar = 64px) |
| Comfortable | 72–80px | 32–40px | 32–40px | Marketing sites, generous brands, the rest (top-of-page) state |
| Hero / oversized | 88px+ | 40px+ | 48px+ | Editorial, luxury, landing pages with a transparent-over-hero treatment |

Rules that matter:

**Use** **`min-height`****, not** **`height`****.** A hard `height` clips wrapped content, oversized text from user zoom, or a two-line announcement. Let the bar grow.
**The condensed state is a real size, not a hack.** Marketing headers commonly start at ~80px and shrink to ~56px on scroll. Define both as tokens so the transition is a value swap, not magic numbers.
**Mobile is shorter but tap targets aren't.** A 56px mobile header still needs its hamburger, logo, and cart to hit ≥44px targets — you buy that with padding, not bar height.
**Height feeds** **`scroll-padding-top`****.** Whatever your sticky header's height is, the document needs matching `scroll-padding-top` so anchor links don't scroll content _under_ the header (see §9.4). Drive both from one token.

## 5\. States (the ones headers add on top of the usual)
Beyond the per-control states (a header's buttons and links have all eight Button states), the header _as a region_ has its own states that most builds ship without:

**Rest / top-of-page** — the default. On a marketing site this may be the tall, possibly transparent variant.
**Scrolled** — the user has scrolled past a threshold (commonly 8–64px). Triggers the solid background, a shadow/border to separate from content, and often the condensed height. This is the single most common header state and the one people wire up with a jank-y scroll listener instead of `IntersectionObserver` (see §9.5).
**Transparent (over hero)** — the header sits _over_ a hero image/video with no background, light text. Only valid while sufficient contrast exists; the moment content scrolls under it, it must flip to solid. Transparent-forever over arbitrary content is a contrast failure waiting to happen.
**Condensed** — reduced height (and often reduced logo). Usually coupled to _scrolled_ but can be its own toggle for density-conscious apps.
**Hidden (hide-on-scroll-down)** — the header translates off-screen when scrolling down to maximize reading space, and slides back on scroll-up. A deliberate state with a real accessibility contract (see §13).
**Sticky / stuck** — pinned to the top via `position: sticky` or `fixed`. The "stuck" moment can be detected and styled (a common trick: a sentinel + `IntersectionObserver`).
**Menu-open (mobile)** — the hamburger is expanded, the drawer is showing, focus is trapped in the drawer, background scroll is locked, and `aria-expanded="true"`.
**Mega-menu-open (desktop)** — a full-width panel is expanded under a nav item; `aria-expanded="true"` on the trigger, focus and Escape handled.
**Search-active** — the search field is expanded/focused, possibly with a suggestions listbox (`role="combobox"`) attached.
**Elevated (app)** — Material's pattern where the app bar gains elevation/tint when content scrolls beneath it, signaling layering.

The tell of an amateur build: only states 1 and 6 exist. The tell of a senior build: _scrolled_, _stuck_, and _hidden_ are all deliberate, tokenized, and reduced-motion-aware.

## 6\. Types & Variants
Headers come in functional families. Pick by product type, not by taste:

**Marketing / site header:** logo + horizontal nav (often with mega-menus) + one primary CTA. Frequently transparent-over-hero then solid-on-scroll. Brand-forward, generous height.
**App / product top bar:** logo/app name + global search + account + notifications. Denser, usually paired with a persistent Side menu ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216991](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216991)) for section nav. Often sticky, rarely transparent.
**E-commerce header:** logo + prominent search + account + cart, usually with a category nav row and a promo/announcement bar stacked on top. Search is the star.
**Documentation header:** logo + version switcher + search (⌘K) + theme toggle + GitHub link, with the section nav in a left sidebar, not the header.
**Dashboard / admin header:** breadcrumbs or page title + contextual actions + user menu; the section nav lives in a rail. The header is thin and functional.
**Split / two-row header:** utility row (thin, top: language, support, account) above a main row (logo, nav, CTA). Common on enterprise and gov sites.
**Centered-logo header:** logo centered, nav split left/right. Editorial and fashion. Harder to make responsive; the logo usually moves left on mobile.
**Minimal / focus header:** logo + a single exit/close during checkout or onboarding. Deliberately strips nav to reduce abandonment. (A checkout header with full nav is a conversion leak.)

Placement of the primary CTA and search follows the same logic every time: **identity left, wayfinding center, action right** (LTR). Mirror it for RTL with logical properties, not by hand.

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Nearly every page has a header — the question is _what kind_, and when to strip it:

**Use a full header when** the user needs persistent orientation and global actions: marketing sites, content sites, most apps, e-commerce.

**Strip it down (or drop nav) when:**

**Checkout / payment flows.** Remove primary nav; keep logo + a secure-checkout signal + maybe an exit. Every nav link in checkout is an exit ramp.
**Onboarding / focused tasks.** A single-step wizard wants a minimal header (logo + progress + close), not the full site chrome.
**Full-bleed immersive experiences** (maps, canvases, presentations). Use a floating, auto-hiding, or collapsed header so it doesn't eat the canvas.
**Auth screens.** Logo only. No nav to wander off into.

**Prefer another pattern when:**

The primary navigation is deep and hierarchical (docs, admin) → the header holds search + identity, and the real nav goes in a Side menu ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216991](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216991)).
You're tempted to cram 9 top-level links → that's an information-architecture problem; group into a mega-menu or move secondary items to the Footer ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216931](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216931)).

**The sticky question.** Make a header sticky when navigation and search are used _mid-content_ and often (apps, long docs, shops). Keep it static when content is the point and the header would just steal pixels (long-form articles — though hide-on-scroll is a nice middle ground). A sticky header that's 96px tall on a phone is hostile; if you stick it, condense it.

## 8\. Headers Across Design Systems
Same region, different houses:

**Material Design (Google) — "Top App Bar":** 64px default, four types (center-aligned, small, medium, large). The large/medium variants shrink to small on scroll — the canonical condensed pattern. Gains a tonal "elevated" surface tint when content scrolls under it. Leading nav icon (hamburger/back), centered or leading title, trailing action icons.
**Apple HIG — Navigation Bar / Toolbar:** the top bar is restrained, title-centric, with a large-title-collapses-to-inline-title scroll behavior on iOS. Buttons are text or SF Symbols, no borders. In 2026 the bar picks up **Liquid Glass** material by default.
**Fluent (Microsoft):** a thin, acrylic/mica top command bar; app name/waffle left, search center, account right (the Microsoft 365 shell is the archetype). Dense and neutral.
**Ant Design / enterprise:** `Layout.Header` — a fixed-height dark or light bar inside a `Layout`, typically paired with a `Sider`. Menu component runs horizontally inside it. Built for admin density.
**Tailwind / utility-first:** no prescribed header; you compose `<header class="sticky top-0 …">`. Tailwind UI ships proven header + flyout-menu blocks you adapt. Freedom plus the risk of re-inventing the a11y each time.
**shadcn/Radix (2024+ web standard):** no single "Header" component — you assemble it from `NavigationMenu` (Radix, handles mega-menu keyboard/focus), `Sheet` (the mobile drawer), `DropdownMenu` (account), and `Button`. Accessibility comes from the primitives; the bar and layout are yours.
**Bootstrap:** the `.navbar` component _is_ the header for most Bootstrap sites — `navbar-expand-lg` gives you the responsive collapse-to-hamburger for free, `.sticky-top` makes it stick. Convention over ownership.

## 9\. The Code
### 9.1 HTML (get the landmarks right first)
The whole component rests on correct semantics. This is the version to master before any framework.

```plain
<!-- Skip link: first focusable thing on the page -->
```

@Skip to content

```plain
<!-- ONE top-level header => the banner landmark, implicitly -->
<header class="site-header" id="top">
```

```plain
<!-- Brand: a link to home with a REAL accessible name -->
    <a class="brand" href="/" aria-label="Urban Jungle Goddess — home">
      <img src="/logo.svg" alt="" width="32" height="32" aria-hidden="true" />
      <span class="brand__word">Urban Jungle Goddess</span>
</a>
```

```plain
<!-- Primary navigation: label it so screen readers can tell it apart -->
    <nav class="primary-nav" aria-label="Primary">
      <ul>
        <li><a href="/work">Work</a></li>
        <li>
          <button type="button" aria-expanded="false" aria-controls="services-menu">
            Services
          </button>
          <div id="services-menu" class="mega-menu" hidden> … </div>
        </li>
        <li><a href="/about">About</a></li>
      </ul>
</nav>
```

```plain
<!-- Search is a landmark of its own -->
    <form class="header-search" role="search" action="/search">
      <label for="site-search" class="visually-hidden">Search the site</label>
      <input id="site-search" name="q" type="search" placeholder="Search…" />
</form>
```

```plain
<!-- Actions cluster -->
    <div class="header-actions">
      <a class="btn btn--primary" href="/start">Book a call</a>
      <button type="button" class="account" aria-haspopup="menu" aria-expanded="false">
        <img src="/avatar.jpg" alt="Your account" width="32" height="32" />
      </button>
</div>
```

```plain
<!-- Mobile menu toggle: icon-only => aria-label is mandatory -->
    <button type="button" class="menu-toggle" aria-label="Open menu"
            aria-expanded="false" aria-controls="mobile-drawer">
      <svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><!-- hamburger --></svg>
    </button>
  </div>
```

```plain
<main id="main"> … </main>
```

Key HTML rules:

**A top-level** **`<header>`** **is the** **`banner`** **landmark automatically** — no `role="banner"` needed. Only add `role="banner"` if you're forced to use a non-`<header>` element. A `<header>` nested inside `<main>`, `<article>`, or `<section>` is _not_ a banner — that's the escape hatch for section headers.
**Exactly one banner per page.** Two top-level `<header>`s = two banners = a screen-reader user hears "banner" twice and can't trust the landmark. Audit for it.
**Label your navs.** If there's more than one `<nav>` on the page (primary + footer + breadcrumbs), each needs a distinct `aria-label` so they're distinguishable in the landmarks list.
**The logo** **`alt`** **carries the accessible name.** If the `<img>` has the brand name in `alt`, the decorative SVG version uses `alt=""`/`aria-hidden`. Don't double up ("Logo Logo").
**Skip link targets** **`#main`****,** and `#main` must be focusable (`tabindex="-1"` if it's not naturally). This is what lets keyboard users bypass the header on every single page.

Three things here separate senior from junior CSS:

**`scroll-padding-top`** **on** **`html`****.** Without it, clicking an in-page anchor scrolls the target _under_ the fixed/sticky header and the user lands on hidden content. One line, constantly missed.
**`position: sticky`** **over** **`fixed`** **when you can.** `sticky` keeps the header in normal flow, so it doesn't overlap content and doesn't need a spacer `<div>`/`padding-top` on `<body>` the way `fixed` does. Use `fixed` only when you truly need the header out of flow (e.g. over a scroll container it doesn't belong to).
**Reduced-motion kills the hide/condense animation** and, critically, disables the hide-on-scroll transform entirely — a header that vanishes and reappears is exactly the kind of motion that triggers vestibular discomfort.

### 9.3 The `fixed` gotcha: content-jump
If you use `position: fixed` (out of flow), the header no longer occupies space, so the first chunk of content slides up underneath it. You must reserve the space:

```css
.site-header { position: fixed; inset-inline: 0; top: 0; height: var(--header-h); }
body { padding-top: var(--header-h); }   /* reserve the header's footprint */
```

Forget this and your hero's first line hides behind the bar. `position: sticky` sidesteps the whole problem because it stays in flow — which is why it's the default recommendation. The related bug: a header that starts non-sticky and _becomes_ `fixed` on scroll causes a **layout jump** at the switch point because space suddenly collapses. Fix it by reserving the space up front or by using `sticky` + a class toggle instead of switching `position`.

### 9.4 Focus obscured by a sticky header (WCAG 2.4.11)
This is the header accessibility bug almost everyone ships. When a keyboard user Tabs to something near the top of the scroll region, the browser scrolls it into view — but a `sticky`/`fixed` header can cover it, so the focused element is invisible even though it "has focus." **WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 2.4.11 (Focus Not Obscured, Minimum)** makes this a conformance failure.

`scroll-padding-top` fixes anchor jumps but does **not** by itself fix programmatic focus scrolling in every browser. The robust fixes:

```css
/* 1. Give every focusable target a scroll margin equal to the header height,
      so the browser leaves room when it scrolls focus into view. */
:target,
a[href], button, input, select, textarea, [tabindex] {
  scroll-margin-top: calc(var(--header-h) + 8px);
}
```

```plain
// 2. Belt-and-suspenders: after focus moves, if the element is hidden behind
//    the header, nudge the scroll down by the header's height.
const header = document.querySelector(".site-header");
document.addEventListener("focusin", (e) => {
  const el = e.target;
  if (!(el instanceof HTMLElement)) return;
  const headerBottom = header.getBoundingClientRect().bottom;
  const elTop = el.getBoundingClientRect().top;
  if (elTop < headerBottom) {
    window.scrollBy({ top: elTop - headerBottom - 8, behavior: "auto" });
  }
});
```

If you only remember one accessibility fact about headers: **a sticky header can eat keyboard focus, and that's a WCAG 2.2 failure.** Test it by Tabbing through your page with the header stuck.

### 9.5 Vanilla JS: scrolled state (do it with IntersectionObserver, not `scroll`)
The amateur version listens to `scroll` and reads `window.scrollY` on every frame — a main-thread tax that causes jank. The senior version puts an invisible sentinel at the top of the page and lets the browser tell you when it leaves the viewport:

const header = document.querySelector(".site-header");

```plain
// A 1px sentinel sitting where "top of page" is.
const sentinel = document.createElement("div");
sentinel.style.cssText = "position:absolute;top:0;height:1px;width:1px;";
```

document.body.prepend(sentinel);

```plain
const io = new IntersectionObserver(
  ([entry]) => header.classList.toggle("is-scrolled", !entry.isIntersecting),
  { rootMargin: "0px" }
);
io.observe(sentinel);
```

No scroll handler, no layout thrash, the browser does the work off the main thread. This is the pattern for the _scrolled_ and _stuck_ states both.

### 9.6 Vanilla JS: hide-on-scroll-down / show-on-scroll-up
Reclaim reading space by hiding the header on downward scroll and revealing it on any upward scroll. Do it in a `requestAnimationFrame` and respect reduced-motion:

```plain
const header = document.querySelector(".site-header");
const reduce = matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)").matches;
let lastY = window.scrollY;
```

let ticking = false;

```plain
function onScroll() {
  const y = window.scrollY;
  const goingDown = y > lastY;
  // don't hide near the very top; hide only past a threshold
  if (!reduce && goingDown && y > 120) header.classList.add("is-hidden");
  else header.classList.remove("is-hidden");
  lastY = y;
  ticking = false;
```

}

```plain
addEventListener("scroll", () => {
  if (!ticking) { requestAnimationFrame(onScroll); ticking = true; }
}, { passive: true });   // passive => never blocks scrolling
```

The accessibility contract on hide-on-scroll: the header must **come back on focus**, not just on scroll-up — a keyboard user Tabbing into a hidden header needs it revealed. Add a `focusin` handler that removes `.is-hidden`, and never hide the header while a mobile drawer or mega-menu is open.

## 10\. Accessibility (non-negotiable)
The header is the entry point for every assistive-tech user, so this section is the point of the whole doc.

**One** **`banner`** **landmark.** A top-level `<header>` is `banner` automatically. Never ship two. A `<header>` inside `<main>`/`<article>` is deliberately _not_ a banner — use that for section headers.
**Skip link first.** The first focusable element on the page should let keyboard users jump past the header to `#main`. Without it, every keyboard user Tabs through the entire header on every page. `#main` must be focusable (`tabindex="-1"`).
**Label every nav.** Multiple `<nav>`s (primary, footer, breadcrumb) each need a unique `aria-label` so they're distinguishable in the landmark list.
**The hamburger's contract:** `aria-label` (it's icon-only), `aria-expanded` (open/closed), `aria-controls` (points to the drawer id). Label text and `aria-expanded` must both track state.
**Mobile drawer = modal semantics:** `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"`, focus trapped inside while open, Escape closes, focus returns to the toggle, background scroll locked, and the rest of the page is inert.
**Focus not obscured (WCAG 2.4.11).** A sticky/fixed header must not cover a focused element. Fix with `scroll-margin-top` on focusables plus the JS nudge in §9.4. **Test by Tabbing with the header stuck.**
**Active section:** mark the current page's nav link with `aria-current="page"` — semantic and styleable.
**Keyboard map for the mega-menu:** trigger opens on Enter/Space/Down, Escape closes and returns focus, Tab moves through items, arrow keys navigate within the panel. Radix `NavigationMenu` implements this; hand-rolled menus usually get it wrong.
**Contrast in every header state.** The transparent-over-hero state is the trap: light logo/text over a bright part of the hero image fails 4.5:1. Add a scrim/gradient behind the header text, or flip to solid sooner.
**Reduced motion:** disable hide-on-scroll, condense animations, and any header parallax under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. A header that jumps around is a vestibular hazard.
**Touch targets ≥44px** for the hamburger, cart, and account controls even in a 56px condensed bar.

### Keyboard map (header controls)

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| Tab | Skip link → brand → nav items → search → actions → toggle, in DOM order |
| Enter / Space | Activate a link/button; open the mega-menu trigger or account menu |
| Escape | Close an open mega-menu / account menu / mobile drawer, return focus to trigger |
| Arrow keys | Move within an open mega-menu or menu (not between top-level nav links) |

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas (2026-aware)
**Liquid Glass headers.** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe navigation bars now render in Liquid Glass by default — translucent material that refracts the content scrolling beneath it with a specular rim. The web is approximating it with layered `backdrop-filter` + highlights. For UJG this sings over deep Eminence/Night surfaces (see §16).
**Command palette as the new header search.** The ⌘K palette (Linear, Vercel, Raycast) is eating the header search box for apps — one input that searches, navigates, and runs commands. The header shows a compact "⌘K" affordance that opens the palette.
**Scroll-driven animations, CSS-native.** `animation-timeline: scroll()` and `view()` let the condense/hide/tint transitions run off the compositor with zero JS — the scrolled state as a pure-CSS scroll-linked animation. Guard with `prefers-reduced-motion`.
**View Transitions across navigations.** The header persists visually while the page morphs under it (MPA View Transitions API), so the brand/nav never flicker between pages.
**AI-native header surfaces.** An "Ask" affordance in the header that opens an agentic assistant instead of, or beside, search — streaming answers where autocomplete used to be.
**Container queries for the header itself.** Instead of viewport breakpoints, the header responds to _its own_ width via container queries — the same header component adapts correctly whether it's full-bleed or inside a narrow embedded layout.
**Dynamic Island-style contextual headers.** Mobile headers that morph to show live context (playing track, active call, order status) borrowed from iOS — the header as a live status surface, not just chrome.

## 12\. Performance & Bundle Cost
The header is above the fold and on every page, so its cost is paid on every first paint.

**It's the #1 CLS offender.** A logo image without `width`/`height` (or `aspect-ratio`) reflows the bar when it loads and shoves the page — measurable Cumulative Layout Shift. Always reserve the logo's box, and reserve the header's height so content below doesn't jump. Ship the header at its final height from the first paint.
**Don't hydrate the whole header.** In Next/Astro, keep the shell as server-rendered HTML and make only the toggle/scrolled-state interactive (island). A fully-client header ships needless JS for markup that never changes.
**`IntersectionObserver`****, not** **`scroll`****.** A `scroll` listener that reads layout every frame janks the main thread during the exact interaction (scrolling) where smoothness matters most. Sentinel + observer moves it off-thread (see §9.5). If you must use `scroll`, make it `{ passive: true }` and `requestAnimationFrame`\-batched.
**Animate only** **`transform`****/****`opacity`****.** Hide-on-scroll should translate, not animate `top`/`height` (which reflow every frame). The condense transition on `min-height` is cheap because it's infrequent, but prefer `transform: scale` on the logo over resizing it.
**Tree-shake header icons.** Named imports (`import { Menu } from "lucide-react"`), never `import *` — the same trap the Button doc calls out. A header with six namespace-imported icons can add hundreds of KB.
**Inline the critical header CSS.** Because it's above the fold, its styles are render-blocking-critical; inlining them (and the logo as an optimized SVG) shaves the first meaningful paint.
**`content-visibility`** **doesn't apply to the header** — it's always visible — but it does apply to the mega-menu panels, which can be `content-visibility: auto` until opened.

## 13\. Conversion & UX Killers (the silent ones)
None of these throw an error. They just bleed trust and revenue.

**Logo that doesn't go home.** The most learned convention on the web. A logo that isn't a link to `/` taxes every visitor and reads as broken. Free fix, constantly missed.
**Too many top-level links.** Nine competing nav items = decision paralysis and a header that can't collapse cleanly. Group into a mega-menu, or push secondary links to the footer. If everything's in the nav, nothing is.
**Sticky header that's too tall on mobile.** A 96px bar pinned over a phone screen eats a third of the viewport. If you stick it, condense it — or hide-on-scroll it.
**Transparent header with unreadable text.** Light nav text over a bright hero photo fails contrast the moment the image is light. Add a scrim, or flip to solid earlier. This is a trust-_and_\-accessibility failure at once.
**No visible active state.** Users can't tell what section they're in. `aria-current="page"` + a visible indicator orients them; without it the header is a dead map.
**Search buried behind an icon on a search-driven site.** On e-commerce, hiding the highest-converting element (search) behind a magnifying-glass tap adds friction to your best users. Show the box.
**Header content-jump on load.** The bar renders at one height, then the logo/CTA arrives and it grows, shoving the hero. Reserve space; it reads as slow and janky even when it's fast.
**Focus obscured by the sticky bar.** Keyboard users Tab to a link and it's hidden behind the header — the WCAG 2.4.11 failure from §9.4. Silent, but it locks out keyboard users entirely near the top of the page.
**Full nav in checkout.** Every nav link during checkout is an exit ramp. Strip the header to logo + trust signal + exit; measure the abandonment drop.
**Hide-on-scroll that never comes back for keyboards.** If the hidden header only returns on scroll-up, a keyboard user who Tabs into it gets nothing. Reveal on `focusin` too.

**The through-line:** every one fails quietly. No crash, just a slightly lower conversion number and a slightly less trusted brand. Audit the header before the ad spend.

## 14\. Advanced Patterns
### Scroll-linked states with pure CSS (no JS)
2026's scroll-driven animations let the scrolled/condensed transition run on the compositor:

```css
@supports (animation-timeline: scroll()) {
  .site-header {
    animation: condense linear both;
    animation-timeline: scroll(root);
    animation-range: 0 200px;
  }
  @keyframes condense {
    to { min-height: var(--header-h-condensed); background: #0A0A0A; }
  }
}
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .site-header { animation: none; }
}
```

### The "stuck" detector (sentinel + IntersectionObserver)
Styling a `position: sticky` element _when it becomes stuck_ isn't possible in CSS alone. The sentinel trick from §9.5 is the standard workaround — observe a 1px element at the top; when it leaves the viewport, the header is stuck. (A future `:stuck` pseudo-class is proposed but not shipped.)

### Mega-menu integration
A mega-menu is a full-width panel under a nav trigger. The header's job is to host it correctly: the trigger is a `<button aria-expanded aria-controls>`, the panel is `hidden` until opened, Escape closes and returns focus, and the panel spans the header's full width via `position: absolute; inset-inline: 0` off the sticky header. Keyboard nav inside the panel is arrow-key driven. Use Radix `NavigationMenu` unless you enjoy re-implementing focus management. Cross-link: the panel contents are Navigation ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216971](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-216971)) at heart.

### Design tokens for the whole bar
Drive every header dimension from tokens so a rebrand or a density change is a value swap:

```css
:root {
  --header-h: 64px;
  --header-h-condensed: 56px;
  --header-pad-x: 24px;
  --header-bg: #0A0A0A;
  --header-bg-scrolled: #0A0A0A;
  --header-border: color-mix(in oklab, #5F2C82 40%, transparent);
  --header-z: 1000;
}
```

Critically, `scroll-padding-top: var(--header-h)` reads the _same_ token, so height and anchor-offset can never drift apart.

### Container-query header
Make the header respond to its own width, not the viewport — the same component works full-bleed or embedded:

```css
.header-shell { container-type: inline-size; }
@container (max-width: 640px) {
  .primary-nav { display: none; }
  .menu-toggle { display: inline-flex; }
}
```

## 15\. Security
The header's attack surface is the search form, account/auth controls, and any CMS-driven navigation content.

**Header search is user input hitting the server.** The search query (`?q=…`) is untrusted. Validate length (cap at ~120 chars), parameterize the query (never string-concat into SQL), rate-limit per IP (autocomplete fires per keystroke), and sanitize the displayed "Showing results for X" echo to prevent reflected XSS. See the full endpoint in §9.11.

**Nav labels from a CMS are XSS vectors.** If navigation items (mega-menu headings, link labels) come from an admin panel or CMS, render them as text (framework interpolation), never `innerHTML`. A stored-XSS in a nav label runs on every page of the site because the header is global.

**Account menu and auth state.** The header shows different content pre/post-auth (Sign In vs. avatar + menu). Never expose the logged-in user's PII (full email, avatar URL) in server-rendered HTML visible to crawlers or unauthenticated users. Render auth-dependent header content on the client or behind an auth gate.

**CSRF on the mobile drawer's forms.** If the mobile drawer contains a newsletter or quick-action form, it needs the same CSRF protection as any form. Server Actions (Next.js) handle this by construction; raw endpoints need `SameSite` cookies or a token.

**Open-redirect risk in login/return links.** A "Sign in" link often carries a `?returnTo=…` parameter. Validate it against your own domain; an unvalidated `returnTo` is a phishing vector (attacker crafts a URL that logs the user in then redirects to a fake site).

**Third-party scripts in the header.** Analytics, chat widgets, and A/B-test snippets injected into the header are render-blocking and a supply-chain risk. Pin versions, audit with CSP, and never `document.write` from the header.

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Exactly one top-level `<header>` → one `banner` landmark; no nested top-level headers.
Logo is a link to `/` with a real accessible name.
Skip link is the first focusable element and targets a focusable `#main`.
Every `<nav>` has a distinct `aria-label`; active link marked `aria-current="page"`.
Hamburger has `aria-label` + `aria-expanded` + `aria-controls`; label tracks state.
Mobile drawer traps focus, closes on Escape, restores focus to the toggle, locks body scroll.
`scroll-padding-top` (and focusable `scroll-margin-top`) match the header height — anchors and focus clear the bar (WCAG 2.4.11 tested by Tabbing while stuck).
Scrolled state uses `IntersectionObserver`, not a `scroll` listener.
Hide-on-scroll reveals on `focusin`, is disabled under reduced motion, and never hides while a menu is open.
Transparent-over-hero has a scrim or flips to solid before contrast fails (≥4.5:1 in every state).
Logo image has `width`/`height`/`aspect-ratio`; header ships at final height (no CLS).
Header icons are named imports (tree-shaken); shell is server-rendered where possible.
Touch targets ≥44px for toggle, cart, account even in the condensed bar.
Mobile safe-area insets respected (`env(safe-area-inset-*)`).
Checkout/onboarding headers strip nav to reduce exits.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the same header, eleven skins)
Style is skin; the banner landmark, keyboard path, focus ring, single-`banner` rule, and 44px targets never change across any of these. See them all in the 🎨 Design Styles (visual languages) ([https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551)) gallery.

**Flat:** solid bar, crisp bottom border on scroll, no shadow. Reads instantly, scales fast.
**Material:** the top app bar — subtle elevation, tonal surface tint that deepens when content scrolls under it, 64px, ripple on the menu icon.
**Glassmorphism:** translucent bar with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over the content beneath. Gorgeous, and a contrast trap — guard the text against whatever scrolls under it.
**Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe material — the bar _refracts_ the content passing beneath it with a specular rim and a moving sheen, not a static blur. Pill-ish inner controls, reactive to scroll. Native on SwiftUI via the default nav bar; an honest approximation on the web with layered highlights. Guard for contrast _and_ reduced-motion.
**Neumorphism:** the bar as a soft extruded surface, controls as gentle bumps. Pretty, contrast-poor — accent only, never for a text-dense header.
**Skeuomorphism:** a beveled, gradient "physical shelf" masthead with an inner highlight. The tactile, real-object look.
**Neo-Brutalism:** thick black bottom border, hard offset shadow, zero radius, high-contrast clashing color. Loud, on-brand for campaign moments.
**Claymorphism:** puffy, big-radius bar with soft inner light and a gentle glow. Friendly, approachable products.
**Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue gradient bar on dark. Premium; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (kill the animation, keep the gradient).
**Minimal / Swiss:** typography and a hairline rule do all the work — near-zero decoration, generous whitespace, sharp corners. The editorial default.
**UJG Brand:** the house style — deep Eminence/Night bar, Goldenrod wordmark and focus ring, a warm glow on the primary CTA, confident radius. Lean the scrolled state toward a Liquid Glass accent over the dark surface (the saturated backdrop makes the refraction sing), keep Flat/Swiss for dense app headers, and reserve Brutalism/Aurora for landing-page moments.

**Rule that never changes:** the style is the skin. The header's landmark semantics, keyboard path, focus visibility, single-banner rule, and 44px targets stay identical across all eleven.
