# Portal

# The Portal: A Senior Engineer's Complete Breakdown
The render-elsewhere primitive. It moves DOM without moving the React tree — and that one trick is what makes every overlay in your app actually work.

A Portal isn't something a user ever sees. It's a _mechanism_: render this subtree into a **different** DOM node than where it lives in the component tree, while keeping the tree, the state, the context, and the event flow completely intact. Nail this and your modals stop getting clipped, your tooltips stop hiding behind sibling cards, and your dropdowns stop fighting `z-index`. Miss it and you'll spend a week debugging an overlay that's rendering perfectly — just three `overflow: hidden` containers too deep to ever be seen.

**Try it live (coming in a later pass):** the companion playground will let you toggle a portal target on and off, watch children jump between an `overflow: hidden` clipping box and `document.body`, and see the React event still bubble to the _logical_ parent even though the DOM node is somewhere else entirely. There'll be an audit angle too — paste an overlay's markup and it'll flag missing portal roots, broken focus return, and severed `aria-owns` relationships.

Portal is infrastructure for two components that _do_ have a visual surface: the **Overlay** primitive (backdrop, positioning, dismiss) and the **Modal** (a focus-trapped overlay). Read this one first — they both stand on it.
* * *

## 1\. What a Portal Actually Is
A portal renders a component's children into a DOM node that lives **outside** the parent's DOM hierarchy, while keeping that component exactly where it is in the _framework_ tree. Two trees, deliberately out of sync:
*   **The framework tree** (React/Vue/Svelte component tree): unchanged. State flows down, context is inherited, events bubble to logical parents. The portalled component is still "inside" your `<Card>` as far as your code is concerned.
*   **The DOM tree** (what the browser actually paints): the children get physically appended to `document.body` (or a dedicated portal root), escaping every ancestor between them and the root.

That's the whole idea. The word gets muddied because people conflate it with things it isn't:
*   **Portal (mechanism):** _where_ something renders in the DOM. No styling, no behavior, no visuals of its own. `createPortal(children, domNode)`.
*   **Overlay (component):** a thing that floats above the page — backdrop, positioning, click-outside-to-dismiss. Overlays _usually use_ a portal, but the overlay is the visual component; the portal is just how it escapes.
*   **Modal / Dialog:** an overlay that also traps focus and blocks the page behind it. Uses a portal, uses an overlay, adds focus management. Three layers, often confused as one.
*   **Teleport / Top layer /** **`position: fixed`****:** all _alternatives or successors_ to portalling — covered below. `position: fixed` alone does **not** escape a `transform`ed ancestor; that's the trap that sends people reaching for portals in the first place.

The one-line version: **a portal changes the DOM address of a subtree without changing its logical parentage.** Everything surprising about portals falls out of that sentence.
* * *

## 2\. Why It Matters (the clipping and stacking problem)
Portals exist to solve one specific, brutal category of bug: an element that is _rendered correctly_ but _cannot be seen correctly_ because an ancestor is trapping it. Three ancestor properties cause it:

**1.** **`overflow: hidden`** **/** **`auto`** **/** **`scroll`** **clips it.** A dropdown inside a card with `overflow: hidden` gets its bottom half sliced off at the card's edge. The menu is in the DOM, styled fine — the parent is just wearing scissors.

**2.** **`z-index`** **and stacking contexts bury it.** `z-index` is not global. The moment an ancestor creates a _stacking context_ (via `opacity < 1`, `transform`, `filter`, `will-change`, `position` + `z-index`, and more), your child's `z-index: 9999` only competes _within that context_. A sibling card with a higher-stacked context will paint on top of your "always on top" modal, and no z-index number you type will fix it, because you're not even in the same race.

**3.** **`transform`****,** **`filter`****, and** **`perspective`** **re-anchor** **`position: fixed`****.** This is the cruelest one. `position: fixed` is supposed to position relative to the viewport. But if _any_ ancestor has a `transform` (even `transform: translateZ(0)` for a GPU hack, even a CSS animation), that ancestor becomes the containing block, and your "fixed to the viewport" modal is now fixed to a `<div>` three levels deep. It scrolls with the page, sits in the wrong spot, and gets clipped.

You cannot reliably fix these from CSS at the child, because the _cause is an ancestor_ and you often don't control it (it's in a design-system card, a third-party layout, a virtualized list). The portal sidesteps all three by relocating the child to `document.body`, where it has **no** clipping ancestor, **no** competing stacking context, and **no** transformed containing block. The stakes:
*   **Conversion:** a checkout modal clipped to 60% height, or a "Buy" dropdown hidden behind the next section, is lost revenue that never throws an error.
*   **Trust:** overlays that flicker, mis-position, or get half-eaten read as "this app is broken" even when the logic is perfect.
*   **Accessibility:** the fix (relocate in the DOM) _creates_ a new problem (DOM order no longer matches logical order), which is why §10 exists and why a lazy portal is an a11y regression waiting to happen.
* * *

## 3\. Anatomy of a Portal
A portal has fewer moving parts than a visual component, but each one is load-bearing:
*   **The source (logical parent):** where the component lives in your code and the framework tree. Owns the state, the props, the context. Renders _conceptually_ here.
*   **The children:** the subtree being relocated. React/Vue keep rendering and updating these normally; only their DOM destination changes.
*   **The target (mount node / portal root):** the real DOM element the children get appended into. Usually `document.body` or a dedicated `<div id="portal-root">`. **Must exist before the portal renders.**
*   **The portal boundary:** the invisible seam between "framework parentage" (intact) and "DOM parentage" (relocated). Events cross it following the _framework_ tree; focus and screen-reader order follow the _DOM_ tree. The mismatch is the whole difficulty.
*   **The bridge / context pass-through:** in React, context providers above the portal still reach the children. That's not automatic in every system (Web Components lose it), and it's the feature that makes portals usable at all.
*   **Cleanup:** on unmount, the portal must remove its DOM node from the target. Leak this and you accumulate orphaned nodes on every open/close.
* * *

## 4\. Where Portals Mount (targets and roots)
There's no "size" for a portal — it has no visual scale. The equivalent axis is **mount strategy**: where you send the children, and who owns that node.

| Strategy | Target | Owns the node | When to use |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Body-direct | `document.body` | The portal appends/removes | Quick overlays, one-offs. Simplest. |
| Dedicated root | `<div id="portal-root">` in HTML | The HTML author | App-wide standard; predictable stacking, one place to scope styles. |
| Lazy root | created on first use, appended to body | The portal, memoized | Libraries that can't assume a root exists in host HTML. |
| Named targets | multiple roots by id (`#modal-root`, `#toast-root`) | HTML author | Layering: toasts above modals above tooltips, each its own root in a known order. |
| Container prop | caller passes any element | The caller | Reusable components (Radix, MUI) that let consumers choose the destination. |

The rules that matter:
*   **A dedicated** **`#portal-root`** **as a direct child of** **`<body>`****, placed** **_after_** **your** **`#root`****/****`#app`****, is the professional default.** It guarantees a clean stacking context at the top of the document and a single place to reason about overlay order.
*   **Ordering of roots = layering.** Because they're plain DOM siblings with no competing stacking context, source order decides who's on top. Put `#toast-root` after `#modal-root` and toasts win without a single `z-index`.
*   **Never portal into a node that a framework also manages** (e.g. into your React `#root`). You'll get "removeChild on a node that is no longer a child" errors when both React and your cleanup try to touch it. Portal to a node React doesn't own.
* * *

## 5\. States & Lifecycle
A portal itself has no hover/active/pressed states — it's not interactive. Its states are _lifecycle_ states, and getting them right is what separates a portal that works from one that crashes on the first render or leaks on the hundredth.
1. **Unmounted / target-missing:** the portal root doesn't exist yet (SSR, or before `useEffect`). The portal must render **nothing**, not throw. This is the #1 portal crash: `createPortal(children, null)`.
2. **Mounting:** target confirmed to exist; children appended to it. In React this happens synchronously once the target ref is set.
3. **Mounted / active:** children live in the target, update normally, receive context and events from the logical parent.
4. **Updating:** props/state change in the source; children re-render in place at the target. No DOM churn beyond the normal diff.
5. **Unmounting / cleanup:** component removed; portal must detach its node from the target. React handles this for `createPortal` children automatically, but _any_ portal _root_ you created manually you must remove manually.
6. **Hydration (SSR):** the tricky one. On the server there is no `document.body`, so the portal renders nothing; on the client it must render _after_ mount so the target exists — otherwise hydration mismatches (server HTML ≠ first client render). Pattern: gate the portal behind a `mounted` flag set in `useEffect`.

The lifecycle killers, stated plainly:
*   **Rendering before the target exists** → crash or silent no-op.
*   **Not removing a manually-created root** → DOM node leak, one per mount.
*   **Rendering on the server** → hydration mismatch, React blows away and re-renders the tree.
* * *

## 6\. Types & Variants
"Variant" for a portal means _how it's implemented and what it targets_, not how it looks.
*   **Static-target portal:** targets a root that already exists in the HTML (`document.getElementById("portal-root")`). Simplest, most predictable. The default for app code.
*   **Lazy/self-creating portal:** creates its own target `<div>` on first render, appends it to `body`, removes it on unmount. The default for _libraries_ that can't assume host HTML has a root.
*   **Configurable-container portal:** exposes a `container` prop so the consumer picks the destination (render inside a specific panel, a shadow root, an iframe). This is what mature libraries ship (Radix `Portal`, MUI `Portal`, Vue `Teleport to`).
*   **Named-layer portals:** several roots (`#tooltip-root`, `#modal-root`, `#toast-root`) whose source order encodes z-layering without z-index.
*   **Disabled portal:** a `disabled`/`disablePortal` escape hatch that renders in place instead of teleporting — useful when the parent _isn't_ a clipping context and you want to keep DOM locality (better for some a11y and print cases).
*   **Top-layer "portal" (the modern non-portal):** `<dialog>.showModal()` and `popover` render into the browser's **top layer**, above everything, no portal and no z-index needed. Not a portal at all — the successor that often makes portals unnecessary (§11).
* * *

## 7\. When to Use One (and When Not To)
Reach for a portal when a subtree must **visually escape its ancestors** but stay **logically owned** by them. The canonical cases:
*   **Modals & dialogs** — must cover the whole viewport and trap focus, can't be clipped by a card.
*   **Tooltips & popovers** — anchored to a trigger deep in the tree but must float above everything.
*   **Dropdown menus & comboboxes** — the listbox overflows the input's container; portal it out so it isn't clipped.
*   **Toasts & notifications** — belong to a global stack, not to whatever component fired them.
*   **Full-screen overlays, lightboxes, command palettes** — page-level UI triggered from anywhere.

Don't portal when:
*   **The parent isn't a clipping/stacking/transform trap.** If nothing above you hides overflow or makes a stacking context, a portal is complexity for nothing — `position: absolute` in place is simpler and keeps DOM order honest.
*   **The element is part of the content flow.** Inline validation, an expanding panel, an accordion body — these belong where they are. Teleporting them wrecks reading order and screen-reader flow.
*   **The browser's top layer solves it.** For a true modal, `<dialog>`/`popover` gives you top-layer rendering, backdrop, and focus handling with less code and no portal (§11). Prefer it when your baseline supports it.
*   **You'd break the a11y relationship and can't repair it.** A portal that severs the `aria-owns`/focus link between trigger and content, with no fix, is worse than a clipped-but-connected element. Only portal if you'll also do §10.

Heuristic: **portal for overlays, not for content.** If it floats above the UI, portal it. If it's part of the page, leave it home.
* * *

## 8\. Portals Across Frameworks & Design Systems
The _mechanism_ is universal; every serious framework ships one because the DOM problem is universal.
*   **React** — `createPortal(children, domNode)` from `react-dom`. Events bubble through the React tree, not the DOM tree (the defining surprise). The reference implementation everyone else is compared to.
*   **Vue 3** — `<Teleport to="#target">`. First-class built-in component, `to` selector or element, `:disabled` to render in place, `:defer` (3.5+) for targets that mount later. The cleanest ergonomics of the bunch.
*   **Svelte** — no built-in; a tiny `use:portal` **action** that appends the node to `document.body` on mount and removes it on destroy. Idiomatic and ~10 lines.
*   **Angular** — the **CDK** `Portal` / `PortalOutlet` / `Overlay` package. `Overlay` is the batteries-included layer (positioning strategies, scroll strategies, backdrop) built on top of `DomPortalOutlet`. Enterprise-grade, verbose.
*   **Solid / Preact / Qwik** — `<Portal>` (Solid), `createPortal` (Preact, React-compatible), and Qwik's own — same primitive, framework-shaped syntax.
*   **Radix UI / Headless UI / MUI (Base) / Ark** — every headless library ships a `Portal` primitive with a `container` prop and a `forceMount`/`disabled` escape hatch, because their overlays can't assume anything about the host's clipping ancestors.
*   **Web Components** — no portal API. `<slot>` and `<template>` project _light DOM into shadow DOM_, which is the opposite direction (composition, not relocation). Escaping a shadow boundary means manually appending to a node in the outer document — and you lose framework context across the boundary.

The philosophical split: **React/Solid/Preact** treat portalling as a render-target argument; **Vue** makes it a declarative component; **Svelte** makes it an imperative action; **Angular** makes it a service-driven overlay system. Same escape, four ergonomics.

## 9\. The Code
The biggest section. Portal is lighter than Button, so the depth here is in _the surprises_ — SSR guards, event bubbling, cleanup — not in twenty variants.

### 9.1 React `createPortal` (the primitive)
The entire API is one function. It takes what to render and where to put it, and returns something you return from a component like any JSX.

```javascript
import { createPortal } from "react-dom";

function Tooltip({ children }) {
  // Renders `children` into document.body, but this component is still
  // logically a child of whoever rendered <Tooltip/>.
  return createPortal(
    <div className="tooltip" role="tooltip">{children}</div>,
    document.body
  );
}
```

That's it — but naked like this it has two bugs waiting: it assumes `document.body` exists (breaks in SSR) and it hardcodes the target. The reusable version fixes both.

### 9.2 A reusable `<Portal>` with an SSR guard
This is the component you actually ship. It creates its own mount node, only renders after the client has mounted (so SSR renders nothing and hydration matches), and cleans up after itself.

```typescript
import { useEffect, useRef, useState, type ReactNode } from "react";
import { createPortal } from "react-dom";

interface PortalProps {
  children: ReactNode;
  /** Optional existing node or selector; defaults to a fresh div on <body>. */
  container?: HTMLElement | string;
  /** Escape hatch: render in place instead of teleporting. */
  disabled?: boolean;
}

export function Portal({ children, container, disabled = false }: PortalProps) {
  // `mounted` is false during SSR and the first client render, true after.
  const [mounted, setMounted] = useState(false);
  const nodeRef = useRef<HTMLElement | null>(null);
  const createdRef = useRef(false); // did WE create the node? then WE remove it.

  useEffect(() => {
    let target: HTMLElement;
    if (container instanceof HTMLElement) {
      target = container;
    } else if (typeof container === "string") {
      target = document.querySelector<HTMLElement>(container) ?? document.body;
    } else {
      target = document.createElement("div");
      target.setAttribute("data-portal", "");
      document.body.appendChild(target);
      createdRef.current = true;
    }
    nodeRef.current = target;
    setMounted(true);

    return () => {
      // Only remove a node we created; never yank one the host owns.
      if (createdRef.current && target.parentNode) {
        target.parentNode.removeChild(target);
      }
      setMounted(false);
    };
  }, [container]);

  if (disabled) return <>{children}</>;
  if (!mounted || !nodeRef.current) return null; // SSR + pre-mount: render nothing
  return createPortal(children, nodeRef.current);
}
```

Why this is the senior version, not the junior one:
*   **`mounted`** **gate** means the server renders `null` and the first client render also renders `null`, so hydration matches. Then `useEffect` (client-only) flips it and the portal appears. No hydration mismatch, no `document is not defined` crash.
*   **`createdRef`** tracks ownership. You remove a node **only if you made it**. Removing a host-owned node (or one React manages) throws `NotFoundError: removeChild`.
*   **`container`** **prop** lets consumers redirect the portal (into a modal root, a shadow host, a specific panel) without forking the component.
*   **`disabled`** renders in place — for when the parent isn't a trap and you want to keep DOM locality.

### 9.3 The portal-root pattern (index.html)
The cleanest production setup: a sibling root, declared once, that every overlay targets. No lazy creation, predictable stacking.

```html
<body>
  <div id="root"></div>          <!-- your app -->
  <div id="modal-root"></div>    <!-- overlays mount here, above the app -->
  <div id="toast-root"></div>    <!-- toasts mount here, above modals -->
</body>
```

```typescript
// Portal that targets a known root — no node creation, no cleanup of the root.
export function ModalPortal({ children }: { children: React.ReactNode }) {
  const root = typeof document !== "undefined"
    ? document.getElementById("modal-root")
    : null;
  return root ? createPortal(children, root) : null;
}
```

Because `#modal-root` and `#toast-root` are plain siblings _after_ `#root`, later-in-source wins the paint order. Toasts sit above modals sit above the app **without a single** **`z-index`** — DOM order is doing the layering. This is the trick most teams miss: fight stacking contexts with document structure, not with escalating z-index numbers.

### 9.4 The event-bubbling surprise (the thing that trips everyone)
The single most counterintuitive fact about React portals: **events bubble through the React tree, not the DOM tree.** A click inside a portalled child fires handlers on its _logical_ React parent — even though, in the real DOM, the child is on `document.body` and shares no ancestor with that parent.

```typescript
function Card() {
  // This onClick FIRES when the button inside the portal is clicked,
  // even though the button is physically on <body>, not inside this div.
  return (
    <div onClick={() => console.log("card caught it")}>
      <Portal>
        <button>I live on document.body</button>
      </Portal>
    </div>
  );
}
```

Consequences you must design around:
*   **Click-outside logic breaks if you only check the DOM.** A "close on click outside the menu" handler on the parent will _also_ fire for clicks _inside_ a portalled menu (React re-dispatches them to the parent). Use `event.target` DOM containment checks against the _portal node_, not the logical parent, or Radix-style `onPointerDownOutside`.
*   **`stopPropagation`** **inside the portal stops React-tree propagation**, which can be surprising when you expected DOM isolation. It behaves as if the child were still nested.
*   **Native (non-React) listeners** on `document`/`window` see the _DOM_ location — they fire based on where the node really is. So React-synthetic and native handlers disagree about "inside." This mismatch is the root cause of 90% of "my dropdown closes when I click it" bugs.

### 9.5 The browser top-layer alternative (often no portal needed)
The modern move: for true modals, skip the portal entirely. `<dialog>.showModal()` and the `popover` attribute render into the browser's **top layer** — a layer above the entire document, immune to `z-index`, `overflow`, and `transform` ancestors _by specification_, with a real `::backdrop` and built-in focus/dismiss.

```html
<!-- Popover: top layer, zero JS, no portal -->
<button popovertarget="menu">Open menu</button>
<div id="menu" popover>
  <button>Profile</button>
  <button>Sign out</button>
</div>

<!-- Modal dialog: top layer, focus trap + Esc + ::backdrop for free -->
<dialog id="confirm">
  <form method="dialog">
    <p>Delete this project?</p>
    <button value="cancel">Cancel</button>
    <button value="ok">Delete</button>
  </form>
</dialog>
<script>
  document.querySelector("#open").addEventListener("click", () =>
    document.querySelector("#confirm").showModal()  // renders in the top layer
  );
</script>
```

Why this matters: the top layer solves the _exact_ problem portals were invented for — escaping clipping/stacking/transform ancestors — but does it natively, without relocating DOM, without a root, and without the event-bubbling and a11y-order caveats. **In 2026, reach for** **`<dialog>`****/****`popover`** **first; portal only when you need behavior the top layer doesn't give you** (e.g. a non-modal floating panel with custom positioning across a legacy baseline). The portal isn't dead — it's just no longer the default for modals.

### 9.6 Vue 3 `<Teleport>`
Vue makes the portal a declarative built-in. No import, no ref dance.

```html
<script setup lang="ts">
import { ref } from "vue";
const open = ref(false);
</script>

<template>
  <button @click="open = true">Open</button>

  <!-- Renders to #modal-root in the DOM; stays logically here for state/props. -->
  <Teleport to="#modal-root" :disabled="false">
    <div v-if="open" class="modal" role="dialog" aria-modal="true">
      <p>I'm painted at #modal-root, owned by this component.</p>
      <button @click="open = false">Close</button>
    </div>
  </Teleport>
</template>
```

Vue notes: `to` accepts a selector or an element; `:disabled` renders in place (great for responsive "portal on mobile, inline on desktop"); and Vue 3.5+ adds **`defer`** so a `<Teleport>` can target a node that mounts _later in the same render_ — the SSR/order problem React makes you solve by hand. Multiple teleports to the same target _append_ in order.

### 9.7 Angular CDK Portal / Overlay
Angular doesn't do a one-liner; it does a system. `Overlay` is the high-level API (positioning, scroll strategies, backdrop) built on `DomPortalOutlet`.

```typescript
import { Component, TemplateRef, ViewChild, inject } from "@angular/core";
import { Overlay, OverlayRef } from "@angular/cdk/overlay";
import { TemplatePortal } from "@angular/cdk/portal";
import { ViewContainerRef } from "@angular/core";

@Component({
  selector: "app-menu",
  template: `
    <button (click)="open()">Open</button>
    <ng-template #menu>
      <div class="menu" role="menu">…</div>
    </ng-template>`,
})
export class MenuComponent {
  private overlay = inject(Overlay);
  private vcr = inject(ViewContainerRef);
  @ViewChild("menu") tpl!: TemplateRef<unknown>;
  private ref?: OverlayRef;

  open() {
    this.ref = this.overlay.create({
      positionStrategy: this.overlay.position().global().centerHorizontally().top("10vh"),
      scrollStrategy: this.overlay.scrollStrategies.block(),
      hasBackdrop: true,
    });
    this.ref.attach(new TemplatePortal(this.tpl, this.vcr)); // portal into the overlay
    this.ref.backdropClick().subscribe(() => this.ref?.dispose()); // cleanup
  }
}
```

The CDK is verbose but complete: `Overlay` handles the target creation, positioning, backdrop, scroll-blocking, and disposal that you'd otherwise hand-roll around `createPortal`. If you're in Angular, don't reinvent it — `@angular/cdk/overlay` is the answer.

### 9.8 Svelte portal action
Svelte ships no portal, but the idiomatic solution is a tiny **action** — a function that runs on an element when it mounts and returns a `destroy` cleanup.

```typescript
// portal.ts
export function portal(node: HTMLElement, target: HTMLElement | string = document.body) {
  const el = typeof target === "string"
    ? document.querySelector(target) ?? document.body
    : target;
  el.appendChild(node);            // relocate on mount
  return {
    destroy() { node.remove(); },  // clean up on unmount — no leak
  };
}
```

```html
<script lang="ts">
  import { portal } from "./portal";
  let open = false;
</script>

{#if open}
  <div use:portal={"#modal-root"} class="modal" role="dialog" aria-modal="true">
    Painted at #modal-root, still owned by this component's state.
  </div>
{/if}
```

Ten lines, no dependency. The `destroy` return is the cleanup that prevents the classic leak — Svelte calls it automatically when the `{#if}` block tears down.

### 9.9 Web Components & `<template>` (the honest limitation)
There is **no portal primitive in the web-components world** — and pretending otherwise is a common mistake, so say it plainly. `<slot>` and `<template>` solve _composition_, not _relocation_:
*   **`<template>`** is inert, un-rendered markup you clone into the DOM later. It's a _stamp_, not a portal.
*   **`<slot>`** projects the host's light-DOM children _into_ a component's shadow DOM. That's the **opposite** direction of a portal (pulling content in, not pushing it out) and it stays within the element.

To actually escape a shadow boundary and mount into the top document, you manually append to an outer node — and you **lose your shadow-scoped styles and any framework context** across that boundary. That's the real tradeoff: Web Components give you encapsulation, and encapsulation is precisely what a portal has to break.

```javascript
class FloatingMenu extends HTMLElement {
  connectedCallback() {
    // Manual "portal": move the menu to a top-level root. No API does this for you.
    this._node = this.querySelector(".menu");
    document.getElementById("modal-root")?.appendChild(this._node);
  }
  disconnectedCallback() {
    this._node?.remove(); // you own cleanup entirely
  }
}
customElements.define("floating-menu", FloatingMenu);
```

For real overlays in a web-components stack, the honest answer in 2026 is **use the top layer** (`popover`, `<dialog>`) — it's framework-agnostic by design and sidesteps the shadow-boundary problem completely.

### 9.10 Solid `<Portal>`
Solid ships a first-class `<Portal>` from `solid-js/web`. By default it renders children into a fresh `<div>` appended to `document.body`; pass `mount={node}` to target an existing root, and `useShadow` to render into a style-isolating shadow root. The one real divergence from React: Solid has no synthetic event system, so events on portalled nodes bubble through the _real_ DOM tree, not a framework tree — the click-outside check from §9.4 still applies, just for the opposite reason.

```tsx
import { Portal } from "solid-js/web";
import { createSignal, Show } from "solid-js";

function Menu() {
  const [open, setOpen] = createSignal(false);
  return (
    <>
      <button onClick={() => setOpen(true)}>Open</button>
      <Show when={open()}>
        {/* mount defaults to a new div on <body>; pass an element to target a root */}
        <Portal mount={document.getElementById("modal-root")!}>
          <div class="modal" role="dialog" aria-modal="true">
            <p>Painted at #modal-root, still owned by Menu.</p>
            <button onClick={() => setOpen(false)}>Close</button>
          </div>
        </Portal>
      </Show>
    </>
  );
}
```

### 9.11 Preact `createPortal`
Preact mirrors React through `preact/compat`: identical `createPortal(children, container)` signature and the same "events follow the component tree, not the DOM tree" behavior. It's a drop-in for the React pattern, so the SSR guard and ownership-tracked cleanup of §9.2 carry over unchanged — here's the known-root variant.

```tsx
import { createPortal } from "preact/compat";

export function Toast({ message }: { message: string }) {
  const root = typeof document !== "undefined"
    ? document.getElementById("toast-root")
    : null;                                  // SSR-safe: nothing on the server
  return root
    ? createPortal(<div class="toast" role="status">{message}</div>, root)
    : null;
}
```

### 9.12 SwiftUI — the presentation-layer equivalent (honest mapping)
SwiftUI has no DOM and therefore no portal, but the same _need_ — "render this above everything, still owned by this view" — has a real native answer. `.overlay` and `ZStack` layer content _within_ the view hierarchy; the true portal analog is the presentation modifiers — `.sheet`, `.fullScreenCover`, `.popover`, `.alert` — which present in a **separate, system-managed layer** that escapes the parent's bounds the way a portal escapes `overflow: hidden`. State still flows from the owning view, mirroring context pass-through.

```swift
struct ContentView: View {
    @State private var showDialog = false
    var body: some View {
        Button("Open") { showDialog = true }
            // .sheet presents in a system layer above this view — the portal-equivalent.
            .sheet(isPresented: $showDialog) {
                VStack(spacing: 16) {
                    Text("Owned by ContentView, presented above it")
                    Button("Close") { showDialog = false }
                }
                .padding()
            }
        // For in-hierarchy layering (a badge, a custom scrim) use .overlay/ZStack instead.
    }
}
```

### 9.13 Jetpack Compose — `Popup` / `Dialog` (honest mapping)
Compose has a genuine portal-equivalent primitive. `Popup` and `Dialog` render their content in a **separate Android window** — a sub-composition rooted _outside_ the parent — so they escape the parent's clip bounds and draw above the rest of the UI, exactly the portal contract minus the DOM. CompositionLocals (Compose's context) still flow in from the caller.

```kotlin
@Composable
fun Menu() {
    var open by remember { mutableStateOf(false) }
    Button(onClick = { open = true }) { Text("Open") }
    if (open) {
        // Popup renders in its own window, above and outside the parent's clip.
        Popup(
            alignment = Alignment.Center,
            onDismissRequest = { open = false },
        ) {
            Surface(tonalElevation = 6.dp) { // still receives CompositionLocals from Menu
                Button(onClick = { open = false }) { Text("Close") }
            }
        }
    }
}
```

### 9.14 Flutter — `Overlay` / `OverlayEntry` (honest mapping)
Flutter's direct portal analog is the `Overlay`: a stack of `OverlayEntry` widgets that paint above the rest of the tree, independent of the inserting widget's position or clip. Grab the nearest overlay with `Overlay.of(context)`, insert an entry, and — like a manually-created portal root (§9.2) — **you own removal** (`entry.remove()`). `showDialog`/`showModalBottomSheet` are the batteries-included wrappers on top.

```dart
class _MenuState extends State<Menu> {
  OverlayEntry? _entry;

  void _open(BuildContext context) {
    _entry = OverlayEntry(
      builder: (_) => const Positioned(
        top: 80, left: 40,
        child: Material(child: Text('Rendered above the tree')),
      ),
    );
    Overlay.of(context).insert(_entry!); // insert into the app-wide overlay
  }

  void _close() {
    _entry?.remove(); // you own cleanup — no leak
    _entry = null;
  }

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) => ElevatedButton(
        onPressed: () => _open(context),
        child: const Text('Open'),
      );
}
```

### 9.15 React Native — `Modal` / a `Portal` host (honest mapping)
React Native has no DOM, so there is no `createPortal` — but the need is identical and the platform answer is native. `<Modal>` renders its children in a new native container above the entire app: the portal-equivalent for full-screen overlays. For tooltips and menus that must escape a parent's `overflow: 'hidden'` without a full modal, the community `Portal`/`PortalHost` libraries (react-native-paper, `@gorhom/portal`) reproduce the "render here, mount there" pattern directly.

```tsx
import { Modal, View, Text, Button } from "react-native";
import { useState } from "react";

export function ConfirmSheet() {
  const [open, setOpen] = useState(false);
  return (
    <>
      <Button title="Open" onPress={() => setOpen(true)} />
      {/* Modal mounts in a native container above the app — the RN portal analog. */}
      <Modal
        visible={open}
        transparent
        animationType="fade"
        onRequestClose={() => setOpen(false)}
      >
        <View style={{ flex: 1, justifyContent: "center", padding: 24 }}>
          <Text>Owned by this component, presented above the app</Text>
          <Button title="Close" onPress={() => setOpen(false)} />
        </View>
      </Modal>
    </>
  );
}
```

### 9.16 Testing a portal
Portals need three specific tests that non-portal components don't: that children render into the **right target**, that events still reach the **logical parent**, and that unmount **cleans up**.

```typescript
import { render, screen, cleanup } from "@testing-library/react";
import userEvent from "@testing-library/user-event";
import { describe, it, expect, vi, afterEach } from "vitest";
import { axe } from "vitest-axe";
import { Portal } from "./Portal";

afterEach(cleanup);

describe("Portal", () => {
  it("renders children into document.body, not the source location", async () => {
    const { container } = render(<Portal><button>Hi</button></Portal>);
    // Not in the component's own DOM subtree…
    expect(container.querySelector("button")).toBeNull();
    // …but reachable in the document (it went to body).
    expect(await screen.findByRole("button", { name: "Hi" })).toBeInTheDocument();
  });

  it("bubbles events to the LOGICAL parent, not the DOM parent", async () => {
    const onParentClick = vi.fn();
    render(
      <div onClick={onParentClick}>
        <Portal><button>Click</button></Portal>
      </div>
    );
    await userEvent.click(await screen.findByRole("button"));
    expect(onParentClick).toHaveBeenCalledTimes(1); // React tree bubbling proven
  });

  it("removes its mount node on unmount (no leak)", async () => {
    const { unmount } = render(<Portal><span>x</span></Portal>);
    await screen.findByText("x");
    const before = document.querySelectorAll("[data-portal]").length;
    unmount();
    expect(document.querySelectorAll("[data-portal]").length).toBe(before - 1);
  });

  it("has no automatically-detectable a11y violations", async () => {
    const { container } = render(
      <Portal>
        <div role="dialog" aria-modal="true" aria-label="Test">content</div>
      </Portal>
    );
    expect(await axe(document.body)).toHaveNoViolations(); // scan body, portal lives there
  });
});
```

What to actually assert (and skip):
*   **Do test:** children land in the target, events reach the logical parent, unmount removes the node, and `axe` runs against `document.body` (not just `container` — the portal isn't in `container`).
*   **Don't test:** the exact target node identity, inline styles, or that `createPortal` was called. Those are implementation details.
*   **Gotcha:** with Testing Library, query with `screen.*` (whole document), never `container.*` — a portal's output is not inside `container`. Half the "my portal test can't find the element" confusion is exactly this.

## 10\. Accessibility (the DOM-order trap)
Portals create a specific, dangerous a11y problem: **DOM order is no longer logical order.** A screen reader and the keyboard traverse the _DOM_, but the user's mental model follows the _interaction_. Portal a menu to `document.body` and, in the DOM, it's now the _last thing on the page_ — miles from the button that opened it. If you don't repair the relationship, assistive-tech users get lost.

The repairs, non-negotiable for interactive overlays:
*   **Preserve the trigger↔content relationship with ARIA.** DOM adjacency is gone, so re-assert the logical link: `aria-controls` (trigger → the portalled panel's id), `aria-expanded` on the trigger, and for ownership that crosses the DOM gap, **`aria-owns`** (parent → portalled child id) tells AT "this node is logically my child even though it's elsewhere in the DOM." This is the single most-skipped portal a11y step.
*   **Manage focus across the boundary.** When the overlay opens, **move focus into it** (first focusable element or the dialog container). When it closes, **return focus to the trigger** — because the DOM won't do it for you; the trigger and the panel aren't siblings anymore. Save the trigger element on open, `.focus()` it on close.
*   **Trap focus inside modal overlays.** For `aria-modal="true"` dialogs, Tab must cycle within the overlay, not escape into the page behind it. (The top layer / `<dialog>.showModal()` does this for free — another reason to prefer it.)
*   **Match reading order to visual order.** Portalling to the _end_ of `<body>` usually _helps_ here (overlay reads last, which matches "it's on top"), but a portal into an arbitrary mid-document node can put content in a nonsensical AT order. Prefer a root at the end of `<body>`.
*   **Escape and dismiss.** Esc closes overlays; document it and wire it. Click-outside must use DOM containment against the _portal node_ (see §9.4), not the logical parent.
*   **`inert`** **the background.** When a modal is open, mark the rest of the app `inert` so AT and Tab can't reach the page underneath. Pair with the portal, not instead of it.

The through-line: a portal is a _visual_ fix that _creates an accessibility gap_. `aria-owns`/`aria-controls` + focus management are how you sew the logical tree back together after the DOM tree tore it apart. Skip them and you've traded a clipping bug for a screen-reader bug — a worse trade.

## 11\. Innovative & Emerging Ideas
The portal looks like a solved primitive, but the ground under it is shifting fast — mostly _away_ from portalling:
*   **The top layer is eating the modal portal.** `popover`, `<dialog>`, and the CSS `:popover-open` / `::backdrop` stack now solve escaping ancestors _natively_, in the top layer, with no DOM relocation. In 2026 this is the default for modals and menus; portals retreat to the cases the top layer doesn't cover.
*   **Anchor Positioning API** (`anchor-name` / `position-anchor` / `@position-try`) lets a top-layer popover _stay visually attached_ to a trigger anywhere on the page — killing the last big reason to portal-then-manually-reposition. The old "portal it out, then measure and follow the anchor with JS" dance is becoming pure CSS.
*   **`inert`** **as a first-class citizen** — the attribute is now everywhere, making "portal the modal + inert the background" a clean two-step instead of a focus-trap hack.
*   **View Transitions across a portal boundary** — morphing a triggering element into its portalled overlay is now doable with the View Transitions API, so the overlay _feels_ connected to its source even though the DOM says otherwise.
*   **RSC / streaming** — in React Server Components, portals stay client-only (they touch `document`), so the pattern is "server-render the trigger, client-portal the overlay," and `Suspense` boundaries coordinate the reveal.
*   **Vue** **`defer`****, Radix** **`forceMount`** — libraries are adding primitives to solve "the target mounts later" and "keep it mounted for exit animations" declaratively, retiring another class of manual timing bugs.

**For UJG:** default overlays to the **top layer** (`<dialog>`/`popover`) over deep Eminence/Night surfaces — you get correct stacking for free and skip the portal a11y tax entirely. Keep a hand-rolled `<Portal>` in the kit for the legacy-baseline and custom-positioning cases, but treat it as the fallback, not the front line.
* * *

## 12\. UX & Correctness Killers (the silent ones)
None of these throw. They just quietly break overlays in ways that look like flakiness:
*   **Portalling on the server.** No `document` on the server → crash or hydration mismatch. Always gate behind a mounted flag (§9.2). The #1 SSR portal bug.
*   **Assuming the target exists.** `createPortal(children, document.getElementById("x"))` where `#x` isn't in the HTML → renders into `null` → crash or nothing. Verify or create the root.
*   **Leaking mount nodes.** Creating a root and never removing it leaves an orphaned `<div>` per open. A thousand opens later, a thousand dead nodes. Always clean up what you created.
*   **Click-outside using the logical parent.** Because React re-dispatches events to the logical parent (§9.4), a naive "click outside closes it" fires for clicks _inside_ the menu. Check DOM containment against the portal node.
*   **Fighting stacking with z-index instead of DOM order.** Escalating to `z-index: 999999` when the real fix is _portal to a sibling root at the end of_ _`<body>`_. If you're in a z-index arms race, you're portalling wrong.
*   **Forgetting focus return.** Overlay closes, focus vanishes to `<body>`, keyboard user is dumped at the top of the page. Save and restore the trigger.
*   **Severing the a11y relationship.** Portalled content with no `aria-owns`/`aria-controls`/focus management is invisible-in-context to screen readers. A clean-looking overlay that's an AT dead end.
*   **Portalling content that isn't an overlay.** Teleporting an inline panel or form section wrecks reading order for zero benefit. Portals are for things that _float_, not things that _flow_.

The pattern: portals fail _silently and environmentally_ — fine on your machine, broken in SSR, broken for keyboard users, broken three cards deep in production. Audit the boundary, not just the render.
* * *

## 13\. Advanced Patterns
*   **Layered roots without z-index.** Declare `#tooltip-root`, `#modal-root`, `#toast-root` as `<body>` siblings in that source order; layering falls out of the DOM order. Add a new layer by adding a sibling, never by bumping a z-index.
*   **A** **`PortalProvider`** **\+ context.** Wrap the app in a provider that owns the roots and hands portals their target via context, so components never hardcode `getElementById`. Testable (swap the target in tests), SSR-safe (provider creates roots on mount), and consistent.
*   **`disablePortal`** **responsive escape hatch.** Portal on mobile (where a bottom-sheet must cover the viewport) but render in place on desktop (where an inline popover reads better) — one `disabled` prop driven by a media query. Vue's `:disabled` and MUI's `disablePortal` exist for exactly this.
*   **Portal into a shadow root or iframe.** Pass a node inside a shadow root (or an iframe's document) as the `container` to render _into_ an encapsulated boundary — needed for embeds and micro-frontends. Watch that styles and context don't cross for free.
*   **`forceMount`** **for exit animations.** Keep the portalled node mounted while an exit transition plays, then unmount on animation end — otherwise the element vanishes before it can animate out. Radix's `forceMount` + presence detection is the clean version.
*   **TS-enforced roots.** Type the allowed targets as a union (`"modal-root" | "toast-root" | "tooltip-root"`) so a typo'd target id fails at compile time instead of silently portalling into `null` at runtime.
* * *

## 14\. Performance & Bundle Cost
A portal is cheap — it's one `appendChild` and a diff at a different node — but the surrounding overlay isn't, and a few portal-specific costs bite at scale:
*   **Node creation churn.** A lazy portal that `createElement` + `appendChild` + `removeChild` on _every_ open/close does real DOM work. For frequently-toggled overlays (tooltips on a big list), prefer a **shared persistent root** created once, and just mount/unmount the _children_ into it.
*   **Don't portal a thousand list items.** One portal per open overlay is fine; a portal per row in a virtualized 10k-row table is a mount-node explosion. Use a single shared overlay positioned to the active row instead.
*   **Reflow on position.** Portalled overlays usually need JS positioning (measure the trigger, place the panel). Measuring forces layout; batch reads then writes, and prefer the CSS **Anchor Positioning API** (§11) to skip the JS measure-and-follow loop entirely.
*   **`contain`** **the root.** `#modal-root { contain: layout style; }` isolates the overlay's internals so its reflow doesn't ripple into the app layout behind it.
*   **Top layer is cheaper than a portal.** `<dialog>`/`popover` render in the top layer with no relocation, no extra mount node, and browser-optimized painting — another reason it's the 2026 default.
*   **SSR weight is zero.** Because portals render `null` on the server, they add nothing to your HTML payload or hydration cost beyond the client component itself — a genuine upside.
* * *

## 15\. Security
A portal is a render-target trick, not a security boundary — moving a subtree to `document.body` changes _where_ it paints, never _whether_ its contents are trusted. Anything rendered through a portal is still **your** content and carries the exact same XSS and sanitization duties it would anywhere else: a portal doesn't launder untrusted HTML, so a string you'd never drop into `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` in place is just as dangerous once it's teleported. Because portals overwhelmingly host overlays, dialogs, and menus — the surfaces most likely to render user- or CMS-supplied strings — keep the portal target _inside_ your app's trust perimeter: a `#portal-root` that lives under the same document, the same Content-Security-Policy, and the same SSR/hydration boundary as `#root`. Portal into a node _outside_ your app root (a hand-appended element, a third-party container, a detached document) and you can silently escape framework-level escaping, lose CSP `nonce` inheritance for any injected `<style>`/`<script>`, and skip the sanitization your renderer would otherwise apply. Be just as clear about what a portal is _not_: rendering into `document.body` breaks CSS-based clipping and containment, but it is **not** an isolation or sandbox boundary — never reason "it's in a portal, so it's contained." And because events bubble through the framework tree, not the DOM tree (§9.4), a click handler on a logical ancestor still fires for a portalled child — so don't treat a portal as event isolation for security purposes either; a listener you assumed you'd escaped is still listening. Treat every portalled overlay with the same input-sanitization, CSP, and trust discipline as the rest of the app, because in every way that matters to security, it _is_ the rest of the app.
* * *

## 16\. Senior-Level Checklist (ship-ready)
Before a portal is "done":
- [ ] Renders `null` on the server and the first client render (SSR-safe, no hydration mismatch).
- [ ] Target verified to exist, or created safely, before `createPortal` runs.
- [ ] Cleanup removes any mount node the component **created** (and never one the host owns).
- [ ] Click-outside checks DOM containment against the **portal node**, not the logical parent.
- [ ] Layering done with DOM-ordered sibling roots, not escalating `z-index`.
- [ ] Focus moves **into** the overlay on open and **returns to the trigger** on close.
- [ ] `aria-owns` / `aria-controls` / `aria-expanded` preserve the trigger↔content relationship across the DOM gap.
- [ ] Modal overlays trap focus and `inert` the background.
- [ ] Esc closes; dismiss paths documented.
- [ ] Considered the **top layer** (`<dialog>`/`popover`) first, and portalled only because it was genuinely needed.
- [ ] Tests cover: renders into target, events reach logical parent, unmount cleans up, `axe` scans `document.body`.

## 17\. Visual Styles (the overlay a portal carries, eleven skins)
A portal has no look of its own — it's a mechanism. But the overlay it _delivers_ wears a skin, and the point of this section holds: **style is skin, behavior is skeleton.** No matter which of the eleven a portalled dialog wears, its target, focus management, `aria-owns` link, Esc handling, and 44px targets are identical. The skin never touches the mechanism. See them live in the 🎨 Design Styles (visual languages) — [https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551](https://app.clickup.com/8495850/docs/838qa-81211/838qa-200551).
*   **Flat:** solid backdrop scrim, crisp-edged panel, no shadow. Reads instantly, cheapest to paint.
*   **Material:** elevated surface (24dp for dialogs), scrim at ~32% black, motion on enter/exit. The portal root is where that elevation finally stacks correctly.
*   **Glassmorphism:** translucent panel with `backdrop-filter: blur()` over the page behind it — a look that _depends_ on the portal sitting above real content. Guard contrast; text on frosted glass fails AA fast.
*   **Liquid Glass (the 2026 trend):** Apple's iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe material — refractive, specular-rimmed, with a moving sheen that reacts to what's behind it. This is _the_ style the top layer was built to render; on the web it's an honest approximation, in SwiftUI it's native `.glassEffect()`. Overlays are its natural home. Guard for `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Neumorphism:** same-surface dual shadows. Weak for overlays (needs contrast a floating panel can't afford); accent only.
*   **Skeuomorphism:** beveled, gradient-filled "real object" panel — a physical dialog card. Heavy but tactile.
*   **Neo-Brutalism:** hard offset shadow, thick border, zero radius on the panel, loud scrim. High personality for campaign overlays.
*   **Claymorphism:** big radius, soft inner light, puffy friendly dialog. Reads approachable.
*   **Aurora / Gradient:** animated multi-hue panel or scrim on dark. Premium; honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*   **Minimal / Swiss:** near-zero decoration, type and grid carry the panel, sharp corners, thin scrim.
*   **UJG Brand:** Goldenrod accents and rim on a deep Eminence/Night panel over a dimmed scrim, confident radius, warm glow — the Afro-Futurist house overlay. The dark, saturated backdrop is exactly where a Liquid-Glass rim sings, so lean the brand overlay toward that in 2026.

The rule that never changes: swap all eleven skins and the portal underneath — its target, its cleanup, its focus return, its `aria-owns` — does not move a pixel. The skeleton is the same in every skin.
* * *

**Bottom line:** a portal is a _mechanism_, not a component — it changes a subtree's DOM address without changing its logical parentage, and that single move is what lets overlays escape the `overflow`/`z-index`/`transform` traps that no child-side CSS can fix. Master the three surprises — events bubble through the _React_ tree, the target must exist _before_ you render (SSR guard), and you own the _cleanup_ — then sew the accessibility tree back together with `aria-owns` and focus management, and reach for the browser **top layer** before you reach for a portal at all. Get those right and every Overlay and Modal you build on top of it stands on solid ground.
