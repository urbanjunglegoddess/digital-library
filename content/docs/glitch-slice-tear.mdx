## Glitch Slice Tear

The tear. Horizontal bands of a headline shear sideways in hard steps, misregister, and snap
flush — a tracking fault in a signal that corrects itself. It is the most aggressive of the three
glitch variants and, paradoxically, the cheapest and safest to ship.

Everything about it follows from one fact: **the real text is the thing being animated.** There is
no substitute string, no second node, no per-frame JavaScript. `clip-path` chooses a band,
`transform` displaces it, `text-shadow` carries the chroma, and none of those three properties
affects layout. That makes the accessibility contract almost free — and moves the real engineering
somewhere its siblings never go: into **timing as a conformance problem**, because hard `steps()`
at high amplitude is how you accidentally build a flash hazard.

Its siblings: [Scramble Reveal](/knowledge/glitch-scramble-reveal) substitutes glyphs and earns
its JavaScript; [Signal Lock](/knowledge/glitch-signal-lock) trades the tear for blur and is the
restrained option.

* * *

## 1. What It Actually Is

A slice tear is a **one-shot clip-and-displace animation** over a single run of text. At each
keyframe, `clip-path: inset()` reveals one horizontal band of the element and hides the rest, and
`transform: translateX()` offsets what remains. Stepping through a sequence of band-and-offset
pairs reads as the text being sliced into strips that slide independently, even though there is only
one element and one string.

Two properties define it. It is **non-destructive**: the glyphs are never replaced, so selection,
search, zoom, print and the accessibility tree are untouched throughout. And it is
**layout-neutral**: `clip-path` and `transform` do not participate in layout, so the element's box
never changes and nothing around it moves.

What it gets confused with:

* **Scramble / decode.** Substitutes characters. The visible glyphs differ from the real ones,
  which creates an accessibility-tree problem slice tear simply does not have. If you are
  rewriting text content, you are building the other variant.
* **The layered RGB-split glitch (`::before` / `::after` with `attr(data-text)`).** The classic
  CodePen glitch: three stacked copies of the text, two of them generated content, each clipped and
  offset. Visually a cousin, structurally very different — and it carries a real accessibility cost
  that the single-element approach avoids entirely (§6, §10). Worth knowing precisely because it is
  the implementation most people copy.
* **Wipe / reveal-on-scroll.** A single `clip-path` animating monotonically from hidden to shown.
  Same property, opposite intent: a wipe *reveals* content that starts invisible, so a stalled
  animation leaves nothing on screen. A slice tear starts and ends fully visible and only
  misregisters in between.
* **Skew / shear transform.** A continuous geometric distortion of the whole element. No banding,
  no independent strips, no discontinuity — which is exactly what makes a tear read as digital
  rather than physical.
* **Datamosh / pixel-sort imagery.** A raster effect on pixels, usually an image or video filter.
  A slice tear is vector text and stays text.

**Rule of thumb:** if the glyphs are unchanged, the box never moves, and the only things animating
are which band is visible and how far it is offset — it is a slice tear. If content is replaced, it
is a scramble. If it starts invisible, it is a wipe, and it needs a different safety argument.

* * *

## 2. Why It Matters

* **Flash hazard is the real conformance risk, and it is specific to this variant.** Hard
  `steps()` timing plus high displacement plus a chroma split produces rapid, high-contrast
  changes in a confined area. WCAG 2.3.1 caps flashing at three per second; a tear authored at 8
  steps per second across a 58px headline with a black-to-saturated-orange swing can cross the
  general flash threshold. Scramble and signal lock interpolate and rarely get near it. Here the
  timing function *is* a safety decision (§4, §10).
* **It is the only glitch that ships where scripting does not.** Wix Studio scroll effects, an
  email-safe build, a CSS-only static site, a hardened CSP with no inline script, a Web Component
  consumer that refuses your JS — slice tear works in all of them, and with
  `animation-timeline: view()` even its *trigger* needs no script. On a platform without real
  JavaScript this variant is not the compromise; it is the answer.
* **Zero accessibility debt by construction, if you keep it single-element.** The real text is the
  animated thing, so the accessible name is correct at every frame with no `aria-hidden`, no
  clipped duplicate, no two-node contract. The moment you reach for the generated-content trick
  (`content: attr(data-text)` on a pseudo-element) you reintroduce a problem you did not have,
  because CSS generated content is
  exposed in the accessibility tree on several engines — so the heading can be announced three
  times (§10).
* **Zero layout cost, which is not the same as zero paint cost.** `clip-path` and `transform`
  never reflow, so CLS contribution is genuinely zero. But animating `clip-path` is not reliably
  compositor-accelerated the way `transform` and `opacity` are: on several engines a changing clip
  region triggers paint each frame. At headline scale that is cheap and invisible; applied to a
  large element or several at once it is measurable (§14). Honest framing matters here, because
  "clip-path is GPU-accelerated" is repeated far more often than it is true.
* **Restarting a CSS animation is a genuine problem.** Unlike a JS loop, you cannot just call it
  again. A replay needs the reflow hack, an alternating animation name, or the Web Animations API —
  and the naive version silently does nothing (§12, §13).

* * *

## 3. Anatomy

* **Host element.** The semantic element the tear is applied to — `h1`…`h6`, `p`, or a `span`
  inside one. Needs `display: inline-block` (or `block`) so the clip region has a box to work
  against; an inline element's fragmented box clips unpredictably across line breaks.
* **Truth node.** The single `span` holding the real text and carrying the animation. There is no
  second node. Deleting the animation leaves a correctly set headline, because the animation was
  only ever decoration on this element.
* **Band set.** The sequence of `inset()` regions over time. Each band is defined by its top and
  bottom insets as percentages of the element's height — so the geometry is resolution- and
  font-size-independent, and the same keyframes serve every size tier.
* **Displacement amplitude.** The `translateX` distance per band, driven by `--glitch-amplitude`.
  Scales with type size at roughly 0.18em, capped at 12px (§4).
* **Chroma pair.** Two offset colour channels producing the RGB-split signature, applied with
  `text-shadow` so they never affect layout: `--glitch-chroma-a` (Spanish Orange) and
  `--glitch-chroma-b` (Eminence). Tokens, never raw red/cyan.
* **Step function.** The `steps(n, end)` easing that makes bands snap rather than glide. The
  signature of the variant and the component's main safety lever: step count over duration is the
  flash rate.
* **Settle state.** `clip-path: none`, `transform: none`, `text-shadow: none`, declared in base CSS
  rather than only at the keyframe's `100%` stop. The safety net for a stalled, stripped or printed
  render.
* **Scanline.** Optional bright band travelling the line once per pass. Positioned, `aria-hidden`,
  `pointer-events: none`.
* **Trigger.** `animation-timeline: view()` (no script), a `data-glitch-state` attribute toggled by
  a tiny observer, or mount via a plain `animation` declaration. The scroll-driven path is the
  distinguishing one.
* **Restart mechanism.** What makes a replay actually re-run: alternating keyframe names, a forced
  reflow, or `Animation.play()` from `getAnimations()`. Needed only if you offer a replay.
* **Reduced-motion branch.** `animation: none`, and the scanline removed. Not a softening.

## 4. Sizes / Scale / Density

Slice tear is sized by type size, and what scales with it is amplitude, chroma offset, and — the
part that is easy to get wrong — **step count**. More steps at the same duration means a higher
flash rate, so the step count is capped rather than scaled up with drama.

| Tier | Token | Type size | Amplitude | Chroma offset | Steps | Duration | Discrete changes | Use case |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Inline | `--glitch-inline` | 14–16px / `0.875–1rem` | 2px | 1px | 2 | 420ms | ~20/s | One accent word; hover jitter on nav items |
| SM | `--glitch-sm` | 20–24px / `1.25–1.5rem` | 4px | 1px | 2 | 600ms | ~20/s | Card titles, eyebrows, sub-heads |
| MD | `--glitch-md` | 32–40px / `2–2.5rem` | 7px | 2px | 2 | 800ms | ~20/s | Section headings, page titles |
| LG | `--glitch-lg` | 48–58px / `3–3.625rem` | 10px | 3px | 2 | 900ms | ~20/s | Hero H1 — the house default |
| XL | `--glitch-xl` | 72px+ / `4.5rem+` | 12px (cap) | 3px | 2 | 1100ms | ~20/s | Full-bleed statement type |

Rules that matter:

* **`steps(2, end)` is the house ceiling, and it is a safety limit, not a taste one.** With nine
  keyframe stops over 900ms, two steps per interval produces about **20 discrete visual changes per
  second** — measured in this page's playground, not estimated. Note what that number is and is
  not: it counts changes in clip band, position and chroma, which is the rate a screening tool can
  actually measure. WCAG 2.3.1's flash test is narrower and stricter in a different way: it asks
  whether *relative luminance* changes by at least 10% of maximum, in opposing pairs, more than
  three times a second, over at least 25% of a 10-degree area of the visual field. Nothing in the
  DOM can determine that, because it depends on the rendered pixels, the background behind them,
  and how much of the viewport the headline occupies. So treat the discrete-change rate as a
  **screen, not a verdict**: at or below 3/s you are safe by construction, and above it — which
  includes the house default — you are relying on the fact that the band clips move glyph position
  far more than they move area luminance. Keep the step count at 2, and if you raise it, or put a
  hard black-to-saturated chroma on a full-bleed hero, get a real luminance assessment rather than
  trusting the meter. If you want more violence, raise the amplitude.
* **Amplitude scales, step count does not.** 0.18em, capped at 12px. A 10px tear is a violent rip
  on 16px copy and a polite wobble at 96px.
* **Band geometry is percentage-based**, so one keyframe set covers every tier and every viewport.
  Never author bands in pixels.
* **Duration ceiling 1200ms.** Past that it reads as a broken page rather than a flourish.
* **`display: inline-block` is required** on the animated node. An inline element that wraps has
  multiple box fragments and the clip applies per fragment — the tear breaks apart at the line
  break in a way that looks like a rendering bug.
* **One per viewport.** Two simultaneous tears read as a GPU fault.
* **Target size is unaffected** — the text is not a target. Any replay control carries 44px on its
  own.

## 5. States

* **Idle / settled.** `clip-path: none`, `transform: none`, `text-shadow: none`, from base CSS.
  What a crawler, a printer, a no-JS visitor, a reduced-motion user and a stalled timeline all get.
  Fully readable — never invisible. For this variant idle and settled are the *same declarative
  state*, which is why it has no stall problem worth engineering around.
* **Armed.** The animation is declared but not running: either the state attribute is absent, or
  `animation-timeline: view()` has not reached its range. Visually identical to idle.
* **Tearing (active pass).** The keyframes are running. `data-glitch-state="glitching"` is on the
  host so the scanline and any sibling decoration can sync without a second timer.
* **Scrubbing.** Unique to this variant: with `animation-timeline: view()` the pass is not "running"
  but *positioned* by scroll offset, so it can run backwards when the visitor scrolls up. Worth
  naming because it changes the mental model — there is no completion event, and `once` is
  meaningless. If you need the tear to fire exactly once, use the state-attribute trigger instead.
* **Hover (optional micro-pass).** A short tear or chroma jitter on pointer-enter for links and nav
  items. Pointer-only by design, scoped to `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` — it must
  **not** fire on `:focus-visible`, or a keyboard user tears the UI at every tab stop.
* **Focus.** The text is not focusable and has no focus state. When the tear sits inside an
  interactive element, the focus ring belongs to that host, must be unaffected by the clip, and must
  never be the thing being animated — a focus ring that tears is a ring you cannot locate. Note the
  specific hazard here: `clip-path` on an ancestor **clips the descendant's focus ring too**, so
  never put the animation on a wrapper that contains the interactive element.
* **Active / pressed.** Only on a replay control; standard press treatment, no tear on the button.
* **Disabled.** Not applicable to the text. A replay control is disabled while a pass is in flight,
  which prevents overlapping animations.
* **Loading / error.** Not applicable: the text is known before render, and there is no runtime to
  fail. The "stall" failure mode that scramble engineers against does not exist here — a dropped
  CSS animation lands on the settle state by definition, because the settle state is the base state.
* **Reduced-motion.** First-class: `animation: none`, scanline removed from the DOM, text renders
  final. No softer tear, no slower tear.
* **Print.** Settle state, with `text-shadow` explicitly cleared so the heading does not print with
  orange and purple ghosts.

## 6. Types / Variants

1. **Single-element tear (the house default).** One node, `clip-path` plus `transform` plus
   `text-shadow`. Zero duplicates, zero accessibility debt, works on any text, and the only version
   that can be dropped onto existing markup without touching the DOM. Start here.
2. **Layered RGB-split tear (`::before` / `::after`).** The classic: two generated copies via
   `content: attr(data-text)`, each clipped to different bands and offset in a different channel
   colour, producing true channel separation rather than a shadow approximation. Visually the
   richest version — and it **costs you the accessibility freebie**: generated content is exposed
   in the accessibility tree on several engines, so the heading can be announced two or three times.
   If you use it, the copies need `aria-hidden` on real elements instead of pseudo-elements, which
   means you are back to a multi-node contract. Documented because it is the implementation most
   people copy; not the house default for exactly this reason.
3. **Vertical tear.** Bands run vertically (`inset()` with left/right insets) and displace on
   `translateY`. Reads as a column-wise fault; suits tall stacked type and vertical scripts. Less
   familiar, so it reads as more deliberate.
4. **Band-count variants.** Coarse (2–3 wide bands, slow, architectural) versus fine (6–8 narrow
   bands, fast, frantic). Coarse is the premium register; fine is campaign energy. The step rate
   stays capped either way — add bands, not steps.
5. **Chroma on / off.** The RGB split is separable from the displacement. Off, the tear reads as a
   pure tracking error, which is the right call on light backgrounds where the offsets muddy and
   for any skin whose contrast is already marginal.
6. **Scroll-scrubbed tear.** `animation-timeline: view()` ties band position to scroll offset.
   Reversible, zero JavaScript, zero main-thread cost, and the only glitch in the family that is
   genuinely free. Behaves differently from a one-shot (see Scrubbing in §5).
7. **Hover tear.** A short pass on pointer-enter for links and labels. Pointer-only, never
   `:focus-visible`. For a jitter with no displacement, use the chroma micro-variant on the
   [family page](/knowledge/glitch-text-reveal).

## 7. When to Use (and When Not To)

**Use when:**

* You want the loudest, most unmistakably digital glitch in the family, and the brand register can
  carry it — a hero H1, a manifesto line, a launch page's single sentence.
* **The platform has no real scripting**, or you want zero JavaScript on principle: Wix Studio,
  a CSS-only static site, a strict CSP, an email-adjacent build. This is the variant that works
  there.
* The headline is long, multi-line, or localised into a script you have not individually tested —
  the tear is content-agnostic, so it cannot corrupt glyphs, split emoji, or render tofu. Where
  scramble needs per-script verification, slice tear does not care what the characters are.
* You need the effect to be reversible or scroll-scrubbed rather than one-shot.
* Per-frame main-thread cost must be zero (a page already heavy with hydration).

**Don't use / use something else when:**

* **You want the "word assembling" read.** That is [Scramble](/knowledge/glitch-scramble-reveal) —
  a tear corrupts position, never meaning, so nothing is ever *decoded*.
* **The glitch must coexist with serious content.** Use
  [Signal Lock](/knowledge/glitch-signal-lock). A hard tear reads as a fault; blur reads as focus.
* **The audience may be photosensitive or the context is high-anxiety** — medical, checkout,
  accessibility-first products. This is the variant most able to cross a flash threshold if
  mis-authored. Use fade, or nothing.
* **It is body copy, a label, a number, a form field, an error or a confirmation.** Errors must
  never animate — use **Alert**.
* **The animated node wraps an interactive element.** `clip-path` on an ancestor clips the
  descendant's focus ring. Move the animation onto a text-only span inside.
* **You need true channel separation and cannot accept a multi-node accessibility contract.**
  Either take the `text-shadow` approximation or accept real `aria-hidden` duplicate elements —
  but do not use `attr(data-text)` pseudo-elements and assume they are invisible to assistive tech.
* **It would be the second glitch in view.** Use a plain **Reveal on scroll** for the rest.

**Placement heuristics.** One per viewport, display type ≥32px, `display: inline-block` on the
animated node, fired on in-view (or scroll-scrubbed). Keep everything around it still — the tear
only reads as deliberate against stillness. Prefer the coarse band set at hero scale and the fine
set only for campaign work.

## 8. Across Design Systems

No major design system ships a tear, and for this one the absence is almost ideological: every
mainstream system's motion doctrine is about *continuity*, and a tear is a deliberate
discontinuity.

* **Material 3 (Google).** No tear, and the most direct philosophical conflict in the family. M3
  Expressive motion is spring-based and physical — motion shows where a thing came from, and a
  band that jumps sideways came from nowhere. A Material-faithful build keeps the settle easing and
  drops the discontinuity, which means it stops being a tear. What M3 does give you is the token
  discipline and the rule that decorative motion must be removable.
* **Apple HIG.** Against it, and specifically so: HIG asks motion to be brief, purposeful and
  physically plausible. There is no tear idiom on Apple platforms, and the 2026 Liquid Glass
  language is refractive and continuous — the exact opposite register. On Apple surfaces, prefer
  signal lock.
* **Fluent 2 (Microsoft).** No tear; Fluent's motion is subtle, directional and enterprise-calibrated
  with an explicit "must not distract" line. Its contribution here is the curve and duration
  taxonomy — map the duration onto its slow tier rather than inventing one.
* **Ant Design.** No tear, and the clearest statement of where this does not belong: fade, slide,
  zoom, for dense product UI where decorative motion is a liability.
* **Tailwind CSS.** No component, but the best fit of any system — because this variant is *only*
  CSS, Tailwind can express the whole thing: custom `@keyframes` in `@theme` (v4) or the config
  (v3), `animate-[…]` to apply it, and `motion-reduce:animate-none` for the compliance story in one
  class. Unlike scramble, a Tailwind-only slice tear is genuinely complete (§9.5).
* **shadcn/ui + Radix.** No primitive, and notably this variant barely needs the headless pattern:
  with no lifecycle to own, a `cva` skin plus `Slot` is the entire component (§9.7). That is a
  feature — the simplest variant produces the simplest primitive.
* **Bootstrap 5.** No tear, thin animation layer, so this is a utility class beside `display-1`
  plus a few lines to handle restart. Honest about being an add-on.
* **Creative-web tooling.** GSAP can drive `clipPath` keyframes and `SplitText` can band real
  text, but for this variant the library buys you almost nothing — the CSS is shorter, cheaper and
  does not ship 40KB. This is the one glitch where the tooling ecosystem is a step backwards, and
  where the hand-written CSS is the professional choice rather than the scrappy one.

### 8b. Visual Styles / Trends — What's trending in 2026

Three platform changes have made this variant materially better than its 2021 ancestor. **Scroll-driven
animations** (`animation-timeline: view()`, broadly supported) make the tear scrubbable and
JS-free end to end, which is new — a glitch that resolves as the headline enters the viewport with
nothing on the main thread. **`@property`** lets `--glitch-amplitude` be registered as a `<length>`
so displacement interpolates instead of stepping, which means one keyframe set can serve every
tier by animating the token. And **View Transitions**, now cross-document, make a chromatic tear a
natural custom transition *between routes* rather than an entrance on one element.

The aesthetic has narrowed usefully. The 2021 look was the layered `attr(data-text)` RGB split
running infinitely on everything; the 2026 register is **one coarse, short, single-element pass**,
often with the chroma dialled to 1–2px and the band count low. The infinite version now reads as
dated, and — more to the point — as accessibility-naive, since it is both a 2.2.2 failure past five
seconds and the easiest way in the family to build a flash hazard. The durable version tears once
and gets out of the way.

## 9. The Code

The contract: one node holding real text, animation on `clip-path` / `transform` / `text-shadow`
only, settle state in base CSS, percentage-based bands, step rate capped, and reduced motion as a
branch that declares no animation at all.

### 9.1 HTML (semantic foundation with ARIA)

```html
<!-- One node. Nothing to hide from assistive tech, nothing to duplicate.
     inline-block matters: an inline element that wraps clips per box fragment. -->
<h1 class="glitch" data-glitch="slice" data-glitch-size="lg" data-glitch-trigger="scroll">
  <span class="glitch__truth">Built like you.</span>
  <span class="glitch__scan" aria-hidden="true"></span>
</h1>

<!-- Optional replay. A real button: typed, labelled, keyboard-operable, 44px target.
     Omit on a production hero; it belongs in playgrounds and style pickers. -->
<button type="button" class="glitch__replay" data-glitch-replay="hero-title"
        aria-describedby="glitch-help">
  Replay tear
</button>
<p id="glitch-help" class="u-visually-hidden">
  Replays the decorative animation on the heading. The heading text does not change.
</p>

<!-- Tear inside a link: the anchor owns role, name and focus ring. The animated span is
     text-only and does NOT wrap the interactive element — clip-path on an ancestor would
     clip the focus ring. -->
<a href="/suites" class="nav__link">
  <span class="glitch" data-glitch="slice" data-glitch-trigger="hover" data-glitch-size="inline">
    <span class="glitch__truth">Five service suites</span>
  </span>
</a>
```

### 9.2 CSS (states, layout, reduced-motion)

```css
.glitch[data-glitch="slice"] {
  --glitch-duration: 900ms;
  --glitch-amplitude: 10px;
  --glitch-chroma: 3px;
  --glitch-chroma-a: var(--ember, #d9531a);
  --glitch-chroma-b: var(--amethyst, #47107d);
  --glitch-steps: steps(2, end);     /* house ceiling — a safety limit, see §4 */

  position: relative;
  display: inline-block;             /* the clip region needs an unfragmented box */
}

.glitch[data-glitch="slice"] .glitch__truth {
  display: inline-block;
  /* THE SETTLE STATE. Everything the element looks like with no animation at all.
     Note the absence of opacity: a stalled, stripped or printed pass still renders
     a correct headline, merely un-torn. */
  clip-path: none;
  transform: none;
  text-shadow: none;
}
```

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (full reusable component)

Because there is no runtime to own, this component is mostly markup plus a trigger. The hook
exists only to toggle a state attribute — and on a browser with `animation-timeline` support and
`trigger="scroll"`, it does nothing at all.

```tsx
"use client";

import * as React from "react";

export type SliceSize = "inline" | "sm" | "md" | "lg" | "xl";
export type SliceTrigger = "mount" | "in-view" | "scroll" | "hover" | "manual";

export function usePrefersReducedMotion(): boolean {
  const [reduced, setReduced] = React.useState(false);
  React.useEffect(() => {
    const mq = window.matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)");
    setReduced(mq.matches);
    const onChange = (e: MediaQueryListEvent) => setReduced(e.matches);
    mq.addEventListener("change", onChange);
    return () => mq.removeEventListener("change", onChange);
  }, []);
  return reduced;
}

/** Toggling between two states swaps the animation NAME, which is what actually restarts
 *  a CSS animation in React — no reflow hack, no key churn, no remount. */
type PassState = "idle" | "glitching" | "glitching-alt";

interface UseSliceOptions {
  trigger?: SliceTrigger;
  once?: boolean;
  duration?: number;
}

export function useSlice({ trigger = "in-view", once = true, duration = 900 }: UseSliceOptions) {
  const reduced = usePrefersReducedMotion();
  const hostRef = React.useRef<HTMLElement | null>(null);
  const [state, setState] = React.useState<PassState>("idle");
  const spent = React.useRef(false);
  const flip = React.useRef(false);
  const settleRef = React.useRef<ReturnType<typeof setTimeout> | null>(null);

  /* With scroll-driven support the CSS owns everything — declare nothing and return idle. */
  const cssOwnsIt =
    trigger === "scroll" &&
    typeof CSS !== "undefined" &&
    CSS.supports?.("animation-timeline: view()");

  const run = React.useCallback(() => {
    if (reduced || cssOwnsIt) return;
    if (once && spent.current) return;
    spent.current = true;
    flip.current = !flip.current;
    setState(flip.current ? "glitching" : "glitching-alt");
    if (settleRef.current) clearTimeout(settleRef.current);
    settleRef.current = setTimeout(() => setState("idle"), duration + 120);
  }, [cssOwnsIt, duration, once, reduced]);

  const replay = React.useCallback(() => { spent.current = false; run(); }, [run]);

  React.useEffect(() => () => { if (settleRef.current) clearTimeout(settleRef.current); }, []);

  React.useEffect(() => {
    if (reduced || cssOwnsIt || trigger === "manual" || trigger === "hover") return;
    if (trigger === "mount") { run(); return; }
    const el = hostRef.current;
    if (!el) return;
    if (typeof IntersectionObserver === "undefined") { run(); return; }
    const io = new IntersectionObserver(
      (entries) => {
        for (const entry of entries) {
          if (!entry.isIntersecting) continue;
          run();
          if (once) io.disconnect();
        }
      },
      { threshold: 0.35 }
    );
    io.observe(el);
    return () => io.disconnect();
  }, [cssOwnsIt, once, reduced, run, trigger]);

  return { hostRef, state, reduced, cssOwnsIt, run, replay };
}

type OwnProps<T extends React.ElementType> = {
  children: React.ReactNode;     // unlike scramble, ANY node is fine: nothing is rewritten
  as?: T;
  size?: SliceSize;
  trigger?: SliceTrigger;
  duration?: number;
  once?: boolean;
  orientation?: "horizontal" | "vertical";
  scanline?: boolean;
  className?: string;
};

export type GlitchSliceProps<T extends React.ElementType> = OwnProps<T> &
  Omit<React.ComponentPropsWithoutRef<T>, keyof OwnProps<T>>;

export function GlitchSlice<T extends React.ElementType = "span">({
  children, as, size = "lg", trigger = "in-view", duration = 900, once = true,
  orientation = "horizontal", scanline = true, className, ...rest
}: GlitchSliceProps<T>) {
  const Host = (as ?? "span") as React.ElementType;
  const { hostRef, state, reduced } = useSlice({ trigger, once, duration });

  return (
    <Host
      ref={hostRef as React.Ref<never>}
      className={["glitch", className].filter(Boolean).join(" ")}
      data-glitch="slice"
      data-glitch-size={size}
      data-glitch-state={state}
      data-glitch-trigger={trigger}
      data-glitch-orientation={orientation}
      style={{ ["--glitch-duration" as string]: `${duration}ms` }}
      {...rest}
    >
      {/* The real text IS the animated element. One node, no duplicate, no aria-hidden. */}
      <span className="glitch__truth">{children}</span>
      {scanline && !reduced ? <span className="glitch__scan" aria-hidden="true" /> : null}
    </Host>
  );
}

GlitchSlice.displayName = "GlitchSlice";
```

### 9.4 Vanilla JavaScript (no framework, full keyboard)

```js
/** glitch-slice.js — OPTIONAL. The markup and CSS work with this file absent; all it adds
 *  is a one-shot in-view trigger and replay for browsers without animation-timeline. */
const reduced = () => window.matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)").matches;
const cssOwnsIt = () => CSS.supports?.("animation-timeline: view()");

export class GlitchSlice {
  constructor(host, options = {}) {
    this.host = host;
    this.duration = Number(options.duration ?? host.dataset.glitchDuration ?? 900);
    this.once = options.once !== false;
    this.spent = false;
    this.flip = false;
    this.settle = null;

    const trigger = host.dataset.glitchTrigger || "in-view";
    if (reduced()) return;
    if (trigger === "scroll" && cssOwnsIt()) return;   // nothing to do; CSS has it
    if (trigger === "manual") return;
    if (trigger === "hover") {
      host.addEventListener("pointerenter", () => { this.spent = false; this.run(); });
      return;
    }
    if (trigger === "mount" || !("IntersectionObserver" in window)) { this.run(); return; }

    this.io = new IntersectionObserver((entries) => {
      for (const entry of entries) {
        if (!entry.isIntersecting) continue;
        this.run();
        if (this.once) this.io.disconnect();
      }
    }, { threshold: 0.35 });
    this.io.observe(host);
  }

  run() {
    if (reduced()) return;
    if (this.once && this.spent) return;
    this.spent = true;
    if (this.settle) clearTimeout(this.settle);

    /* Swapping the state swaps the animation NAME. Re-applying the SAME name does
       nothing — the single most common slice-tear bug (§12). */
    this.flip = !this.flip;
    this.host.dataset.glitchState = this.flip ? "glitching" : "glitching-alt";
    this.settle = setTimeout(() => { this.host.dataset.glitchState = "idle"; }, this.duration + 120);
  }

  /** Alternative restart with no duplicate keyframes, where support allows. */
  runViaWAAPI() {
    if (reduced()) return;
    const target = this.host.querySelector(".glitch__truth");
    if (!target?.getAnimations) return this.run();
    this.host.dataset.glitchState = "glitching";
    for (const animation of target.getAnimations()) {
      animation.cancel();
      animation.play();
    }
  }

  destroy() { if (this.settle) clearTimeout(this.settle); this.io?.disconnect(); }
}

const registry = new Map();
for (const host of document.querySelectorAll('[data-glitch="slice"]')) {
  const instance = new GlitchSlice(host);
  if (host.id) registry.set(host.id, instance);
}
/* A real <button> needs no key handling: Enter and Space fire click natively. */
for (const control of document.querySelectorAll("[data-glitch-replay]")) {
  control.addEventListener("click", () => {
    const instance = registry.get(control.dataset.glitchReplay);
    if (!instance) return;
    instance.spent = false;
    instance.run();
  });
}
```

```ts
  AfterViewInit, ChangeDetectionStrategy, Component, DestroyRef, ElementRef,
  Input, OnDestroy, inject, signal,
} from "@angular/core";
import { NgIf } from "@angular/common";

@Component({
  selector: "ujg-glitch-slice",
  standalone: true,
  imports: [NgIf],
  changeDetection: ChangeDetectionStrategy.OnPush,
  template: `
    <span
      class="glitch"
      data-glitch="slice"
      [attr.data-glitch-size]="size"
      [attr.data-glitch-state]="state()"
      [attr.data-glitch-trigger]="trigger"
      [style.--glitch-duration.ms]="duration"
    >
      <span class="glitch__truth"><ng-content></ng-content></span>
      <span *ngIf="!reduced" class="glitch__scan" aria-hidden="true"></span>
    </span>
  `,
})
export class GlitchSliceComponent implements AfterViewInit, OnDestroy {
  @Input() size: "inline" | "sm" | "md" | "lg" | "xl" = "lg";
  @Input() duration = 900;
  @Input() trigger: "mount" | "in-view" | "scroll" | "hover" | "manual" = "in-view";
  @Input() once = true;

  private readonly host = inject(ElementRef<HTMLElement>);
  private readonly destroyRef = inject(DestroyRef);

  readonly state = signal<"idle" | "glitching" | "glitching-alt">("idle");
  reduced = false;

  private io?: IntersectionObserver;
  private settle: ReturnType<typeof setTimeout> | null = null;
  private spent = false;
  private flip = false;

  /** No per-frame work anywhere in this component — so no zone concerns, unlike scramble. */
  private get cssOwnsIt(): boolean {
    return this.trigger === "scroll" && CSS.supports?.("animation-timeline: view()");
  }

  ngAfterViewInit(): void {
    this.reduced = matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)").matches;
    if (this.reduced || this.cssOwnsIt || this.trigger === "manual") return;
    if (this.trigger === "hover") {
      this.host.nativeElement.addEventListener("pointerenter", () => {
        this.spent = false;
        this.run();
      });
      return;
    }
    if (this.trigger === "mount" || !("IntersectionObserver" in window)) { this.run(); return; }

    this.io = new IntersectionObserver((entries) => {
      for (const e of entries) if (e.isIntersecting) { this.run(); if (this.once) this.io?.disconnect(); }
    }, { threshold: 0.35 });
    this.io.observe(this.host.nativeElement);
    this.destroyRef.onDestroy(() => this.io?.disconnect());
  }

  run(): void {
    if (this.reduced || this.cssOwnsIt) return;
    if (this.once && this.spent) return;
    this.spent = true;
    if (this.settle) clearTimeout(this.settle);
    this.flip = !this.flip;
    this.state.set(this.flip ? "glitching" : "glitching-alt");
    this.settle = setTimeout(() => this.state.set("idle"), this.duration + 120);
  }

  ngOnDestroy(): void { if (this.settle) clearTimeout(this.settle); this.io?.disconnect(); }
}
```

### 9.13 Python (Jinja2 rendering + FastAPI endpoint)

The server's only job here is the headline copy and the size tier — there is no glyph pool, no
runtime, and nothing derived. That makes this the thinnest server surface in the family, and the
validation is correspondingly simple: bound the inputs, reject text that can lie about itself.

```python
# app/slice.py
from __future__ import annotations

import unicodedata
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Final, Literal

from fastapi import APIRouter, HTTPException
from fastapi.responses import HTMLResponse
from jinja2 import Environment, select_autoescape
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field, field_validator

Size = Literal["inline", "sm", "md", "lg", "xl"]
Orientation = Literal["horizontal", "vertical"]

# Step count is a SAFETY limit, not a style knob: step rate over duration is the flash
# rate, and WCAG 2.3.1 caps flashing at three per second. The server owns the ceiling.
MAX_STEPS: Final[int] = 2

env = Environment(autoescape=select_autoescape(["html"]))

TEMPLATE = env.from_string(
    """
<h1 class="glitch" data-glitch="slice" data-glitch-size="{{ size }}"
    data-glitch-orientation="{{ orientation }}" data-glitch-trigger="scroll"
    style="--glitch-duration:{{ duration }}ms; --glitch-steps:steps({{ steps }}, end)">
  <span class="glitch__truth">{{ headline }}</span>
</h1>
"""
)


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class SliceCopy:
    headline: str
    size: Size = "lg"
    orientation: Orientation = "horizontal"
    duration: int = 900
    steps: int = MAX_STEPS

    def render(self) -> str:
        # One node, real text, autoescaped. Nothing derived is ever rendered here.
        return TEMPLATE.render(
            headline=self.headline,
            size=self.size,
            orientation=self.orientation,
            duration=self.duration,
            steps=min(self.steps, MAX_STEPS),
        )


class HeadlineIn(BaseModel):
    headline: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=160)
    size: Size = "lg"
    orientation: Orientation = "horizontal"
    duration: int = Field(default=900, ge=200, le=1200)
    steps: int = Field(default=MAX_STEPS, ge=1, le=MAX_STEPS)

    @field_validator("headline")
    @classmethod
    def clean(cls, v: str) -> str:
        """Reject control characters and bidi overrides.

        Bidi override codepoints (U+202E and relatives) make rendered text read
        differently from its source — a spoofing vector in any string a server echoes
        into a page, and a headline is the most visible place to do it.
        """
        cleaned = v.strip()
        if not cleaned:
            raise ValueError("headline is empty after trimming")
        if any(unicodedata.category(ch) in {"Cc", "Cf"} for ch in cleaned):
            raise ValueError("headline contains control or formatting characters")
        return cleaned


router = APIRouter(prefix="/api/hero", tags=["hero"])


@router.post("/slice", response_class=HTMLResponse)
async def render_slice(payload: HeadlineIn) -> HTMLResponse:
    """Render a slice-tear headline fragment.

    Note the asymmetry with the scramble endpoint: no character cap is needed, because
    a tear is content-agnostic — it cannot corrupt glyphs, split graphemes or render
    tofu. The only hard ceiling is the step count, for flash safety.
    """
    try:
        copy = SliceCopy(
            headline=payload.headline,
            size=payload.size,
            orientation=payload.orientation,
            duration=payload.duration,
            steps=payload.steps,
        )
    except ValueError as exc:  # pragma: no cover - defensive
        raise HTTPException(status_code=422, detail=str(exc)) from exc
    return HTMLResponse(content=copy.render())
```

### 9.14 SwiftUI (iOS)

```swift
import SwiftUI

/// Band-clipped tear. Each band is a clipped copy of the SAME Text, offset — so the
/// accessibility tree sees one label, not one per band.
struct GlitchSlice: View {
    let text: String
    var duration: Double = 0.9
    var amplitude: CGFloat = 10

    /// Bands as fractional (top, height) pairs — resolution independent, like inset %.
    private let bands: [(top: CGFloat, height: CGFloat, offset: CGFloat)] = [
        (0.10, 0.26, -1.0),
        (0.46, 0.24,  1.2),
        (0.74, 0.20, -0.7),
        (0.26, 0.22,  0.8),
    ]

    @State private var phase: Int = -1
    @State private var task: Task<Void, Never>?
    @Environment(\.accessibilityReduceMotion) private var reduceMotion

    var body: some View {
        ZStack(alignment: .leading) {
            // Settled text: always present, always the accessible label.
            Text(text).opacity(phase < 0 ? 1 : 0.001)

            if phase >= 0 {
                GeometryReader { geo in
                    ForEach(Array(bands.enumerated()), id: \.offset) { index, band in
                        Text(text)
                            .offset(x: index == phase % bands.count ? band.offset * amplitude : 0)
                            .frame(width: geo.size.width, height: geo.size.height, alignment: .leading)
                            .clipped()
                            .mask(
                                Rectangle()
                                    .frame(height: geo.size.height * band.height)
                                    .offset(y: geo.size.height * band.top)
                                    .frame(height: geo.size.height, alignment: .top)
                            )
                            .accessibilityHidden(true)   // bands are decoration
                    }
                }
            }
        }
        .accessibilityElement(children: .ignore)
        .accessibilityLabel(text)
        .accessibilityAddTraits(.isHeader)
        .onAppear(perform: start)
        .onDisappear { task?.cancel() }
    }

    private func start() {
        guard !reduceMotion else { phase = -1; return }   // branch, not a softening
        task?.cancel()
        task = Task { @MainActor in
            defer { phase = -1 }                          // settle on every exit path
            let steps = 8
            for step in 0..<steps {
                if Task.isCancelled { return }
                phase = step
                try? await Task.sleep(for: .milliseconds(Int(duration * 1000) / steps))
            }
        }
    }
}
```

```kotlin
import androidx.compose.animation.core.*
import androidx.compose.foundation.layout.*
import androidx.compose.material3.MaterialTheme
import androidx.compose.material3.Text
import androidx.compose.runtime.*

import androidx.compose.ui.semantics.heading
import androidx.compose.ui.semantics.semantics
import androidx.compose.ui.text.TextStyle
import androidx.compose.ui.unit.dp

private data class Band(val top: Float, val height: Float, val offset: Float)

private val BANDS = listOf(
    Band(0.10f, 0.26f, -1.0f),
    Band(0.46f, 0.24f, 1.2f),
    Band(0.74f, 0.20f, -0.7f),
    Band(0.26f, 0.22f, 0.8f),
)

@Composable
private fun reduceMotion(): Boolean {
    val context = LocalContext.current
    return remember(context) {
        android.provider.Settings.Global.getFloat(
            context.contentResolver,
            android.provider.Settings.Global.ANIMATOR_DURATION_SCALE,
            1f
        ) == 0f
    }
}

@Composable
fun GlitchSlice(
    text: String,
    modifier: Modifier = Modifier,
    durationMillis: Int = 900,
    amplitudeDp: Float = 10f,
    style: TextStyle = MaterialTheme.typography.displaySmall,
) {
    val reduced = reduceMotion()
    val density = LocalDensity.current

    // A stepped keyframe spec: the Compose equivalent of steps(2, end). Keep the step
    // count low — step rate over duration is the flash rate (§4).
    val phase by if (reduced) {
        remember { mutableStateOf(BANDS.size.toFloat()) }
    } else {
        val animatable = remember { Animatable(0f) }
        LaunchedEffect(text) {
            animatable.snapTo(0f)
            animatable.animateTo(
                targetValue = BANDS.size.toFloat(),
                animationSpec = tween(durationMillis, easing = LinearEasing),
            )
        }
        animatable.asState()
    }

    Box(
        modifier = modifier.semantics {
            heading()
            contentDescription = text   // one label for the whole stack
        }
    ) {
        if (reduced || phase >= BANDS.size) {
            Text(text = text, style = style)
        } else {
            // Settled copy underneath keeps the box sized and the text present.
            Text(text = text, style = style, modifier = Modifier.graphicsLayer { alpha = 0.001f })
            BANDS.forEachIndexed { index, band ->
                val active = phase.toInt() == index
                Text(
                    text = text,
                    style = style,
                    modifier = Modifier
                        .graphicsLayer {
                            translationX = if (active) with(density) { (band.offset * amplitudeDp).dp.toPx() } else 0f
                        }
                        .layout { measurable, constraints ->
                            val placeable = measurable.measure(constraints)
                            layout(placeable.width, placeable.height) {
                                placeable.placeRelative(0, 0)
                            }
                        }
                        .clipToBounds(),
                )
            }
        }
    }
}
```

### 9.16 Flutter (Dart)

```dart
import 'package:flutter/material.dart';

/// Band-clipped tear. One Semantics label for the whole stack; the bands are excluded.
class GlitchSlice extends StatefulWidget {
  const GlitchSlice({
    super.key,
    required this.text,
    this.duration = const Duration(milliseconds: 900),
    this.amplitude = 10,
    this.style,
  });

  final String text;
  final Duration duration;
  final double amplitude;
  final TextStyle? style;

  @override
  State<GlitchSlice> createState() => _GlitchSliceState();
}

class _GlitchSliceState extends State<GlitchSlice>
    with SingleTickerProviderStateMixin {
  late final AnimationController _controller =
      AnimationController(vsync: this, duration: widget.duration);

  /// (topFraction, heightFraction, offsetMultiplier) — resolution independent.
  static const List<(double, double, double)> _bands = [
    (0.10, 0.26, -1.0),
    (0.46, 0.24, 1.2),
    (0.74, 0.20, -0.7),
    (0.26, 0.22, 0.8),
  ];

  @override
  void didChangeDependencies() {
    super.didChangeDependencies();
    // disableAnimations mirrors the OS reduce-motion setting: branch, don't soften.
    if (MediaQuery.of(context).disableAnimations) {
      _controller.value = 1;
    } else if (!_controller.isAnimating && _controller.value == 0) {
      _controller.forward();
    }
  }

  @override
  void dispose() {
    _controller.dispose();
    super.dispose();
  }

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final TextStyle? style = widget.style ?? Theme.of(context).textTheme.displaySmall;

    return Semantics(
      header: true,
      label: widget.text,
      child: ExcludeSemantics(
        child: AnimatedBuilder(
          animation: _controller,
          builder: (context, _) {
            final double t = _controller.value;
            if (t >= 1) return Text(widget.text, style: style);

            // Stepped, not interpolated: the index snaps, which is the tear's signature.
            final int active = (t * _bands.length).floor().clamp(0, _bands.length - 1);

            return Stack(
              children: [
                Opacity(opacity: 0.001, child: Text(widget.text, style: style)),
                for (int i = 0; i < _bands.length; i++)
                  if (i == active)
                    _Band(
                      top: _bands[i].$1,
                      height: _bands[i].$2,
                      dx: _bands[i].$3 * widget.amplitude,
                      child: Text(widget.text, style: style),
                    ),
              ],
            );
          },
        ),
      ),
    );
  }
}

class _Band extends StatelessWidget {
  const _Band({
    required this.top,
    required this.height,
    required this.dx,
    required this.child,
  });

  final double top;
  final double height;
  final double dx;
  final Widget child;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return LayoutBuilder(
      builder: (context, constraints) {
        return ClipRect(
          clipper: _BandClipper(top: top, height: height),
          child: Transform.translate(offset: Offset(dx, 0), child: child),
        );
      },
    );
  }
}

class _BandClipper extends CustomClipper<Rect> {
  const _BandClipper({required this.top, required this.height});

  final double top;
  final double height;

  @override
  Rect getClip(Size size) => Rect.fromLTWH(
        0,
        size.height * top,
        size.width,
        size.height * height,
      );

  @override
  bool shouldReclip(_BandClipper oldClipper) =>
      oldClipper.top != top || oldClipper.height != height;
}
```

## 10. Accessibility

The single-element implementation starts from a strong position — the real text is the animated
thing, so the accessible name is correct at every frame with no duplicate and no `aria-hidden` on
content. Most of this section is therefore about **not giving that away**.

**Roles and attributes.**

* The host keeps its native semantics — a torn `h1` is an `h1` with exactly one accessible name.
  The variant adds no role.
* **There is no noise node and no text duplicate.** Any `aria-hidden` in the markup belongs to the
  scanline, and nothing else. If your implementation has an `aria-hidden` copy of the headline, you
  have built the layered variant and taken on its contract.
* **`content: attr(data-text)` is not invisible to assistive technology.** CSS generated content is
  exposed in the accessibility tree on several engines, so the classic three-layer glitch can have
  its heading announced two or three times. Pseudo-elements cannot take `aria-hidden` — only real
  elements can — so if you need true channel separation, use real `aria-hidden` spans and accept a
  multi-node contract. This is the most important accessibility fact on this page, because the
  pseudo-element version is the most widely copied implementation of the effect.
* **No `aria-live`** on or around the element. Nothing here is a status change.
* The scanline is `aria-hidden="true"` and `pointer-events: none`.
* Inside a link or button, the interactive host owns role, name and focus ring.

**Keyboard map.** The text is not focusable and has no keyboard interaction — correct for
decoration. Only an optional replay control is operable:

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| `Tab` | Moves focus to the replay control (if present) |
| `Shift + Tab` | Moves focus to the previous control |
| `Enter` | Fires the replay (native `button` behaviour) |
| `Space` | Fires the replay (native `button` behaviour) |
| `Esc` | No binding — a tear is not a dismissible surface |

**Focus management.** Nothing about a tear moves focus, and this variant has one focus hazard its
siblings do not: **`clip-path` on an ancestor clips the descendant's focus ring.** Put the
animation on a text-only span, never on a wrapper containing an interactive element, or a keyboard
user tabbing to a link inside will find its focus indicator sliced off. Beyond that: a focus ring
must never itself be animated, and the hover tear must be scoped to
`@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` with `:hover` only — never `:focus-visible`.

**Contrast.** The settled text carries the obligation: 4.5:1, or 3:1 for large text (≥24px bold /
≥18.66px). Measure the **settled** state; the chroma offsets are transient and decorative, but they
must never be the only thing carrying meaning, and the scanline must not drop the text below
threshold at its brightest frame. Over imagery, verify against the darkest and lightest regions the
line crosses, not an average. One variant-specific note: mid-tear, a band can sit over a different
part of a background image than the settled text does — so check the *displaced* position too when
the backdrop is busy.

**Target size.** The text is not a target. A replay control meets 44×44px (WCAG 2.5.8 AA asks
24×24px; the house standard is 44px), delivered with padding.

**Flash and seizure safety — the variant-specific obligation.** This is the glitch most able to
cross a flash threshold, because `steps()` makes changes instantaneous and high amplitude makes
them large. WCAG 2.3.1 permits at most three flashes in any one second, where a flash is a pair of
opposing changes in *relative luminance* of at least 10% of maximum, over at least 25% of a
10-degree area of the visual field.

Be precise about what can and cannot be verified here. The house configuration produces about **20
discrete visual changes per second** (`steps(2, end)`, nine keyframe stops, 900ms), measured by the
playground and by the sampling test in §9.17. That is not 20 flashes: most of those changes move
glyph *position* rather than area luminance, and the chroma fades across the pass rather than
toggling. But the honest statement is that **no DOM-based test can tell you whether this passes
2.3.1**, because the answer depends on rendered pixel luminance, the background behind the text,
and the fraction of the visual field the headline fills. The meter is a screen: at or below 3/s
you are safe by construction; above it you are making a judgement.

Three rules follow, and they are what keep that judgement defensible: **raise amplitude, never step
rate**; never pair a hard step function with a full black-to-white or black-to-saturated swing;
and if the headline is full-bleed, very large, or on a near-black field with near-white text, get a
real luminance assessment (a photosensitive-epilepsy analysis tool on a screen recording) instead
of trusting a DOM meter.

**What changes under `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Everything, as a branch: `animation: none` on the
animated node *and* on the scroll-driven declaration, scanline removed from the DOM, text renders
final. Not a shorter tear, not a smaller one. WCAG 2.3.3 wants interaction-triggered motion
disableable; the scroll-scrubbed variant is motion triggered by scrolling, so it is covered by the
same branch.

**Common accessibility failures specific to this variant.**

* Using `::before` / `::after` with `attr(data-text)` and assuming the copies are invisible to
  screen readers — the heading gets announced two or three times.
* Putting the animation on a wrapper that contains a link or button, so `clip-path` slices the
  focus ring.
* Authoring a hard `steps()` tear at high amplitude with a black/white chroma swing, producing a
  flash hazard.
* Running the tear infinitely with no pause control (WCAG 2.2.2 failure past five seconds).
* Firing the hover tear on `:focus-visible`, tearing the UI at every tab stop.
* Omitting `aria-hidden` on the scanline.
* Animating a `clip-path` wipe from fully hidden instead of fully visible, so a stalled or
  stripped animation leaves no headline.
* Forgetting `display: inline-block`, so a wrapped headline clips per box fragment and looks like a
  rendering fault rather than a designed effect.

## 11. Innovative / Emerging Ideas

* **Scroll-driven, genuinely zero-JS.** `animation-timeline: view()` with
  `animation-range: entry 10% entry 60%` scrubs the tear against viewport entry — no observer, no
  script, no main-thread work, and naturally reversible when the visitor scrolls back. Of the three
  variants this is the only one that becomes *completely* free, because there was never a runtime
  to remove. Shipped in §9.2 behind `@supports`.
* **`@property`-typed amplitude.** Registering `--glitch-amplitude` as a `<length>` makes
  displacement interpolate rather than step, so a single keyframe set serves every size tier by
  animating the token instead of the transform — and a mid-pass size change no longer snaps.
* **View Transitions as a route-level tear.** With cross-document view transitions, a chromatic
  tear becomes the transition *between* pages using `::view-transition-old` and
  `::view-transition-new`. The tear stops being decoration on a headline and becomes the site's
  navigation grammar — a much stronger use of the gesture than repeating it per section.
* **`clip-path: shape()` for organic bands.** The newer `shape()` function allows curved and
  angled band edges where `inset()` only gives rectangles, so a tear can shear along a diagonal or
  a torn-paper edge. The geometry becomes a design decision rather than a constraint.
* **Variable-font axis tear.** Stepping a variable font's width or a custom axis alongside the
  displacement distorts the letterforms themselves rather than only their position — a tear made of
  type instead of applied to it. One extra property, and it degrades to nothing on a static font.
* **Houdini paint worklet band noise.** A `paint()` worklet can generate the torn edge and its
  static far more cheaply than stacked gradients, seeded from a registered custom property.
  Chromium-only, so it needs a gradient fallback.

## 12. Conversion / UX Killers

* **The replay that silently does nothing.** Re-adding the same class or re-setting the same
  `animation-name` does not restart a CSS animation — the browser sees no change. The naive replay
  button appears broken, with no error. Fix: alternate between two identically-defined keyframe
  names (§9.2), force a reflow (`void el.offsetWidth`), or use
  `getAnimations()` plus `cancel()` and `play()`.
* **A flash hazard built by accident.** High step rate plus high amplitude plus a black/white
  chroma swing crosses WCAG 2.3.1 and can genuinely harm a photosensitive visitor. Fix: cap
  `steps(2, end)`, keep the chroma fading rather than toggling, and verify with the sampling test
  in §9.17. This is the one failure on this page that is a safety issue rather than a quality one.
* **The clipped focus ring.** `clip-path` on an ancestor clips descendants, so a tear on a wrapper
  containing a link slices that link's focus indicator off. A keyboard user cannot see where they
  are. Fix: animate a text-only span inside, never a wrapper around interactive content.
* **Pseudo-element copies read aloud.** `content: attr(data-text)` is exposed in the accessibility
  tree on several engines, so the most-copied implementation announces the heading two or three
  times — and pseudo-elements cannot be `aria-hidden`. Fix: single-element with `text-shadow`
  chroma, or real `aria-hidden` spans.
* **The wipe mistaken for a tear.** Animating `clip-path` from `inset(0 100% 0 0)` (fully hidden)
  rather than from `inset(0)` means a stalled, stripped or printed render shows **no headline**.
  Fix: start and end fully visible; put the settle state in base CSS.
* **The inline element that falls apart.** Without `display: inline-block`, a wrapped headline has
  multiple box fragments and the clip applies to each, so bands break at the line break. It reads
  as a rendering bug, not a design. Fix: `inline-block` on the animated node, and a reserved block
  height.
* **Tearing on every scroll-by.** An observer without a spent flag re-fires each time the headline
  re-enters the viewport, stuttering repeatedly as the visitor moves up and down. Fix: `once` plus
  `io.disconnect()` — or embrace it and use the scroll-scrubbed variant, where reversibility is the
  intent rather than a bug.
* **Tear on the wrong content.** Over a price, an error, a form label or a confirmation number, the
  displacement makes the visitor doubt the *data* — and doubt near money costs the sale. Fix:
  display type only.
* **Two tears at once.** Simultaneous passes read as a GPU fault rather than a flourish. Fix: one
  per viewport.

## 13. Advanced Patterns

* **Restart as a declarative state machine, not an imperative hack.** The two-identical-keyframes
  pattern (`glitch-slice-a` / `glitch-slice-b`, selected by `data-glitch-state`) replaces the
  classic `void el.offsetWidth` reflow hack with a state change. It is better on three counts: it
  works identically in React, Vue, Svelte and Angular without a remount or a `key` change; it
  never forces a synchronous layout; and the restart is visible in the DOM, so it is testable
  (§9.17). The cost is a duplicated keyframe block, which a preprocessor loop or a `@property`-typed
  amplitude can generate.

* **Percentage band geometry as a token tier.** Bands are authored as percentages of element
  height, so one keyframe set is resolution-, viewport- and font-size-independent. Three tiers do
  the rest of the work: a **global** tier (`--ember`, `--amethyst` from the palette), a
  **component** tier (`--glitch-amplitude`, `--glitch-chroma`, `--glitch-duration`,
  `--glitch-steps`), and an **instance** tier (per-element `data-glitch-size` or inline style).
  Keyframes reference only the component tier and compute with `calc()`, so a palette change or a
  new size tier never touches a `@keyframes` block.

* **Progressive enhancement by capability, not by browser.** The React hook checks
  `CSS.supports("animation-timeline: view()")` and, when it is true with `trigger="scroll"`,
  **does nothing at all** — no observer, no state, no timer. This inverts the usual polyfill
  shape: the modern path is the zero-code path, and the JavaScript exists only as a fallback for
  older engines. It is worth writing this way round because the fallback can then be deleted
  outright when support is universal, with no change to the component's API.

* **TypeScript's role is smaller here, and that is informative.** Scramble needs
  `children: string` because it rewrites text; slice tear accepts `ReactNode` because it rewrites
  nothing — a tear over `<em>Built</em> like you.` is perfectly correct. Where typing earns its
  place is the step ceiling, which should not be a free number:

  ```ts
  type StepCount = 1 | 2;                       // flash-safety ceiling, enforced at compile time
  interface SliceProps { steps?: StepCount; amplitude?: number; }
  ```

  Encoding the safety limit in the type makes `steps={8}` a build error rather than an
  accessibility incident found in an audit.

* **Compositor honesty as a performance pattern.** `transform` and `opacity` are
  compositor-driven; `clip-path` often is not, and animating it can trigger paint each frame. The
  pattern is to keep the *displacement* on `transform` (cheap, compositor) and the *banding* on
  `clip-path` (paint, but applied to a small text element), rather than attempting the whole effect
  in one property. At headline scale this is free; the discipline matters because the same
  keyframes applied to a full-bleed section are not.

## 14. Performance & Bundle Cost

* **Zero JavaScript, and that is the headline number.** With `animation-timeline: view()` this
  variant ships about 1.2KB of gzipped CSS and nothing else — no hook, no observer, no per-frame
  work, no hydration. Scramble's runtime is small but non-zero; this one is actually zero. On a
  page already heavy with hydration, that difference lands exactly where it hurts most.
* **`clip-path` is paint, not composite — be honest about it.** `transform` and `opacity` are
  compositor-driven, but an animating `clip-path` triggers a paint on several engines. At headline
  scale, on a small text element, this is cheap and invisible. Applied to a full-bleed section, a
  large image, or several elements at once, it is measurable and can drop frames. Keep the clip on
  the smallest element that produces the effect.
* **`text-shadow` is paint-only and cheap at this size**, but it scales with blur radius and
  shadow count. Two hard-edged offsets (no blur) is the correct configuration; a blurred multi-shadow
  chroma on a 72px headline is a different performance profile entirely.
* **Tree-shaking and import discipline.** This variant needs no JavaScript, so it must not pull
  any: export it as CSS plus optional markup, never through a barrel file that also exports
  `useScramble`. A page using slice tear should download zero bytes of the glitch runtime. Do not
  reach for GSAP to drive `clipPath` keyframes here — the library is ~40KB+ gzipped to replace
  thirty lines of CSS, and the CSS version runs off the main thread for its transform component.
* **Nothing to lazy-load, nothing to defer.** There is no runtime to dynamically import. The text
  is in the initial HTML and the animation is in the stylesheet, which means the effect is complete
  at first paint — the only glitch variant for which that is true.
* **Core Web Vitals.** CLS contribution is genuinely zero: `clip-path`, `transform` and
  `text-shadow` do not participate in layout, verified by the bounding-box test in §9.17. LCP is
  unaffected because the headline is server-rendered with no hydration dependency. INP is unaffected
  because there is no main-thread work. This is the only variant in the family with no Core Web
  Vitals caveat at all.
* **Font loading interaction, and the absence of one.** Scramble has a real
  `font-display: swap` hazard, because its glyph pool must exist in the subset. A tear is
  content-agnostic — it displaces whatever glyphs are there — so a font swap mid-pass changes
  metrics but cannot produce tofu or corruption. One less thing to gate on `document.fonts.ready`.

## 15. Security

This variant has **no meaningful attack surface of its own**, and the honest version of this
section is short rather than padded.

There is no runtime in the default build: no JavaScript, no DOM writes, no derived string, no
configurable glyph pool, no `innerHTML`, no `eval`, no storage, no network call, no third-party
script. The component is a stylesheet and one element containing text. Where the optional JS
trigger is used (§9.4), it only toggles a `data-` attribute and sets a timer — it never writes
content, so the XSS vector that scramble has to engineer against does not exist here.

What remains is the **headline string itself**, and it is not this component's boundary. The text
is rendered through the framework's normal escaping (Jinja autoescape in §9.13, JSX interpolation
in §9.3), which is the same protection any other text on the page gets. The server-side validation
in §9.13 is therefore thin by design: bound the duration (200–1200ms), restrict size and
orientation to a closed set, cap length, and reject Unicode control and bidi-formatting codepoints
— that last one because bidi overrides (U+202E and relatives) can make rendered text read
differently from its source, which is a spoofing vector in any string a server echoes into a page,
and a headline is the most visible place to attempt it. Note the asymmetry worth understanding:
scramble needs a *cell cap* because per-frame work is linear in character count; a tear has no
per-character work, so length is only a layout concern.

**The one thing that is genuinely this component's own**, and the reason the step count is clamped
server-side rather than left to the client: a mis-authored step rate is a **safety** problem, not a
security one, but it is enforced in the same place and for the same reason — a client-supplied value
should not be able to put a visitor at risk. `steps` is capped at 2 in §9.13 and typed as `1 | 2`
in §13.

**Authorization.** The component gates nothing and renders no privileged content. The only caveat
is a CMS-driven hero: whoever can edit the headline can put arbitrary text on the most prominent
element of the page, so that write path needs the same role check as any other published content.
That is the CMS's boundary, not the component's.

## 16. Senior-Level Checklist

* **Semantics** — the host keeps its native element and exactly one accessible name; the variant
  adds no role.
* **Single element** — one copy of the text, no `aria-hidden` duplicate, and no
  `content: attr(data-text)` pseudo-element copies (they are exposed to assistive tech).
* **`display: inline-block`** on the animated node, so a wrapped headline does not clip per box
  fragment.
* **Animate only layout-neutral properties** — `clip-path`, `transform`, `text-shadow`. Never
  `width`, `height`, `top`, `left` or `margin`. Enforced by the keyframe-property test in §9.17.
* **Visible base** — the clip starts and ends at `inset(0)`, never from fully hidden; the settle
  state lives in base CSS, so a stalled, stripped or printed render shows a correct headline.
* **Percentage band geometry** — never pixel bands, so one keyframe set serves every tier and
  viewport.
* **Flash safety** — `steps(2, end)` ceiling, chroma fades rather than toggles, no black-to-white
  swing, at most three high-contrast changes per second, verified by the sampling test.
* **Raise amplitude, never step rate** — more drama comes from distance, not frequency.
* **Focus ring unclipped** — the animation never sits on an ancestor of an interactive element.
* **Hover is pointer-only** — `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` plus `:hover`, never
  `:focus-visible`.
* **Restart works** — alternating keyframe names, a forced reflow, or `getAnimations()`; a replay
  that re-applies the same animation name is a silent no-op.
* **One-shot and one-per-viewport** — spent flag holds and the observer disconnects, or the
  scroll-scrubbed variant is used deliberately for reversibility.
* **Reduced motion** — `animation: none` on both the state-driven and the scroll-driven
  declarations, scanline removed, on web and all three native targets.
* **Contrast** — settled text at 4.5:1 (3:1 large), checked at the displaced band position too
  when the backdrop is busy, scanline at its brightest frame included.
* **No `aria-live`**, and `aria-hidden` on the scanline only.
* **Zero JavaScript on the modern path** — no hook, no observer, no barrel import that drags in
  the scramble runtime.
* **Keyboard** — the text is not focusable; any replay control is a real `button` at a ≥44px
  target with a visible ring.
* **Content fit** — display type only (≥32px for the default tier); never on prices, numbers,
  labels, errors, form text, or anything a user must trust.

## 17. Visual Styles

The same band-and-displace skeleton across the 11 core visual languages. Each note is about *this*
variant — the band set, the amplitude, the chroma, and the snap.

1. **Flat.** Coarse bands, one flat accent as the chroma (a single offset, not a channel split),
   hard `steps()`, no scanline. The tear is purely positional: blocks of type sliding against a flat
   field. The safest tear to put on a dense page, and the one that scales to any size.
2. **Material.** The discontinuity is the problem: M3 motion is continuous, so a faithful Material
   build softens the step into an emphasized-decelerate settle and drops the chroma — at which point
   it stops being a tear and becomes a displacement reveal. Keep the scanline as a scrim sweep. The
   honest note is that this skin and this variant disagree.
3. **Glassmorphism.** The bands tear the *frosted panel* as much as the type, so the blur shifts
   behind the letters and the chroma catches the panel's light border. Striking, but the displaced
   band sits over a different part of the backdrop than the settled text — keep a solid scrim under
   the headline band so no frame drops below contrast.
4. **Liquid Glass (2026).** A tear is the wrong grammar for a refractive language, so soften it:
   two wide bands, low amplitude, 1–2px chroma, and a specular glint instead of a hard scanline. The
   effect reads as the glass momentarily failing to focus. If you want a real glitch on this skin,
   use [Signal Lock](/knowledge/glitch-signal-lock) instead.
5. **Neumorphism.** Chroma to zero — this style's contrast is already its weak point — and the tear
   becomes a shadow misregistration: the dual light/dark shadows slide out of alignment with the
   extruded glyphs and snap back. Subtle, short, and only on a calm headline.
6. **Skeuomorphism.** The full CRT tracking fault: fine bands, horizontal roll, a lingering
   scanline with bloom, and a slight barrel curve across the band. This is the native habitat of the
   tear, so it earns the highest band count in the family and the loudest scanline — just keep the
   step rate capped, because this skin tempts you to crank it.
7. **Neo-Brutalism.** Maximum amplitude (the 12px cap, and the one skin where exceeding it reads as
   intentional), clashing saturated chroma, zero radius, thick offset shadow, monospaced or
   ultra-bold type, hard `steps()` with no easing at all. The loudest expression of the variant —
   campaign pages only, and the one that most needs the flash-rate check.
8. **Claymorphism.** The tear squashes rather than shears: puffy big-radius letterforms with two
   wide bands, low amplitude, and the chroma replaced by a warm inner light so the bands look
   pressed rather than broken. The one skin where the effect stops meaning "broken."
9. **Aurora / Gradient.** The bands displace the *gradient* independently of the glyphs, so colour
   stripes slide through the letterforms before locking to the type. Spectacular on a dark field.
   Verify 4.5:1 at every stop the text crosses rather than the average, and freeze gradient drift
   under reduced motion.
10. **Minimal / Swiss.** One band, 2–3px amplitude, no chroma, no scanline, tightly tracked type on
    a strict grid. The tear is a single frame of misregistration you feel more than see — precision
    briefly lost and immediately reasserted. The restrained version, and the one that survives on an
    editorial or institutional page.
11. **UJG Brand (house default).** Platinum display type on deep Night/Eminence, coarse bands at
    10px amplitude, chroma set to Spanish Orange and Eminence (never red/cyan), a single Goldenrod
    scanline riding the line once, Methanerse for the headline face, `steps(2, end)` over 900ms.
    Reads as a confident signal fault rather than a malfunction — the most Shon of the three
    variants, and the one to reach for when the page has no JavaScript budget at all.

**The rule that never changes:** style is skin, behaviour is the skeleton. Across all 11 skins
there is one copy of the real text, the animation touches only `clip-path`, `transform` and
`text-shadow`, bands are percentages, the clip starts and ends fully visible, the settle state lives
in base CSS, the step rate stays under the flash threshold, reduced motion declares no animation at
all, and the tear never sits on an ancestor of an interactive element or on content a user has to
trust. Semantics, the accessible name, focus behaviour of adjacent controls, and the 44px target on
any replay control are identical no matter which skin is on top.
