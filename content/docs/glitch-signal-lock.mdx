## Glitch Signal Lock

The focus pull. A headline arrives soft and over-contrasted, fights through two or three failed
attempts at sharpness, and locks. It reads as a feed acquiring signal rather than a machine
breaking, which makes it the member of the glitch family you can put on a page that also has to be
taken seriously.

It is pure CSS, like [Slice Tear](/knowledge/glitch-slice-tear), and shares that variant's clean
accessibility story — one node, real text, nothing substituted. But it swaps that sibling's
problems for a different set, and they are the interesting part of this page. `filter` is the one
property in the family whose cost is **raster**, not script or layout: blur is genuinely expensive
on large type and weak GPUs. `filter` also quietly changes the box's semantics — it creates a
stacking context and a containing block, which breaks positioned descendants in ways that look
unrelated to the animation. And blur is the only glitch that makes text **temporarily
illegible**, which turns a styling decision into an accessibility argument.

Its siblings: [Scramble Reveal](/knowledge/glitch-scramble-reveal) substitutes glyphs and earns its
JavaScript; [Slice Tear](/knowledge/glitch-slice-tear) is the loud one and the cheapest to run.

* * *

## 1. What It Actually Is

A signal lock is a **one-shot filter animation** over a single run of text. `filter: blur()` moves
the text in and out of focus while `contrast()` swings its luminance, and the keyframes resolve to
`filter: none`. Nothing is substituted, nothing is displaced, nothing is clipped: the glyphs are
exactly where they will end up, and only their *rendering* changes.

Two properties define it. It is **interpolated**, not stepped — the transitions between keyframes
are smooth, which is precisely why it reads as restrained where a tear reads as violent. And it is
**non-destructive and layout-neutral**: `filter` does not participate in layout, so the box never
changes and the accessible name is correct at every frame.

What it gets confused with:

* **Plain blur-in / fade-up reveal.** A monotonic blur from soft to sharp, usually paired with
  opacity and a small translate. The standard entrance animation in every design system. A signal
  lock is *non-monotonic* — it goes sharp, fails back to soft, and tries again. That
  back-and-forth is the entire difference between "elegant reveal" and "glitch," and it is why this
  variant belongs in this family at all. If your keyframes only decrease the blur radius, you have
  built a blur-in.
* **Focus pull (cinematic rack focus).** A continuous, smooth transfer of sharpness between two
  depths, usually across two elements. Same optical vocabulary, but it is a *transfer* of attention
  with no failure and no flicker.
* **Text-shadow glow pulse.** A soft halo breathing around sharp text. The glyph edges stay crisp;
  only the surrounding light changes. Cheap, and often mistaken for blur because it looks soft.
* **Backdrop blur (frosted glass).** `backdrop-filter` blurs what is *behind* an element and leaves
  its content sharp. The opposite operation, and a completely different performance profile. A
  signal lock blurs the text itself.
* **Scramble / slice tear.** Its siblings. Scramble replaces characters; slice tear displaces
  bands. Signal lock touches neither position nor content.

**Rule of thumb:** if the glyphs never move or change, the box never moves, and sharpness goes
*backwards* at least once before settling — it is a signal lock. If sharpness only improves, it is
a blur-in, and it is not a glitch.

* * *

## 2. Why It Matters

* **It is the glitch you can use on serious content.** Distortion that reads as "broken" is
  unusable next to a price, a credential, or a medical claim. An interpolated focus pull reads as
  *cinematic* rather than faulty, which makes this the only variant in the family with a credible
  case on a consulting page, a case study, or anything where the brand has to carry authority. On
  Apple surfaces, where the whole motion doctrine is continuity, it is the only one that fits at
  all (§8).
* **Blur has a real raster cost, and it is the family's only genuine performance risk.** `transform`
  and `opacity` are compositor-driven; `clip-path` triggers paint; `filter: blur()` triggers a
  comparatively expensive **raster** pass, every frame, scaled by the blurred area and the radius.
  On a 58px headline it is invisible. On a full-bleed section, a 72px display face on a low-end
  Android, or several elements at once, it is the variant that drops frames — and it does so during
  the first second of the page, which is exactly when the visitor forms their judgement (§14).
* **`filter` changes the box's semantics, not just its pixels.** A non-`none` filter creates a
  **stacking context** and a **containing block for fixed and absolutely positioned descendants**.
  Put it on a wrapper and a `position: fixed` child suddenly positions against the wrapper instead
  of the viewport, `z-index` relationships reshuffle, and a sticky header inside stops sticking.
  These bugs look nothing like an animation bug, which is what makes them expensive (§12).
* **Blur is the only glitch that makes text illegible.** Scramble substitutes glyphs but keeps
  them crisp; a tear displaces bands but every band stays readable. Blur genuinely removes the
  ability to read, which is a different kind of claim on the visitor — and it is why duration
  discipline here is an accessibility argument rather than a taste one. Soft text is also
  **lower-contrast text**: blurring blends glyph edges into the background, so mid-pass contrast is
  measurably worse than the settled value you tested (§10).
* **Its failure mode is the quietest and the worst.** A stalled scramble leaves visible noise; a
  stalled tear leaves readable text at an offset. A stalled signal lock can leave a headline
  **permanently blurred** — present in the DOM, in the accessibility tree, and unreadable to
  everyone looking at the screen. Hence `filter: none` as the base state, not just the final
  keyframe (§5).

## 3. Anatomy

* **Host element.** The semantic element the lock is applied to — `h1`…`h6`, or a `span` inside
  one. The animation must **not** live here if the host wraps positioned or interactive content; it
  belongs on a text-only leaf (§12).
* **Truth node.** The single `span` holding the real text and carrying the filter. There is no
  second node. Remove the animation and a correctly set headline remains, because the animation was
  only ever decoration on this element.
* **Blur ramp.** The sequence of `blur()` radii over time. The non-monotonic shape — sharp, soft
  again, sharp — is the signature; a monotonic ramp is a blur-in.
* **Contrast swing.** The `contrast()` multiplier riding alongside the blur. It supplies the
  luminance flicker that makes the pass read as *signal* rather than *focus*, and it is the part
  that interacts with flash-safety limits.
* **Lock points.** The keyframes at `filter: none` mid-pass (28% and 46% in the house ramp) where
  the text is briefly fully sharp. These are what make it a *lock* — the feed almost acquires,
  loses it, and acquires again.
* **Settle state.** `filter: none`, declared in base CSS rather than only at the keyframe's `100%`
  stop. The safety net, and more load-bearing here than in any sibling, because the stalled state
  is unreadable rather than merely untidy.
* **Containment.** `contain: paint` (and `display: inline-block`) on the filtered node, keeping the
  raster region as small as the effect allows.
* **`will-change` window.** `will-change: filter` applied **only** while the pass runs and removed
  after. A promoted layer left on permanently costs memory for the life of the page.
* **Scanline.** Optional bright band travelling the line once per pass. Positioned, `aria-hidden`,
  `pointer-events: none`. Sits outside the filtered node so it is not itself blurred.
* **Chroma pair (optional).** Offset colour channels via `text-shadow`. Weaker here than in its
  siblings, because blur smears the offsets into a muddy halo — usually dialled to 1px or off.
* **Trigger.** `animation-timeline: view()` (no script), a `data-glitch-state` attribute toggled by
  a small observer, or a plain mount declaration.
* **Reduced-motion branch.** `animation: none` **and** `filter: none`, both forced. The second is
  not redundant: an interrupted pass can leave an inline or computed filter in place.

## 4. Sizes / Scale / Density

Signal lock is sized by type size, and the thing that scales with it is the **blur radius** — which
is also the cost driver. Radius must scale with the glyph weight, because 6px of blur on 16px text
is unreadable mush while on 76px text it is a gentle softness. The house ceiling is 6px, and it is
a performance limit as much as a legibility one.

| Tier | Token | Type size | Max blur | Contrast swing | Lock points | Duration | Use case |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Inline | `--glitch-inline` | 14–16px / `0.875–1rem` | 1.5px | 1.0–1.15 | 1 | 420ms | One accent word; hover softening on nav items |
| SM | `--glitch-sm` | 20–24px / `1.25–1.5rem` | 2.5px | 1.0–1.25 | 1 | 600ms | Card titles, eyebrows, sub-heads |
| MD | `--glitch-md` | 32–40px / `2–2.5rem` | 4px | 1.0–1.4 | 2 | 800ms | Section headings, page titles |
| LG | `--glitch-lg` | 48–58px / `3–3.625rem` | 6px | 1.0–1.7 | 2 | 900ms | Hero H1 — the house default |
| XL | `--glitch-xl` | 72px+ / `4.5rem+` | 6px (cap) | 1.0–1.7 | 2–3 | 1100ms | Full-bleed statement type |

Rules that matter:

* **6px is the radius ceiling, for two reasons at once.** Beyond it the raster cost climbs
  noticeably on mobile GPUs, and the text stops reading as "soft" and starts reading as "gone" —
  which lengthens the period of genuine illegibility.
* **Scale the radius with the type, never with the drama.** Roughly `0.1em`, capped at 6px. For
  more intensity, add a lock point or widen the contrast swing; do not reach for a bigger radius.
* **Keep the blurred area small.** Raster cost scales with the filtered region, so the filter goes
  on a text-only `inline-block` span with `contain: paint` — never on a section, a card, or a
  wrapper that also contains images.
* **Duration ceiling 1200ms**, and here it is also a legibility budget: the total time the headline
  is meaningfully unreadable should stay under about 400ms, which the lock points guarantee by
  returning to sharp twice.
* **Contrast swing caps at 1.7.** Past that the luminance change per keyframe gets large enough to
  approach flash territory when combined with a dark background (§10).
* **One per viewport.** Two simultaneous blur passes is the most expensive thing this family can do
  to a frame budget.
* **Target size is unaffected** — the text is not a target. Any replay control carries 44px on its
  own.

## 5. States

* **Idle / settled.** `filter: none`, from base CSS. What a crawler, a printer, a no-JS visitor, a
  reduced-motion user and a stalled animation all get. Sharp and fully readable. For this variant
  the base state is the *most* important declaration in the stylesheet, because the alternative is
  an unreadable headline.
* **Armed.** The animation is declared but not running — the state attribute is absent, or
  `animation-timeline: view()` has not reached its range. Visually identical to idle.
* **Acquiring (active pass).** The keyframes are running: blur and contrast cycling.
  `data-glitch-state="glitching"` is on the host so the scanline can sync, and it is also what gates
  `will-change: filter` to the duration of the pass.
* **Locked (mid-pass).** The brief `filter: none` keyframes at 28% and 46%. Worth naming as a state
  because it is the variant's signature: a visitor reads the headline, loses it, reads it again. If
  your implementation has no lock points, the pass is a blur-in.
* **Stalled → unreadable (the failure mode to design against).** If the animation is interrupted
  mid-pass — a `fill-mode` left on `forwards`, an inline style applied by script and not cleared, a
  class removed at the wrong moment — the computed filter can persist and the headline stays
  blurred indefinitely. This is the one genuinely dangerous state in the glitch family, and it is
  why §9.2 forces `filter: none` in the base rule, in the reduced-motion branch, and in print,
  rather than relying on the keyframe's final stop.
* **Scrubbing.** With `animation-timeline: view()` the pass is positioned by scroll offset rather
  than running, so it can reverse when the visitor scrolls up. There is no completion event and
  `once` is meaningless. Use the state-attribute trigger if the lock must happen exactly once —
  and note the specific risk here: scrolling to a position mid-range parks the headline *blurred*.
  Keep the `animation-range` narrow and near the entry edge so a parked state is almost always
  sharp.
* **Hover (optional micro-pass).** A brief softening on pointer-enter for links and labels.
  Pointer-only, scoped to `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` — never `:focus-visible`.
  Keep the radius at the inline tier: a blurred link label under the cursor is harder to read at
  exactly the moment the visitor is reading it.
* **Focus.** The text is not focusable and has no focus state. Inside an interactive element, the
  focus ring belongs to that host — and this variant has a specific hazard: a `filter` on an
  ancestor **blurs the descendant's focus ring too**. Never animate a wrapper that contains
  interactive content.
* **Active / pressed.** Only on a replay control; standard press treatment, no blur on the button.
* **Disabled.** Not applicable to the text. A replay control is disabled while a pass is in flight.
* **Loading / error.** Not applicable: the text is known before render and there is no runtime to
  fail.
* **Reduced-motion.** First-class: `animation: none` *and* `filter: none`, scanline removed, text
  renders sharp. Not a smaller blur, not a shorter one.
* **Print.** Settle state, with `filter` explicitly cleared — a blurred heading in a PDF is a
  defect that outlives the session.

## 6. Types / Variants

1. **Signal lock (the house default).** Non-monotonic blur with a contrast swing and two lock
   points. Reads as a feed acquiring. The restrained member of the family and the one to reach for
   when the glitch has to coexist with authority.
2. **Focus stutter (blur only).** The contrast multiplier dropped entirely, leaving pure sharpness
   oscillation. The most restrained version in the whole library — closer to a camera hunting for
   focus than to a glitch. Right for luxury, editorial and institutional registers, and the only
   variant that survives on a light background without looking washed out.
3. **Luminance flicker (contrast only).** Blur dropped, contrast and brightness swinging. Keeps
   text sharp and legible throughout, which makes it the safest option for anything with real
   reading work nearby. It is also the one to watch for flash safety, since luminance is doing all
   the work (§10).
4. **Chromatic aberration lock.** Blur paired with offset channel shadows so the softening smears
   into colour fringing, like a cheap lens. Richer and more "broken" than the default; keep the
   chroma at 1–2px, because blur multiplies the apparent offset and 3px turns into a muddy halo.
5. **Interlace lock.** The blur ramp combined with a repeating-gradient scanline overlay at low
   opacity, so the pass reads as a CRT or video feed rather than an optical lens. The scanline sits
   *outside* the filtered node — blur it and it turns to grey wash.
6. **Scroll-scrubbed lock.** `animation-timeline: view()` ties sharpness to scroll offset. Zero
   JavaScript and reversible, but see the parking hazard in §5: keep the range narrow and near the
   entry edge, or a visitor can stop scrolling with the headline soft.
7. **Hover softening.** A brief inline-tier pass on pointer-enter for links. Pointer-only, never
   `:focus-visible`, and the smallest radius you have — a label that blurs while being read is a
   worse interaction than no effect at all.

## 7. When to Use (and When Not To)

**Use when:**

* The brand needs glitch *texture* without glitch *violence* — a consulting hero, a case study
  opener, a credentials page, anything where "broken" would undercut the message.
* You want the closest thing in the family to a conventional reveal, so the effect reads as
  cinematic polish rather than a stylistic statement. It is the variant most likely to survive a
  stakeholder review.
* **The platform has no scripting**, or you want zero JavaScript: like slice tear, this is CSS
  only, and with `animation-timeline: view()` even the trigger is script-free.
* The headline is long, multi-line, or localised into an untested script — the pass is
  content-agnostic, so it cannot corrupt glyphs, split graphemes or render tofu.
* The register is optical and continuous rather than digital and discontinuous — and especially on
  Apple platforms, where a hard tear is simply the wrong grammar.

**Don't use / use something else when:**

* **You want the "word assembling" read.** That is [Scramble](/knowledge/glitch-scramble-reveal) —
  nothing here is ever decoded.
* **You want the loud, unmistakably digital glitch.** That is
  [Slice Tear](/knowledge/glitch-slice-tear). Blur reads as optics, not as data corruption.
* **The headline is on a low-end device, is very large, or sits on a page already fighting for
  frames.** Blur is the family's expensive variant; slice tear gives you a glitch for free.
* **Legibility has to hold continuously** — a long headline a visitor is mid-way through reading,
  or anything with a reading-order dependency below it. Use the luminance-flicker variant, which
  keeps text sharp.
* **The animated element wraps positioned, sticky, or interactive content.** `filter` creates a
  stacking context and a containing block, and blurs descendant focus rings. Move it to a text-only
  span, or use a sibling.
* **It is body copy, a label, a number, a form field, an error or a confirmation.** Errors must
  never animate — use **Alert**.
* **You need the effect to park safely at any scroll position.** A scrubbed blur can park soft; a
  scrubbed tear parks readable. Prefer slice tear for long scroll ranges.
* **It would be the second glitch in view.** Use a plain **Reveal on scroll** for the rest.

**Placement heuristics.** One per viewport, display type ≥32px, on a text-only `inline-block` node
with `contain: paint`, fired on in-view with a narrow range. Keep total unreadable time under about
400ms by keeping both lock points. Pair with a static eyebrow and sub-line — like every glitch, it
only reads as deliberate against stillness.

## 8. Across Design Systems

No major design system ships a "signal lock," but unlike its siblings this variant has genuine
relatives in several of them — because a blur-based entrance is a mainstream idea, and the glitch
version is one keyframe away from it.

* **Material 3 (Google).** No glitch, but the closest philosophical fit of any system to this
  variant: M3 Expressive motion is continuous and physical, and an interpolated focus pull is
  continuous and physical. A Material-faithful build keeps the blur ramp and the easing, drops the
  contrast flicker, and removes one lock point — which lands on M3's own blur-in entrance. That is
  not a failure; it means this variant degrades gracefully into Material rather than fighting it.
* **Apple HIG.** The only variant with a real case on Apple platforms. HIG wants motion brief,
  purposeful and physically plausible, and an optical focus pull is all three — the 2026 Liquid
  Glass language is itself built on refraction and softness. Use the focus-stutter sub-variant, keep
  the radius low, and treat `accessibilityReduceMotion` as the hard branch it is on that platform
  (§9.14).
* **Fluent 2 (Microsoft).** No glitch, and Fluent's acrylic material is a *backdrop* blur rather
  than a content blur — the opposite operation, worth knowing so you do not reach for
  `backdrop-filter` by analogy. Fluent's contribution is its curve and duration taxonomy; map the
  pass onto its slow tier.
* **Ant Design.** No glitch. Ant's animation set is utilitarian and its surfaces are dense, so a
  blurred heading next to a data table is exactly the wrong call. Useful as a boundary marker.
* **Tailwind CSS.** No component, but full expressive coverage: `blur-*` utilities exist, custom
  `@keyframes` go in `@theme` (v4) or the config (v3), and `motion-reduce:animate-none` is a true
  and sufficient compliance story — like slice tear and unlike scramble, a Tailwind-only build is
  complete (§9.5). Watch the one trap: Tailwind's `blur-sm`-style utilities set a filter on the
  element permanently, so compose them with care around the stacking-context side effect.
* **shadcn/ui + Radix.** No primitive, and barely any need for the headless pattern: with no
  lifecycle to own, a `cva` skin plus `Slot` is the whole component (§9.7).
* **Bootstrap 5.** No glitch and a thin animation layer, so this is a utility class beside
  `display-1`. Bootstrap does ship `.blur` helpers in some builds, which makes the honest add-on
  shape obvious.
* **Creative-web tooling.** GSAP can tween `filter` and Motion can animate it, but neither buys
  much here — the CSS is shorter and the library cannot make blur cheaper, because the cost is in
  the compositor's raster pass, not in the tweening. Where GSAP genuinely helps is sequencing a lock
  against other elements on a shared timeline; for a single headline, hand-written CSS wins.

### 8b. Visual Styles / Trends — What's trending in 2026

Blur is in the middle of a mainstream moment, and this variant benefits from it in a specific way:
a soft-focus reveal no longer reads as a 2019 hero cliché, because the surrounding visual language
— Apple's Liquid Glass, refractive UI, depth and material — has made optical softness feel current
again. The glitch reading comes entirely from the *non-monotonic* ramp, which is a cheap difference
to author and a large difference to perceive.

Three platform changes matter. **Scroll-driven animations** make the lock scrubbable with no
script, with the parking caveat in §5. **`@property`** lets a `--glitch-blur` token be registered as
a `<length>` so radius interpolates smoothly and one keyframe set serves every tier — more useful
here than in any sibling, because radius is the thing that must scale. And **View Transitions**,
now cross-document, make a focus pull a natural route transition: the incoming page resolves into
sharpness, which is a far better use of the gesture than repeating it per section.

Against all that, the honest trend note is a performance one: as blur has become fashionable,
pages have started stacking `backdrop-filter` panels, blurred images and blurred text in the same
viewport, and the raster cost compounds. The 2026 discipline is **one blurred thing at a time**.

## 9. The Code

The contract: one node holding real text, animation on `filter` only, `filter: none` forced in the
base rule and every escape hatch, the filter scoped to a contained text-only leaf, `will-change`
gated to the pass, and reduced motion as a branch that declares no animation and no filter.

### 9.1 HTML (semantic foundation with ARIA)

```html
<!-- One node. Nothing to hide from assistive tech, nothing to duplicate.
     The scanline sits OUTSIDE the filtered node, or the blur turns it to grey wash. -->
<h1 class="glitch" data-glitch="signal" data-glitch-size="lg" data-glitch-trigger="scroll">
  <span class="glitch__truth">Built like you.</span>
  <span class="glitch__scan" aria-hidden="true"></span>
</h1>

<!-- Optional replay. A real button: typed, labelled, keyboard-operable, 44px target. -->
<button type="button" class="glitch__replay" data-glitch-replay="hero-title"
        aria-describedby="glitch-help">
  Replay lock
</button>
<p id="glitch-help" class="u-visually-hidden">
  Replays the decorative focus animation on the heading. The heading text does not change.
</p>

<!-- Lock inside a link: the anchor owns role, name and focus ring. The animated span is
     text-only and does NOT wrap the interactive element — a filter on an ancestor blurs
     the descendant's focus ring and creates a containing block for positioned children. -->
<a href="/suites" class="nav__link">
  <span class="glitch" data-glitch="signal" data-glitch-trigger="hover" data-glitch-size="inline">
    <span class="glitch__truth">Five service suites</span>

</a>

```

### 9.2 CSS (states, layout, reduced-motion)

```css
.glitch[data-glitch="signal"] {
  --glitch-duration: 900ms;
  --glitch-blur: 6px;
  --glitch-contrast: 1.7;
  --glitch-chroma: 0px;              /* blur smears offsets; usually off for this variant */
  --glitch-chroma-a: var(--ember, #d9531a);
  --glitch-chroma-b: var(--amethyst, #47107d);

  position: relative;
  display: inline-block;
}

.glitch[data-glitch="signal"] .glitch__truth {
  display: inline-block;
  /* Keep the raster region as small as the effect allows. */
  contain: paint;
  /* THE SETTLE STATE — and the most load-bearing declaration on this page. If the pass
     is interrupted, this is what keeps the headline readable rather than permanently
     blurred. Never rely on the keyframe's 100% stop alone. */
```

### 9.3 React + TypeScript (full reusable component)

```tsx
"use client";

import * as React from "react";

export type SignalSize = "inline" | "sm" | "md" | "lg" | "xl";
export type SignalTrigger = "mount" | "in-view" | "scroll" | "hover" | "manual";
export type SignalFlavour = "lock" | "focus" | "luma" | "chroma";

export function usePrefersReducedMotion(): boolean {
  const [reduced, setReduced] = React.useState(false);
  React.useEffect(() => {
    const mq = window.matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)");
    setReduced(mq.matches);
    const onChange = (e: MediaQueryListEvent) => setReduced(e.matches);
    mq.addEventListener("change", onChange);
    return () => mq.removeEventListener("change", onChange);
  }, []);
  return reduced;
}

type PassState = "idle" | "glitching" | "glitching-alt";

interface UseSignalOptions {
  trigger?: SignalTrigger;
  once?: boolean;
  duration?: number;
}

export function useSignal({ trigger = "in-view", once = true, duration = 900 }: UseSignalOptions) {
  const reduced = usePrefersReducedMotion();
  const hostRef = React.useRef<HTMLElement | null>(null);
  const [state, setState] = React.useState<PassState>("idle");
  const spent = React.useRef(false);
  const flip = React.useRef(false);
  const settleRef = React.useRef<ReturnType<typeof setTimeout> | null>(null);

  const cssOwnsIt =
    trigger === "scroll" &&
    typeof CSS !== "undefined" &&
    CSS.supports?.("animation-timeline: view()");

  const run = React.useCallback(() => {
    if (reduced || cssOwnsIt) return;
    if (once && spent.current) return;
    spent.current = true;
    flip.current = !flip.current;
    setState(flip.current ? "glitching" : "glitching-alt");
    if (settleRef.current) clearTimeout(settleRef.current);
    /* Returning to "idle" does two jobs: it drops will-change (releasing the promoted
       layer) and it guarantees the element is back on the unfiltered base rule. For this
       variant that second job is the difference between readable and not. */
    settleRef.current = setTimeout(() => setState("idle"), duration + 120);
  }, [cssOwnsIt, duration, once, reduced]);

  const replay = React.useCallback(() => { spent.current = false; run(); }, [run]);

  React.useEffect(() => () => { if (settleRef.current) clearTimeout(settleRef.current); }, []);

  React.useEffect(() => {
    if (reduced || cssOwnsIt || trigger === "manual" || trigger === "hover") return;
    if (trigger === "mount") { run(); return; }
    const el = hostRef.current;
    if (!el) return;
    if (typeof IntersectionObserver === "undefined") { run(); return; }
    const io = new IntersectionObserver(
      (entries) => {
        for (const entry of entries) {
          if (!entry.isIntersecting) continue;
          run();
          if (once) io.disconnect();
        }
      },
      { threshold: 0.35 }
    );
    io.observe(el);
    return () => io.disconnect();
  }, [cssOwnsIt, once, reduced, run, trigger]);

  return { hostRef, state, reduced, cssOwnsIt, run, replay };
}

type OwnProps<T extends React.ElementType> = {
  children: React.ReactNode;      // any node: nothing is rewritten
  as?: T;
  size?: SignalSize;
  flavour?: SignalFlavour;
  trigger?: SignalTrigger;
  duration?: number;
  once?: boolean;
  /** Blur radius ceiling in px. Capped at 6 — a performance AND legibility limit (§4). */
  blur?: 1.5 | 2.5 | 4 | 6;
  scanline?: boolean;
  interlace?: boolean;
  className?: string;
};

export type GlitchSignalProps<T extends React.ElementType> = OwnProps<T> &
  Omit<React.ComponentPropsWithoutRef<T>, keyof OwnProps<T>>;

export function GlitchSignal<T extends React.ElementType = "span">({
  children, as, size = "lg", flavour = "lock", trigger = "in-view", duration = 900,
  once = true, blur, scanline = true, interlace = false, className, ...rest
}: GlitchSignalProps<T>) {
  const Host = (as ?? "span") as React.ElementType;
  const { hostRef, state, reduced } = useSignal({ trigger, once, duration });

  return (
    <Host
      ref={hostRef as React.Ref<never>}
      className={["glitch", className].filter(Boolean).join(" ")}
      data-glitch="signal"
      data-glitch-size={size}
      data-glitch-variant={flavour}
      data-glitch-state={state}
      data-glitch-trigger={trigger}
      style={{
        ["--glitch-duration" as string]: `${duration}ms`,
        ...(blur ? { ["--glitch-blur" as string]: `${blur}px` } : null),
      }}
      {...rest}
    >
      {/* The real text IS the filtered element — a text-only leaf, so the filter cannot
          create a containing block around anything positioned or interactive. */}
      <span className="glitch__truth">{children}</span>
      {/* Decoration lives OUTSIDE the filtered node or the blur would grey it out. */}
      {interlace && !reduced ? <span className="glitch__interlace" aria-hidden="true" /> : null}
      {scanline && !reduced ? <span className="glitch__scan" aria-hidden="true" /> : null}
    </Host>
  );
}

GlitchSignal.displayName = "GlitchSignal";
```

### 9.4 Vanilla JavaScript (no framework, full keyboard)

```js
/** glitch-signal.js — OPTIONAL. Markup plus CSS works with this absent; it only adds a
 *  one-shot in-view trigger and replay for engines without animation-timeline. */
const reduced = () => window.matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)").matches;
const cssOwnsIt = () => CSS.supports?.("animation-timeline: view()");

export class GlitchSignal {
  constructor(host, options = {}) {
    this.host = host;
    this.target = host.querySelector(".glitch__truth");
    this.duration = Number(options.duration ?? host.dataset.glitchDuration ?? 900);
    this.once = options.once !== false;
    this.spent = false;
    this.flip = false;
    this.settle = null;

    const trigger = host.dataset.glitchTrigger || "in-view";
    if (reduced()) { this.#clearFilter(); return; }
    if (trigger === "scroll" && cssOwnsIt()) return;
    if (trigger === "manual") return;
    if (trigger === "hover") {
      host.addEventListener("pointerenter", () => { this.spent = false; this.run(); });
      return;
    }
    if (trigger === "mount" || !("IntersectionObserver" in window)) { this.run(); return; }

    this.io = new IntersectionObserver((entries) => {
      for (const entry of entries) {
        if (!entry.isIntersecting) continue;
        this.run();
        if (this.once) this.io.disconnect();
      }
    }, { threshold: 0.35 });
    this.io.observe(host);
  }

  run() {
    if (reduced()) { this.#clearFilter(); return; }
    if (this.once && this.spent) return;
    this.spent = true;
    if (this.settle) clearTimeout(this.settle);

    // Swapping the state swaps the animation NAME — re-applying the same name is a no-op.
    this.flip = !this.flip;
    this.host.dataset.glitchState = this.flip ? "glitching" : "glitching-alt";

    /* The settle timer matters more here than in any sibling: returning to "idle" drops
       will-change AND puts the node back on the unfiltered base rule. Without it an
       interrupted pass can leave the headline permanently blurred. */
    this.settle = setTimeout(() => {
      this.host.dataset.glitchState = "idle";
      this.#clearFilter();
    }, this.duration + 120);
  }

  /** Belt and braces: clear any inline filter a script or a cancelled animation left. */
  #clearFilter() {
    if (this.target) this.target.style.filter = "";
    this.host.dataset.glitchState = "idle";
  }

  destroy() {
    if (this.settle) clearTimeout(this.settle);
    this.io?.disconnect();
    this.#clearFilter();
  }
}

const registry = new Map();
for (const host of document.querySelectorAll('[data-glitch="signal"]')) {
  const instance = new GlitchSignal(host);
  if (host.id) registry.set(host.id, instance);
}
/* A real <button> needs no key handling: Enter and Space fire click natively. */
for (const control of document.querySelectorAll("[data-glitch-replay]")) {
  control.addEventListener("click", () => {
    const instance = registry.get(control.dataset.glitchReplay);
    if (!instance) return;
    instance.spent = false;
    instance.run();
  });
}
```

### 9.13 Python (Jinja2 rendering + FastAPI endpoint)

The server owns two ceilings here, and both are more than hygiene: **blur radius** (a performance
and legibility limit) and **duration** (a legibility budget). Unlike scramble there is no glyph
pool, and unlike slice tear the dangerous input is not step rate but radius.

```python
# app/signal.py
from __future__ import annotations

import unicodedata
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Final, Literal

from fastapi import APIRouter, HTTPException
from fastapi.responses import HTMLResponse
from jinja2 import Environment, select_autoescape
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field, field_validator

Size = Literal["inline", "sm", "md", "lg", "xl"]
Flavour = Literal["lock", "focus", "luma", "chroma"]

# Blur radius is capped for two reasons at once: raster cost on weak GPUs, and the
# length of time the headline is genuinely unreadable. 6px is the house ceiling (§4).
MAX_BLUR_PX: Final[float] = 6.0
# Total pass length is a legibility budget, not just a taste one.
MAX_DURATION_MS: Final[int] = 1200

env = Environment(autoescape=select_autoescape(["html"]))

TEMPLATE = env.from_string(
    """
<h1 class="glitch" data-glitch="signal" data-glitch-size="{{ size }}"
    data-glitch-variant="{{ flavour }}" data-glitch-trigger="scroll"
    style="--glitch-duration:{{ duration }}ms; --glitch-blur:{{ blur }}px">
  <span class="glitch__truth">{{ headline }}</span>
</h1>
"""
)

# Per-tier radius ceilings. A client may request less, never more.
TIER_BLUR: Final[dict[str, float]] = {
    "inline": 1.5,
    "sm": 2.5,
    "md": 4.0,
    "lg": 6.0,
    "xl": 6.0,
}


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class SignalCopy:
    headline: str
    size: Size = "lg"
    flavour: Flavour = "lock"
    duration: int = 900
    blur: float = MAX_BLUR_PX

    def render(self) -> str:
        # One node, real text, autoescaped. The radius is clamped to the tier ceiling
        # here rather than trusted from the client.
        ceiling = TIER_BLUR[self.size]
        return TEMPLATE.render(
            headline=self.headline,
            size=self.size,
            flavour=self.flavour,
            duration=min(self.duration, MAX_DURATION_MS),
            blur=min(self.blur, ceiling),
        )


class HeadlineIn(BaseModel):
    headline: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=160)
    size: Size = "lg"
    flavour: Flavour = "lock"
    duration: int = Field(default=900, ge=200, le=MAX_DURATION_MS)
    blur: float = Field(default=MAX_BLUR_PX, gt=0, le=MAX_BLUR_PX)

    @field_validator("headline")
    @classmethod
    def clean(cls, v: str) -> str:
        """Reject control characters and bidi overrides.

        Bidi override codepoints (U+202E and relatives) make rendered text read
        differently from its source — a spoofing vector in any string a server echoes
        into a page, and a headline is the most visible place to try it.
        """
        cleaned = v.strip()
        if not cleaned:
            raise ValueError("headline is empty after trimming")
        if any(unicodedata.category(ch) in {"Cc", "Cf"} for ch in cleaned):
            raise ValueError("headline contains control or formatting characters")
        return cleaned


router = APIRouter(prefix="/api/hero", tags=["hero"])


@router.post("/signal", response_class=HTMLResponse)
async def render_signal(payload: HeadlineIn) -> HTMLResponse:
    """Render a signal-lock headline fragment.

    No character cap is needed — a filter is content-agnostic and cannot corrupt
    glyphs, split graphemes or render tofu. What is clamped is the radius and the
    duration, because together they decide how long the headline is unreadable.
    """
    try:
        copy = SignalCopy(
            headline=payload.headline,
            size=payload.size,
            flavour=payload.flavour,
            duration=payload.duration,
            blur=payload.blur,
        )
    except ValueError as exc:  # pragma: no cover - defensive
        raise HTTPException(status_code=422, detail=str(exc)) from exc
    return HTMLResponse(content=copy.render())
```

### 9.14 SwiftUI (iOS)

```swift
import SwiftUI

/// Focus lock. The text is never replaced or moved, so the accessibility label is simply
/// the text — no label juggling needed, unlike the scramble variant.
struct GlitchSignal: View {
    let text: String
    var duration: Double = 0.9
    var maxBlur: CGFloat = 6

    /// (atProgress, blurMultiplier) — non-monotonic, with lock points at 0 blur.
    private let ramp: [(progress: Double, blur: CGFloat)] = [
        (0.00, 1.00),
        (0.12, 0.17),
        (0.16, 0.83),
        (0.28, 0.00),   // lock point 1
        (0.33, 0.67),
        (0.46, 0.00),   // lock point 2
        (0.52, 0.33),
        (1.00, 0.00),
    ]

    @State private var blur: CGFloat = 0
    @State private var task: Task<Void, Never>?
    @Environment(\.accessibilityReduceMotion) private var reduceMotion

    var body: some View {
        Text(text)
            .blur(radius: blur)
            .accessibilityAddTraits(.isHeader)
            .onAppear(perform: start)
            .onDisappear {
                task?.cancel()
                blur = 0          // never leave the headline soft
            }
    }

    private func start() {
        // Reduce Motion is a hard branch on Apple platforms.
        guard !reduceMotion else { blur = 0; return }
        task?.cancel()
        task = Task { @MainActor in
            defer { blur = 0 }    // settle on every exit path, including cancellation
            for index in 0..<(ramp.count - 1) {
                if Task.isCancelled { return }
                let from = ramp[index]
                let to = ramp[index + 1]
                let segment = (to.progress - from.progress) * duration
                withAnimation(.easeOut(duration: segment)) {
                    blur = to.blur * maxBlur
                }
                try? await Task.sleep(for: .seconds(segment))
            }
        }
    }
}
```

```kotlin
@Composable
private fun reduceMotion(): Boolean {
    val context = LocalContext.current
    return remember(context) {
        android.provider.Settings.Global.getFloat(
            context.contentResolver,
            android.provider.Settings.Global.ANIMATOR_DURATION_SCALE,
            1f
        ) == 0f
    }

@Composable
fun GlitchSignal(
    text: String,
    modifier: Modifier = Modifier,
    durationMillis: Int = 900,
    maxBlurDp: Float = 6f,
    style: TextStyle = MaterialTheme.typography.displaySmall,
) {
    val reduced = reduceMotion()

    // Non-monotonic keyframes with lock points at 0f — the Compose expression of the
    // CSS ramp. Modifier.blur is hardware-accelerated on API 31+ and a no-op below it,
    // which is a graceful degradation rather than a break.
    val blurMultiplier by if (reduced) {
        remember { mutableFloatStateOf(0f) }
    } else {
        val animatable = remember { Animatable(1f) }
        LaunchedEffect(text) {
            animatable.snapTo(1f)
            animatable.animateTo(
                targetValue = 0f,
                animationSpec = keyframes {
                    durationMillis = durationMillis
                    1.00f at 0
                    0.17f at (durationMillis * 0.12f).toInt()
                    0.83f at (durationMillis * 0.16f).toInt()
                    0.00f at (durationMillis * 0.28f).toInt()   // lock point 1
                    0.67f at (durationMillis * 0.33f).toInt()
                    0.00f at (durationMillis * 0.46f).toInt()   // lock point 2
                    0.33f at (durationMillis * 0.52f).toInt()
                    0.00f at durationMillis
                },
            )
        }
        animatable.asState()
    }

    val radius = (blurMultiplier * maxBlurDp).dp

    Text(
        text = text,
        style = style,
        modifier = modifier
            .then(
                if (radius.value > 0.1f) {
                    Modifier.blur(radius, edgeTreatment = BlurredEdgeTreatment.Unbounded)
                } else {
                    Modifier   // drop the blur layer entirely once settled
                }
            )
            .semantics {
                heading()
                contentDescription = text
            },
    )
}
```

```dart
import 'package:flutter/material.dart';

/// Focus lock. Text is never replaced or moved, so Semantics is trivial.
class GlitchSignal extends StatefulWidget {
  const GlitchSignal({
    super.key,
    required this.text,
    this.duration = const Duration(milliseconds: 900),
    this.maxBlur = 6,
    this.style,
  });

  final String text;
  final Duration duration;
  final double maxBlur;
  final TextStyle? style;

  @override
  State<GlitchSignal> createState() => _GlitchSignalState();
}

class _GlitchSignalState extends State<GlitchSignal>
    with SingleTickerProviderStateMixin {
  late final AnimationController _controller =
      AnimationController(vsync: this, duration: widget.duration);

  /// Non-monotonic ramp with lock points at 0 — a TweenSequence is the Flutter
  /// equivalent of the CSS keyframes.
  late final Animation<double> _blur = TweenSequence<double>([
    TweenSequenceItem(tween: Tween(begin: 1.00, end: 0.17), weight: 12),
    TweenSequenceItem(tween: Tween(begin: 0.17, end: 0.83), weight: 4),
    TweenSequenceItem(tween: Tween(begin: 0.83, end: 0.00), weight: 12), // lock 1
    TweenSequenceItem(tween: Tween(begin: 0.00, end: 0.67), weight: 5),
    TweenSequenceItem(tween: Tween(begin: 0.67, end: 0.00), weight: 13), // lock 2
    TweenSequenceItem(tween: Tween(begin: 0.00, end: 0.33), weight: 6),
    TweenSequenceItem(tween: Tween(begin: 0.33, end: 0.00), weight: 48),
  ]).animate(CurvedAnimation(parent: _controller, curve: Curves.easeOut));

  @override
  void didChangeDependencies() {
    super.didChangeDependencies();
    // disableAnimations mirrors the OS reduce-motion setting: branch, don't soften.
    if (MediaQuery.of(context).disableAnimations) {
      _controller.value = 1;
    } else if (!_controller.isAnimating && _controller.value == 0) {
      _controller.forward();
    }
  }

  @override
  void dispose() {
    _controller.dispose();
    super.dispose();
  }

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final TextStyle? style = widget.style ?? Theme.of(context).textTheme.displaySmall;

    return Semantics(
      header: true,
      label: widget.text,
      child: AnimatedBuilder(
        animation: _blur,
        builder: (context, child) {
          final double sigma = _blur.value * widget.maxBlur;
          // Drop the ImageFiltered layer entirely once settled: an identity filter
          // still costs a saveLayer on every frame.
          if (sigma < 0.1) return child!;
          return ImageFiltered(
            imageFilter: ui.ImageFilter.blur(sigmaX: sigma, sigmaY: sigma),
            child: child,
          );
        },
        child: Text(widget.text, style: style),
      ),
    );
  }
}
```

## 10. Accessibility

The single-element implementation inherits slice tear's strong starting position — real text,
animated directly, correct accessible name at every frame. What is specific here is that blur makes
text **temporarily unreadable**, and softened text is **lower-contrast** text. Those two facts
carry this section.

**Roles and attributes.**

* The host keeps its native semantics — a locked `h1` is an `h1` with exactly one accessible name.
  The variant adds no role.
* **There is no noise node and no text duplicate.** Any `aria-hidden` in the markup belongs to the
  scanline or the interlace overlay, and nothing else.
* **No `aria-live`** on or around the element. Nothing here is a status change.
* Decoration (scanline, interlace) is `aria-hidden`, `pointer-events: none`, and lives **outside**
  the filtered node — blurring it both wastes raster and turns it to grey wash.
* Inside a link or button, the interactive host owns role, name and focus ring.

**Keyboard map.** The text is not focusable and has no keyboard interaction — correct for
decoration. Only an optional replay control is operable:

| Key | Action |
| ---| --- |
| `Tab` | Moves focus to the replay control (if present) |
| `Shift + Tab` | Moves focus to the previous control |
| `Enter` | Fires the replay (native `button` behaviour) |
| `Space` | Fires the replay (native `button` behaviour) |
| `Esc` | No binding — a lock is not a dismissible surface |

**Focus management.** Nothing about a lock moves focus, and this variant has a focus hazard of its
own: **a `filter` on an ancestor blurs the descendant's focus ring**, and because a filtered element
also becomes a containing block, a focus ring positioned with `absolute` can land in the wrong
place entirely. Put the animation on a text-only span, never on a wrapper containing interactive
content. The hover softening must be scoped to `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` with
`:hover` only — never `:focus-visible`.

**Contrast — the variant-specific obligation.** Two distinct claims, and both matter:

1. The **settled** text carries the formal obligation: 4.5:1, or 3:1 for large text (≥24px bold /
   ≥18.66px). That is what WCAG 1.4.3 measures and what an audit will check.
2. The **blurred** text is genuinely lower-contrast than the settled text, because blur blends
   glyph edges into the background — a 4.6:1 headline can measure below 3:1 mid-pass. WCAG does not
   test transient states, so this will never show up in an automated audit; it is still a real
   reduction in legibility for a low-vision visitor. The mitigations are the ones already in the
   spec: keep the radius ≤6px, keep the lock points so sharp frames recur, keep the total
   meaningfully-unreadable time under about 400ms, and do not combine a high blur with a
   contrast-reducing background. Over imagery, verify the settled state against the darkest and
   lightest regions the line crosses, and keep a scrim.

**Target size.** The text is not a target. A replay control meets 44×44px (WCAG 2.5.8 AA asks
24×24px; the house standard is 44px), delivered with padding.

**Flash and seizure safety.** Gentler than slice tear, because the transitions are interpolated
rather than stepped — but the `contrast()` swing is a luminance change, and the luminance-flicker
sub-variant makes it the only moving part. The house configuration (contrast capped at 1.7, two
lock points, 900ms, eased transitions) produces well under three high-contrast changes per second.
Two rules follow: **cap the contrast swing at 1.7**, and never pair the luminance-flicker variant
with a near-black background and near-white text, where the swing is at its most severe.

**Cognitive and vestibular load.** Blur is implicated in motion sickness less than displacement is,
which is a point in this variant's favour. The cognitive cost is the opposite: a visitor who began
reading and then loses sharpness has to re-fixate. That is the argument for keeping the pass short
and the lock points frequent, and the argument against ever using this on body copy or on a
headline a visitor is likely to arrive mid-read.

**What changes under `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Everything, as a branch: `animation: none` **and**
`filter: none`, both forced, plus `will-change: auto` and the decoration removed from the DOM. The
doubled declaration is deliberate — an interrupted pass or a script-applied inline style can leave a
computed filter in place, and for this variant the stalled state is unreadable rather than untidy.
On native: `accessibilityReduceMotion`, `ANIMATOR_DURATION_SCALE == 0`, and
`MediaQuery.disableAnimations`, all honoured in §9.14–9.16. WCAG 2.3.3 wants interaction-triggered
motion disableable; the scroll-scrubbed variant is motion triggered by scrolling and is covered by
the same branch.

**Common accessibility failures specific to this variant.**

* Leaving the headline blurred because the settle state was only in the keyframe's `100%` stop, or
  because `animation-fill-mode: forwards` held a soft frame.
* Putting the filter on a wrapper containing a link, button, sticky header or absolutely positioned
  element — blurring the focus ring and breaking the containing block.
* A radius above 6px, extending the unreadable window and crushing contrast.
* Pairing the luminance-flicker variant with maximum contrast on a black background, producing a
  luminance swing near the flash threshold.
* Scroll-scrubbing over a wide `animation-range`, so a visitor can park with the headline soft
  indefinitely.
* Blurring the scanline or interlace along with the text, which greys out the decoration and
  obscures the glyphs further.
* Running the pass infinitely with no pause control (WCAG 2.2.2 failure past five seconds) — and
  here an infinite blur is worse than an infinite tear, because the text is periodically unreadable
  rather than merely moving.
* Firing the hover softening on `:focus-visible`, blurring labels for keyboard users at every tab
  stop.
* Printing with the filter still applied, producing a permanently blurred heading in a PDF.

## 11. Innovative / Emerging Ideas

* **`@property`-typed blur radius.** Registering `--glitch-blur` as a `<length>` makes the radius
  interpolate smoothly and lets a single keyframe set serve every tier by animating the token. More
  valuable here than in any sibling, because radius is the thing that *must* scale with type size —
  one ramp, five tiers, no duplicated keyframes.
* **Scroll-driven lock with a narrow range.** `animation-timeline: view()` with
  `animation-range: entry 15% entry 45%` scrubs sharpness against viewport entry with no script.
  The narrow range is the innovation, not the timeline: it keeps a parked scroll position almost
  always on the sharp side, which is what makes a scrubbed blur safe to ship at all.
* **View Transitions as a route-level focus pull.** With cross-document view transitions, the
  incoming page can resolve into sharpness using `::view-transition-new`, which is a far better use
  of the gesture than repeating it per section — the site's navigation becomes the camera.
* **`backdrop-filter` inversion.** Blurring the *backdrop* while the text stays sharp produces the
  same "signal acquiring" read with none of the legibility cost, because the glyphs never soften.
  Different performance profile (the blurred region is larger), but worth testing when the headline
  sits over imagery and contrast is already tight.
* **SVG filter primitives for real signal artefacts.** `feTurbulence` plus `feDisplacementMap` can
  produce genuine analogue distortion — rolling noise rather than a uniform Gaussian blur — driven
  by an animated `baseFrequency`. Far more authentic than `blur()`, and far more expensive; viable
  for a one-shot hero on desktop, not for anything on mobile.
* **Variable-font optical-size axis instead of blur.** Animating `opsz` (or a custom weight axis)
  changes how the letterforms are *drawn* rather than rasterising and smearing them, so the text
  never truly loses its edges. It reads as a focus pull without the contrast cost or the raster
  pass — the most promising direction for making this variant genuinely cheap, and it degrades to
  nothing on a static font.

## 12. Conversion / UX Killers

* **The headline that stays blurred.** The worst failure in the glitch family: a `fill-mode:
  forwards` holding a soft frame, an inline filter a script never cleared, or a settle state that
  lived only in the keyframe's `100%` stop. The page looks broken in a way that cannot be read
  around, and it will not throw an error. Fix: `filter: none` in the base rule, in the
  reduced-motion branch, and in print; clear inline filters on settle (§9.4); return to an explicit
  `idle` state.
* **`filter` on a wrapper, breaking things that look unrelated.** A non-`none` filter creates a
  stacking context *and* a containing block for fixed and absolutely positioned descendants. Put it
  on a hero wrapper and a `position: sticky` header inside stops sticking, a `position: fixed`
  modal positions against the wrapper, and `z-index` ordering reshuffles. Nothing about those
  symptoms points at an animation. Fix: filter a text-only leaf, verified by the ancestor test in
  §9.17.
* **Blur as the expensive mistake on mobile.** Raster cost scales with the filtered area and the
  radius, so a 6px blur on a full-bleed section or a 76px headline drops frames on low-end Android —
  during the first second of the page. Fix: smallest possible element, `contain: paint`, radius
  ≤6px, and slice tear when the device budget is tight.
* **`will-change: filter` left on permanently.** The classic premature-optimisation bug: a promoted
  layer that costs memory for the life of the page, multiplied by every instance. Fix: gate it to
  `data-glitch-state="glitching"` and drop it on idle (§9.2), verified in §9.17.
* **The blurred scanline.** Putting the scanline or interlace overlay *inside* the filtered node
  blurs it into grey wash, which both loses the effect and further obscures the glyphs. Fix:
  decoration is a sibling of the filtered node, never a child.
* **A monotonic ramp sold as a glitch.** Blur that only ever decreases is a blur-in — a perfectly
  good entrance animation, but it is not this component, and shipping it as "the glitch option"
  means the brand gesture never lands. Fix: at least one lock point, with sharpness going backwards
  after it.
* **The scrubbed lock parked soft.** A wide `animation-range` lets a visitor stop scrolling with the
  headline mid-blur and leave it there. Fix: narrow range near the entry edge, or use the one-shot
  trigger.
* **The replay that silently does nothing.** Re-applying the same `animation-name` does not restart
  a CSS animation. Fix: alternate between two identically-defined keyframe names (§9.2), or use
  `getAnimations()` with `cancel()` and `play()`.
* **Text antialiasing shifting at the edges of the pass.** Applying any filter typically forces text
  onto grayscale antialiasing instead of subpixel, so glyph weight changes subtly the instant the
  filter is applied and again when it is removed. It reads as a tiny unexplained flicker on the
  settled headline. Fix: accept it (it is small), or keep a permanent `filter: opacity(1)` so the
  rendering path never changes — at the cost of a permanent layer, which is usually the wrong trade.
* **Lock on the wrong content.** Over a price, an error, a form label or a confirmation number,
  blur makes the visitor doubt the *data* while also preventing them from reading it. Fix: display
  type only.

## 13. Advanced Patterns

* **The ramp as data, not keyframes.** Expressing the pass as a list of `(progress, multiplier)`
  pairs — as §9.14's `ramp`, §9.15's `keyframes` block and §9.16's `TweenSequence` all do — rather
  than hardcoding radii makes the lock points explicit, makes the tier scaling a single
  multiplication, and makes the "is this monotonic?" question answerable by inspection. On the web
  the same shape is achievable with `@property`-typed tokens plus `calc()` in the keyframes (§9.2),
  which is why those keyframes multiply `var(--glitch-blur)` rather than naming pixel values.

* **Containment as a performance contract.** `display: inline-block` plus `contain: paint` on the
  filtered node is not cosmetic: it bounds the raster region to the element's own box, so the
  compositor does not have to consider ancestors or siblings when rasterising the blur. Paired with
  the rule that the filter goes on a text-only leaf, this turns an unbounded cost into a bounded
  one — and it is why the same keyframes are cheap on a headline and expensive on a section.

* **A `will-change` window rather than a `will-change` declaration.** `will-change: filter` is only
  correct *during* the pass. The pattern is to bind it to the same state attribute that drives the
  animation and to set `will-change: auto` on idle, so the promoted layer exists for 900ms and not
  for the life of the page. This generalises: any `will-change` that is not scoped to a state is
  usually a memory leak with good intentions.

* **TypeScript enforcing the safety ceiling.** Scramble needs `children: string`; signal lock needs
  the radius bounded, because radius is simultaneously a performance limit and a legibility limit:

  ```ts
  type BlurRadius = 1.5 | 2.5 | 4 | 6;          // tier ceilings; 6px is the house max
  interface SignalProps { blur?: BlurRadius; contrast?: number & { __max?: 1.7 } }
  ```

  A literal union makes `blur={12}` a build error rather than an audit finding, and it documents the
  tier table in the type. The §9.17 test asserts this with `@ts-expect-error`, so the ceiling is
  covered by the test suite rather than by a comment.

* **Capability-gated progressive enhancement.** The hook checks
  `CSS.supports("animation-timeline: view()")` and, when it is true with `trigger="scroll"`, does
  nothing at all — no observer, no state, no timer. The modern path is the zero-code path and the
  JavaScript is the legacy fallback, which means the fallback can be deleted outright when support
  is universal without touching the component's API.

* **Knowing when to leave the family.** The genuinely senior judgement on this variant is that two
  of its own alternatives are often better than it: `backdrop-filter` on the surface behind the text
  gives the same read with no legibility cost, and animating a variable font's optical-size axis
  gives it with no raster cost. Reaching for `filter: blur()` on text should be a decision, not a
  default — and this page's job is to make the trade explicit rather than to sell the effect.

## 14. Performance & Bundle Cost

* **Zero JavaScript, non-zero raster — the family's one real trade.** With
  `animation-timeline: view()` this variant ships about 1.3KB of gzipped CSS and no script. But
  where slice tear's properties are compositor and paint, `filter: blur()` triggers a **raster**
  pass each frame, and its cost scales with both the filtered area and the radius. Slice tear is
  free in both senses; signal lock is free in bundle terms and paid for in GPU terms.
* **Bound the area, cap the radius.** The two levers that matter: keep the filter on the smallest
  element that produces the effect (a text-only `inline-block` span with `contain: paint`), and keep
  the radius ≤6px. A 6px blur on a 58px headline is invisible in a frame profile; the same keyframes
  on a full-bleed section or at 3× the radius is a different component performance-wise.
* **`will-change: filter` gated to the pass.** Promoting the layer makes the blur meaningfully
  cheaper to re-raster during the animation, and leaving it promoted afterwards costs memory for the
  life of the page. Scope it to the glitching state and release it on idle (§9.2, §13), and verify
  with the computed-style test in §9.17.
* **The grayscale-antialiasing side effect.** Applying any filter typically moves text off subpixel
  antialiasing, so there is a small rendering change at both edges of the pass. It costs nothing in
  frame time but it is a visible artefact; §12 covers the trade-off between accepting it and holding
  a permanent layer.
* **Mobile is where this is decided.** On a mid-range Android, a 6px blur on a hero headline is
  comfortably within budget; stacked with a `backdrop-filter` panel and a blurred hero image in the
  same viewport, the raster passes compound and frames drop. The 2026 discipline, as §8b puts it, is
  one blurred thing at a time — and this component should be the one that yields when something
  else on the page already blurs.
* **Tree-shaking and import discipline.** No JavaScript is required, so none should be pulled:
  export it as CSS plus optional markup, never through a barrel file that also exports
  `useScramble`. Do not reach for GSAP to tween `filter` — the library cannot make raster cheaper,
  which is the only cost here.
* **Nothing to lazy-load.** No runtime to dynamically import; the text is in the initial HTML and
  the animation is in the stylesheet, so the effect is complete at first paint.
* **Core Web Vitals.** CLS is zero (`filter` does not participate in layout, verified in §9.17).
  LCP is unaffected by hydration, but note one subtlety specific to this variant: if the LCP element
  is the blurred text, a browser may record the LCP paint at the blurred frame — the element is
  painted, just soft. That does not change the metric's timing, but it does mean the visitor's *first
  readable* paint is later than LCP suggests, which is a real experience cost the number hides. INP
  is unaffected: no main-thread work.
* **Font loading interaction, and the absence of one.** No glyph pool, so no tofu hazard and nothing
  to gate on `document.fonts.ready`. A font swap mid-pass changes metrics but cannot corrupt
  anything — one less failure mode than scramble.

## 15. Security

This variant has **no meaningful attack surface of its own**, and the honest version of this section
is short rather than padded.

There is no runtime in the default build: no JavaScript, no DOM writes, no derived string, no
glyph pool, no `innerHTML`, no `eval`, no storage, no network call, no third-party script. The
component is a stylesheet and one element containing text. Where the optional JS trigger is used
(§9.4), it toggles a `data-` attribute, sets a timer, and clears an inline `filter` — it never
writes content, so the XSS vector scramble must engineer against does not exist here.

What remains is the **headline string**, and it is not this component's boundary: the text is
rendered through the framework's normal escaping (Jinja autoescape in §9.13, JSX interpolation in
§9.3), the same protection any other text on the page gets. The server-side validation is therefore
thin by design — bound the duration (200–1200ms), restrict size and flavour to closed sets, cap
length, and reject Unicode control and bidi-formatting codepoints, that last one because bidi
overrides (U+202E and relatives) can make rendered text read differently from its source, a
spoofing vector in any string a server echoes into a page.

**The one input that is genuinely this component's own is the blur radius**, and it is clamped
server-side (§9.13) and typed as a literal union (§13) for a reason that is about **safety and
accessibility rather than security**: an unbounded radius makes the headline unreadable for longer
and crushes its effective contrast, and an unbounded radius on a large element is a self-inflicted
frame-rate problem. A client-supplied value should not be able to degrade a visitor's ability to
read the page, so it is enforced in the same place and with the same finality as any other
untrusted input. The same logic covers the contrast multiplier, capped at 1.7 for flash safety.

**Authorization.** The component gates nothing and renders no privileged content. The only caveat
is a CMS-driven hero: whoever can edit the headline can put arbitrary text on the most prominent
element of the page, so that write path needs the same role check as any other published content.
That is the CMS's boundary, not the component's.

## 16. Senior-Level Checklist

* **Semantics** — the host keeps its native element and exactly one accessible name; the variant
  adds no role.
* **Single element** — one copy of the text, no `aria-hidden` duplicate, no generated-content
  copies.
* **`filter: none` is the base state** — declared in the base rule, the reduced-motion branch and
  the print branch, never only at the keyframe's `100%` stop. The headline must be unable to stay
  blurred.
* **Filter on a text-only leaf** — never on a wrapper containing positioned, sticky or interactive
  content, because a filtered element is a stacking context and a containing block, and it blurs
  descendant focus rings. Verified by the ancestor test.
* **Decoration outside the filtered node** — scanline and interlace are siblings, not children.
* **Non-monotonic ramp with lock points** — at least one return to sharp mid-pass, or it is a
  blur-in rather than a glitch.
* **Radius ≤6px, scaled to the type** (~0.1em), with the ceiling enforced in the type system and
  clamped server-side.
* **Contrast swing ≤1.7**, and never the luminance-flicker variant on a near-black field with
  near-white text.
* **Unreadable time under ~400ms**, measured, not assumed.
* **`contain: paint` and `display: inline-block`** on the filtered node, bounding the raster region.
* **`will-change: filter` gated to the pass** and released on idle; never a permanent declaration.
* **Restart works** — alternating keyframe names, or `getAnimations()` with `cancel()` and
  `play()`; re-applying the same animation name is a silent no-op.
* **Scrubbed range is narrow** and near the entry edge, so a parked scroll position lands sharp.
* **Reduced motion** — `animation: none` *and* `filter: none` forced, `will-change: auto`,
  decoration removed, on web and all three native targets.
* **Print** — filter cleared; a blurred heading in a PDF outlives the session.
* **Contrast** — settled text at 4.5:1 (3:1 large), verified against the darkest and lightest
  regions it crosses, with the understanding that mid-pass contrast is lower and is mitigated by
  radius and duration rather than measured.
* **Keyboard and focus** — the text is not focusable; any replay control is a real `button` at a
  ≥44px target with a visible ring; hover softening never fires on `:focus-visible`.
* **No `aria-live`**, and `aria-hidden` on decoration only.
* **Zero JavaScript on the modern path** — no hook, no observer, no barrel import dragging in the
  scramble runtime.
* **Content fit** — display type only (≥32px for the default tier); never on prices, numbers,
  labels, errors, form text, body copy, or anything a user must read continuously or trust.

## 17. Visual Styles

The same blur-and-lock skeleton across the 11 core visual languages. Each note is about *this*
variant — the radius, the contrast swing, the lock points, and what the softness reads as.

1. **Flat.** Low radius, no contrast swing (the focus-stutter sub-variant), one lock point, no
   scanline. On a flat field the blur is the only depth cue in the design, so it reads as
   surprisingly sophisticated — and it is the version that survives on a light background, where
   bigger radii wash out.
2. **Material.** The most natural fit in the family: M3 motion is continuous, so the lock becomes an
   M3 blur-in with one extra sharpness failure. Use display type roles, emphasized-decelerate
   easing, drop the contrast swing, and keep a single lock point. This is the one skin where the
   glitch and the system genuinely agree.
3. **Glassmorphism.** Risky and worth the care: the panel is already blurred, so blurring the text
   on top compounds the softness and contrast collapses. Use the luminance-flicker sub-variant
   instead — sharp glyphs, swinging brightness — or move the blur to the backdrop and leave the text
   crisp (§11).
4. **Liquid Glass (2026).** The variant this skin was waiting for. The lock reads as refraction
   finding focus, with a specular glint instead of a hard scanline and the radius kept low so the
   type never fully dissolves. Pair it with the chromatic-aberration sub-variant at 1px for a lens
   reading. The right glitch for an Apple-adjacent surface, where a tear would be the wrong grammar
   entirely.
5. **Neumorphism.** Blur plus soft shadows is a legibility trap — this style's contrast is already
   marginal, and softening the glyphs pushes it under. Use the shortest duration, the smallest
   radius, one lock point, no contrast swing, and treat it as a 300ms grace note rather than an
   effect.
6. **Skeuomorphism.** Full analogue signal: the interlace overlay at low opacity, a lingering
   scanline with bloom, a brightness swing alongside the blur, and the SVG `feTurbulence` route
   (§11) if the budget allows. This skin wants genuine noise rather than a clean Gaussian, and it is
   the one place the expensive version is justified.
7. **Neo-Brutalism.** An awkward pairing worth naming: brutalism is about hard edges, and blur
   softens them. If the skin demands a glitch, use [Slice Tear](/knowledge/glitch-slice-tear). If
   you must lock, use the luminance-flicker sub-variant — maximum contrast swing, zero blur — so the
   type stays as hard as the design language.
8. **Claymorphism.** The blur reads as depth of field rather than failure: puffy letterforms
   drifting in and out of focus against a soft field, warm contrast swing, no scanline. Gentle and
   quite beautiful, and the one skin where this variant is unambiguously the right pick over its
   siblings.
9. **Aurora / Gradient.** The blur smears the gradient fill into the letterforms, so colour bleeds
   through the glyph edges as sharpness returns — the most visually interesting version of this
   variant. Verify 4.5:1 at every stop the settled text crosses, and keep the radius at 4px so the
   bleed reads as intentional rather than as a rendering fault.
10. **Minimal / Swiss.** One lock point, 2px radius, no contrast swing, no scanline, tightly tracked
    type on a strict grid. The softness is almost subliminal — a single beat of the page finding its
    focus before precision reasserts itself. The most restrained thing in the entire glitch library,
    and the one that belongs on an editorial or institutional page.
11. **UJG Brand (house default).** Platinum display type on deep Night/Eminence, 6px radius with the
    contrast swing at 1.7, two lock points, a single Goldenrod scanline riding the line once,
    Methanerse for the headline face, 900ms on the house easing. Reads as a transmission coming in
    from somewhere — the Amazonia-adjacent register of the glitch family, warmer and more patient
    than Shon's tear, and the right choice for a UJG page that has to carry authority as well as
    attitude.

**The rule that never changes:** style is skin, behaviour is the skeleton. Across all 11 skins there
is one copy of the real text, the animation touches only `filter`, `filter: none` is the base state
in every escape hatch, the filter sits on a contained text-only leaf, the ramp is non-monotonic with
lock points, radius and contrast stay under their ceilings, `will-change` is scoped to the pass,
reduced motion declares no animation and no filter, and the lock never sits on an ancestor of
interactive content or on text a visitor has to read continuously. Semantics, the accessible name,
focus behaviour of adjacent controls, and the 44px target on any replay control are identical no
matter which skin is on top.
