## 1 · Definition
**Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)** is the time, measured from when the page
starts loading, at which the largest image or text block visible in the viewport
has rendered. It is one of Google's **Core Web Vitals**.

It is **not** the same as First Contentful Paint (the *first* pixel of content),
nor "page fully loaded" — LCP tracks only the single largest element currently in
view, and that element can change as the page loads. Rule of thumb: if you asked
a user "what's the main thing on this screen?", LCP is roughly when that thing
appeared.

## 2 · Etymology & origin
Introduced by the Chrome team as part of the **Web Vitals** initiative in 2020,
as a more perception-aligned successor to older proxies like "load" and First
Meaningful Paint. It is exposed to pages through the browser's
`PerformanceObserver` as a `largest-contentful-paint` entry, specified by the
W3C Largest Contentful Paint API.

## 3 · Operation instances — Common
- **Hero image.** On a marketing or product page, the banner image is usually the LCP element.
- **Headline text block.** On an article, the `<h1>` or lead paragraph is often largest in view.
- **Above-the-fold poster.** A video's poster frame or a large card image standing in before media loads.

## 4 · Operation instances — Rare
- **CSS `background-image` LCP.** When the largest element is painted via a background image rather than an `<img>`, it still counts — and is easy to miss when optimizing.
- **Late element swap.** The LCP element is reported, then a larger element paints later and replaces it, moving the reported time.
- **SVG or `<video>` first frame.** Less common largest-elements that still qualify under the spec.

## 5 · Research findings
web.dev documents the field thresholds: an LCP of **2.5 s or less** is "good",
2.5–4 s "needs improvement", and over 4 s "poor", assessed at the **75th
percentile** of page loads across mobile and desktop. Google's guidance ties
faster LCP to better page experience; the exact commercial effect varies by site
and should be measured per-property rather than assumed from a benchmark.

## 6 · Proven occurrences
- **Shipped as a Core Web Vital (2020).** LCP is a stable, measurable entry in the browser Performance API — verifiable by observing `largest-contentful-paint` entries in any Chromium browser.
- **Became part of Google Search ranking signals.** Google's page-experience update incorporated Core Web Vitals (including LCP) into ranking, as documented on Google Search Central.

## 7 · Distinctions & commonly confused with
- **First Contentful Paint (FCP)** — the first *any* content paints; LCP is the *largest*. A fast FCP with a slow LCP means something big is arriving late.
- **Time to Interactive (TTI)** — when the page can reliably respond to input; LCP is about visible rendering, not interactivity.
- **Speed Index** — the average visual completeness over time; LCP is a single timestamp, not an area-under-curve.

## 8 · Usage & register
Used by front-end and performance engineers, and in SEO/page-experience
conversations. Formal and precise — always say which percentile and which
form-factor you mean.

> "Our p75 mobile LCP regressed to 3.1 s after the new hero carousel — it's the
> background-image, not the markup."
